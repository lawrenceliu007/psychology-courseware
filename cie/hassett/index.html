🐵 Hassett et al. (2008)

Sex Differences in Rhesus Monkey Toy Preferences Parallel Those of Children
（恒河猴玩具偏好的性别差异与人类儿童平行）
Hassett, S.E., Siebert, E.R., & Wallen, K. (2008)
Evolution and Human Behavior, 29(4), 264–271
🐵 Males = Wheeled Toys 👩 Females = Plush Dolls 🧬 Biological Basis? 🧠 Biological Approach

1️⃣ Background（研究背景）

🎯

Research Question

Are gender-typed toy preferences innate (biological) or learned (socialised)?

The Nature vs Nurture Debate

One of the most enduring questions in developmental psychology is whether behavioural differences between males and females are the result of biological factors (nature) or socialisation processes (nurture).

Children's toy preferences have long been observed to follow gender-stereotyped patterns:

  • Boys tend to prefer wheeled toys, vehicles, and construction sets
  • Girls tend to prefer dolls, plush toys, and role-play items
Figure 2.6: Gender-stereotyped toy displays in retail stores — wheeled toys (left) typically marketed to boys, plush dolls (right) marketed to girls
Gender-typed toy preferences: The tendency for individuals to show consistent preferences for toys that are culturally associated with their own gender.

Previous Research Limitations

Studies with human children cannot definitively separate biological from social influences because:

  1. Children are exposed to gender socialisation from birth (parents, peers, media)
  2. Ethical constraints prevent controlled experiments on human infants
  3. Even very young children may have already learned gender-appropriate behaviours

The Rhesus Monkey Solution 🔑

Hassett et al. (2008) proposed an elegant solution: study rhesus monkeys (Macaca mulatta), a non-human primate species that:

  • Shares approximately 93-95% of human DNA
  • Exhibits similar brain structure and hormonal influences
  • Has NOT been exposed to human gender socialisation or cultural stereotypes
  • Can be observed in naturalistic settings without ethical concerns about manipulation

⚠️ Critical Thinking Point

If monkeys show the same gender-typed toy preferences as human children, this would provide compelling evidence for a biological basis of such preferences — as monkeys have not been influenced by human culture, media, or peer pressure.

§1 研究背景

逻辑链：人类儿童存在性别刻板印象的玩具偏好 → 无法确定是先天还是后天 → 用恒河猴测试（无社会化影响）→ 如果猴子也有同样偏好，说明可能是生物因素。

  • Nature vs Nurture（先天 vs 后天）：发展心理学的核心争论
  • Gender Socialisation（性别社会化）：从出生开始接受的社会影响
  • Rhesus Monkeys（恒河猴）：与人类基因高度相似的非人灵长类动物

💡 考试要点：CIE 常问 "Why use rhesus monkeys?" 答案：排除社会化变量、伦理可行性、与人类高度相似的生物学基础。

▶ Exam Key Points — Background AO1+AO3
  • Gender differences in toy preferences well-documented in children Must Know
  • Cannot determine nature vs nurture from human studies alone Common Q
  • Rhesus monkeys share ~93-95% DNA with humans Context
  • Monkeys provide "natural experiment" free from human socialisation Must Know
  • If monkeys show same pattern → supports biological explanation AO2 Gold

2️⃣ Aim（研究目的）

🎯 Research Aim

To investigate whether rhesus monkeys exhibit sex differences in toy preferences that parallel those observed in human children.

Specific Objectives

  1. To measure the amount of time male and female monkeys spend interacting with different types of toys
  2. To compare these preferences across sexes
  3. To determine if any observed differences mirror human gender-typed toy preferences

Research Hypotheses

Alternative Hypothesis (H₁)

Male monkeys will spend significantly more time with wheeled toys, while female monkeys will spend significantly more time with plush toys.

This would support a biological basis for gender-typed preferences.

Null Hypothesis (H₀)

There will be no significant difference in toy preferences between male and female rhesus monkeys.

This would suggest that gender-typed preferences in humans are primarily due to socialisation.

§2 研究目的

核心目标：调查恒河猴是否表现出与人类儿童平行的性别差异玩具偏好。

  • H₁ (备择假设)：雄猴偏好轮式玩具，雌猴偏好毛绒玩具 → 支持生物基础论
  • H₀ (零假设)：两性之间无显著差异 → 支持社会化理论

💡 考试技巧：CIE 可能要求你写出 H₁ 和 H₀。记住：H₁ 是研究者希望证明的假设（有方向性），H₀ 是无差异假设。

▶ Exam Key Points — Aim & Hypotheses AO1
  • Aim: Investigate sex differences in monkey toy preferences Must Know
  • H₁: Males prefer wheeled toys; females prefer plush toys Must Know
  • H₀: No significant difference between sexes Common Q
  • Directional (one-tailed) hypothesis based on previous research AO2 Gold

⚠️ Why This Sample Size Matters

N=84 is a reasonably large sample for primate research. Larger samples increase statistical power and make it easier to detect genuine effects. The near-equal split (40M : 44F) also allows fair comparison between sexes.

3.3 Materials / Stimuli 🧸

Researchers selected six categories of toys, classified into three types:

Toy TypeExamplesHuman Gender Association
Wheeled Toys ("Masculine") Dump truck, wagon, race car, police car, fire truck Typically preferred by boys
Plush Toys ("Feminine") Doll, stuffed animals, teddy bear, rag doll Typically preferred by girls
Neutral Toys Picture books, non-wheeled non-plush items No clear gender association
Operationalisation of DV: The dependent variable was operationalised as the duration of physical contact (in seconds) between each monkey and each type of toy during observation sessions.

3.4 Controls 🛡️

  • All toys were novel to the monkeys (no prior exposure)
  • Toys were presented in a standardised arrangement
  • Observation conditions were kept consistent across sessions
  • Same observers used throughout to maintain inter-rater reliability
  • Time of day and environmental conditions were controlled
§3 研究方法

设计类型：Naturalistic Observation（自然观察法） + Independent Measures（独立样本设计）

  • IV (自变量)：猴子的性别（雄性/雌性）

  • Sample (样本)：84只恒河猴（40雄 + 44雌），年龄1-3岁
  • Controls (控制变量)：玩具新颖性、标准化摆放、一致的环境条件

💡 考试要点：能说出 IV/DV 的操作化定义是 AO1 高分关键！DV = 物理接触时长（秒）。

4️⃣ Procedure（实验程序）

📋

Step-by-Step Process

Standardised presentation of toys + timed observation sessions

Phase 1: Preparation

  1. Six different toys (two from each category) were selected and cleaned
  2. Toys were placed in the monkeys' familiar living enclosure in a standardised arrangement
  3. Recording equipment was set up to capture interactions unobtrusively

Phase 2: Data Collection

  1. Individual monkeys were allowed to freely explore the toy area
  2. Observers recorded the duration of contact between each monkey and each toy using stopwatches
  3. Contact was defined as physical touching or holding of the toy
  4. Each monkey was observed over multiple sessions to ensure reliability
  5. Total observation time varied but was consistent across individuals

Phase 3: Data Recording

  • For each monkey, researchers calculated:
    • Total time spent with wheeled toys
    • Total time spent with plush toys
    • Total time spent with neutral toys
  • Data was then analysed comparing males vs females across all categories

💡 Think About It

Why did researchers use the monkeys' familiar enclosure rather than a novel testing room?

Answer: Using a familiar environment reduces stress and allows more natural behaviour — increasing ecological validity. Stressed animals may not show typical preferences.

§4 实验程序

三阶段流程：

  • Phase 1 (准备)：选择6个玩具（每类2个），清洁后放入熟悉的生活区域
  • Phase 2 (数据收集)：让猴子自由探索，记录员用秒表记录每次接触时长
  • Phase 3 (数据整理)：计算每只猴子与三类玩具的总接触时间

💡 关键细节："Contact" 的操作化定义 = 物理触摸或持有玩具的时间。在熟悉环境中进行 = 提高生态效度。

▶ Exam Key Points — Procedure AO1

  • Free exploration — no forced interaction Common Q
  • DV measured via stopwatch: duration of physical contact Must Know
  • Multiple sessions per individual for reliability Context
  • Familiar environment increases ecological validity AO2 Gold

The fact that monkeys — who have never been exposed to human gender stereotypes, media, or social pressure — show the same pattern as human children strongly suggests that:

Gender-typed toy preferences may have a biological / evolutionary basis 🧬

§5 研究结果

核心发现：支持 H₁（备择假设）！雄猴和雌猴表现出与人类儿童平行的玩具偏好差异。

  • Male Monkeys (雄猴)：花更多时间玩轮式玩具（车辆类）→ 类似男孩偏好
  • Female Monkeys (雌猴)：花更多时间玩毛绒玩具（娃娃类）→ 类似女孩偏好
  • Neutral Toys (中性玩具)：两性无显著差异

💡 关键推论：猴子未受人类社会化影响 → 偏好可能是先天的/生物性的 → 支持 Nature 论点。

  • Females preferred plush toys significantly more than males Must Know

6️⃣ Discussion（讨论）

6.1 Explanation of Findings

Hassett et al. interpreted their findings as evidence that gender-typed toy preferences may have an evolutionary/biological basis. Several mechanisms were proposed:

Evolutionary Explanations: Throughout evolutionary history, males and females faced different selective pressures. Males may have developed preferences for objects that involve movement and spatial skills (useful for hunting/navigation), while females may have developed preferences for nurturing-related objects (useful for infant care).

Possible Mechanisms

  1. Prenatal hormone exposure: Testosterone levels in utero may influence later toy preferences
  2. Brain organisation theory: Early hormone exposure causes structural/functional brain differences that manifest as behavioural preferences
  3. Evolutionary psychology: These preferences conferred adaptive advantages to our ancestors

6.2 Comparison with Previous Research

Consistency with Human Studies

The findings align with extensive research on human children showing:

  • Boys as young as 12 months show preference for wheeled/moving toys
  • Girls show earlier interest in faces and social stimuli (which dolls represent)
  • Cross-cultural studies find similar patterns across diverse societies

6.3 Alternative Explanations Considered

The researchers acknowledged alternative explanations:

  • Early learning within monkey colonies: Even without human socialisation, young monkeys might learn from observing adults
  • Differential activity levels: Male monkeys are generally more active, which might lead to more interaction with movable (wheeled) objects
  • Sensory preferences: Males might be more attracted to certain colours, shapes, or textures independent of "gender" categorisation
§6 讨论

核心解释：结果支持进化心理学观点 —— 性别差异的玩具偏好可能具有生物/进化基础。

  • Prenatal Hormones (产前激素)：子宫内睾酮水平可能影响后续偏好
  • Brain Organisation (脑组织理论)：早期激素导致脑结构差异
  • Selective Pressure (选择压力)：男性和女性在进化史上面临不同适应需求

💡 考试技巧：CIE 喜欢问 "Outline one explanation for the findings"。答案模板：进化压力不同 → 男性的空间/运动技能（狩猎导航）vs 女性的抚育相关偏好（婴儿照料）。

▶ Exam Key Points — Discussion AO3
  • Findings support biological/evolutionary basis for toy preferences Must Know
  • Prenatal hormones may play a role Common Q
  • Consistent with cross-cultural human studies AO2 Gold
  • Alternative: differential activity levels in males Context

7️⃣ Conclusions（结论）

🎯 Main Conclusions

  1. Rhesus monkeys exhibit sex differences in toy preferences that parallel those found in human children
  2. Males show greater preference for wheeled toys; females show greater preference for plush toys
  3. These findings suggest that gender-typed toy preferences are not entirely socially constructed
  4. There appears to be a biological/evolutionary component to such preferences
  5. This does NOT mean socialisation is irrelevant — rather, biology and environment likely interact
"The present findings suggest that gender-typed toy preferences may reflect evolved biases rather than solely socialisation practices."
— Hassett et al. (2008, p. 270)
§7 结论

五大结论：

  1. 恒河猴表现出与人类儿童平行的性别玩具偏好差异
  2. 雄猴偏好轮式玩具，雌猴偏好毛绒玩具
  3. 这种偏好不完全是社会建构的结果
  4. 可能存在生物/进化基础
  5. 生物和环境 likely 相互作用（不是非此即彼）

💡 记忆口诀："猴子也分男女爱，车轮娃娃各有所" → 猴子研究证明偏好有生物基础。

▶ Exam Key Points — Conclusions AO1+AO3
  • Sex differences in toy preferences exist in non-human primates Must Know
  • Pattern parallels human children Must Know
  • Supports biological contribution (not purely social) Must Know
  • Biology × Environment interaction emphasised AO2 Gold

8️⃣ Evaluation（评价）

Strengths (✅ 优势)

▶ View Strengths AO3
  1. Use of non-human primates: Eliminates confounding variable of human socialisation/culture — unique strength of this study Must Know
  2. Large sample (N=84): Good statistical power, more reliable than small-sample primate studies Common Q
  3. Naturalistic setting: High ecological validity — monkeys behaved naturally in familiar environment Must Know
  4. Objective measurement: DV operationalised as duration of contact (quantifiable, reliable) Common Q
  5. Controlled variables: Novel toys, standardised procedure, consistent timing Context
  6. Evolutionary significance: Provides rare empirical evidence for biological basis of gendered behaviour AO2 Gold

Limitations (❌ 局限)

▶ View Limitations AO3
  1. Cannot rule out early learning completely: Young monkeys may observe and imitate adults in their colony Must Know
  2. Species generalisation issues: Rhesus monkeys ≠ humans; caution needed when extrapolating Common Q
  3. Correlational design: Cannot establish causation — only shows association between sex and preference Must Know
  4. Alternative explanations: Differential activity levels, sensory preferences not controlled AO2 Gold
  5. Limited age range: Only juveniles (1-3 years) studied; preferences may change with age Context
  6. Artificial toy categories: "Wheeled" vs "Plush" classification based on human stereotypes — may not map onto monkey cognition AO2 Gold
§8 评价

优势（6点）：

  • 用非人灵长类动物 → 排除社会化干扰（最大亮点！）
  • N=84 大样本 → 统计效力强
  • 自然观察环境 → 生态效度高
  • DV 客观测量（接触时长）→ 可靠

局限（6点）：

  • 不能完全排除早期学习（猴子也可能模仿成年猴）
  • 物种推广问题（猴≠人）
  • 相关设计 → 无因果关系
  • 玩具分类基于人类刻板印象 → 可能不适合猴子认知

💡 CIE 技巧：评价要 balanced！每条优缺点都要解释 WHY 重要。

9️⃣ Ethics（伦理考量）

Ethical Strengths

  • Non-invasive observation: No harm, pain, or stress inflicted on animals
  • Naturalistic setting: Monkeys remained in their normal living environment
  • Institutional approval: Study conducted at accredited primate research centre following ethical guidelines
  • No deception or manipulation: Observational only, no experimental intervention

Potential Ethical Concerns

  • Captivity: All monkeys were in captivity (though this was their normal living situation)
  • Generalisability justification: Is it ethical to study animals primarily to inform about humans? (Speciesism debate)

⚠️ CIE Ethics Evaluation Tip

When discussing ethics in animal studies, consider:
1. Were animals harmed? (No — observational)
2. Was there scientific merit? (Yes — addresses important nature/nurture question)
3. Could the study be done with humans? (Not with this level of control over socialisation)
4. Were fewer animals used than necessary? (Reasonable sample size)

Q1 Outline the sample used in Hassett et al.'s (2008) study. [4 marks] ▼

Model Answer (AO1):

The sample consisted of 84 rhesus monkeys (macaques) aged 1–3 years from the California National Primate Research Centre. There were 40 males and 44 females. The researchers used opportunity sampling — all available monkeys in the colony were included. The large sample size provided good statistical power, and the near-equal gender split allowed fair comparison between sexes. (4 marks)

💡 Mark Scheme Tips: 1 mark for N=84, 1 mark for age range, 1 mark for gender split, 1 mark for sampling method/species.

Q2 Describe the procedure of Hassett et al.'s (2008) study. [6 marks] ▼

Model Answer (AO1):

First, researchers selected six toys representing three categories: wheeled toys (masculine), plush toys (feminine), and neutral toys. These were placed in the monkeys' familiar living enclosure in a standardised arrangement. Individual monkeys were then allowed to freely explore the toy area. Observers used stopwatches to record the duration of physical contact between each monkey and each toy. Each monkey was observed over multiple sessions. Finally, total contact times were calculated for each toy category and compared between males and females. (6 marks)

💡 Mark Scheme Tips: 2 marks for materials/toy categories, 2 marks for procedure (free exploration + timing), 2 marks for data collection/recording details.

Q3 Discuss the findings of Hassett et al. (2008). What do they suggest about the origins of gender-typed toy preferences? [8 marks] ▼

Model Answer (AO1 + AO3):

AO1 (Description): The study found statistically significant sex differences in toy preferences. Male monkeys spent significantly more time with wheeled toys (vehicles, trucks), while female monkeys spent significantly more time with plush toys (dolls, stuffed animals). There was no significant difference for neutral toys. (4 marks)

AO3 (Evaluation): These findings suggest that gender-typed toy preferences may have a biological or evolutionary basis rather than being purely socially constructed. Because rhesus monkeys have not been exposed to human gender stereotypes, media, or social pressure, yet show the same pattern as human children, this indicates that such preferences could be innate or influenced by prenatal factors (such as hormone exposure in utero). However, the study cannot completely rule out early learning within monkey colonies, and caution is needed when generalising from monkeys to humans. (4 marks)

💡 Mark Scheme Tips: Balance description (what they found) with evaluation (what it means). Always mention both support for biological basis AND limitations/cautions.

Q4 Evaluate Hassett et al.'s (2008) study in terms of its strengths and limitations. [12 marks] ▼

Model Answer (AO3):

Strengths (6 marks):

  1. Use of non-human primates: Unique strength — eliminates the confounding variable of human socialisation and culture, allowing clearer test of biological influences. (2 marks)
  2. Large sample (N=84): Greater statistical power than typical primate studies; near-equal gender split allows fair comparison. (2 marks)
  3. High ecological validity: Naturalistic observation in familiar environment means monkeys exhibited natural behaviour. (2 marks)

Limitations (6 marks):

  1. Cannot establish causation: Correlational design shows association between sex and preference, but cannot prove biology causes preference. (2 marks)
  2. Species generalisation: Rhesus monkeys share much DNA with humans but are not identical; caution needed when extrapolating findings. (2 marks)
  3. Alternative explanations: Differential activity levels (males more active → interact more with movable toys) or early learning within colonies not fully ruled out. (2 marks)

💡 Exam Tip: Always give equal weight to strengths and limitations. Use psychological terminology precisely (ecological validity, confounding variable, causation vs correlation).

1️⃣2️⃣ Key Terms（核心术语）

Nature vs Nurture: The debate about whether behaviour is determined by genetics/biology (nature) or environment/upbringing (nurture).
Gender-typed behaviour: Behaviour that differs systematically between males and females and is consistent with cultural gender stereotypes.
Naturalistic observation: A research method where behaviour is observed in a natural environment without intervention or manipulation by the researcher.
Ecological validity: The extent to which findings can be generalised to real-life settings outside the laboratory.
Operationalisation: The process of defining a conceptual variable in terms of the specific method used to measure it.
Evolutionary psychology: An approach explaining behaviour as adaptations that enhanced survival and reproduction in our ancestral environment.
Prenatal hormones: Hormones (such as testosterone) present during foetal development that may influence later behaviour and preferences.
Independent measures design: An experimental design where different participants are used in each condition (here: males vs females are naturally different groups).
