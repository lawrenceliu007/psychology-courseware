Sex Differences in Rhesus Monkey Toy Preferences Parallel Those of Children

Hassett, J. M., Siebert, E. R., & Wallen, K. (2008). Sex differences in rhesus monkey toy preferences parallel those of children. Hormones and Behaviour, 54(3), 359–364.

The Psychology Being Investigated （研究主题）

Sex Differences （性别差异）

Sex differences （性别差异） are the ways that males and females differ that are caused directly by sex, and include both physical （生理） and behavioural differences （行为差异）.

There are differences in male and female brains, and in their hormones （激素）, that impact on human behaviour. For example, in our discussion of the brain for the key study by Hölzel et al., we will describe how language functions are localised （局部化） to areas in the left hemisphere. This is the case for men and women, although in women language functions are relatively more bilateral （双侧的）, i.e. spread over both sides of the brain.

There are several differences in hormones too. The most obvious is that, post puberty, males have about ten times more testosterone （睾酮） than females, and females have much more oestrogen （雌激素） than males.

However, it is difficult to determine the extent to which some behavioural differences between males and females are the product of biological differences （生物差异） due to their sex, or social differences （社会差异） in their experiences due to the different ways in which society treats males and females. Many factors are involved in the way a child experiences the social world and these add together to create the process of socialisation （社会化）.

Gender Differences in Play （玩耍中的性别差异）

One behaviour in which differences can be seen between males and females is in play （玩耍）. We often see boys engaging in active, sometimes aggressive, play with guns, toy tractors and building sets. Girls, however, are more usually seen engaging in nurturing play （养育性游戏） with dolls, playhouses and kitchen sets, i.e. being more passive and caring.

In terms of socialisation, this isn't surprising, as girls and boys are encouraged to play with gender-stereotyped toys （性别刻板印象玩具）, that is, toys that fit society's view of gender-appropriate behaviour (Figure 2.6). This influence has long been assumed to be the primary cause of gender differences in play.

📌 KEY WORDS （关键词）

socialisation: the process of learning to behave in socially acceptable ways. This may differ somewhat for the two genders and in different cultures.
gender stereotype: a bias exhibited in society, which may be held by people and represented, for example, in books or toys that assign particular traits, behaviours, emotions, occupations, etc. to males and females.
play: behaviour typical of childhood, that appears to be done for fun rather than any useful purpose. It may be solitary or social and may or may not involve interaction with an object. Objects designed for the purpose of play are called 'toys'.
2

Background & Aim （背景与目的）

Background （研究背景）

Humans are complicated. Our brains are highly complex in structure and function and our behaviour is affected by our social and physical environments as well as our biology. This complexity makes humans difficult to study, as so many factors could be affecting any one outcome.

In some research, the complexity of the human experience can be simplified by excluding many variables, for example in a laboratory experiment （实验室实验） where the effect of a single independent variable （自变量） can be studied by controlling other, potentially confounding variables （混淆变量）, and by having a control condition （控制条件） from which the independent variable is absent.

🔬 Key Finding: Prenatal Hormonal Exposure

Hassett et al. described an important finding that prenatal hormonal exposure （产前激素暴露） of human infants (the hormones they are exposed to during gestation) affects toy preferences in children.

Some girls have a genetic condition （遗传疾病） that causes increased androgens （雄激素） (male hormones) during foetal development. Such girls preferred to play with traditionally 'boys' toys than their unaffected sisters or control females — even when they received increased socialisation to encourage 'female-appropriate' activities.

Why Use Animals? （为什么使用动物？）

It would be unethical to deliberately expose developing human foetuses or children to hormones, or to remove key factors from a child's environment to control their experience. However, a model （模型） for such research exists — a representation of a phenomenon, often on a smaller or simpler scale.

Higher primates, closely related to humans genetically, and with some very similar behaviours, can provide such a model. Social activities such as 'rough-and-tumble', peer preferences and interest in infants differ between the genders. These activities are seen not only in children but also in other primates, who show gender differences which match those of children.

Young primates, like young humans, have different levels of hormones according to their sex. Prepubescent males have more testosterone (associated with strength, aggressive behaviour and spatial skills). Young females have more oestrogen than males (associated with nurturing behaviours such as caring for offspring).

Aim （研究目的）

The aim of the study was to investigate whether toy preferences in monkeys resemble those in children, in order to test whether sex differences in toy choice is biologically determined by sex.

（目的是调查猴子的玩具偏好是否与儿童相似，以测试玩具选择中的性别差异是否由生物性别决定。）

3

Method （研究方法）

Research Method and Design （研究方法与设计）

This was a field experiment （现场实验） as the environment in which the participants were tested was their normal, outdoor housing area and they were free to interact with the toys or not.

The experimental design （实验设计） was independent measures （独立测量设计） as the independent variable was gender （性别）; each animal being recorded was either male or female.

Observations （观察法） were used as a technique to measure the dependent variable （因变量） of activities with the toys. A correlation （相关分析） was also conducted to look for a relationship between individual monkeys' ranks within the social hierarchy （社会等级） and the frequency or duration of activities with each toy type.

Sample （样本）

🐵 Participants (被试)

  • 21 male and 61 female rhesus monkeys （21只雄性和61只雌性恒河猴）
  • Living in natal groups （出生群体） as part of a wider group of 135 animals
  • Location: Yerkes primate research station （耶基斯灵长类研究中心）, USA

Exclusions:

  • 14 adults were not studied (had received hormone treatments)
  • 39 young infants were excluded (could not be reliably identified)
  • Final sample: 11 males and 23 females

Each natal group was housed in a 25 × 25 metre outdoor area with access to a temperature-controlled indoor environment. They had water available, standard monkey feed twice daily, plus additional fruit and vegetables once daily.

4

Procedure （实验程序）

Toy Categories （玩具分类）

To reflect differences in activity preferences in sexually differentiated behaviours, the toys chosen were divided into two categories by their properties as objects rather than gender-typing, although the outcome was comparable to traditional 'boys' toys', 'girls' toys' divide:

🚗 Wheeled Toys ('Masculine') （轮式玩具/'男性化'）

Six wheeled toys ranging in length from 16 to 46 cm:

  1. Wagon (四轮马车)
  2. Truck (卡车)
  3. Car (小汽车)
  4. Construction vehicle (工程车)
  5. Shopping cart (购物车)
  6. Dump truck (翻斗车)

🧸 Plush Toys ('Feminine') （毛绒玩具/'女性化'）

Seven plush toys ranging in length from 14 to 73 cm:

  1. Winnie-the-Pooh™ (小熊维尼)
  2. Raggedy-Ann™ (布娃娃)
  3. Koala hand puppet (考拉手偶)
  4. Armadillo (犰狳)
  5. Teddy bear (泰迪熊)
  6. Scooby-Doo™ (史酷比)
  7. Turtle (乌龟)

Apart from size, the toys also varied in terms of shape and colour.

Experimental Procedure （实验程序）

  1. For each social group, seven trials （7次试验） were observed, each lasting 25 minutes
  2. Two video cameras recorded the sessions
  3. Each trial began with all monkeys indoors while one plush toy and one wheeled toy were placed 10 metres apart in the outdoor enclosure
  4. Toys were counterbalanced （平衡/轮换） between left and right locations across trials
  5. After each trial, toys were removed and videos analysed by two observers working together to achieve consensus
  6. Observers identified each animal interacting with a toy and coded specific activities using a behavioural checklist (see Table 2.3)
  7. Both frequency and duration of continuous activities were recorded

⚠️ Note: Research Methods Issue

Rhesus monkeys are a highly intelligent, social species, so their needs in terms of housing and protection from pain and distress are great. However, those same characteristics make them excellent models for human behaviour. There is therefore a balance between potential benefits of valid research and costs to individual animals involved.

Results （结果）

Data Analysis （数据分析）

To analyse the data, records for each behaviour for each animal were converted into an overall average frequency and duration for that individual. Not all individuals interacted with the toys — those with fewer than five total recorded behaviours were excluded (14 males and 3 females). Final analysis: 11 males and 23 females.

Comparisons were made between males and females and between each gender's preference for wheeled or plush toys using both frequency （频率） and duration （时长）.

Key Findings （主要发现）

✅ Statistical Analysis Results:

  1. Males preferred wheeled compared to plush toys (using frequency data)
  2. Females did not show a significant preference for plush toys over wheeled toys
  3. Females interacted with plush toys more than males, but no sex difference for wheeled toys
  4. Males interacted for longer time with wheeled toys than plush toys
  5. No significant difference in total time between sexes

Magnitude of Preference （偏好幅度）

Further comparisons calculated 'magnitude of preference' （偏好幅度） — how much males preferred 'masculine' toys and how much females preferred 'feminine' toys:

  • Male monkeys had a higher preference for 'masculine' (wheeled) toys ✅
  • Female monkeys had a higher preference for 'feminine' (plush) toys ✅

Additional Findings （其他发现）

  • Rank correlation: Rank correlated with toy interactions in females but NOT males → unlikely that social rank explains sex differences
  • Age comparison: No difference between juvenile, subadult, adult, and old animals in terms of frequency or duration

Hassett et al. compared monkey data to children's data from another study (Berenbaum and Hines, 1992). Both rhesus monkeys and human children showed gender differences, with males preferring masculine toys and females preferring feminine toys. The preference was much more marked for males than for females in both species.

7

Conclusion （结论）

🎯 Main Conclusions:

  1. Like boys, male monkeys have a strong preference for masculine-type toys (ones with wheels)
  2. Female monkeys, like young girls, are more variable in their toy preferences
  3. Male monkeys showed much stronger preferences for wheeled toys than female monkeys did for plush toys

Biological Explanation （生物学解释）

Hassett et al. argue that their findings support a biological explanation （生物学解释） for toy preferences. Specifically, they concluded that these preferences develop in the absence of socialisation （在没有社会化的情况下）.

Why? In monkeys, unlike in humans, only hormonal differences (not social ones) can be responsible for the differences in development of males and females.

Nature-Nurture Interaction （先天-后天交互作用）

Importantly, such biologically driven differences could interact with social pressures （社会压力） on children:

  • Boys choosing girls' toys tend to receive more negative responses than girls choosing boys' toys
  • This would reinforce a pre-existing biological tendency for boys to be more masculine in their toy choices
  • Girls remain more variable in their choices
8

Strengths and Weaknesses （优点与缺点）

Strengths （优点）

✅ Key Strengths:

  1. Elimination of socialisation confound （消除社会化混淆变量）: Using animals removes the effect of socialisation, allowing clearer investigation of biological factors
  2. Control of confounding variables: Age and social rank were known and controlled (monkey group established for 25+ years; social rank assessed through grooming/dominance observations)
  3. Highly standardised procedure （标准化程序）: Clear operational definitions of behaviours improved reliability; trials of consistent length (25 minutes)
  4. Inter-coder reliability （编码者间信度）: Two observers worked together to achieve consensus when analysing videos

Weaknesses （缺点）

❌ Key Weaknesses:

  1. Methodological differences in cross-species comparison:
    • Toys chosen based on object properties, not gender stereotypes
    • Different measures for monkeys (frequency) vs children (duration)
    • Monkeys tested in groups; children tested individually
  2. Contradictory findings from Alexander and Hines (2002): Vervet monkeys showed different pattern — males played equally with both types, while females spent significantly longer with feminine toys
  3. Possible explanation: How toys were presented (one at a time vs together) may have influenced findings

Issues and Debates: Nature versus Nurture （先天与后天）

⚖️ Nature vs Nurture Debate:

Question: Has the study overlooked the possibility that early socialisation in primates may influence gendered development of play behaviours?

Evidence FOR nature view: Unlike human boys, male vervets play as much with masculine as feminine toys. Female vervets played significantly longer with feminine toys (unlike human girls who spend similar amounts of time with both types). This seems unlikely if female vervets were more strongly socialised than human girls.

Conclusion: Overall findings from animal studies support the idea that gender differences in toy preferences are the result of nature （性别差异是天生的）.

9

Ethical Issues （伦理问题）

📋 Ethical Considerations:

  • Guidelines followed: Conducted following appropriate ethical guidelines for care of animals in the USA
  • Approval obtained: Approved by Emory University's （埃默里大学） ethical committee on animal care and use
  • Feeding: Regular appropriate food (twice daily monkey feed + daily fruit/vegetables), constant water access
  • Housing: Ethically housed in family groups, large enclosures, temperature-controlled indoor + outdoor areas
  • Distress assessment: Camera observation would not be distressing; level of toy interaction suggests novelty was not source of psychological distress or physical pain
  • Incident: One toy was destroyed — unknown whether playful act or distress reflection
10

Summary （总结）

📝 Summary (中文总结):

Hassett et al.'s study investigated whether rhesus monkeys show a similar pattern of sex differences in toy preferences to children.

Key points:

  1. In studies with children, it's difficult to separate biological causation from social effects
  2. In monkeys, the role of hormonal influences on toy preferences without socialisation effects would be clear
  3. Videoed observations of monkeys given masculine (wheeled) and feminine (plush) toys were analysed using behavioural checklist
  4. Findings: Male monkeys prefer masculine toys (like boys); female monkeys have less fixed preferences (like girls)
  5. Conclusion: Evidence supports biological explanation because monkeys' preferences developed without socialisation
11

Exam Practice （考试练习）

🎯 Exam Key Points (考试要点)

  • Aim: Test if toy preferences are biologically determined (use monkeys to eliminate socialisation)
  • Method: Field experiment, independent measures, video observation with behavioural checklist
  • Sample: 11 male + 23 female rhesus monkeys at Yerkes station
  • Toys: 6 wheeled (masculine) vs 7 plush (feminine), counterbalanced positions
  • Results: Males significantly preferred wheeled toys (73%); females showed no significant preference but interacted more with plush (30%)
  • Conclusion: Supports biological explanation; preferences develop without socialisation
  • Strength: Eliminated socialisation confound; standardised procedure with operational definitions
  • Weakness: Cross-species methodology differs; group vs individual testing
  • Ethics: Emory University approved; appropriate housing; minimal distress

Practice Questions （练习题 — 来自课本原题）

Q4. Hassett et al. used two types of toy, 'masculine toys' and 'feminine toys'. How were these two types of toy different? （来自课本原题 Question 4）

✅ Model Answer (参考答案):

The two types of toy differed in their object properties:

  • Wheeled toys ('masculine'): Six toys including wagon, truck, car, construction vehicle, shopping cart, dump truck — ranging from 16–46 cm. Characterised by having wheels and being suitable for active, manipulative play.
  • Plush toys ('feminine'): Seven toys including Winnie-the-Pooh™, Raggedy-Ann™, koala puppet, armadillo, teddy bear, Scooby-Doo™, turtle — ranging from 14–73 cm. Characterised by being soft, cuddly, and suitable for nurturing play.

（两种玩具在物体属性上不同：轮式玩具有轮子，适合主动操作；毛绒玩具柔软，适合养育性游戏。）

Q5. Why did Hassett et al. exclude some of the monkeys from this study? （来自课本原题 Question 5）

✅ Model Answer (参考答案):

Monkeys were excluded for the following reasons:

  1. 14 adults were excluded because they had received hormone treatments — this would confound the results as the study aimed to investigate natural hormonal influences
  2. 39 young infants were excluded because they could not be reliably identified during video analysis — this would affect data accuracy
  3. Additionally, individuals with fewer than five total recorded behaviours were excluded from final analysis (14 males, 3 females) as they didn't provide sufficient data

（排除原因：接受过激素治疗的成年猴；无法可靠识别的幼猴；互动次数不足的个体。）

Q6. How was the data about children obtained? （来自课本原题 Question 6）

✅ Model Answer (参考答案):

The data about children was obtained from a different study by Berenbaum and Hines (1992). Key points about this data:

  • Children were tested individually (unlike monkeys tested in groups)
  • Only measured duration of play (not frequency like the monkey study)
  • Used different toys than those used with monkeys
  • This methodological difference is noted as a weakness of Hassett et al.'s study

（儿童数据来自 Berenbaum 和 Hines (1992) 的另一项研究，使用不同的方法和度量标准。）

Q7. Why was a correlation of social rank and total frequency of interactions conducted? （来自课本原题 Question 7）

✅ Model Answer (参考答案):

The correlation was conducted to investigate whether social hierarchy (rank) could explain the observed sex differences in toy preference:

  • If higher-ranked monkeys (who might have priority access to toys) showed different patterns, it could suggest social factors rather than biology caused the differences
  • Result: Rank correlated with toy interactions in females but not males
  • Conclusion: It is unlikely that social rank is responsible for the sex differences in toy preference overall
  • This strengthens the argument for a biological explanation

（进行相关分析是为了检验社会等级是否能解释玩具偏好的性别差异。结果显示社会等级不能解释整体差异，支持生物学解释。）

Additional Practice Questions （补充练习题）

Q8. What was the aim of the study by Hassett et al. (2008)?

✅ Answer:

The aim was to investigate whether toy preferences in monkeys resemble those in children, in order to test whether sex differences in toy choice are biologically determined by sex. （目的是调查猴子的玩具偏好是否与儿童相似，以测试玩具选择的性别差异是否由生物性别决定。）

Q9. Why was it useful to use rhesus monkeys in this study?

✅ Answer:

Rhesus monkeys are genetically closely related to humans and show similar behaviours (rough-and-tumble play, peer preferences, infant interest). Using monkeys eliminates the effect of socialisation, allowing researchers to isolate biological factors. It would be unethical to manipulate hormones or social experiences in human children. （恒河猴与人类基因相近且行为相似。使用猴子可消除社会化影响，分离生物因素。对人类儿童进行这类操纵是不道德的。）

Q10. Describe one strength and one weakness of this study.

✅ Answer:

Strength: Use of animals to eliminate socialisation confound — since monkeys don't experience human-type socialisation regarding toys, any preferences must be biologically driven. Also, highly standardised procedure with clear operational definitions improved reliability. （优点：使用动物消除社会化混淆；标准化程序提高信度。）

Weakness: Methodological differences in comparing monkeys and children — different toys, different measures (frequency vs duration), different testing conditions (group vs individual). This limits validity of cross-species conclusions. （缺点：跨物种比较的方法学差异限制了结论效度。）
