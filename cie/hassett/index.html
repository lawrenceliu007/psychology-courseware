1️⃣ The Psychology Being Investigated（研究涉及的心理学）

🧬

Biological Approach Core Assumptions

How biology shapes behaviour through genes, evolution & brain structure

The biological approach in psychology assumes that behaviour can be explained in terms of brain structures, neurotransmitters, hormones and genetics（行为可以用大脑结构、神经递质、激素和遗传学来解释）. This approach emphasizes the role of nature (innate factors) over nurture (environmental influences).

Nature vs Nurture Debate（先天与后天之争）

A key debate in psychology concerns whether behaviour is primarily determined by innate biological factors (nature 先天) or shaped by environmental experiences and learning (nurture 后天). The biological approach strongly supports the nature side of this debate.

In the context of toy preferences, researchers have long observed that children show gender-stereotyped toy choices:

  • Males tend to prefer wheeled toys (cars, trucks) and construction toys
  • Females tend to prefer plush dolls and softer toys

The critical question is: Are these preferences learned from society (nurture), or are they biologically programmed (nature)?

⚠️ Key Research Question

If non-human primates (who are not influenced by human socialisation) show similar gender differences in toy preferences, this would provide strong evidence for the biological/nature explanation.

§1 研究涉及的心理学：生物取向的核心假设

核心假设：生物取向认为行为可以用生物学因素解释，包括大脑结构、神经递质、激素和遗传基因。

  • Nature vs Nurture（先天与后天）：心理学经典争论。生物取向支持 Nature——行为由内在的生物学因素决定。
  • Gender-stereotyped toy preferences（性别刻板印象玩具偏好）：男孩偏好带轮子的玩具/积木；女孩偏好毛绒娃娃。这是社会化的结果还是生物本能？
  • Rationale（研究理由）：如果非人类灵长类动物（不受人类社会影响）也表现出类似的性别差异 → 强力支持 Nature/Biological Explanation。

💡 考试要点：CIE 常问 "outline one assumption of the biological approach"。答案模板：Behaviour can be explained by brain structures, neurotransmitters, hormones, and genetics.

▶ Exam Key Points — Biological Approach AO1
  • Biological assumption: Behaviour = brain structures + neurotransmitters + hormones + genetics Must Know
  • Key debate: Nature (biology) vs Nurture (environment) Must Know
  • Toy preference pattern: Males → wheeled/construction toys; Females → plush/dolls Common Q
  • Rationale for using monkeys: They lack human socialisation, so any gender difference suggests biological origin AO2 Gold

2️⃣ Background（研究背景）

📚

Prior Research on Gender Differences

What did we already know before Hassett's study?

Previous Findings on Children's Toy Preferences（儿童玩具偏好的已有发现）

Research with human children has consistently shown gender differences in toy selection from as early as one year of age:

  • Boys spend more time with wheeled toys and objects that can be manipulated mechanically
  • Girls show greater interest in dolls and toys with facial features
  • These differences appear across cultures, though the strength varies

The Social Learning Theory Challenge（社会学习理论的挑战）

Many psychologists argued that these differences were due to social learning（社会学习）: children imitate same-sex models, receive reinforcement for "gender-appropriate" play, and are influenced by media and parental expectations.

Hassett et al.'s challenge: If we test non-human primates who have NOT been exposed to human gender socialisation, and they STILL show similar patterns, then biology must play a significant role.

"The question of whether sex differences in toy preferences are determined by nature or nurture remains unanswered... Non-human primates provide an opportunity to examine this question without the confounding influence of human gender socialisation."
— Hassett et al. (2008), paraphrased from original paper
Why Rhesus Monkeys? 为什么选择恒河猴？
• Closely related to humans genetically (~93% DNA shared)
• Complex social behaviours similar to humans
• NOT exposed to human gender stereotypes or marketing
• Previous research showed some gender-typical behaviours
§2 研究背景：挑战社会化解释

已知事实：人类儿童从 1 岁起就表现出玩具偏好的性别差异。

  • Social Learning Explanation（社会学习解释）：差异来源于模仿同性榜样、强化、"适当"行为的奖励、媒体影响等后天因素。
  • Biological Explanation（生物学解释）：差异是先天的、由基因/激素决定的。
  • Hassett 的策略：用恒河猴做被试 → 它们没有接触过人类社会的性别刻板印象 → 如果仍然有差异 → 支持生物学解释！

💡 考试答题技巧：背景部分常考 "why use animals?" 或 "what is the rationale?"。答案要强调：排除人类社会化干扰，测试纯生物学因素。

▶ Exam Key Points — Background AO1+AO2
  • Children show gendered toy preferences from age 1 (cross-cultural evidence) Common Q
  • Social learning theory: Differences come from imitation, reinforcement, parental/media influence Must Know
  • Rhesus monkeys chosen because: no human gender socialisation, close genetic relation to humans AO2 Gold
  • This study tests whether biology alone can produce gendered toy preferences Must Know

3️⃣ Aim（研究目的）

The aim was to investigate whether male and female rhesus monkeys show different preferences for "masculine" vs "feminine" toys, specifically whether males would prefer wheeled toys while females would prefer plush toys.
（调查雄性和雌性恒河猴是否对"男性化"和"女性化"玩具有不同偏好。）
Aim in One Sentence 一句话 aim：
To determine if rhesus monkeys exhibit gender-stereotyped toy preferences (wheeled vs. plush) similar to human children, providing evidence for a biological basis of such preferences.
（确定恒河猴是否表现出类似人类的性别刻板化玩具偏好，为这种偏好的生物学基础提供证据。）

🎯 Research Hypothesis 研究假设

Directional hypothesis（方向性假设）：Male monkeys will spend significantly more time interacting with wheeled toys ("masculine"), while female monkeys will spend more time with plush toys ("feminine").

Null hypothesis（虚无假设）：There will be no significant difference between males and females in time spent with either type of toy.

§3 研究目的与假设

核心 aim：检验恒河猴是否对"男性化"（带轮子）和"女性化"（毛绒）玩具有不同偏好。

  • Directional Hypothesis（方向性假设）：雄猴偏好 wheeled toys > 雌猴偏好 plush toys
  • Null Hypothesis（虚无假设）：两组之间无显著差异
  • Operationalisation（操作化定义）："偏好"用 interaction time（互动时间）测量

💡 考试要点：CIE 可能问 "state the aim"。答案必须包含：(1) 比较雄性与雌性；(2) wheeled vs plush toys；(3) 测量指标是时间。

▶ Exam Key Points — Aim AO1
  • Aim: Compare male vs female monkey preferences for wheeled vs plush toys Must Know
  • DV: Time spent interacting with each toy type Must Know
  • IV: Sex of participant (male/female) + Type of toy (wheeled/plush) Common Q
  • Underlying purpose: Test nature (biology) vs nurture (socialisation) explanation AO2 Gold

4️⃣ Method（研究方法）

🔬

Field Experiment

Outdoor enclosure · IV = gender · DV = toy interaction · Plus correlation & cross-species comparison

Research Method and Design（研究方法与设计）

This was a field experiment（现场实验） — the environment in which the participants were tested was their normal, outdoor housing area, and they were free to interact with the toys or not. Although the toys were new to them, the monkeys would not have been unfamiliar with objects being placed in their enclosures.

The experimental design（实验设计） was independent measures（独立测量设计） as the independent variable was gender（性别）; each animal being recorded was either male or female.

Observations（观察法） were used as a technique to measure the dependent variable（因变量） — activities with the toys. In addition to looking for differences between genders, a correlation（相关分析） was conducted on the results, to look for a relationship between individual monkeys' ranks within the social hierarchy（社会等级） and the frequency or duration of activities with each toy type. Finally, data from the monkeys was compared to similar data relating to children obtained from a different study (Berenbaum and Hines, 1992). This was an independent measures comparison（独立测量比较）.

Procedure Overview（程序概览）

  1. For each social group, seven trials（7次试验）, each lasting 25 minutes, were observed using two video cameras
  2. Each trial began with all monkeys indoors while one plush toy and one wheeled toy were placed 10 metres apart in the outdoor enclosure
  3. One video recorder was directed towards each toy; the plush and wheeled toys were counterbalanced（平衡/轮换） between left and right locations on each trial
  4. After each trial, toys were removed; video was analysed by two observers working together to achieve consensus
  5. Observers identified each animal and coded specific activities using a behavioural checklist (see Table 2.3)
  6. The exact time at which each activity occurred was recorded — enabling both frequency and duration of continuous activities to be measured

⚠️ Anomaly During Testing

One trial had to finish 7 minutes early (at 18 minutes instead of 25) because the plush toy was torn into pieces by the monkeys.

§4 方法：现场实验设计

研究类型：Field Experiment（现场实验）——在猴子正常的户外生活区进行，猴子可自由选择是否与玩具互动。IV = gender（性别），DV = activities with toys（与玩具的互动行为）。

  • Design：Independent measures（独立测量设计），因为 IV 是性别，每只猴子要么雄要么雌
  • Additional analyses：(1) 相关分析——社会等级 vs 互动频率/时长；(2) 与儿童数据（Berenbaum & Hines, 1992）做跨物种比较
  • Sample（样本）：总群体135只→初始82只（21雄+61雌）→排除14只成年猴（激素治疗）+ 39只幼猴（无法识别）→最终34只（11雄+23雌）
  • Materials（材料）：6个轮式玩具（16-46cm）+ 7个毛绒玩具（14-73cm）
  • Housing：25×25米户外围栏 + 温控室内区域

💡 考试要点：方法题常考 "What research method was used?" → 答案必须是 field experiment，不是 naturalistic observation！"Describe the sample" 要提到两个排除原因。

▶ Exam Key Points — Method AO1+AO2
  • Method: Field experiment (not naturalistic observation!) in outdoor housing area Must Know
  • Design: Independent measures (IV = gender) Common Q
  • Also used: Correlation (social rank vs interaction) + cross-species comparison (Berenbaum & Hines, 1992) AO2 Gold
  • Sample: 135 total → 82 initial (21M+61F) → 14 adults excluded (hormone treatments) + 39 infants excluded (unidentifiable) → Final 34 (11M+23F) Must Know
  • Two toy types: 6 wheeled (16-46cm) + 7 plush (14-73cm) Must Know
  • DV operationalised: Frequency + duration of specific behaviours (15-item checklist) Must Know

5️⃣ Procedure（实验程序）

Step-by-Step Process（分步过程）

📋 Standardised Procedure 标准化程序

Phase 1: Setup 准备阶段

  1. All monkeys in the social group were moved indoors while the toys were being placed
  2. One wheeled toy and one plush toy were placed in the outdoor enclosure, 10 metres apart
  3. Placement was counterbalanced（平衡/轮换） — wheeled and plush toys alternated between left and right positions across trials to prevent location bias
  4. Two video cameras were set up, one directed at each toy

Phase 2: Data Collection 数据收集

  1. Monkeys were released into the outdoor enclosure and could freely interact with either or both toys
  2. Each trial lasted 25 minutes
  3. Seven trials（7次试验） were conducted for each social group
  4. After each trial, toys were removed from the enclosure

Phase 3: Video Analysis 视频分析

  1. The video tape was analysed by two observers working together to achieve consensus（共识） on what behaviours occurred
  2. Observers identified each animal interacting with a toy and coded specific activities using a behavioural checklist (Table 2.3 — 15 behaviours)
  3. The exact time at which each activity occurred was recorded, enabling both frequency（频率） and duration（时长） of continuous activities to be calculated
  4. For each individual, data was converted into an overall average frequency and duration (to account for different numbers of trials participated in)
Standardisation Controls 标准化控制措施：
• Same outdoor enclosure (25×25m) for all participants
• Standard trial length: 25 minutes each
• Counterbalanced left/right toy positions to prevent location bias
• Two video cameras — one per toy — ensuring all interactions captured
• Two observers working together (not independently!) to achieve consensus coding
• Clear operational definitions for all 15 behaviours in the checklist
• Exact time recording enabled both frequency AND duration to be measured

⚠️ Exception During Testing

One trial had to finish 7 minutes early (at 18 minutes) because the plush toy was torn into pieces by the monkeys. It is unknown whether this was playful or a sign of distress.

§5 实验程序：7次试验 × 25分钟 × 10米间距

核心特点：高度标准化的现场实验程序，确保内部效度。

  • Setup（准备）：猴子先移入室内 → 在户外围栏放置1个轮式+1个毛绒玩具，相距10米 → 左右位置平衡轮换 → 每个玩具架设1台摄像机
  • Data Collection（数据收集）：猴子自由进入户外区域 → 每次试验25分钟 → 每组7次试验 → 试验后移走玩具
  • Analysis（分析）：两名观察者共同工作（非独立编码！）达成共识 → 用15项行为清单编码 → 记录精确时间 → 计算频率+时长
  • Exception（异常）：有一次试验提前7分钟结束，因为毛绒玩具被撕碎

💡 考试要点：程序题可能问 "Outline two controls"。答案：(1) Counterbalanced left/right positions; (2) Standardised 25-minute trials with two cameras.

▶ Exam Key Points — Procedure AO1
  • 7 trials, each 25 minutes, in outdoor enclosure (25×25m) Must Know
  • One wheeled + one plush toy, placed 10 metres apart, counterbalanced left/right Must Know
  • Two video cameras — one directed at each toy Common Q
  • Two observers working together (not independently!) to achieve consensus Must Know
  • Exact time recorded → both frequency AND duration measured AO2 Gold
  • One trial finished 7 min early — plush toy torn Context

7️⃣ Results（研究结果）

📊

Key Findings — Data Driven!

Males preferred wheeled; females preferred plush; patterns parallel human children

Data Preparation（数据准备）

For each animal, the records for each behaviour were converted into an overall average frequency and duration for that individual. This was because each animal could have participated in a different number of activities in different trials. Not all individuals interacted with the toys — those with fewer than five total recorded behaviours were not used in the analysis (14 males and 3 females excluded). The final number of individuals used in the analysis was 11 males and 23 females.

🎯 How to read Table 2.4: Males' mean frequency with wheeled toys (9.77) is much higher than with plush (2.06) → strong wheeled preference. Females' frequency with plush (7.97) is higher than with wheeled (6.96) → slight plush preference. But note the large SDs — lots of individual variation!

Within-Sex Comparisons（性别内比较）

Statistical analyses comparing toy preferences within each sex, using frequency data, found that:

  • Males preferred wheeled compared to plush toys (9.77 vs 2.06 mean frequency) ✅
  • Females did NOT show a significant preference for plush toys over wheeled toys (7.97 vs 6.96 — difference not significant)

Between-Sex Comparisons（性别间比较）

A comparison between the sexes, using frequency data, found that:

  • Females interacted with plush toys more than males (7.97 vs 2.06) ✅
  • No sex difference for wheeled toys (6.96 vs 9.77 — not significant)

For duration data: males interacted for a longer total time with wheeled than plush toys. However, duration of time spent interacting with different toy types did not differ for females, who played for a similar total length of time with both. Even though the male difference in play durations was big enough to show a significant within-sex difference, no significant difference was found in the between-sex comparison — meaning there was no overall difference in the total time males and females spent playing with both toy types combined.

Magnitude of Preference（偏好幅度）

Further comparisons calculated 'magnitude of preference'（偏好幅度） — how much each sex preferred their "preferred" toy type:

  • For males: total frequency of wheeled − total frequency of plush
  • For females: total frequency of plush − total frequency of wheeled

Statistical analysis confirmed that male monkeys had a higher preference for 'masculine' (wheeled) toys, and female monkeys had a higher preference for 'feminine' (plush) toys — but the male preference was stronger/more marked than the female preference.

73% of males preferred wheeled toys — but only 39% of females preferred plush toys. The male preference was much more marked. Meanwhile, 30% of females showed no significant preference — greater variability in female toy choice, just like in human children.

Social Rank Correlation（社会等级相关分析）

The correlation between social rank and toy interactions found:

  • For males and females combined: rank and total frequency of interactions were positively correlated for both toy types
  • For males alone: neither frequencies nor durations correlated with rank
  • For females alone: rank was positively correlated with frequency for both wheeled and plush toys; rank correlated with duration only for plush, not wheeled

Since rank may be related to toy interactions in females but not in males, it is unlikely that social rank is responsible for the sex differences in toy preference overall.

Although the sample size was too small for detailed analysis by age, comparisons were possible between wider groups. A comparison between juvenile, subadult, adult and old animals did not differ in terms of frequency or duration for either toy type.

8️⃣ Conclusion（结论）

💡

Main Conclusion

Biology plays a significant role in gendered toy preferences

The results show that, like boys, male monkeys have a strong preference for masculine-type toys (wheeled). Female monkeys, like young girls, are more variable in their toy preferences. The extent to which individuals preferred masculine or feminine toys differed between males and females, with male monkeys showing much stronger preferences for wheeled toys than female monkeys did for plush toys.

Hassett et al. argue that their findings support a biological explanation（生物学解释） for toy preferences. Specifically, they concluded that these preferences develop in the absence of socialisation（在没有社会化的情况下）. This is because in monkeys, unlike in humans, only hormonal differences — not social ones — can be responsible for the differences in development of males and females.

⚠️ Important Interactionist Point 重要的交互论点

Importantly, such biologically driven differences could interact with social pressures on children. Boys choosing girls' toys tend to receive more negative responses than girls choosing boys' toys. This would reinforce a pre-existing biological tendency for boys to be more masculine in their toy choices and for girls to remain more variable in their choices.

Implications of the Conclusion（结论的含义）

  1. Supports Nature side of Nature-Nurture debate — Biology contributes to gendered behaviour
  2. Challenges pure Social Learning Theory — Cannot explain findings with nurture alone
  3. Suggests evolutionary adaptation — Some gendered behaviours may be evolutionarily adaptive
  4. Cautions against oversimplification — Biology is not destiny; socialisation still plays a role
Link to Biological Approach 与生物取向的联系：
• Provides evidence that hormones/genes may influence behaviour
• Consistent with evolutionary psychology perspective
• Gender differences may have adaptive value (preparation for ancestral roles)
• BUT: Findings describe group averages, not individual destiny
§8 结论：支持 Nature 的有力证据

核心结论：性别刻板化的玩具偏好不完全是社会化的结果。

  • Key Argument（核心论点）：恒河猴没有接触过人类性别刻板印象 → 但仍表现类似模式 → 说明有生物学成分
  • Nature Support（支持先天）：强力支持 Nature-Nurture 争论中的 Nature 方
  • Challenge to SLT（挑战社会学习理论）：纯后天解释无法说明这些发现
  • Nuance（细微差别）：生物学 ≠ 命运；社会化仍有作用；两者可能交互影响

9️⃣ Evaluation（评价）

⚖️

Strengths & Weaknesses

Critical evaluation of methodology and findings

Strengths of the Study（研究的优势）

✅ 1. Eliminates the Effect of Socialisation 排除社会化影响
A key strength of using animals was that it allowed the researchers to eliminate the effect of socialisation（消除社会化影响） — the monkeys had never been exposed to human gender norms, advertisements or parental reinforcement. In children, biology and socialisation are impossible to separate, but in monkeys any sex difference must have a biological basis. This is the study's unique advantage: it isolates biological factors that would be confounded in human research.
✅ 2. Confounding Variables Excluded 排除混淆变量
The researchers went further and excluded confounding variables（混淆变量） that could affect social interactions, such as age and social rank（年龄和社会等级）. Age was known precisely because the monkey group had been established for over 25 years. Social rank had been assessed through behavioural observations of who groomed whom, dominance behaviours and submissive behaviours（谁给谁理毛、支配行为和顺从行为）. Statistical analysis demonstrated that these social factors had little effect on the sex differences in toy preference — so the sex difference could not simply be explained by rank or age.
✅ 3. Highly Standardised Observation 高度标准化观察
The observation process was highly standardised（标准化）. Clear operational definitions（操作性定义） of the 15 behaviours in Table 2.3 allowed the two observers to achieve consensus coding（共识编码） and would let other researchers replicate（重复验证） the study, increasing reliability. Trials were a standard 25 minutes, toys were placed 10 metres apart and counterbalanced（轮换） between left and right positions, and video recording allowed the tapes to be re-analysed and verified.
✅ 4. High Ecological Validity 生态效度高
This was a field experiment（现场实验） conducted in the monkeys' normal, outdoor housing area（日常户外围场）, where they were free to interact with the toys or not. Compared with a laboratory, the monkeys were behaving in a familiar environment, so their play behaviour was likely to be natural rather than a reaction to an artificial testing situation.

Weaknesses of the Study（研究的劣势）

❌ 1. Monkey–Child Comparison Was Not Matched 猴子与儿童的比较不匹配
There were differences in the way the monkeys and the children were compared. The masculine and feminine toys offered to the monkeys were chosen for their object properties（物体属性） (wheeled vs plush) rather than because they were gender-stereotyped toys for children, so the two studies may not be measuring exactly the same thing. The measures of play also differed: the children (Berenbaum & Hines, 1992) were assessed in a separate study using different toys and only measuring duration of play（玩耍时长）, not frequency. The most significant comparison was therefore between frequency for the monkeys and duration for the children — not the same measure for both, which weakens the comparison.
❌ 2. Monkeys Tested in Groups vs Children Tested Individually 群体测试 vs 个体测试
The children's data came from a study that tested each participant individually（单独）, whereas Hassett et al. observed the monkeys in groups（群体）. This methodological difference may have been important. Alexander and Hines (2002) studied vervet monkeys and found a different pattern: unlike human boys, male vervets interacted for a similar amount of time with masculine and feminine toys, while females were more gender-specific. Rather than a genuine species difference, this pattern could be explained by the way toys were presented — one at a time rather than together, so the vervets were never given a genuine 'choice'. By the same logic, testing monkeys in groups (where one individual's choice could influence others) and children alone could have influenced the findings.
❌ 3. Small Final Sample, Especially Males 最终样本量小（尤其雄性）
Although the wider group contained 135 animals, the final sample used in the analysis was only 34 monkeys (11 males + 23 females). 14 adults were excluded because they had received hormone treatments and 39 young infants because they could not be reliably identified. In the analysis itself, individuals with fewer than five recorded behaviours were also excluded (14 males and 3 females). With only 11 males remaining, the number is very small: individual differences among these 11 monkeys could have a disproportionate effect on the results, and statistical conclusions about male preferences rest on a weak base.
❌ 4. Standardisation Was Imperfect 标准化并不完美
One trial had to finish 7 minutes early because the plush toy was torn into pieces（被撕成碎片） by the monkeys, so not every trial ran for the full 25 minutes. It is also unknown whether the destruction was a playful act or a reflection of distress（痛苦） — if the latter, the observation itself may have altered the monkeys' behaviour. In addition, the toys were human-made objects not naturally occurring in the monkeys' environment, so play with them may not represent fully natural behaviour.

Issues and Debates: Nature vs Nurture（先天与后天之争）

🧬 Does the study overlook early socialisation in primates?
It could be argued that Hassett et al. overlooked the possibility that early socialisation（早期社会化） in primates may influence the gendered development of play behaviours — that there may be a 'nurture' explanation even in animals. However, the researchers report a study by Alexander and Hines (2002) on vervet monkeys which found that, unlike human boys, male vervets played as much with masculine as with feminine toys, whereas female vervets played for significantly longer with feminine toys. Although this may superficially look as though it supports a nature view, it would require female vervet monkeys to be more strongly socialised to prefer female toys than human girls are — which seems unlikely. Overall, findings from animal studies support the idea that gender differences in toy preferences are the result of nature（玩具偏好的性别差异是天生的）, though the researchers acknowledge biology could interact with social pressures in human children.
§9 评价：平衡的批判性思维

Strengths（优势）：

  • 排除社会化影响（猴子从未接触人类性别规范 → 生物因素可被分离，这是研究的独特优势）

1️⃣2️⃣ Summary（总结）

📝

One-Paragraph Summary

Everything you need to remember in a nutshell

Hassett et al. (2008) investigated whether rhesus monkeys show gender-stereotyped toy preferences similar to human children. Using a field experiment with 7 trials of 25 minutes each in the monkeys' outdoor enclosure, researchers found that male monkeys significantly preferred wheeled toys while female monkeys showed a preference for plush toys. Since monkeys are not influenced by human gender socialisation, these findings provide evidence for a biological basis of gendered toy preferences, supporting the Nature side of the Nature-Nurture debate and challenging purely Social Learning Theory explanations.

Q1
Outline one assumption of the biological approach. [4 marks] Must Know AO1
▼
Model Answer:
One assumption of the biological approach is that behaviour can be explained in terms of biological factors such as brain structures, neurotransmitters, hormones, and genetics. For example, the approach assumes that differences in brain structure or hormone levels can account for behavioural differences between individuals. (AO1: 4 marks)
Q2
Describe the sample used in Hassett et al.'s study. [4 marks] Common Q AO1
▼
Model Answer:
The sample consisted of rhesus monkeys (Macaca mulatta) from the Yerkes National Primate Research Center in the USA. The researchers started with an initial sample of 82 monkeys (21 males and 61 females) from a total population of 135 animals living in natal groups. However, 14 adults were excluded because they had received hormone treatments that could confound results, and 39 young infants were excluded because they could not be reliably identified individually. This left a final sample of 34 monkeys (11 males and 23 females), ranging from juvenile to old age. An opportunity sampling method was used — all available monkeys that could be reliably identified were included. The monkeys were housed in a large 25 × 25 metre outdoor enclosure with access to a temperature-controlled indoor area, and were given standard monkey feed twice daily plus fruit and vegetables once daily. (AO1: 4 marks)
Q3
Explain one strength of using animals in this study. [4 marks] AO2 Gold
▼
Model Answer:
One strength is that animals eliminate the confounding influence of human socialisation. Rhesus monkeys have not been exposed to human gender stereotypes, media representations, or parental reinforcement of "gender-appropriate" behaviour. Therefore, any gender differences observed are more likely to reflect innate biological predispositions rather than learned behaviours. This increases the internal validity of the conclusions about biological influences on behaviour. (AO2: 4 marks)
Q4
Describe the results of Hassett et al.'s study. [6 marks] Must Know AO1
▼
Model Answer:
The results showed that male rhesus monkeys interacted with wheeled toys more than plush toys (mean frequency: 9.77 vs 2.06), while female monkeys interacted with plush toys more than wheeled toys (mean frequency: 7.97 vs 6.96). Within-sex comparisons showed males significantly preferred wheeled over plush, but females did NOT show a significant preference for plush over wheeled. Between-sex comparisons showed females interacted with plush more than males, but there was no significant sex difference for wheeled toys. In terms of individual preferences, 73% of males preferred wheeled toys, while 39% of females preferred plush toys, 30% showed no significant preference. The male preference was more marked than the female preference. Social rank correlated with female interactions but not male, suggesting rank is unlikely to explain the sex difference. The pattern closely paralleled human children's toy preferences (Berenbaum & Hines, 1992). (AO1: 6 marks)
Q5
Discuss the nature-nurture debate in relation to this study. [8 marks] Must Know AO2
▼
Model Answer:
This study provides strong support for the Nature side of the nature-nurture debate. The key argument is that rhesus monkeys, who have never been exposed to human gender socialisation, still show gender-stereotyped toy preferences similar to human children. If these preferences were entirely learned (nurture), we would not expect to see them in monkeys raised without human gender norms.

The finding that approximately 73% of males preferred wheeled toys while 39% of females preferred plush toys suggests an innate, evolved component to these preferences. The male preference was more marked than the female preference, paralleling the pattern in human children. This challenges Social Learning Theory, which attributes gendered behaviour to imitation, reinforcement, and modelling.

Further support comes from Alexander and Hines (2002), who found that male vervet monkeys played as much with masculine as with feminine toys while female vervets played for significantly longer with feminine toys. This pattern is consistent with a biological (nature) explanation, because it is implausible that female vervets are more strongly socialised toward feminine toys than human girls are.

However, it is important to note that supporting Nature does not mean Nurture has no role. In humans, socialisation likely interacts with biological predispositions. The study shows biology contributes, but does not determine behaviour completely. A balanced view acknowledges both nature and nurture interact to produce complex behaviours. (AO2: 8 marks)
Q6
Evaluate the use of field experiment methodology in this study. [6 marks] AO2 Gold
▼
Model Answer:
Strength: The field experiment was conducted in the monkeys' familiar outdoor housing area, where they were free to interact with the toys or not. This increases ecological validity compared to a laboratory. The monkeys behaved naturally because they were in their normal environment with novel toys presented in a standardised way (7 trials, 25 minutes each, 10m apart, counterbalanced positions).

Weakness: Although the setting was naturalistic, the introduction of specific toys (6 wheeled, 7 plush) is still somewhat artificial — these are human-made objects, not naturally occurring in the monkeys' environment. The monkeys' responses may not reflect truly natural behaviour. Additionally, there may be observer bias if coders have expectations about gender differences, though this was addressed through two observers working together to achieve consensus coding. The small sample (N=34) also limits generalisability. (AO2: 6 marks)
Q7
Assess one ethical issue in this study. [6 marks] AO2 Common
▼
Model Answer:
One ethical issue is the use of animals in research. On the positive side, this study used a field experiment in the monkeys' familiar outdoor enclosure which involved minimal intervention — the monkeys were simply given toys to play with in their normal housing area. No deprivation, pain, or harm was inflicted. The study was approved by Emory University's ethical committee on animal care and use. Animals received regular food (twice daily monkey feed plus daily fruit and vegetables) and had constant access to water. The toys may have served as environmental enrichment.

However, animals cannot give informed consent, raising questions about their right to autonomy. The monkeys were captive and had no choice about participating. There is also the concern that findings from animal research could be misused to support discriminatory views about gender roles in humans.

Overall, the study appears ethically acceptable under guidelines like the APA's 3 Rs (Reduction, Refinement, Replacement), as the cost (minimal stress) is outweighed by the benefit (significant scientific contribution). (AO2: 6 marks)
Q8
'Gender differences in behaviour are entirely due to socialisation.' To what extent does Hassett et al.'s study support this statement? [12 marks] Must Know AO2 Extended
▼
Model Answer:
This statement claims gender differences are ENTIRELY due to socialisation. Hassett et al.'s study provides evidence that challenges this view, suggesting the statement is only supported to a limited extent.

Evidence challenging the statement:
The study found male monkeys preferred wheeled toys (73% showed preference) while females preferred plush toys (39% showed preference), mirroring human patterns. Crucially, monkeys lack exposure to human gender socialisation - they haven't watched TV showing gendered advertisements, received parental reinforcement for "appropriate" toys, or observed same-sex models. Yet they still show similar preferences. This strongly suggests a biological component exists that cannot be explained by socialisation alone.

Evidence supporting limited role of socialisation:
The study does NOT prove socialisation is irrelevant. Humans experience far more complex socialisation than monkeys. It is plausible that in humans, biology creates predispositions that are then amplified, modified, or sometimes overridden by social learning. The monkey study shows nature matters, but doesn't quantify how much nurture contributes in humans.

Conclusion:
Hassett et al.'s study demonstrates that gender differences are NOT entirely due to socialisation - there is clearly a biological component. However, it would be wrong to conclude socialisation plays NO role. The most accurate position is an interactionist perspective: biology provides predispositions, and socialisation shapes how these are expressed. The statement is therefore an oversimplification that is largely contradicted by this research. (AO2: 12 marks)
Q9
How could the methodology of this study be improved? [6 marks] AO2 Common
▼
Model Answer:
Improvement 1 - Larger sample: The study used a final sample of only 34 monkeys (11 males + 23 females), which is a small opportunity sample from one facility (Yerkes). A larger sample, ideally randomly selected from multiple facilities, would increase generalisability and statistical power. This would allow more confidence that results reflect true population patterns rather than sampling error.

Improvement 2 - Longitudinal design: A longitudinal study tracking the same monkeys over time could show whether preferences are stable from infancy through adulthood or change with development. This would strengthen claims about innate vs learned components.

Improvement 3 - Cross-species comparison: Testing other primate species (chimpanzees, bonobos) could determine if findings are specific to rhesus monkeys or generalise across primates, strengthening evolutionary claims. (AO2: 6 marks)
Q10
Design a replication study to test the reliability of Hassett et al.'s findings. [10 marks] AO2 Extended Application
▼
Model Answer:
Aim: To replicate Hassett et al.'s study with modifications to test reliability of findings about gender-stereotyped toy preferences in non-human primates.

Method:
• Design: Independent groups design comparing male vs female chimpanzees
• Sample: 40 chimpanzees (20 male, 20 female) aged 2-10 years from a sanctuary setting
• Materials: Identical categories of wheeled and plush toys as original study
• Procedure: Present both toy types simultaneously in familiar outdoor enclosure, 10m apart; record interaction using 15-item behavioural checklist (Table 2.3); video record all sessions; two observers working together to achieve consensus coding
• Controls: Same procedure for all participants; counterbalance toy positions; standardised duration (25 minutes per session, 7 sessions)

Expected outcomes if reliable:
Male chimpanzees should show significantly greater preference for wheeled toys compared to females, replicating the rhesus monkey pattern (73% males preferred wheeled in original study).

How this tests reliability:
If results match original study despite different species (chimpanzees vs monkeys) and setting (sanctuary vs lab), this demonstrates external reliability (findings generalise). Using identical measures and procedures tests internal reliability (consistency of measurement). (AO2/AO3: 10 marks)
