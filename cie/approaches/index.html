  • Q1-Q3 · 从 study 抽 fact（1-4 marks）

§1 · Cheat Sheet

8 Assumptions 速查（一页浓缩）

考前 5 分钟过这一页。所有 assumption 文本均出自 CIE AS Psychology Syllabus 2024-2026（634461）p.16-19 原文，可直接抄进答卷。

🧠 Biological Approach（syllabus p.16）

  • A1（p.16）：Behaviour, cognitions and emotions can be explained in terms of the working of the brain and the effect of hormones, genetics and evolution.
  • A2（p.16）：Similarities and differences between people can be understood in terms of biological factors and their interaction with other factors.

💭 Cognitive Approach（syllabus p.17）

  • A1（p.17）：Information is processed through the same route in all humans: input – process – output, in a similar way to how information is processed by a computer.
  • A2（p.17）：There are individual differences in cognitive processes such as attention, language, thinking and memory. These processes can also help to explain behaviour and emotion.

👥 Social Approach（syllabus p.19）

  • A1（p.19）：Behaviour, cognitions and emotions are influenced by social contexts, social environments and groups.
  • A2（p.19）：Behaviour, cognitions and emotions can be influenced by the actual, implied or imagined presence of others.

🎓 Learning Approach（syllabus p.18）

  • A1（p.18）：We all begin life as a blank slate. Experiences and interactions with the environment shape our behaviour and these changes are directly observable.
  • A2（p.18）：We learn through the processes of operant conditioning, classical conditioning and social learning. This can be understood using the stimulus-response model.

§2 Biological Approach / 生物取向

Brain · Hormones · Genetics · Evolution — 3 core studies: Holzel · Dement · Hassett

📘 Syllabus p.16 · Paper 2 Q4(a) · Paper 1 I&D
A1

Brain, hormones, genetics & evolution explain behaviour

Syllabus p.16 verbatim
Behaviour, cognitions and emotions can be explained in terms of the working of the brain and the effect of hormones, genetics and evolution.

💡 关键概念 echo（学生答 Q4(a) 模板）：必须命中 syllabus 关键词 brain、hormones、genetics、evolution 至少 2 个，example 必须连回其中之一。

Holzel et al. (2011) ★ Best fit
MRI scan 找到灰质密度变化 → MBSR → mindfulness ↑
Link：MBSR causes brain structure change in hippocampus（hippocampus 是 brain structure）→ 直接支持 assumption 中"the working of the brain"。
→ 看完整 study
Dement & Kleitman (1957)
EEG 记录 REM sleep 脑波 + 梦境报告
Link：EEG measures brain wave patterns correlate with dreaming → supports "working of the brain explains cognitions (dreams)"。
→ 看完整 study
Hassett et al. (2008)
雄性恒河猴 vs 雌性偏好 wheeled vs plush toy
Link：跨物种相同偏好 → supports evolution（进化）影响行为，因为 monkeys 不受人类社会化影响。
→ 看完整 study
⭐ Q4(a) Model Answer — 3 marks AO1×2 AO2×1
One assumption of the biological approach is that behaviour, cognitions and emotions can be explained in terms of the working of the brain and the effect of hormones, genetics and evolution (AO1 = 2 marks). For example, Holzel et al. (2011) used MRI scans and found that participants who completed an 8-week mindfulness programme showed increased grey matter density in the brain's hippocampus (a brain structure linked to memory and emotional regulation) (AO2 = 1 mark, example directly linked to the brain keyword). 生物取向的一个假设是，行为、认知和情绪可以通过大脑的工作以及激素、基因和进化的影响来解释。例：Holzel 等人(2011)用 MRI 扫描发现，完成 8 周正念课程的参与者大脑海马回（与记忆和情绪调节相关的脑区）灰质密度增加。
⚠️ MS 反面教材警示："Do not credit answers that describe the aim or procedure, rather than a finding linked to the assumption." If you write "Holzel used MRI scanners", that is procedure, not a finding linked to the assumption. Must be finding (e.g., "increased grey matter") + keyword (e.g., "brain structure").

📝 Exam Practice — Bio A1

Q4(a) · 3 marks · AO1 ×2 + AO2 ×1
"Outline one assumption of the biological approach, including an example from the study by Dement and Kleitman (1957)."
AO1 (2 marks): One assumption of the biological approach is that behaviour, cognitions and emotions can be explained in terms of the working of the brain and the effect of hormones, genetics and evolution. (full syllabus assumption, includes all 4 keywords: brain, hormones, genetics, evolution)

AO2 (1 mark): Dement and Kleitman used EEG to record brain wave patterns and found that when participants were awakened during REM sleep (characterised by specific brain wave activity), they were significantly more likely to report a vivid dream (a cognition). This directly supports the assumption by showing that specific brain activity explains mental experience. (finding linked to "brain" keyword)
Q4(a) · 3 marks · AO1 ×2 + AO2 ×1
"Outline one assumption of the biological approach, including an example from the study by Hassett et al. (2008)."
AO1 (2 marks): One assumption is that behaviour, cognitions and emotions can be explained in terms of the working of the brain and the effect of hormones, genetics and evolution.

AO2 (1 mark): Hassett found that male and female rhesus monkeys showed sex-typed toy preferences (males preferred wheeled toys, females preferred plush toys) in the same way as human children. This supports the assumption because the preference appeared in monkeys that are not influenced by human socialisation, suggesting an evolutionary / biological origin to gendered play preferences.
A2

Individual differences via biological factors + interactions

Syllabus p.16 verbatim
Similarities and differences between people can be understood in terms of biological factors and their interaction with other factors.

💡 关键概念 echo：命中 biological factors + interaction with other factors。 Example 必须显示"both similarities AND differences" 或 "interaction"。

Hassett et al. (2008) ★ Best fit
Monkeys 跟人类一致（similarity），但 monkey 偏好更弱（difference）
Link：Biological similarities across species + environmental interaction (monkey study ruled out social pressure) → supports assumption that similarities AND differences explained by biology + other factors。
→ 看完整 study
Holzel et al. (2011)
Participants 个体差异 in grey matter change amount
Link：Same training but individual differences in grey matter increase → biology interacts with experience。
→ 看完整 study
Dement & Kleitman (1957)
Dream recall 个体差异：recallers vs non-recallers
Link：Same EEG pattern but individual differences in dream recall → biology + person factor。
→ 看完整 study
⭐ Q4(a) Model Answer — 3 marks AO1×2 AO2×1
Another assumption is that similarities and differences between people can be understood in terms of biological factors and their interaction with other factors (AO1 = 2 marks). For example, Hassett et al. (2008) found that male rhesus monkeys showed the same preference for wheeled toys that human boys do (a similarity explained by biological factors), but monkeys showed a weaker preference than human children, suggesting that biological factors interact with social/cultural factors (AO2 = 1 mark). 另一个假设是，人与人之间的相似性和差异可以通过生物因素及其与其他因素的相互作用来理解。例：Hassett (2008) 发现雄性恒河猴和人类男孩一样偏好轮子类玩具（生物因素解释的相似性），但猴子的偏好弱于儿童，说明生物因素还与社会/文化因素相互作用。
⚠️ MS 警示：这一题最容易"抓瞎"——很多学生把 example 写成"monkeys prefer wheeled toys"（只说 difference），缺了 similarity 维度。MS 会 credit partial，但只给 1/2 marks。必须 example 同时 echo "similarities AND differences"。

📝 Exam Practice — Bio A2

Q4(a) · 3 marks · AO1 ×2 + AO2 ×1
"Outline one assumption of the biological approach, including an example from the study by Holzel et al. (2011)."
AO1 (2 marks): A second assumption of the biological approach is that similarities and differences between people can be understood in terms of biological factors and their interaction with other factors.

AO2 (1 mark): Holzel found that all MBSR participants showed grey matter increase (similarity due to biological factors), but the amount of increase varied considerably between participants (individual differences caused by the interaction between training experience and individual brain biology).

§3 Cognitive Approach / 认知取向

Information Processing · Mental Processes — 3 core studies: Andrade · Baron-Cohen · Pozzulo

📘 Syllabus p.17 · Paper 2 Q4(a) · Paper 1 I&D
A1

Information processed input–process–output (computer model)

Syllabus p.17 verbatim
Information is processed through the same route in all humans: input – process – output, in a similar way to how information is processed by a computer.

💡 关键概念 echo：input – process – output 三段式 + computer metaphor 必须命中。

Andrade (2010) ★ Best fit
Doodling group vs control 在 monitoring task 上的表现
Link：Mind received input (monitoring task) + doodling processing simultaneously + produced output (recall) → demonstrates dual-processing, like a computer running parallel processes。
→ 看完整 study
Baron-Cohen et al. (2001)
Eyes Test：眼照片 → 推测 mental state → 选词
Link：Visual input (eye photos) → cognitive processing (inferring mental states) → verbal output (4 choices) → classic input-process-output。
→ 看完整 study
Pozzulo et al. (2011)
View lineup → encode → retrieve → respond
Link：Input (viewing lineup) → memory encoding/processing → identification output。
→ 看完整 study
⭐ Q4(a) Model Answer — 3 marks AO1×2 AO2×1
One assumption of the cognitive approach is that information is processed through the same route in all humans: input – process – output, in a similar way to how information is processed by a computer (AO1 = 2 marks). For example, Andrade (2010) demonstrated this when participants in the doodling condition simultaneously processed two streams of information (monitoring task input + doodling) and produced output (better recall), similar to how a computer runs parallel processes (AO2 = 1 mark). 认知取向的一个假设是，信息在所有人类中通过相同的路径处理：输入—处理—输出，类似于计算机处理信息的方式。例：Andrade (2010) 中涂鸦组同时处理两个信息流（监控任务输入+涂鸦），产生输出（更好的回忆）。
⚠️ MS 警示："input – process – output" 这个短语必须 verbatim（连字符 + 顺序）。不能在 example 里只说"two tasks"，缺 input/process/output 三段式。Examiner 会 credit 第一段但扣第二段。

📝 Exam Practice — Cog A1

Q4(a) · 3 marks · AO1 ×2 + AO2 ×1
"Outline one assumption of the cognitive approach, including an example from the study by Baron-Cohen et al. (2001)."
AO1 (2 marks): Information is processed through the same route in all humans: input – process – output, in a similar way to how information is processed by a computer.

AO2 (1 mark): Baron-Cohen's Eyes Test required participants to receive visual input (photographs of eyes), mentally process the information to identify emotions, and produce a verbal output by selecting one of four words — demonstrating the input-process-output route.
A2

Individual differences in cognitive processes (attention / language / thinking / memory)

Syllabus p.17 verbatim
There are individual differences in cognitive processes such as attention, language, thinking and memory. These processes can also help to explain behaviour and emotion.

💡 关键概念 echo：individual differences + 至少 1 个 cognitive process（attention/language/thinking/memory）+ "explain behaviour and emotion"。

Baron-Cohen et al. (2001) ★ Best fit
Female > Male on Eyes Test (sex difference in ToM / mind-reading)
Link：Individual differences in theory of mind (a cognitive process related to social attention/perception) → explains sex differences in social behaviour and emotional understanding。
→ 看完整 study
Pozzulo et al. (2011)
Older > Younger in correct identification
Link：Age differences in facial memory → explains individual differences in eyewitness identification behaviour。
→ 看完整 study
Andrade (2010)
Some participants benefited more from doodling
Link：Individual variation in attention capacity → explains why some benefit more from doodling than others。
→ 看完整 study
⭐ Q4(a) Model Answer — 3 marks AO1×2 AO2×1
A second assumption of the cognitive approach is that there are individual differences in cognitive processes such as attention, language, thinking and memory, and these processes can also help to explain behaviour and emotion (AO1 = 2 marks). For example, Baron-Cohen et al. (2001) found that females scored significantly higher than males on the Eyes Test, demonstrating individual differences in theory of mind (a cognitive process), and this cognitive difference helps explain differing social-emotional behaviours between males and females (AO2 = 1 mark). 另一个假设是认知过程（如注意、语言、思维和记忆）存在个体差异，这些过程也可以帮助解释行为和情绪。例：Baron-Cohen 等人(2001) 发现女性在眼测试中得分显著高于男性，说明心智理论的个体差异。
⚠️ MS 警示：写 assumption 时必须 4 个 process 都用 syllabus 顺序（attention → language → thinking → memory）。Examiner 普遍接受少 1-2 个，但必须含 attention 或 memory 一个，因为这两个最常考。

📝 Exam Practice — Cog A2

Q4(a) · 3 marks · AO1 ×2 + AO2 ×1
"Outline one assumption of the cognitive approach, including an example from the study by Pozzulo et al. (2011)."
AO1 (2 marks): A second assumption is that there are individual differences in cognitive processes such as attention, language, thinking and memory. These processes can also help to explain behaviour and emotion.

AO2 (1 mark): Pozzulo found that older children made significantly more correct identifications than younger children — showing individual differences in memory processes, which helps explain age-related differences in behaviour (eyewitness accuracy).

§4 Social Approach / 社会取向

Social Influence · Presence of Others — 3 core studies: Milgram · Piliavin · Perry

📘 Syllabus p.19 · Paper 2 Q4(a) · Paper 1 I&D
A1

Social contexts, environments & groups shape behaviour

Syllabus p.19 verbatim
Behaviour, cognitions and emotions are influenced by social contexts, social environments and groups.

💡 关键概念 echo：social contexts / environments / groups 三者至少 1 个 verbatim 出现。

Milgram (1963) ★ Best fit
Participants obeyed more in Yale lab vs office building
Link：Yale university实验室 context (prestige + scientific legitimacy) → obedience rates 65% vs office 47.5% → 直接示范 social context shapes behaviour。
→ 看完整 study
Piliavin et al. (1969)
Subway carriage: 8.9s critical vs 7+min in line
Link：Subway carriage environment (confined, no escape, public) → fast helping → demonstrates social environment shapes prosocial behaviour。
→ 看完整 study
Perry et al. (2015)
CID task with stranger vs familiar in private room
Link：The social situation (potential interaction with stranger) → observable distance kept → social context shapes personal space behaviour。
→ 看完整 study
⭐ Q4(a) Model Answer — 3 marks AO1×2 AO2×1
One assumption of the social approach is that behaviour, cognitions and emotions are influenced by social contexts, social environments and groups (AO1 = 2 marks). For example, in Milgram's (1963) study, participants were significantly more likely to obey the experimenter in the prestigious Yale University laboratory (a high-status social context) than in a run-down office building — showing that the social context directly shaped participants' obedient behaviour (AO2 = 1 mark). 社会取向的一个假设是行为、认知和情绪受社会情境、社会环境和群体的影响。例：Milgram (1963) 在耶鲁大学实验室比在破旧办公楼中表现出更高的服从率。
⚠️ MS 警示："social" 是关键词。写 example 时必须 verbatim 用 "social context" / "social environment"，否则 example 不算 link 回 assumption。例如写"in a lab"不够，要写"the social context of a prestigious institution"。

📝 Exam Practice — Soc A1

Q4(a) · 3 marks · AO1 ×2 + AO2 ×1
"Outline one assumption of the social approach, including an example from the study by Piliavin et al. (1969)."
AO1 (2 marks): Behaviour, cognitions and emotions are influenced by social contexts, social environments and groups.

AO2 (1 mark): Piliavin conducted the study in a social environment — the subway carriage was a confined, public space with no escape route, and this context produced a 70/40 critical/non-critical helping split, while the same helping behaviour did not occur in less salient contexts. The social environment directly shaped helping behaviour.
A2

Actual, implied or imagined presence of others influences behaviour

Syllabus p.19 verbatim
Behaviour, cognitions and emotions can be influenced by the actual, implied or imagined presence of others.

💡 关键概念 echo：actual / implied / imagined presence 三选一 verbatim + "presence of others" 必须出现。

Perry et al. (2015) ★ Best fit
Empty chair = implied presence (Ot 47.5 vs Saline 38.2 cm)
Link：虽然没人 present，但 empty chair 创造 implied presence of another person → participants kept greater distance → supports assumption 2 because nobody was physically there, showing implied presence alone is enough。
→ 看完整 study
Milgram (1963)
Experimenter physically present in room
Link：Actual presence of authority figure in room → obedience. When experimenter left, obedience dropped (e.g., 20.5% in "remote" variation) → comparison directly supports actual presence mechanism。
→ 看完整 study
Piliavin et al. (1969)
Number of bystanders on train (real presence)
Link：Diffusion of responsibility — increased actual presence of other bystanders → slower helping → supports actual presence mechanism。
→ 看完整 study
⭐ Q4(a) Model Answer — 3 marks AO1×2 AO2×1
A second assumption of the social approach is that behaviour, cognitions and emotions can be influenced by the actual, implied or imagined presence of others (AO1 = 2 marks). For example, Perry et al. (2015) showed that even when no one was physically present in the room, participants kept a greater distance from a chair that was associated with a stranger than from one associated with a familiar person — this implied presence of another person was sufficient to influence their personal space behaviour (AO2 = 1 mark). 社会取向的另一个假设是，行为、认知和情绪可能受他人实际、暗示或想象存在的影响。例：Perry 等人(2015) 在没人实际在场时，参与者对与陌生人关联的椅子保持比与熟人关联的椅子更远的距离。
⚠️ MS 警示："actual/implied/imagined" 是核心辨别点——很多学生混用。Perry = implied presence（空椅子代表他人）；Milgram = actual presence（experimenter 在房间）。Examiner 给 AO2 credit 时会看 example 是否明确指出 presence 是哪种类型。

📝 Exam Practice — Soc A2

Q4(a) · 3 marks · AO1 ×2 + AO2 ×1
"Outline one assumption of the social approach, including an example from the study by Milgram (1963)."
AO1 (2 marks): Behaviour, cognitions and emotions can be influenced by the actual, implied or imagined presence of others.

AO2 (1 mark): Milgram found that 65% of participants delivered the maximum 450V shock when the experimenter was actually present in the room giving orders. When the experimenter gave orders by phone or was absent, obedience dropped dramatically (e.g., 20.5% in the "remote" variant) — showing that the actual presence of an authority figure strongly influenced obedience behaviour.

§5 Learning Approach / 学习取向

Blank Slate · Conditioning · Social Learning — 3 core studies: Bandura · Saavedra · Fagen

📘 Syllabus p.18 · Paper 2 Q4(a) · Paper 1 I&D
A1

Blank slate — environment shapes behaviour

Syllabus p.18 verbatim
We all begin life as a blank slate. Experiences and interactions with the environment shape our behaviour and these changes are directly observable.

💡 关键概念 echo：blank slate + experiences + environment shape behaviour + "directly observable"。

Bandura et al. (1961) ★ Best fit
Children imitated aggressive model
Link：Children weren't born aggressive — they learned it through observing the model (environmental interaction). Imitation behaviour was directly observable (researchers coded it)。
→ 看完整 study
Saavedra & Silverman (2002)
Buttons paired with disgust → fear of buttons
Link：Buttons aren't innately fear-inducing — the phobia was learned through environmental pairing (buttons + disgusting images). Fear was directly observable (avoidance/fear ratings)。
→ 看完整 study
Fagen et al. (2014)
Elephants trained to put leaves on head
Link：Complex behaviour not in elephant's natural repertoire — was learned through shaping (each step reinforced). Behaviour directly observable via video scoring。
→ 看完整 study
⭐ Q4(a) Model Answer — 3 marks AO1×2 AO2×1
One assumption of the learning approach is that we all begin life as a blank slate — experiences and interactions with the environment shape our behaviour and these changes are directly observable (AO1 = 2 marks). For example, Saavedra and Silverman (2002) showed that participants developed a fear of buttons after buttons were paired with disgusting images; buttons were not innately feared, and the learned fear was directly observable through avoidance ratings — demonstrating that the phobia was learned through environmental experience (AO2 = 1 mark). 学习取向的一个假设是我们出生时都是白板——经验和环境的互动塑造了我们的行为，这些变化可以直接观察到。例：Saavedra & Silverman (2002) 表明参与者因按钮与令人厌恶的图像配对而对按钮产生了恐惧。
⚠️ MS 警示："blank slate" 是关键词，不能换"empty mind"或"tabula rasa"（syllabus 原话就是 blank slate）。"Directly observable"——A1 题的 example 必须显示"行为可观察/可测量"，否则 example 拿不到 AO2 第 3 分。

📝 Exam Practice — Lrn A1

Q4(a) · 3 marks · AO1 ×2 + AO2 ×1
"Outline one assumption of the learning approach, including an example from the study by Bandura et al. (1961)."
AO1 (2 marks): We all begin life as a blank slate. Experiences and interactions with the environment shape our behaviour and these changes are directly observable.

AO2 (1 mark): Bandura found that children who observed an aggressive adult model learned by imitating specific aggressive acts (e.g., hitting a Bobo doll with a mallet) that they would not have spontaneously produced. The newly-acquired aggressive behaviour was directly observable in a test phase, showing that the behaviour was shaped by environmental experience (the model), not innate.
A2

Learning through conditioning & social learning (S-R model)

Syllabus p.18 verbatim
We learn through the processes of operant conditioning, classical conditioning and social learning. This can be understood using the stimulus-response model.

💡 关键概念 echo：operant conditioning + classical conditioning + social learning 三者必须都出现 + stimulus-response。

Saavedra & Silverman (2002) ★ Best fit
CS (buttons) + UCS (disgust images) → CR (button fear)
Link：Clear classical conditioning (evaluative conditioning) — neutral stimulus paired with unconditioned stimulus → conditioned response。S-R model fully applies。
→ 看完整 study
Bandura et al. (1961)
Vicarious reinforcement through model
Link：Social learning theory — observed model + observed consequence → imitation. Children imitating rewarded model (vicarious reinforcement) is the stimulus-response pattern via observation, not direct reinforcement。
→ 看完整 study
Fagen et al. (2014)
Shaping with food reward
Link：Operant conditioning — successive approximations reinforced with food → behaviour gradually shaped. Each trial is a stimulus (behaviour demand) → response (elephant action) → consequence (food or not) loop。
→ 看完整 study
⭐ Q4(a) Model Answer — 3 marks AO1×2 AO2×1
A second assumption of the learning approach is that we learn through the processes of operant conditioning, classical conditioning and social learning, and this can be understood using the stimulus-response model (AO1 = 2 marks). For example, Saavedra and Silverman (2002) used a form of classical conditioning (evaluative conditioning): the neutral stimulus (buttons) was repeatedly paired with the unconditioned stimulus (disgusting images), producing a conditioned response (fear/avoidance of buttons) — directly demonstrating the stimulus-response learning process (AO2 = 1 mark). 另一个假设是学习通过操作条件、经典条件反射和社会学习进行，这可以用刺激-反应模型理解。例：Saavedra & Silverman (2002) 评估性条件反射——中性刺激（按钮）与无条件刺激（厌恶图像）配对，产生条件反应。
⚠️ MS 警示："operant conditioning" + "classical conditioning" + "social learning" 三者必须都出现。Examiner 允许用缩写（OC / CC / SLT），但顺序按 syllabus。注意："S-R" 必须写完整 "stimulus-response"，不能写"S→R"。

📝 Exam Practice — Lrn A2

Q4(a) · 3 marks · AO1 ×2 + AO2 ×1
"Outline one assumption of the learning approach, including an example from the study by Fagen et al. (2014)."
AO1 (2 marks): We learn through the processes of operant conditioning, classical conditioning and social learning. This can be understood using the stimulus-response model.

AO2 (1 mark): Fagen used operant conditioning — elephants were trained through shaping (successive approximations), where each correct response to the experimenter's stimulus (the cue) was reinforced with a food reward. Over time, the elephants learned complex behaviours (e.g., placing leaves on their heads) that were not in their natural repertoire, demonstrating the stimulus-response conditioning process.
§6 · AS I&D

Issues & Debates（AS 范围 5 个）

⚠️ 注意：AS syllabus（p.15）只列 5 个 issues，不是 6 个。Reductionism/Holism、Free Will/Determinism 是 A2 (Paper 4) 议题，不进 AS 答卷。

1️⃣ Application of psychology to everyday life / 心理学在日常生活中的应用

把核心研究结论迁移到现实场景（如 Milgram → obedience to authority 在 workplace / Milgram variations Burger 2009；Baron-Cohen → autism screening；Hoelzel → corporate mindfulness）。AO2 常考："Suggest two applications of [study] to everyday life."

把核心研究应用到现实场景是 CIE AS 高频题型。

2️⃣ Individual & situational explanations / 个体与情境解释

Individual = 把行为归因为个人特质/内部原因（如 Hassett 解释 toy preference = genetic/biological）；Situational = 归因为外部环境/情境（如 same Hassett finding could be explained by environmental socialisation）。辩论焦点：行为主要由哪方决定？Hassett 等跨物种研究支持 individual（biological）侧，Milgram 变异支持 situational 侧。

AS 焦点：到底是"性格决定命运"还是"情境决定行为"？

3️⃣ Nature vs nurture / 先天 vs 后天

Nature = 先天生物/基因；Nurture = 后天环境/经验。支持 nature 的研究：Hassett (跨物种+动物研究) · Dement (脑电+睡眠生理) · Hoelzel (脑结构改变) · Baron-Cohen (性别差异+先天 ToM 缺陷)。支持 nurture 的研究：Saavedra (按钮恐惧) · Bandura (观察学习) · Fagen (训练) · Andrade (涂鸦提升记忆)。

答题框架：[Study X] supports the [nature/nurture] side because [specific finding shows ...]，且 MS 接受两种 valid 对比（如 Hassett = nature side / Saavedra = nurture side）。

CIE AS 必考 debate 之一，AO3 题型常见。

4️⃣ Use of children in psychological research / 使用儿童作为研究对象

涉及研究：Baron-Cohen（成人+儿童版本 ToM）· Bandura (1961 children) · Pozzulo (110 个年龄 6/8/10/12 儿童) · Perry (young adults + children eye-contact)。
伦理争议：informed consent (children can't give) · protection from harm (Bandura potentially distressing Bobo) · right to withdraw (often waived) · BPS guidelines on children。
辩护：研究为了解认知/社会发展必不可少；研究后做了 debrief；学校 + 家长同意 + 教师在旁监督。

CIE 必考：孩子在 4 篇 Study 出现，注意"伦理可辩护 vs 不可辩护"区别。

5️⃣ Use of animals in psychological research / 使用动物作为研究对象

涉及研究：Fagen (elephants) · Hassett (rhesus monkeys)。Dement 用的是人类（不是动物）。
伦理争议：动物 welfare 是否被损害 · 物种差异能否 generalize 到人类 · 道德地位。
辩护：Fagen 用 positive reinforcement（无电击）· Hassett 仅观察自然 toy preference 无 invasive procedure · 研究为了解 elephant cognition / 进化心理学有不可替代价值。

极少见动物议题；Fagen/Hassett 是首选例子。Dement 不是动物实验。
📝 I&D 答题金句模板：
  1. "One debate is [debate name]." (1 mark AO1 = naming the debate)
  2. "[Study X] can be related to this debate because [specific finding] supports the [side]." (AO2/AO3 = application)
  3. Counter："However, this is limited because [counterargument]." (AO3 = evaluation, only for 4-mark I&D questions)

综合 Exam Practice（5 考期真题改编）

以下题目改写自 CIE 9990 Paper 2 m25/s25/w25/m26/s26 Q4(a) and Paper 1 Section A 真题。点击每题"Show Model Answer"看 MS 标准答法。

🎯 Paper 2 Q4(a) 类型 · 6 题综合强化

m25 / Q5 P22 adaptation · 3 marks
"Outline one assumption of the cognitive approach, including an example from the study by Baron-Cohen et al. (2001)."
Assumption (AO1 = 2 marks): Information is processed through the same route in all humans: input – process – output, in a similar way to how information is processed by a computer.

Example (AO2 = 1 mark): Baron-Cohen's Eyes Test required participants to receive visual input (eye region photos), mentally process the information (infer the emotion/mental state), and produce an output (word selection from four options) — showing the input-process-output route.
m26 / Q4(a) P12 adaptation · 3 marks
"Outline one assumption of the learning approach, including an example from the study by Bandura et al. (1961)."
Assumption (AO1 = 2 marks): We learn through the processes of operant conditioning, classical conditioning and social learning. This can be understood using the stimulus-response model.

Example (AO2 = 1 mark): Bandura demonstrated social learning: children observed an adult model behaving aggressively toward a Bobo doll and being rewarded. Through vicarious reinforcement, children later imitated the aggressive acts — this observational stimulus-response pattern supported the assumption.
s25 / Q5 P11 adaptation · 3 marks
"Outline one assumption of the biological approach, including an example from the study by Holzel et al. (2011)."
Assumption (AO1 = 2 marks): Behaviour, cognitions and emotions can be explained in terms of the working of the brain and the effect of hormones, genetics and evolution.

Example (AO2 = 1 mark): Holzel found that MBSR participants showed increased grey matter density in the brain's hippocampus (a structure linked to memory and emotion regulation) — direct evidence that brain structure change underlies changes in mindfulness cognition.
s26 / Q4(a) P13 adaptation · 3 marks
"Outline one assumption of the biological approach, including an example from the study by Dement and Kleitman (1957)."
Assumption (AO1 = 2 marks): Similarities and differences between people can be understood in terms of biological factors and their interaction with other factors.

Example (AO2 = 1 mark): Dement found individual differences in dream recall — some participants could vividly recall dreams from every awakening (high recallers) while others could recall none (low recallers). These differences in dream recall reflect variations in biological sleep physiology (REM, eye movement intensity) interacting with personal factors.
s25 / Q5 P12 adaptation · 3 marks
"Outline one assumption of the learning approach, including an example from the study by Saavedra & Silverman (2002)."
Assumption (AO1 = 2 marks): We all begin life as a blank slate. Experiences and interactions with the environment shape our behaviour and these changes are directly observable.

Example (AO2 = 1 mark): Saavedra and Silverman found that participants developed a fear of buttons only after these neutral objects were paired with disgusting images — the phobia was not innate. The newly-acquired fear/avoidance was directly observable through avoidance ratings, supporting that environmental experiences shaped behaviour.
s25 / Q5 P13 adaptation · 3 marks
"Outline one assumption of the social approach, including an example from the study by Piliavin et al. (1969)."
Assumption (AO1 = 2 marks): Behaviour, cognitions and emotions can be influenced by the actual, implied or imagined presence of others.

Example (AO2 = 1 mark): Piliavin found that helping behaviour was influenced by the number of bystanders actually present on the subway carriage — critical-condition helping was 70/40 while there were more passengers, but slowing when the victim was drunk (less "implied" social pressure to help).

🎯 Paper 1 Section A · I&D / Application 类型样题

Paper 1 Section A 不直接考 assumption，但 Application / I&D 题需熟读 assumption 文本来答。

Paper 1 / Q5 style · 4 marks · AO2
"Suggest two applications of the findings of the study by Milgram (1963) to everyday life."
Application 1: Findings can be applied to workplace safety training — knowing that the actual presence of an authority figure influences obedience, employers can design whistleblower systems where employees report safety violations without their manager being present in the room.

Application 2: Findings can be applied to modern remote work settings — if actual presence reduces obedience, organisations should require critical decisions (disciplinary action, financial) to be made in writing/video, with an appeals process that does not involve the direct manager in real-time.
Paper 1 / Q7 style · 2 marks · AO1
"Outline what is meant by the individual/situational explanations debate."
Individual explanations attribute behaviour to personal characteristics (genes, personality, biology), while situational explanations attribute behaviour to the external environment or social context (e.g., Milgram's obedience was driven by the situation, not participants' personalities). The debate concerns whether behaviour is mainly due to the person or the environment.
Paper 1 / Q7 style · 4 marks · AO1+AO3
"Baron-Cohen et al. (2001) can be related to the nature-nurture debate. Discuss."
Nature side (AO2): Baron-Cohen supports nature because females scored significantly higher than males on the Eyes Test, suggesting sex differences in theory of mind have a biological basis (possibly prenatal testosterone).

Counter — Nurture side (AO3): However, this can be challenged because socialisation differences could also explain why females perform better (e.g., girls are encouraged to read facial expressions more). The study cannot definitively separate nature from nurture.
