Approaches & Assumptions

CIE A Level Psychology / 取向与基本假设 — Exam-Focused Revision

Exam Pattern: (a) Outline [2 marks] + (b) Explain how study supports [2 marks]

Biological Approach / 生物取向

Biological systems and processes — behaviour explained by brain structures, hormones, genetics, and evolution.

Learn Mode
1 Brain, hormones, genetics & evolution explain behaviour
Behaviour, cognitions and emotions can be explained in terms of the working of the brain and the effect of hormones, genetics and evolution.
Model Answer for Q5(a) 2 marks
One assumption of the biological approach is that behaviour, cognitions and emotions can be explained by the working of the brain and the effect of hormones, genetics and evolution. For example, brain structures such as the amygdala influence emotional responses, while hormones like testosterone affect aggression levels.

行为、认知和情绪可以通过大脑的工作以及激素、基因和进化的影响来解释。例如，杏仁核等脑结构影响情绪反应，而睾酮等激素影响攻击水平。
Studies supporting this assumption: Dement & Kleitman (1957) — brain/EEG Holzel et al. (2011) — brain/MRI Hassett et al. (2008) — evolution
Dement (1957): Used EEG recordings to show that dreaming correlates with REM sleep and specific brain wave patterns — directly supporting that brain activity explains mental experiences (dreaming).

Holzel (2011): MRI scans revealed increased grey matter density in brain regions after mindfulness training — showing that mental practice causes physical brain changes.

Hassett (2008): Male rhesus monkeys preferred wheeled toys (like human boys), suggesting evolutionary/ genetic influences on gender-typed behaviour.
2 Individual differences via biological factors + interactions
Similarities and differences between people can be understood in terms of biological factors and their interaction with other factors.
Model Answer for Q5(a) 2 marks
Another assumption is that similarities and differences between people can be understood in terms of biological factors and their interaction with other factors. This means individual variations in behaviour may be due to genetic inheritance or brain differences.

人与人之间的相似性和差异可以通过生物因素及其与其他因素的相互作用来理解。这意味着行为的个体差异可能源于遗传或大脑差异。
Studies supporting this assumption: Hassett et al. (2008) — gender differences Holzel et al. (2011) — individual brain changes
Hassett (2008): Found sex differences in toy preferences in both monkeys and humans — suggesting biological (not just social) factors explain gender differences across species.

Holzel (2011): Participants showed individual differences in grey matter changes after mindfulness training — biological factors interact with experience.

Cognitive Approach / 认知取向

The mind as an information processor — mental processes mediate between stimulus and response.

Learn Mode
1 Information processed through input–process–output, like a computer
Information is processed through the same route in all humans: input – process – output, in a similar way to how information is processed by a computer.
Model Answer for Q5(a) 2 marks
One assumption is that information is processed through the same route in all humans: input – process – output, in a similar way to how information is processed by a computer. The mind receives sensory input, processes it mentally (attention, encoding, interpretation), and produces behavioural output.

信息在所有人类中通过相同的路径处理：输入—处理—输出，类似于计算机处理信息的方式。心灵接收感官输入，进行心理处理（注意、编码、解释），然后产生行为输出。
Studies supporting this assumption: Andrade (2010) — attention/dual-task processing Pozzulo et al. (2011) — memory encoding/retrieval Baron-Cohen et al. (2001) — cognitive ToM processing
Andrade (2010): Doodling group performed better on a monitoring task while listening — shows the mind can split attention between two tasks (dual-processing model), like a computer running multiple processes.

Pozzulo (2011): Children's identification accuracy depended on lineup type — demonstrates encoding and retrieval processes in memory, similar to computer data input/output.

Baron-Cohen (2001): Eyes Test requires cognitive processing of facial cues to infer mental states — shows the mind actively interprets social information (input → processing → output).
2 Individual differences in cognitive processes
Individual differences in cognitive processes such as attention, language, thinking and memory. These processes can also help to explain behaviour and emotion.
Model Answer for Q5(a) 2 marks
Another assumption is that there are individual differences in cognitive processes such as attention, language, thinking and memory. These processes can also help to explain behaviour and emotion — people vary in how they process information mentally, and this variation helps explain why we behave and feel differently.

认知过程（如注意、语言、思维和记忆）中存在个体差异。这些过程也可以帮助解释行为和情绪——人们在信息处理方式上存在差异，这种差异有助于解释我们不同的行为和情绪表现。
Studies supporting this assumption: Baron-Cohen et al. (2001) — sex differences in ToM Pozzulo et al. (2011) — age differences in memory Andrade (2010) — individual variation in attention
Baron-Cohen (2001): Females scored significantly higher than males on the Eyes Test — showing sex differences in theory of mind (a cognitive process).

Pozzulo (2011): Younger children were more likely to make incorrect identifications than older children — demonstrating age-related differences in memory and face-processing abilities.

Andrade (2010): Some participants benefited more from doodling than others — showing individual variation in attentional capacity.

Social Approach / 社会取向

Humans as social actors — behaviour shaped by presence of others, contexts, groups, and environments.

Learn Mode
1 Social contexts, environments & groups shape behaviour
Behaviour, cognitions and emotions are influenced by social contexts, social environments and groups.
Model Answer for Q5(a) 2 marks
One assumption is that behaviour, cognitions and emotions are influenced by social contexts, social environments and groups. Where we are and who we're with shapes everything we do, think, and feel.

行为、认知和情绪受到社会情境、社会环境和群体的影响。我们所处的环境以及与谁在一起塑造了我们的一切行为、思维和感受。
Studies supporting this assumption: Milgram (1963) — Yale laboratory context Piliavin et al. (1969) — subway environment Perry et al. (2015) — room selection task context
Milgram (1963): The prestigious Yale University laboratory setting influenced participants' willingness to obey — the legitimate authority context made disobedience feel more difficult.

Piliavin (1969): The unique subway carriage environment (confined space, no escape, public setting) created specific helping patterns not seen in other settings.

Perry (2015): The room selection task context (choosing where to sit relative to another chair) created a measurable behavioural outcome (distance kept) shaped entirely by the social situation.
2 Presence of others influences behaviour
Behaviour, cognitions and emotions can be influenced by the actual, implied or imagined presence of others.
Model Answer for Q5(a) 2 marks
Another assumption is that behaviour, cognitions and emotions can be influenced by the actual, implied or imagined presence of others. This means we don't need someone physically present to be affected — even thinking about others can change how we behave.

行为、认知和情绪会受到他人的实际存在、暗示性存在或想象存在的影响。这意味着不需要有人实际在场，仅仅想到他人就能改变我们的行为方式。
Studies supporting this assumption: Milgram (1963) — authority figure (actual presence) Piliavin et al. (1969) — bystanders (actual presence) Perry et al. (2015) — stranger vs familiar (implied presence)
Milgram (1963): Participants obeyed the authority figure (experimenter) who was actually present in the room — when the experimenter gave orders, participants continued delivering shocks. Shows actual presence of others influences obedience.

Piliavin (1969): Helping behaviour was affected by the number of bystanders present on the subway train — more bystanders initially led to slower helping (diffusion of responsibility). Shows actual presence of others influences prosocial behaviour.

Perry (2015): Participants kept a greater distance from a stranger than from a familiar person — even though no one else was in the room, the implied presence of another person influenced personal space preferences.

Write your answer here... (2 marks available)
Acceptable Answer (either assumption):
Option 1: Behaviour, cognitions and emotions are influenced by social contexts, social environments and groups.

Option 2: Behaviour, cognitions and emotions can be influenced by the actual, implied or imagined presence of others.

(Must include: "social contexts/environments/groups" OR "actual/implied/imagined presence of others")
5 (b) Explain one reason why the study by Piliavin et al. (subway Samaritans) supports the assumption you outlined in part (a).
Write your answer here... (2 marks available)
If you chose Assumption 1 (social contexts):
Piliavin's study took place in a natural social environment (subway carriage) where the confined space, lack of escape routes, and public nature of the setting all influenced helping behaviour. This supports the assumption because it demonstrates that social environments shape our behaviour — helping rates differed based on contextual factors like victim type (drunk vs ill) and passenger density.
If you chose Assumption 2 (presence of others):
Piliavin found that helping behaviour was influenced by the number of bystanders present on the subway train. When more people were present, help was slower (diffusion of responsibility). This supports the assumption because it shows that the actual presence of others (bystanders) directly influenced whether and how quickly participants helped the victim.
Exam Tip: Distinguish carefully between actual presence (someone physically there — Milgram's experimenter, Piliavin's bystanders), implied presence (Perry's stranger represented by an empty chair), and imagined presence (internalised social norms). Examiners reward precise terminology!

Learning Approach / 学习取向

Live to learn or learn to live? — We are born as blank slates; all behaviour is learned through conditioning.

Learn Mode
1 Blank slate — environment shapes behaviour
We all begin life as a blank slate. Experiences and interactions with the environment shape our behaviour and these changes are directly observable.
Model Answer for Q5(a) 2 marks
One assumption is that we all begin life as a blank slate. Experiences and interactions with the environment shape our behaviour and these changes are directly observable. We are not born with innate knowledge — everything is learned through experience.

我们每个人出生时都是一张白纸。与环境的经验和互动塑造了我们的行为，这些变化是可以直接观察到的。我们并非生来就拥有先天知识——所有一切都是通过经验习得的。
Studies supporting this assumption: Bandura et al. (1961) — learned aggression (modelling) Saavedra & Silverman (2002) — learned phobia (conditioning) Fagen et al. (2014) — trained behaviours (operant)
Bandura (1961): Children who observed an aggressive adult model imitated the aggressive behaviour — they weren't born aggressive; they learned it through observation (environmental influence).

Saavedra (2002): Participants developed a fear of buttons after they were paired with disgusting images — buttons are not innately fear-inducing; the phobia was learned through environmental pairing (evaluative conditioning).

Fagen (2014): Elephants learned to perform complex tasks (e.g., putting leaves on head) through — showing that even complex behaviours can be acquired through environmental interaction (operant conditioning).
2 Learning occurs through SLT, operant & classical conditioning (S-R)
The processes of social learning, operant conditioning and classical conditioning are the ways in which humans and animals learn. These processes involve stimulus-response i.e. experiences within our environment (stimuli) shape our behaviour (response).
Model Answer for Q5(a) 2 marks
Another assumption is that learning occurs through three main processes: social learning, operant conditioning, and classical conditioning. All involve stimulus-response associations, where environmental stimuli shape our behavioural responses.

另一个假设是：学习通过三种主要方式进行——社会学习、操作性条件和经典条件反射。这些都涉及刺激-反应关联，即环境刺激塑造我们的行为反应。
Studies supporting this assumption: Bandura et al. (1961) — Social Learning Theory (SLT) Fagen et al. (2014) — Operant Conditioning (SPR/shaping) Saavedra & Silverman (2002) — Classical/Evaluative Conditioning
Bandura (1961): Demonstrated Social Learning Theory — children learned aggression vicariously by observing and then imitating a role model. Those who saw the model rewarded showed the most imitation (vicarious reinforcement).

Fagen (2014): Used Operant Conditioning — elephants were trained using Successive Approximations (shaping), where each correct behaviour was reinforced with food rewards. Clear S-R learning through consequences.

Saavedra (2002): Used Evaluative (Classical) Conditioning — neutral stimulus (buttons) paired with unconditioned stimulus (disgusting images) produced a conditioned response (button fear/avoidance). Classic S-R association formation.
Exam Practice

Paper 2 Style Questions / 考试练习题

5 (a) Outline one assumption of the learning approach.
Write your answer here... (2 marks available)
Acceptable Answer (either assumption):
Option 1: We all begin life as a blank slate. Experiences and interactions with the environment shape our behaviour and these changes are directly observable.

Option 2: The processes of social learning, operant conditioning and classical conditioning are the ways in which humans and animals learn. These involve stimulus-response — experiences within our environment (stimuli) shape our behaviour (response).

(Must include: "blank slate"/"environment shape behaviour" OR "social learning/operant/classical conditioning"/"stimulus-response")
5 (b) Explain one reason why the study by Saavedra and Silverman (button phobia) supports the assumption you outlined in part (a).
Write your answer here... (2 marks available)
If you chose Assumption 1 (blank slate / environment shapes behaviour):
Saavedra showed that participants developed a fear of buttons only after buttons were paired with disgusting images in the experiment. Buttons are not inherently frightening — the fear was learned through environmental interaction (pairing with disgust stimuli). This supports the blank slate assumption because it demonstrates that experiences and interactions with the environment shaped behaviour, and this change was directly observable (measured by avoidance/fear ratings).
If you chose Assumption 2 (learning through conditioning / S-R):
Saavedra used evaluative conditioning — a form of classical conditioning where a neutral stimulus (buttons) was repeatedly paired with an unconditioned stimulus (disgusting images), producing a conditioned response (avoidance/fear of buttons). This supports the assumption because it clearly demonstrates the stimulus-response learning process: environmental stimuli (disgusting button pairings) shaped the participant's behavioural response (button avoidance).
Exam Tip: When answering Learning approach questions, always identify which type of learning the study demonstrates: vicarious reinforcement (Bandura/SLT), shaping and reinforcement (Fagen/operant), or stimulus-response association (Saavedra/classical). Using precise terminology earns marks!
