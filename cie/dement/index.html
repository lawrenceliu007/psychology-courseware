§1 Introduction & Background

The Mystery of Sleep and Dreams

Prior to the 1950s, sleep was largely considered a passive state — a period of rest where the brain simply "shut down." Dreams were viewed as fleeting, unreliable mental phenomena that could not be studied scientifically.

Rapid Eye Movement (REM) Sleep: A stage of sleep characterised by rapid, random movement of the eyes, accompanied by low muscle tone throughout the body, and the propensity of the sleeper to experience vivid dreams.

Aserinsky & Kleitman's Discovery (1953)

In a groundbreaking study at the University of Chicago, Eugene Aserinsky (a graduate student working under Nathaniel Kleitman) discovered that sleep is not uniform. He observed that during certain periods of sleep, the sleeper's eyes would move rapidly beneath closed eyelids. This became known as REM sleep (快速眼动睡眠).

This discovery raised a fundamental question that Dement and Kleitman set out to answer in their 1957 study:

"Is there a relationship between eye movements during sleep and dream activity?"
▶ Exam Key Points — Introduction AO1
  1. 必考 Before 1953, sleep was thought to be a uniform, passive state — no distinction between different stages.
  2. 必考 Aserinsky & Kleitman (1953) first identified REM sleep by observing rapid eye movements during sleep.
  3. 常考 The 1957 study aimed to establish whether eye movements correlate with dreaming.
  4. 背景 This was one of the first studies to treat dreams as an objective, measurable phenomenon.

核心逻辑：1953年发现REM现象 → 提出科学问题：眼动是否与做梦有关？→ 这是首次将"梦"从主观体验转化为可测量客观指标的研究。

记忆口诀："REM发现于53，D&K验证于57"

考试技巧：如果题目问背景，一定要提到Aserinsky & Kleitman (1953)的先驱性发现。

§2 Research Aim & Hypotheses

Overall Aim

To investigate the relationship between eye movements during sleep and dream activity, specifically testing whether eye movements can serve as an objective indicator of dreaming.

Three Specific Studies with Hypotheses

StudyHypothesis
Study 1
Eye Movement & Dream Recall		 Participants will report more vivid dreams when awakened during periods of rapid eye movement (REM) compared to non-REM periods.

Awakenings from REM → higher dream recall rate (expected ~80%)
Awakenings from non-REM → lower recall rate (expected ~10%)
Study 2
Pattern & Dream Content		 The pattern of eye movements will correspond to the content of reported dreams. For example:
• Predominantly vertical eye movements → dreams involving vertical scanning (climbing, looking up)
• Predominantly horizontal movements → dreams involving horizontal scanning (watching a game, two people talking)
Study 3
Dream Interruption		 If a participant is awakened during a REM period and reports a dream, then allowed to return to sleep and awakened again later in the same night, they may be able to report a second, distinct dream — suggesting multiple dream episodes per night.
▶ Exam Key Points — Aim & Hypotheses AO1+AO3
  • 必考 Main aim: Investigate the link between eye movements during sleep and dream activity.
  • 必考 Study 1 hypothesis: More frequent/vivid dream recall from REM awakenings vs. non-REM.
  • 必考 Study 2 hypothesis: Eye movement pattern correlates with dream content (vertical/horizontal).
  • 常考 Study 3 hypothesis: Multiple distinct dream episodes can occur in one night.
  • AO2技巧 The hypotheses are directional (one-tailed) — they predict the direction of the relationship.

核心逻辑：研究分三个子实验，层层递进：
① 有无眼动 = 是否在做梦？（存在性问题）
② 眼动方式 = 梦的内容？（对应关系问题）
③ 一晚多梦？（频率问题）

记忆口诀："一问有无、二问内容、三问次数"

考试技巧：三个假设要能分别复述，考试可能只问其中一个。

§3 Method

3.1 Research Design

This was a laboratory experiment conducted in a controlled sleep environment. The design incorporated elements of:

  • Repeated measures — participants were tested under multiple conditions (REM vs. non-REM awakenings)
  • Within-subjects comparison — each participant served as their own control
  • Quasi-experimental in some aspects — the occurrence of REM periods could not be fully randomised
Laboratory Experiment: A research method conducted in a controlled environment where variables are systematically manipulated and measured. High internal validity but potentially lower ecological validity.

3.2 Sample

CharacteristicDetails
Total Participants9 adult participants (7 male, 2 female)
Age RangeApproximately 23–32 years old
OccupationMost were students or researchers associated with the university
Recruitment MethodOpportunity sampling (机会抽样) — participants were recruited from the researchers' acquaintances and colleagues
Inclusion CriteriaGood physical health, no known sleep disorders, willing to spend multiple nights in the laboratory

3.3 Variables

Variable TypeOperationalisation

(Determined by EEG/EOG monitoring)
Dependent Variable (DV)
Study 1		 Dream recall rate — whether participant reported a dream upon awakening, measured as:
• Presence/absence of dream report
• Vividness rating (subjective scale)
• Length/detailed nature of dream narrative
IV — Study 2 Pattern of eye movements: predominantly vertical vs. predominantly horizontal (observed via EOG)

Controlled Variables • Sleep environment (temperature, light, noise level)

3.4 Materials & Equipment

  • Electroencephalograph (EEG) — to measure brain wave patterns and identify sleep stages
  • Electrooculograph (EOG) — to record eye movements during sleep by detecting electrical potentials around the eyes
  • Electromyograph (EMG) — optional, to monitor muscle tone
  • Standardised dream report form — structured questionnaire completed after each awakening
  • Intercom system — for communication between researcher and sleeping participant
Electroencephalogram (EEG): A method of recording electrical activity of the brain using electrodes placed on the scalp. Different brain wave patterns indicate different stages of sleep.

  • 必考 Sample: N=9 (7M, 2F), opportunity sampling, adults aged ~23–32.
  • 必考 IV Study 1: Type of awakening (REM vs. non-REM).
  • 必考 DV Study 1: Dream recall rate (presence, vividness, length).
  • 常考 Equipment: EEG (brain waves), EOG (eye movements), EMG (muscle tone).
  • AO2技巧 Opportunity sampling limitation: Small, biased sample — limits generalisability.

核心逻辑：实验室环境 + 被内设计（每个被试既是实验组又是对照组）+ 生理仪器客观测量（EEG/EOG）= 高内部效度但样本小、生态效度低。

§4 Procedure

Preparation Phase

  1. Participants arrived at the laboratory in the evening and were familiarised with the equipment and procedures.
  2. Electrodes were attached for EEG (to monitor brain waves), EOG (to detect eye movements), and optionally EMG (muscle tone).
  3. Participants spent 1–2 adaptation nights in the lab to acclimatise to sleeping while wired to equipment.

Data Collection Nights

  1. On experimental nights, researchers monitored participants continuously throughout the night from a separate observation room.
  2. When specific criteria were met (see below for each study), researchers entered the room or used the intercom to awaken the participant.
  3. Upon awakening, participants were immediately asked: "What was going through your mind just prior to waking?"
  4. Participants' responses were recorded verbatim, then rated for vividness on a standardised scale.
  5. After reporting, participants were allowed to return to sleep.

Specific Procedures by Study

StudyAwakening CriteriaNumber of Awakenings
Study 1 Randomly timed awakenings during both REM periods and non-REM periods Multiple per night across several nights (total: 191 awakenings analysed)
Study 2 Awakened only during REM periods with clearly observable vertical or horizontal eye movement patterns 35 awakenings with interpretable eye movement patterns
Study 3 Awakened during REM, allowed to resume sleep, then awakened a second time later (sometimes a third time) Multiple sequential awakenings per participant

Debriefing

After the final experimental night, participants were fully debriefed about the purpose of the study and the significance of their contributions to sleep research.

▶ Exam Key Points — Procedure AO1
  1. 必考 Adaptation nights: 1–2 nights to get used to sleeping with electrodes attached.
  2. 必考 Standard question: "What was going through your mind just prior to waking?"
  3. 必考 Study 1: Random awakenings during REM vs. non-REM (191 total awakenings).
  4. 常考 Study 2: Awakened only during REM with clear vertical/horizontal patterns (35 awakenings).
  5. 背景 Responses recorded verbatim + vividness rating → mixed methods approach.

核心流程：适应夜（1-2晚）→ 实验夜（连续监测）→ 按标准唤醒 → 标准提问 → 记录逐字稿+评分 → 返回睡眠。

关键细节：唤醒问题是开放式的（不暗示"做梦"），避免demand characteristics。

考试技巧：能画出流程图：电极佩戴 → 监测室观察 → 满足条件即唤醒 → 即时访谈 → 继续睡眠。

§5 Results — Study 1: Eye Movement & Dream Recall

Key Finding: Dramatic Difference in Dream Recall

MeasureREM AwakeningNon-REM Awakening
Dream Recall Rate ~80–90% ~7–10%
Vividness Rating High (dreams described as detailed, story-like, visually rich) Low (reports often vague: "I was thinking about...", fragmentary thoughts)
Length of Report Longer narratives (mean: ~200 words) Shorter reports (mean: ~20–30 words, often single sentences)
Core Result (Study 1): Awakenings during REM periods produced dream reports in approximately 80–90% of cases, whereas awakenings during non-REM periods yielded dream reports in only about 7–10% of cases. This difference was highly statistically significant.

Additional Observations

  • Dreams reported after REM awakenings typically had narrative structure — beginning, middle, and end-like sequences.
  • Non-REM reports were more like fragmentary thinking — isolated thoughts, images without context, or simple sensations.
  • The correlation between eye movement intensity and dream vividness was positive but not perfectly linear.
▶ Exam Key Points — Results Study 1 AO1
  • 必考 REM dream recall rate: ~80–90% (the headline figure!)
  • 必考 Non-REM dream recall rate: ~7–10%
  • 必考 Difference is highly statistically significant (p < .01)
  • 常考 REM dreams: longer (~200 words), narrative structure, visually vivid.
  • 常考 Non-REM reports: short (~20–30 words), fragmentary, thought-like.

核心数据：REM唤醒80-90%有梦 vs 非REM仅7-10% → 差距约8-10倍！这是整个研究的基石发现。

记忆口诀："REM八九成、非REM一成"

考试技巧：如果只能记住一个数字，就记80-90%。这是最常考的数据点。

§6 Results — Study 2: Eye Movement Pattern & Dream Content

Key Finding: Correspondence Between Eye Movements and Dream Imagery

Of the 35 REM awakenings with clearly identifiable eye movement patterns:

Eye Movement PatternDream Content ExampleCorrespondence?
Predominantly Vertical Climbing a ladder, looking up at tall buildings, watching objects fall from above ✓ Yes (majority)
Predominantly Horizontal Watching a tennis match, two people having a conversation, scanning a landscape ✓ Yes (majority)
Mixed/Minimal Static scenes, abstract thoughts, no clear visual activity — No clear pattern
Core Result (Study 2): There was a significant correspondence between the direction/pattern of eye movements observed during REM sleep and the visual content of the subsequently reported dream. This suggested that dream imagery might involve actual scanning behaviour similar to wakeful vision.

Limitations of Study 2

  • Small sample size (n=35 interpretable awakenings)
  • Some dreams had ambiguous content that was difficult to categorise
  • The correspondence was not perfect — some vertical eye movement periods did NOT produce vertically-themed dreams
▶ Exam Key Points — Results Study 2 AO1
  • 必考 35 REM awakenings with clear eye movement patterns analysed.
  • 必考 Vertical eye movements → dreams involving vertical activity (climbing, looking up/down).
  • 必考 Horizontal eye movements → dreams involving horizontal activity (watching, scanning).
  • 常考 Suggested dreams involve actual ocular scanning (like being awake).
  • AO2技巧 Not perfect correspondence — some vertical EM didn't match vertical dream content.

核心发现：眼动方向与梦境内容有对应关系——垂直眼动→上下场景、水平眼动→左右场景。这暗示做梦时眼睛真的在"扫描"梦境画面。

记忆口诀："竖看横看梦中现"

考试技巧：注意强调"correspondence"不是"causation"，而且不是100%匹配。

§7 Results — Study 3: Dream Interruption & Multiple Dreams

Key Finding: Multiple Distinct Dreams Per Night

When participants were awakened during REM, reported a dream, returned to sleep, and were awakened again later:

  • Most participants reported a second, distinctly different dream on the subsequent awakening.
  • Some participants experienced three or more separate dream episodes in a single night.
  • The dreams were thematically unrelated — suggesting discrete dream episodes rather than one continuous narrative interrupted by awakenings.
Core Result (Study 3): People experience multiple, distinct dream episodes per night, each associated with separate REM periods. An average adult experiences approximately 4–6 REM cycles per night, meaning 4–6 potential dream episodes nightly.

Estimated Dream Frequency

Based on these findings, Dement and Kleitman estimated that the average person experiences roughly 4 to 6 REM periods per night, each lasting approximately 20–30 minutes, potentially containing a distinct dream episode. Over a lifetime, this translates to approximately 6 years spent dreaming!

▶ Exam Key Points — Results Study 3 AO1
  • 必考 Multiple distinct dreams per night — not one continuous dream.
  • 必考 Each REM period may contain a separate dream episode.
  • 必考 Average: 4–6 REM cycles per night, each ~20–30 minutes.
  • 常考 Lifetime estimate: ~6 years spent dreaming!
  • 背景 Dreams are thematically unrelated across episodes within the same night.

核心发现：一晚做多个不同的梦！每个REM周期对应一个独立的梦。平均每晚4-6个REM周期 = 4-6个梦。

记忆口诀："一夜四到六梦、每梦二三十分钟"

考试技巧："人一生花6年时间做梦"是个很好的essay hook（引子）。

§8 Discussion of Findings

8.1 Interpretation of Study 1 Results

The dramatic difference in dream recall rates between REM and non-REM awakenings provides strong evidence that dreaming is closely associated with the physiological state characterised by rapid eye movements. The researchers concluded that:

"There is a reasonable certainty that dreaming occurs regularly during periods accompanied by rapid eye movements."

8.2 Interpretation of Study 2 Results

The correspondence between eye movement patterns and dream content suggests that the ocular muscles are actively engaged during dreaming in a way that reflects the visual imagery being experienced. This supports the notion that dreaming involves simulated perceptual-motor activity — the dreamer's eyes move as if they were actually observing the dream scene.

8.3 Interpretation of Study 3 Results

The finding of multiple distinct dreams per night challenged the prevailing view that dreaming was a rare or sporadic phenomenon. Instead, it showed that dreaming is a regular, rhythmic feature of normal sleep architecture, occurring in discrete episodes tied to REM cycles.

8.4 Broader Implications

  • Dreams can now be studied objectively — through physiological measurement rather than subjective morning-after reports.
  • Sleep has structure — it is not uniform but consists of alternating cycles of REM and non-REM sleep.
  • New research door opened — this methodology enabled decades of subsequent sleep and dream research.
▶ Exam Key Points — Discussion AO1+AO3
  • 必考 Study 1 proves dreaming is strongly linked to REM sleep.
  • 必考 Study 2 suggests eyes move as if scanning dream scenes (simulated perception).
  • 必考 Study 3 shows dreaming is regular and rhythmic, not sporadic.
  • AO2技巧 Major implication: dreams become objectively measurable — paradigm shift in dream research.
  • 背景 Opened the door to modern sleep science (sleep labs, REM research, dream analysis).

三层递进解读：
① 存在性证明：REM=做梦期（Study 1）
② 机制推测：眼动反映视觉扫描行为（Study 2）
③ 频率揭示：做梦是规律性的、周期性的（Study 3）

最大意义：将"梦"从玄学/精神分析领域拉入实证科学研究范畴——可以用仪器测量了！

§9 Conclusions

Primary Conclusion: There is a clear, demonstrable relationship between rapid eye movements during sleep and dream activity. Eye movements serve as a reliable physiological indicator (生理指标) of dreaming.

Summary of Conclusions

  1. Dreaming occurs predominantly during REM sleep — ~80–90% of REM awakenings produce dream reports vs. ~7–10% for non-REM.
  2. The pattern of eye movements corresponds to dream content — vertical/horizontal eye movements tend to match vertical/horizontal dream imagery.
  3. People have multiple distinct dreams per night — each REM cycle may contain a separate dream episode (4–6 cycles/night).
  4. Dreams can be studied using objective scientific methods — this study established the foundation for modern sleep science.

One-Sentence Summary for Exams

Dement & Kleitman (1957) demonstrated that rapid eye movements during sleep are a reliable indicator of dreaming, establishing that dreaming is a regular, measurable phenomenon occurring in discrete episodes throughout the night.
▶ Exam Key Points — Conclusions AO1+AO2
  • 必考 REM = Dreaming (the fundamental equation of this study).
  • 必考 Eye movements are a reliable objective indicator of dream activity.
  • 常考 Four conclusions map directly onto three studies + overall contribution.
  • AO2技巧 Memorise the one-sentence summary — useful for essay introductions/conclusions.

一句话结论："眼动=做梦"——REM期的快速眼动是梦活动的可靠生理标志。

四个结论对应三个研究：S1→存在性、S2→机制、S3→频率 + 总体方法论贡献。

考试技巧：Essay题用one-sentence summary作为thesis statement非常加分。

§10 Evaluation (AO3)

Strengths (优势)

#StrengthExplanation
1 High Internal Validity
必考		 Laboratory setting allowed tight control over extraneous variables. Standardised procedures, consistent equipment, and controlled environment mean we can be confident that the IV (type of awakening) caused differences in the DV (dream recall). The use of physiological measures (EEG/EOG) eliminated subjectivity in determining sleep stage.
2 Pioneering Methodology
常考		 This was one of the first studies to combine physiological recording with psychological reporting. It established a new research paradigm that enabled decades of subsequent sleep research. The innovation of awakening participants during specific sleep stages was revolutionary.
3 Objective Measurement
常考		 By using EOG to detect eye movements and EEG to confirm sleep stages, the study introduced objective, quantifiable indicators of what had previously been considered purely subjective experience. This moved dream research from introspection/psychoanalysis into empirical science.
4 Within-Subjects Design
AO2技巧		 Each participant experienced both REM and non-REM awakenings, serving as their own control. This controls for individual differences in dream recall ability, sleep patterns, and verbal fluency — increasing sensitivity to detect the true effect.
5 Quantitative Data
背景		 The study produced clear numerical results (80–90% vs. 7–10%) that are easy to analyse statistically and compare across studies. The large number of awakenings (191 in Study 1 alone) provided substantial statistical power.

Weaknesses (劣势)

#WeaknessExplanation
1 Low Ecological Validity
必考		 Sleeping in a laboratory with electrodes attached is highly artificial. Participants may have slept differently due to anxiety, discomfort, or unfamiliarity. The "dreams" reported may differ from natural home-sleep dreams. This limits how well findings generalise to real-world dreaming.
2 Very Small Sample
必考		 Only 9 participants (7 male, 2 female) — extremely small sample. Findings may not generalise to the wider population. The sample was also homogeneous (mostly young, educated, university-associated individuals).
3 Biased Sampling
常考		 Opportunity sampling from the researchers' social/professional circle introduces bias. Participants likely knew they were involved in a sleep study, which may have affected their sleep quality or dream content (demand characteristics).
4 Potential Demand Characteristics
常考		 Although the question "What was going through my mind..." was carefully worded to avoid suggesting dreams, participants who noticed the focus on eye movements might have felt pressured to report dreams during REM awakenings. The researchers' presence and expectations could influence responses.
5 Researcher Bias
AO2技巧		 The researchers themselves coded and interpreted dream content for Study 2. Knowing the eye movement pattern beforehand could have unconsciously influenced their categorisation of dream content (experimenter bias/confirmation bias).
6 Ethical Concerns
背景		 Repeated nighttime awakenings cause sleep deprivation and potential distress. Participants could not give fully informed consent about the extent of disruption. See §11 for full ethical analysis.
7 Imperfect Correspondence in Study 2
背景		 The eye movement–dream content correlation was not perfect. Some vertical eye movement awakenings did not produce vertically-themed dreams. This raises questions about how robust and universal the relationship is.
▶ Exam Key Points — Evaluation AO3

Top 5 Strengths (memorise these!):

  1. High internal validity (lab control, physiological measures)
  2. Pioneering methodology (new paradigm for sleep research)
  3. Objective measurement (EOG/EEG — quantitative data)
  4. Within-subjects design (controls individual differences)
  5. Large amount of data collected (191 awakenings)

Top 5 Weaknesses (memorise these!):

  1. Low ecological validity (artificial lab setting)
  2. Tiny sample (N=9, not representative)
  3. Biased sampling (opportunity, acquaintances)
  4. Demand characteristics possible
  5. Researcher bias in coding dream content

优势口诀："内效高、方法新、客观数据、被内设计、数据量大"

劣势口诀："生态低、样本小、抽样偏、需求特征、研究者偏差"

考试技巧：AO3评价题至少写3优3劣，每条都要展开解释+举例。最高分的答案会讨论"这个劣势有多严重"以及"后续研究是否解决了这个问题"。

§11 Ethical Issues

Key Ethical Considerations

IssueAnalysisModern Verdict
Informed Consent
知情同意		 Participants consented to sleep in the lab with electrodes, but may not have fully understood the extent of repeated nighttime awakenings and resulting sleep deprivation. ⚠️ Partial — consent was obtained but possibly incomplete regarding disruption level.
Right to Withdraw
退出权		 Technically participants could withdraw, but once asleep and wired to equipment, exercising this right would be difficult. Social pressure to continue may have been significant. ⚠️ Problematic — practical barriers to withdrawal during the study.
Protection from Harm
免受伤害		 Repeated awakenings caused sleep deprivation, leading to daytime fatigue, irritability, and potential cognitive impairment. Some participants found the experience stressful or unpleasant. ❌ Concern — harm occurred (sleep deprivation), though temporary.
Debriefing
事后解释		 Participants were debriefed about the study's purpose. However, the full significance of their contribution to sleep science may not have been immediately apparent. ✅ Adequate — debriefing was conducted.
Privacy/Dignity
隐私/尊严		 Sleep is a private state. Being watched/electrodes attached while sleeping could feel invasive. However, participants were in a private room. ✅ Acceptable — private room maintained dignity.

BPS Guidelines Assessment

By modern British Psychological Society standards, this study would require:

  • More explicit informed consent about sleep disruption levels
  • Clearer protocols for the right to withdraw at any point (including mid-night)
  • Risk assessment for sleep deprivation effects
  • Consideration of compensating participants for inconvenience

Note: Ethical standards were less developed in 1957. This study would face greater scrutiny today but was acceptable for its time.

§12 Exam Practice Questions

Q1 (AO1 — 6 marks): Outline Dement and Kleitman's (1957) research into the relationship between eye movements during sleep and dream activity.

Model Answer:

Dement and Kleitman (1957) conducted a laboratory experiment with 9 participants to investigate whether eye movements during sleep relate to dreaming. Participants slept in a lab with EEG and EOG electrodes attached to monitor brain waves and eye movements.

In Study 1, participants were randomly awakened during both REM and non-REM periods. Results showed that awakenings during REM produced dream reports in ~80–90% of cases, compared to only ~7–10% for non-REM awakenings. REM dreams were also longer and more vivid.

In Study 2, the pattern of eye movements (vertical vs. horizontal) was found to correspond to the visual content of reported dreams.

The researchers concluded that rapid eye movements during sleep are a reliable indicator of dream activity, and that people experience multiple distinct dreams per night. [6 marks]

Q2 (AO3 — 12 marks): Evaluate Dement and Kleitman's (1957) study of sleep and dreaming.

Model Answer:

Strengths:

  1. High internal validity — The laboratory setting allowed precise control over extraneous variables. Physiological measures (EEG/EOG) provided objective, quantifiable data about sleep stages, eliminating subjectivity. [3]
  2. Pioneering methodology — This study established a new research paradigm combining physiological recording with psychological reporting, enabling decades of future sleep research. [2]
  3. Within-subjects design — Each participant experienced both conditions, controlling for individual differences in dream recall ability. [2]

Weaknesses:

  1. Very small sample — Only 9 participants (7M, 2F) limits generalisability to the wider population. [2]
  2. Low ecological validity — Sleeping in a lab with electrodes is artificial; dreams reported may differ from natural sleep. [2]
  3. Researcher bias — In Study 2, researchers coded dream content knowing the eye movement pattern, risking confirmation bias. [1]

Overall: Despite limitations, this study revolutionised our understanding of sleep and dreaming, demonstrating that dreams can be studied through objective scientific methods. [Total: 12]

Q3 (AO3 — 8 marks): Discuss ethical issues in Dement and Kleitman's (1957) study.

Model Answer:

The main ethical issue in Dement and Kleitman's study is protection from harm. Participants were repeatedly awakened throughout the night, causing sleep deprivation. This led to daytime fatigue, irritability, and potential cognitive impairment — a form of psychological/physical harm. By modern BPS guidelines, a risk assessment should have been conducted, and the minimum necessary number of awakenings used. [3]

Secondly, informed consent may have been incomplete. While participants agreed to sleep in the lab, they may not have fully understood the extent of disruption they would experience. Modern ethics would require explicit information about expected numbers of awakenings and potential effects. [2]

Thirdly, the right to withdraw was practically compromised. Once asleep and connected to equipment, withdrawing would be difficult and disruptive. [2]

However, participants were debriefed, and the study was conducted before modern ethical codes existed. The scientific value of the findings must be weighed against these concerns. [1] [Total: 8]

💡 Exam Tip: Always contextualise for older studies — acknowledge that ethical standards have evolved. Show you understand the historical context while still identifying genuine concerns.
Q4 (AO1/AO3 — 16 marks Essay): "Dement and Kleitman's (1957) study was more important for its methods than its findings." Discuss.

Model Essay Structure:

Introduction: Briefly introduce the study, its aim, and state your argument (e.g., "While the findings about REM-dreaming linkage were groundbreaking, the methodological innovations arguably had even greater long-term impact."). [2]

Paragraph 1 — Importance of Findings: The core finding (80–90% dream recall from REM vs. 7–10% from non-REM) fundamentally changed our understanding of sleep. Established that dreaming is a regular, measurable phenomenon. Has practical applications (diagnosing sleep disorders, understanding nightmares). [3]

Paragraph 2 — Importance of Methods: Pioneered the combination of physiological measurement (EEG/EOG) with psychological reporting. Created the methodology for all future sleep lab research. Demonstrated that subjective experiences (dreams) can be studied objectively. The "awaken during REM" technique became standard practice. [4]

Paragraph 3 — Evaluation/Critique: Methods had flaws (small sample, low EV, demand characteristics) but these don't diminish the methodological breakthrough. Later studies replicated with larger samples, confirming findings. The template this study created enabled entire fields of sleep medicine and dream research. [4]

Conclusion: Both findings and methods were revolutionary, but the methodological legacy — enabling decades of subsequent research — gives methods the edge in importance. Without these methods, neither these nor any later findings would exist. [3] [Total: 16]

§13 Key Terms Glossary

TermDefinition (Chinese Translation)
REM Sleep
快速眼动睡眠		 A stage of sleep characterised by rapid, random eye movements, low muscle tone, and vivid dreaming. Typically occurs in cycles of ~90 minutes throughout the night.
Non-REM Sleep
非快速眼动睡眠		 All stages of sleep that are not REM. Divided into N1 (light), N2 (intermediate), and N3 (deep/slow-wave) sleep. Less dreaming occurs here.
Electroencephalogram (EEG)
脑电图		 A method of recording electrical activity of the brain using scalp electrodes. Different wave patterns (alpha, beta, delta, theta) indicate different states of consciousness.
Electrooculogram (EOG)
眼电图		 A recording of the electrical potential between electrodes placed around the eyes, used to detect eye movements and position.
Laboratory Experiment
实验室实验		 A research method conducted in a controlled environment where the IV is manipulated and DV measured, with high control over confounding variables.
Opportunity Sampling
机会抽样		 A sampling technique where participants are selected based on who is readily available. Quick and convenient but prone to bias.
Internal Validity
内部效度		 The extent to which a study measures what it claims to measure, free from confounding variables. Lab experiments typically score highly.
Ecological Validity
生态效度		 The extent to which findings can be generalised to real-world settings outside the laboratory. Often lower in controlled experiments.
Demand Characteristics
需求特征		 Cues in the research setting that lead participants to guess the aims and alter their behaviour accordingly (consciously or unconsciously).
Within-Subjects Design
被试内设计		 An experimental design where each participant experiences all conditions, serving as their own control. Controls individual differences but risks order effects.
Dream Recall Rate
梦境回忆率		 The percentage of awakenings that result in a dream report. In this study: ~80–90% for REM, ~7–10% for non-REM.
Sleep Cycle
睡眠周期		 The progression through sleep stages (N1 → N2 → N3 → N2 → REM) taking approximately 90 minutes. Repeated 4–6 times per night.
Debriefing
事后解释		 The process after a study where participants are informed of the full purpose, given opportunity to ask questions, and any deception is explained.
