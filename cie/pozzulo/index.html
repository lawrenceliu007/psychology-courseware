🔍 Pozzulo et al. (2011)
The Culprit in Target-Absent Line-ups: Understanding Young Children's False Positive Responding

Pozzulo, J., Dempsey, J., Bruer, K.C., & Sheahan, C.L. (2011). The culprit in target-absent lineups: Understanding young children's false positive responding. Journal of Police and Criminal Psychology, 27(1), 55–62.
CIE Psychology 认知取向 Core Study 3（认知心理学）

1. Background（研究背景）

The psychology being investigated（研究的心理现象）

Our memory works like a computer's in some ways (see Memory Assumptions in Context) but differs with respect to the way that stored data can be accidentally changed or damaged. Excluding events such as crashing, or getting a software virus, computers reproduce an error-free copy of a "memory". If information has not been deliberately processed, it comes out of storage identical to when it went in. This is not the case for human memory. We may feel as though our recall is perfectly accurate, but cognitive psychologists have shown that memories can be distorted, for example by other information that we are exposed to during and after encoding. Sometimes these changes don't matter, but when a human memory is a key source of evidence in a criminal trial, they are vitally important.

Additional information given after the event seen by an eyewitness, called post-event information（事件后信息）, can produce "false memories（错误记忆）". The witness confidently believes these false memories to be true. For example, asking the witness a question that contains extra information can distort their recall. A classic experiment by Loftus and Palmer (1974) used a video of a vehicle that had an accident. The results showed that the words used in a question affected participants' speed estimates for the vehicle. The adult participants estimated faster speeds when asked "How fast were the cars going when they smashed into each other?" than when they were asked "How fast were the cars going when they hit each other?".

📌 KEY WORDS（关键词）

  • False memory（错误记忆）: a piece of stored information an individual believes to be an accurate memory but which is the consequence of later additional and untrue information, such as in a question about an event seen by an eyewitness
  • Eyewitness testimony（目击者证词）: evidence provided by an individual who has seen (or heard) a crime being committed. This information is used by the legal system.
  • Line-up（排队指认）: a source of evidence used by the legal system. A witness is shown a line of people or array of photographs of faces and is asked to attempt to identify the perpetrator of the crime (although they may not be present).
  • False positive response（错误肯定反应）: giving an affirmative (positive) but incorrect answer to a question (for example, mistakenly picking out a person in a line-up when the real culprit is not there)

Children as well as adults can be witnesses to crimes. Sometimes a child may be the only person to see a crime, so the legal system must rely on their eyewitness testimony（目击者证词）. For this evidence to be useful, children need to be reliable witnesses, but are they?

1.1 Child Witnesses and Line-ups

Pozzulo et al. studied child witnesses and recognised that cognitive effects, such as those caused by post-event information including the way questions are asked, can be responsible for errors in decision-making by children. Some of their earlier research (Pozzulo & Lindsay, 1997) showed that children were less likely than adults to say "I don't know" in response to a question even when they knew this response was possible.

One way that witness information is used by the police is in a line-up（排队指认）, where the witness is asked to identify the perpetrator of the crime from a selection of individuals presented to them (whether or not the perpetrator is actually present). However, this system can make mistakes and therefore miscarriages of justice. A witness might be more likely to make errors if they are asked to make a decision from a line-up and are then presented with another line-up containing the same person. Pozzulo and Lindsay (1998) found that when the culprit is not among the people in a line-up, children are more likely than adults to identify an innocent person — this is called a false positive response（错误肯定反应）.

Figure 3.11: A police line-up（警察排队指认）。目击者需要从队列中辨认出嫌疑人。

1.2 Why Do Children Make More Errors?

This study focused on social effects on child witnesses. Pozzulo et al. suggested that children may be more likely to make incorrect decisions in a line-up as a result of several different social factors:

  • If a child is asked to select which of the people in the line-up is the culprit, they may think that they need to choose one of the people. Not making a choice (i.e. saying the culprit is not there) could be seen as a "non-response" from the child's perspective. They may think "If I am given a selection I must make a choice."
  • A child is likely to view the adult who asks them the question as an authority figure（权威人物）, especially if it is a police officer. This would make them more likely to comply with this request.
  • Children may feel more pressured（压力） to make a choice than adults. They will want to comply with the request and may even fear getting into trouble if they do not choose somebody.

1.3 Four Predictions（四个假设）

The aims were to test the following four predictions:

  1. Children will be as good as adults at identifying cartoon faces in a target-present line-up
  2. Children will be worse than adults at identifying human faces in a target-present line-up
  3. Children will be worse than adults at rejecting cartoon faces in a target-absent line-up
  4. Children will be worse than adults at rejecting human faces in a target-absent line-up
💡 核心逻辑： 如果儿童对卡通人物的识别率接近100%（因为熟悉、认知负荷低），但在 target-absent 条件下的正确拒绝率仍然显著低于成人 → 说明错误主要由社会因素（social factors）驱动，而非认知因素（cognitive factors）。这是本研究的核心假设！

2. Aim（研究目的）

Social factors may have a greater effect on child witnesses than adults'. One eyewitness test（目击者测试） is identification of a suspect, that is, picking them out from a 'target-present' line-up（目标存在队列） where the culprit is among the faces presented. Another eyewitness test is the ability to correctly reject faces when the culprit is not presented, i.e. in a 'target-absent' line-up（目标缺失队列）.

To explore the effect of social versus cognitive factors on children's performance as eyewitenesses Pozzulo et al. needed to minimise any cognitive effects that could impair the children's decision-making. They did this by comparing identification and rejection of a cartoon character（卡通人物）. As cartoon characters are familiar, a child should identify them with the same accuracy as adults (approximately 100%). This is because it is a cognitively easy task; it only requires matching an existing memory to the faces they can see in the line-up. However, in a target-absent task, matching is not possible so a selection must be made. Faced with a harder task, children should rely more heavily on social factors, and make more errors than adults.

3.1 Research method and design

This study was a laboratory experiment（实验室实验）. Participants were in a laboratory whereas real line-ups would take place somewhere official, such as at a police station.

5. Procedure（程序）

To conduct the experiment, the researchers prepared video clips and line-up photograph arrays.

5.1 Stimulus Materials（刺激材料）

Human face targets: Two Caucasian students were used to make two types of stimulus material:

  • Videos: A 6-second clip was filmed of an everyday task: a female brushing her hair and male putting his coat on. Each video was in colour with no sound and showed 2 to 3 seconds of the individual's face.
  • Photoarrays (line-ups): For each target, four "foil" photographs were chosen by three raters to look similar to the targets in terms of facial features, hair length and colour. All photographs were cropped to include only the face, neck and top of the shoulders. Photos were in black and white.

Cartoon face targets: Two cartoon characters were used as targets:

  • Dora the Explorer talking to the audience
  • Go, Diego, Go! putting on a pair of gloves for safety

The videos were 6 seconds in colour with no sound, showing 2–3 seconds of the target character's face. For photoarrays, images of the two cartoon "targets" plus four foils from the internet were selected by three raters. All photos were in black and white to reduce the influence of bright colours on recognition.

5.2 Experimental Procedure

📋 Research Methods Box（研究方法要点）

Line-ups are commonly used in legal cases worldwide. The procedure tends to be highly controlled to minimise errors, for example ensuring photographs of faces are the same size and either all in colour or all in black and white. Nevertheless, there is a problem that cannot be easily resolved. People are less able to accurately identify a person of a different ethnic group from themselves. When setting up studies in psychology to test line-up procedures it is therefore important to consider the ethnic groups of the participants and of the individuals in the line-up as potential confounding variables.

For Children:

  1. Three female experimenters wore professional-casual clothing (not overly formal) to reduce social pressure from authority figures.
  2. Each child was tested individually after making crafts together to build rapport and comfort.
  3. The experimenter explained the child could change their minds at any time and would not get into trouble.
  4. The child watched one video clip (order randomised across participants).
  5. Following the clip, the experimenter asked a free recall filler question: "What did the cartoon character/person look like?" followed twice by "Do you remember anything else?"
  6. The experimenter then displayed the corresponding line-up on a laptop screen (PowerPoint).
  7. The experimenter instructed: "The person you saw may or may not be there. If the correct person is NOT there, please point to the silhouetted box."
  8. The procedure repeated for all 4 videos (Dora + Diego + Female Human + Male Human), each time reminding the child the target may not be present.
  9. After completion, children received crayons and a colouring book as a thank-you gift.

For Adults:

  1. Gave written consent for a study about "memory".
  2. Watched each video clip individually.
  3. After each clip, wrote responses to free recall questions on a sheet ("What did the cartoon character/person look like?" + "Do you remember anything else?").
  4. Viewed the line-up on laptop and wrote down their identification response.
  5. Same 4-video sequence as children, order counterbalanced.

Key control features（控制特征）:

  • Line-up position of target was randomised per participant
  • Video presentation order was counterbalanced（平衡/抵消）
  • All photos in black & white to eliminate colour cues
  • Target-present line-ups: target + 3 foils
  • Target-absent line-ups: 4 foils (similar-looking replacement in original target position)
  • Each line-up included a blank silhouette box representing "not here" option

7. Conclusion（结论）

As predicted, the children could easily find the correct face in the target-present line-up with cartoons, identifying with almost 100% accuracy (a ceiling effect（天花板效应）) in this task. Pozzulo et al. concluded, therefore, that any error in the target-absent line-up for cartoons must be a result of social factors rather than cognitive factors.

This is because it cannot be explained by a faulty memory for the faces of the cartoon characters. The most likely social factor affecting a low correct rejection rate by children is the child's expectation that they should make a selection, rather than a "non-selection", i.e. to correctly say that the target character is absent from the line-up.

Furthermore, this suggests that the difference seen between children and adults in their rejection rates for human faces, which resembles that for the cartoon faces, can also be explained by the effects of social demands. Finally, these effects of cognitive versus social demands are relative not absolute: both tasks, identification and rejection, will be affected to an extent by both cognitive and social factors. The suggestion from the results is only that for children, social factors play a larger part in decision-making in target-absent line-ups than in target-present ones.

8. Strengths and Weaknesses（优缺点）

Strengths（优点）

  1. Standardised laboratory environment（标准化的实验室环境） — 所有参与者在相同条件下接受测试，最大限度减少无关变量干扰。实验程序高度可控。
  2. Mixed design（混合设计） — 使用独立测量比较儿童 vs 成人，同时使用重复测量比较同一被试在卡通 vs 人脸条件的表现。这种设计能排除个体差异对结果的混淆影响。
  3. Quantitative data（定量数据） — 因变量（识别率/拒绝率）是清晰的量化指标，可以使用统计检验（卡方检验 χ²）来验证假设是否成立。
  4. High ecological validity for the line-up task — 使用真实的排队指认格式（simultaneous presentation 同时呈现所有照片 + blank silhouette 选项），模拟真实司法情境中的目击者指认过程。
  5. Inclusion of both cartoon and human stimuli — 通过对比熟悉的卡通和不熟悉的真人面孔，巧妙地分离了认知因素和社会因素的贡献。这是本研究最大的方法学亮点。
  6. Counterbalancing and randomisation — 视频顺序平衡、line-up 位置随机化，有效控制了顺序效应和位置偏差。

Weaknesses（缺点）

  1. Low ecological validity overall（整体生态效度较低） — 真正的目击者在警察局经历的是高度紧张、情绪化的场景。实验室环境缺乏这种真实感和情感投入。成人和儿童面对真正的罪犯时可能会有不同的反应模式。
  2. Demand characteristics（需求特征）问题 — 成人被告知这是一个关于"记忆"的研究，儿童被告知是关于电视节目和电脑游戏的研究。成人可能猜测到实验目的而改变行为；儿童则可能因为想取悦研究者而做出特定选择。
  3. Ethnic homogeneity of targets（目标面孔种族单一） — 所有人类目标和干扰项都是白人面孔，这可能限制了结果的普遍性。现实司法环境中，目击者和嫌疑人的种族往往更加多样化。
  4. Limited age range（年龄范围有限） — 儿童（4-7岁）和成人（17-30岁）之间没有青少年群体作为过渡。无法确定错误率的年龄变化趋势。
  5. No measure of confidence — 研究只记录了选择结果，没有收集参与者对自己答案的信心程度。信心水平可能提供额外的诊断信息。
  6. Small sample size for some cells — 部分条件下（如女性真人目标）的有效样本量较小，可能影响统计效力。

9. Ethical Issues（伦理问题）

Ethical issues are not only about the implications for the participants of research but also make wider society. Clearly, the findings of this study make a valuable contribution to the use of children as witnesses and therefore help to improve lives. However, any potential benefits must be measured against potential damage to the participants. If the children in this research had been victims of real crimes, this would have risked considerable potential for harm.

However, the experimental situation was not crime-related（非犯罪相关） and the children were not at risk. Furthermore, consent was gained from the parents/guardians of each child, as well as informed consent being gained from the children themselves in a child-friendly way（儿童友好方式）. Finally, the children's comfort and their right to withdraw being ensured as well as ongoing monitoring of their fatigue, anxiety and stress to protect them from harm.

  • Informed consent（知情同意）：儿童的父母/监护人签署同意书，儿童本人也以适合其年龄的方式获得知情同意
  • Right to withdraw（退出权）：明确告知可以随时退出且不会受到惩罚
  • Protection from harm（免受伤害保护）：监控疲劳、焦虑和压力；实验前先一起做手工建立信任感
  • Debriefing（事后解释）：完成后给予蜡笔和涂色书作为感谢

10. Issues and Debates（议题与辩论）

10.1 Application to Everyday Life（在日常生活中的应用）

This study has the potential for application to everyday life. The findings suggest that strategies should be devised to minimise social demands which could influence children's responses to line-ups. This is important as it could improve the reliability of child witness information. This could raise the quality of decisions based on children's evidence and thus the effectiveness of court proceedings against perpetrators whose crimes are either only witnessed by children or are against child victims.

However, the evidence only contributes to identifying the problem, and not offering solutions. Further investigation is needed to find out how improvements could be made to the line-up procedure.

Practical solution — The Elimination Line-up: One lineup procedure created to reduce social demands is the elimination line-up（排除法队列） developed by Pozzulo and Lindsay (1999). In this procedure, children make two judgments:

  1. Select the person most similar to the target
  2. Determine whether this most similar person IS actually the target
Empirical data shows this procedure produces very positive results across different aged children under varying conditions (Dempsey & Pozzulo, 2008; Pozzulo et al., 2008–2010). Reliable identification evidence is possible from child eyewitnesses (as reliable as adult identification evidence) when appropriate procedures are used.

10.2 Individual and Situational Explanations（个体 vs 情境解释）

本研究的核心发现——儿童在 target-absent line-ups 中更容易做出错误指认（false positive）——可以用两种不同层面的解释来理解：

🧑 个体解释（Individual Explanation）

从个体因素来看，儿童的错误可能源于认知发展不成熟：

  • 儿童的大脑（尤其是前额叶皮层）仍在发育中，抑制控制能力（inhibitory control）较弱，更难抵制"选一个"的冲动
  • 儿童对不熟悉的人脸识别能力显著低于成人（23% vs 66%），说明其记忆编码和提取能力尚未完全发育
  • 年龄相关的认知差异是先天的、内在的发展过程，每个儿童都会经历

支持证据：儿童识别人脸的正确率（23%）远低于成人（66%）→ 说明认知能力确实存在个体差异

🌍 情境解释（Situational Explanation）

从情境因素来看，儿童的错误主要来自社会情境的压力和要求：

  • Line-up 本身就是一种社会情境：实验者（权威人物）给出一组照片，隐含的信息是"请选一个"
  • 儿童比成人更容易受到服从压力（obedience pressure）的影响，不想让成年人失望
  • "给我看了队列就一定要选一个人"——这是一种习得的社会期望（social expectation）

关键证据：儿童对卡通的 target-present 识别率高达 99%（≈完美），但 target-absent 正确拒绝率仅 74%（vs 成人94%）→ 记忆没有问题，问题出在社会情境！

💡 研究结论倾向：Pozzulo et al. 明确倾向于情境解释。因为如果错误仅由个体认知因素导致，那么儿童在卡通条件下的表现也应该差（但实际接近100%）。卡通和真人条件之间的巨大差异，说明社会需求(social demands)才是儿童高 false positive 率的主要原因。

10.3 Nature versus Nurture（先天 vs 后天）

先天 vs 后天（Nature vs Nurture）是心理学中最持久的辩论之一：我们的行为和特征是由遗传/生物学因素（nature）决定的，还是由环境/经验（nurture）塑造的？Pozzulo et al. 的研究为这一辩论提供了一个有趣的案例。

🧬 先天（Nature）论点

支持"先天"解释的证据包括：

  • 大脑发育的年龄差异：4-7岁儿童的前额叶皮层尚未完全成熟，负责冲动控制和决策抑制的神经网络仍在发展中。这可能使儿童在"不确定时拒绝选择"方面存在生理性的困难
  • 人脸识别的神经基础：梭状回面孔区（FFA）在儿童期仍在精细化，这可以解释为什么儿童对不熟悉真人面孔的识别率（23%）远低于成人（66%）
  • 年龄作为连续变量：错误指认率可能随着年龄增长自然下降，反映的是神经系统发育的时间表，而非学习过程

🌱 后天（Nurture）论点

支持"后天"解释的证据更强，且与本研究结论一致：

  • 社会化的服从行为：儿童从小被教育"听大人话"、"回答问题"、"不要让大人失望"——这些是习得的社交规则，而非天生本能
  • 卡通条件的关键证据：儿童对卡通人物的识别能力接近100%（排除认知缺陷），但正确拒绝率仍显著低于成人 → 说明问题不在"能不能记住"，而在"敢不敢不选"——这是后天社会化过程中形成的对权威的反应模式
  • 跨文化差异的可能性：不同文化中儿童对权威的态度不同（例如集体主义文化可能更强调服从），如果研究在其他文化中重复，结果可能因社会化方式不同而有差异——这进一步支持 nurture 解释
💡 综合判断：Pozzulo et al. 的研究更支持 Nurture（后天） 解释。核心逻辑链：
① 儿童记忆能力正常（卡通识别99%）→ 排除认知/先天缺陷
② 但拒绝率低（74% vs 成人94%）→ 错误来自决策过程而非记忆过程
③ 决策过程中的错误与社会压力相关（权威人物、选择期望）→ 这些都是后天社会化产物
→ 结论：儿童的 false positive responding 更多是后天习得的社会顺从行为，而非先天的认知局限。

当然，正如作者在 Limitation 中承认的，现实情况很可能是 先天和后天的交互作用（interaction）：儿童的神经发育水平（nature）决定了他们对情境压力的易感性程度，而具体的社会化经历（nurture）则塑造了这种易感性如何在 line-up 情境中表现出来。

10.4 Children as Participants（儿童作为参与者）

与其他许多心理学研究不同，Pozzulo et al. 的研究直接以 4-7 岁儿童作为被试。这使得"儿童作为参与者"这一议题在本研究中具有特别重要的地位。

为什么使用儿童？—— 研究的必要性

在真实的司法情境中，儿童往往是唯一的目击者（例如家庭暴力、虐待、绑架等案件中）。如果研究只用成人被试，其结论可能无法推广到儿童目击者身上。Pozzulo et al. 选择使用儿童是因为：

  • 之前的元分析（Pozzulo & Lindsay, 1998）已发现儿童和成人在 line-up 表现上存在差异
  • 需要直接测试这种差异是否由社会因素驱动（而非仅仅假设）
  • 只有用真实儿童数据才能为司法实践提供可靠建议

本研究的儿童友好设计

Pozzulo et al. 展示了如何负责任且有效地以儿童为研究对象：

常规成人程序本研究的儿童适配目的
书面知情同意书父母/监护人同意 + 儿童口头同意（适合年龄的语言解释）保障儿童自主权
纸笔作答手指指向选择答案 + 实验者记录降低读写能力要求
正式进入实验先一起做手工建立信任感减少焦虑和陌生感
统一着装实验者穿休闲职业装（非制服/非正装）降低权威人物压力
标准事后解释蜡笔+涂色书作为感谢礼物积极结束体验
固定监控方式持续监测疲劳、焦虑、压力信号及时保护儿童福祉

使用儿童的伦理考量

  • 知情同意的特殊性：儿童不能签署法律文件，需要"双重同意"程序——监护人书面同意 + 儿童本人口头/行为同意。研究者必须用适合该年龄段的语言解释研究内容
  • 退出权的强调：反复告知儿童"可以随时改变主意"、"不会惹麻烦"——这对儿童尤其重要，因为他们比成人更容易感到"必须完成"的社会压力
  • 免受伤害的保护：本研究使用了非犯罪相关的视频内容（卡通+日常生活动作），避免了让儿童接触暴力或创伤性材料
  • 去识别化（anonymity）：所有儿童数据匿名处理，保护隐私

对其他研究的启示

Pozzulo et al. 的方法为后续以儿童为对象的心理学研究提供了一个优秀范例：通过适当的程序调整和心理准备，年幼儿童完全可以成为可靠、有效的研究参与者。这打破了"只能用成人做实验"的限制，使得发展心理学和应用认知心理学的研究能够更准确地反映不同年龄群体的真实情况。

对比：Baron-Cohen et al. (2001) 的 Eyes Test 使用的是成人男性被试和成人眼睛照片。如果要测试儿童的心理理论(ToM)能力，Baron-Cohen 开发了更适合儿童的"Sally-Anne 测试（莎莉-安妮测试）"——用玩偶表演代替眼睛图片，用更简单的语言代替复杂的情绪词汇。这表明同一研究问题在不同年龄群体中需要采用完全不同的方法论。

11. Summary（总结）

Pozzulo et al.'s study explored factors affecting children's accuracy as eyewitnwsses when presented with a line-up. Adult and child participants were tested using a line-up photoarray that tested cognition: remembering and identifying the culprit when they are present. This was compared to a task that could be readily influenced by social factors: a target-absent line-up where the task was rejection rather than identification.

In this situation it is possible for the child to give a false positive response by incorrectly identifying an innocent individual as the perpetrator. To be certain that the tasks were of cartoon characters as targets and foils, as well as humans. The results showed that children were as good as adults at identifying cartoon characters, but less able to correctly identify humans and less able to correctly reject either type of target. The tendency of children to choose an incorrect face (cartoon or human) in target-absent line-ups suggests that these errors are due to social effects. They cannot be cognitive errors as the children's memory for the faces was excellent, as their identification was accurate. Therefore, the social demand of choosing a face when the target was not there must have been the cause of the rejection errors.
