🧠 Andrade (2010)

What does doodling do?
涂鸦对注意与记忆的影响
🧠 CIE Cognitive Approach 认知取向 📄 Applied Cognitive Psychology, 24(1), 100–106 🔬 Core Study — Applied Cognitive Psychology ✅ Based on CIE Textbook 基于教材原文
🧠 Cognitive Approach 📐 Lab Experiment + Independent Measures 💡 Divided Attention & Working Memory

1. Background（研究背景）

1.1 What is doodling?（什么是涂鸦？）

Sometimes when our minds wander, we scribble absent-mindedly. We might draw a picture or sketch a pattern, perhaps shading in the shapes while we are thinking about something else. This is called doodling （涂鸦）.

Teachers often tell learners off for doodling. We might defend ourselves by saying, "I didn't even know I was doing it." But perhaps the teacher's complaint is justified: research has shown that we perform less well when our attention （注意） is divided between tasks.

"Doodling" 不是认真画画，而是边做其他事边无意识乱画。比如你一边听课一边在笔记本边缘画小图案，这就是 doodling。很多人觉得这是不专心的表现，但 Andrade 的研究正是要挑战这个直觉——涂鸦可能反而有助于保持注意。

1.2 Divided attention（分配性注意）

Doodling, even though we feel it is "unconscious", takes up some of our information-processing capacity （信息加工容量）. One possibility is that doodling takes cognitive resources away from the intended primary task （主任务） by dividing attention. If we perform a divided attention task （分配性注意任务）, doodling would therefore worsen performance.

However, divided attention is easier when the tasks are simple, well practised and automatic. For example, an experienced driver can hold a conversation while driving, but a new driver may find this difficult. In this case, driving is the primary task and talking is the concurrent task （并发任务）.

Primary task 是你主要要做的事；concurrent task 是同时发生的另一件事。如果你同时做两件事，你的 attention 就被分配了。如果两件事都很熟练（如老手开车+聊天），影响就小；如果都不熟练，表现就会下降。关键在于：当两个任务使用不同的认知资源时，它们不会互相干扰——这正是 Andrade 选择听觉主任务 + 视觉空间涂鸦任务的原因。

1.3 An Alternative Explanation: Arousal and Focused Attention（另一种解释：唤醒与集中注意）

An alternative explanation is that doodling could improve performance by raising arousal （唤醒水平） and enhancing focused attention （集中注意） on the primary task. Doodling might aid concentration by reducing daydreaming （白日梦） so that you stay focused.

This idea is based on the working memory model （工作记忆模型） by Baddeley and Hitch. This model of short-term memory suggests that two different types of current or "working" memory can be used at the same time:

  • one is visuo-spatial （视觉空间） — for visual and spatial information;
  • the other is auditory （听觉） — for sounds and words.

These are governed by an overall central executive （中央执行系统）.

In this model, a primary "listening" task would use the auditory part of working memory, while a concurrent "visual" task (doodling) would use the visuo-spatial part. Because they use different memory resources, they may not interfere with each other.

工作记忆模型认为人的短期记忆不是单一仓库，而是有多个"子系统"。听觉任务和视觉任务可以分别使用不同系统，因此同时进行时不一定会互相干扰。例如：一边听讲座（听觉）一边涂鸦（视觉空间），可能比一边听讲座一边做白日梦（也用听觉/认知资源）干扰更小。Andrade 选择听觉主任务 + 视觉涂鸦任务正是基于这一理论——两者使用不同的工作记忆模块，不会直接竞争同一资源。

1.4 Daydreaming and Boredom（白日梦与无聊——研究设计的关键）

Daydreaming is also linked to arousal. When we are bored, we are more likely to daydream. Daydreaming uses important cognitive processing resources, which can inhibit performance on tasks that use the same resources, such as attention and memory.

Alternatively, doodling may help maintain arousal by giving you something physical to do while you think. It could raise arousal to keep you awake if you are sleepy, or reduce arousal if you are agitated because you are bored. These effects would also enhance performance on the primary task.

Andrade defines doodling as the sketching of patterns and figures that are unrelated to the primary task. In the opening of the original paper, she describes a familiar scenario: the call centre has put you on hold, you start thinking about a holiday, and then you realise the person you were waiting to speak to has already started talking — you haven't taken in anything they said. This illustrates how daydreaming starts in moments of boredom and distracts attention from the task at hand.

Andrade used several strategies to increase boredom in her participants (see Procedure). These increased the possibility of boredom affecting attention, therefore creating a situation in which doodling would have the potential to affect attention, for better or worse. Her expectation was that the concurrent task of doodling would interfere less with overall processing than devoting a greater amount of central executive function to daydreaming. Doodling should, therefore, improve attention and thus recall should increase.

两种相互竞争的理论（考试常考）：

  • 消极预测（divided attention 观点）：Doodling 占用认知资源 → 分散注意力 → 损害表现。
  • 积极预测（arousal 观点）：Doodling 防止白日梦 → 保持适当唤醒 → 改善注意和记忆。Andrade 正是支持这一预测。
ER Insight: 本研究的逻辑链是 无聊 → 白日梦 → 分心 → 表现差。Andrade 认为涂鸦（视觉空间任务）比白日梦（占用中央执行资源）干扰更小。考官喜欢的表述：她通过 降低白日梦 来 维持注意，而不是"涂鸦让手有事可做"这么简单。

2. Aim（研究目的）

Doodling, such as shading in all the Os in "psychology" on a worksheet, is a common activity. Andrade was interested to know whether this activity assisted information processing. This could happen because doodling enables people to attend more effectively, or it could enhance their memory.

一句话 aim： To investigate whether doodling helps concentration and memory during a boring listening task.
（研究涂鸦是否有助于在枯燥的听觉任务中保持注意和记忆。）

Andrade noted that this was the first experimental test of the prediction that doodling aids concentration. She chose an auditory primary task so that doodling (a visuo-spatial task) would compete minimally for modality-specific resources.

ER Insight: 关键短语是 "first experimental test of whether doodling aids concentration"。回答 aim 时必须包含：(1) 涂鸦是否能 帮助注意/记忆；(2) 在一个 枯燥的听觉任务 上检验；(3) 注意解释 vs 记忆解释两个方向。IV = 涂鸦与否；DV = 回忆成绩。

3. Method（研究方法）

3.1 Research Method and Design（研究方法与实验设计）

This was a laboratory experiment （实验室实验）. The environment was not the normal place in which people would respond to telephone messages, but the situation was controlled. The design was independent measures （独立测量设计） because participants were either in the control group （控制组） or in the doodling group. Participants were randomly assigned （随机分配） to one of the two conditions.

Laboratory experiment 意味着研究者在控制条件下做实验，而不是在真实生活中。Independent measures 意思是每个被试只参加一种条件：要么涂鸦，要么不涂鸦。这样两组被试不同，不会因为先参加一次测试而影响第二次（避免顺序效应）。随机分配 确保两组被试在实验前的差异尽可能小。

3.2 Sample（样本）

The participants were 40 members of a participant panel at the UK's Medical Research Council (MRC) Applied Psychology Unit (now the Cognition and Brain Sciences Unit). The panel was made up of members of the general population aged 18–55 years, and they were paid a small honorarium （酬金） for participation.

The sample was mainly female. One participant in the doodling condition did not doodle and was replaced.

ER Insight: 样本的关键数字：40 人（各 20）、18–55 岁、MRC 被试库志愿者、小额酬金、以女性为主、涂鸦组 1 人未涂鸦被替换。这些是描述样本（[3] 分题）的得分点，也是抽样评价（偏差：志愿者/被试库/主要女性）的依据。

3.3 Materials（材料）

A mock telephone message was recorded onto audio cassette tape in a fairly monotone （单调的） voice at an average speaking rate of 227 words per minute, and played at a comfortable listening volume. The script included:

  • eight names of people attending a party;
  • names of three people and one cat who could not attend;
  • eight place names;
  • much irrelevant material.

Participants in the doodling condition used a pencil to shade shapes of approximately 1 cm diameter printed on a piece of A4 paper, with 10 shapes per row and alternating rows of squares and circles. A 4.5 cm wide margin on the left-hand side allowed space for writing the target information. Control participants wrote the target information on a lined piece of paper.

这张图就是研究里发给涂鸦组的纸张。上面印满了交替排列的方形和圆形（每行10个，直径约1cm），被试的任务就是边听录音边用铅笔把每个形状涂上颜色，左侧留了4.5cm宽的空白用来写他们记住的人名。注意：涂这些形状本身和"电话内容"没有任何关系——这就是 doodling 的本质：与主任务无关的视觉空间任务。Andrade 没有让被试自由涂鸦，而是统一用涂色任务，是为了避免被试因为画画内容而产生自我意识或怀疑研究的真实目的。

4. Procedure（实验程序）

4.1 Recruiting a Bored Sample（招募"无聊"的被试）

Participants were recruited just after finishing an unrelated experiment (on ways of giving directions to different locations) for another researcher, and asked if they would mind spending another 5 minutes helping with research. The intention was to enhance the boredom of the task by testing people who were already thinking about going home.

Participants were tested individually in a quiet and visually dull room. All participants were given a piece of paper to use as a response sheet.

  • For the control group, this was a sheet of lined paper so they could have doodled if they wanted to. However, they were given no specific instructions about doodling.
  • For the doodling group, participants had a sheet of A4 with printed shapes and a wide margin in which to write their responses. They were asked to shade in the shapes with a pencil, without worrying about speed or neatness. The task was described as "just something to relieve the boredom".

Participants were given standardised instructions:

"I am going to play you a tape. I want you to pretend that the speaker is a friend who has telephoned you to invite you to a party. The tape is rather dull but that's okay because I don't want you to remember any of it. Just write down the names of people who will definitely or probably be coming to the party (excluding yourself). Ignore the names of those who can't come. Do not write anything else."

Participants in the doodling condition were also told: "It doesn't matter how neatly or how quickly you do this — it is just something to help relieve the boredom."

研究者故意告诉被试"不需要记住内容"，这是为了让他们放松，更容易感到无聊。这同时也是一个 deception（欺骗）环节，因为接下来突然测试他们的记忆。另外，被试是在做完别的实验后被"顺便"拉来参加的，他们本来就想着回家，这进一步增加了无聊感——这是 Andrade 精心设计的。

Participants listened to the tape, which lasted 2.5 minutes, and wrote down the names as instructed. When the tape finished, the experimenter collected the response sheets, and engaged participants in conversation for 1 minute including an apology for misleading them about the memory test.

4.2 Tasks and Measures（任务与测量）

During this task, participants were expected to either doodle (the experimental group) or not doodle (the control group). This was the independent variable (IV) （自变量）.

They were told beforehand they would be tested on the names of people who were attending the party (and not the ones who were not going to be there). This was the monitoring task （监控任务）.

They also had an unexpected test on the names of places mentioned. This was the recall task （回忆任务）.

The order of these tests was counterbalanced （顺序平衡） — half the participants in each condition were asked to recall the names of party-goers first, then the places; the other half recalled the places first, then the names. These two tasks were the measures of the dependent variable (DV) （因变量） of recall.

Monitoring task 是被试知道会被测试的任务（监控已知信息）；recall task 是突如其来的测试（回忆未被告知要记住的信息）。两者都测记忆，但 monitoring 测的是有意注意，recall 测的是附带学习（incidental learning）。如果涂鸦只在 monitoring 上有优势，说明它帮助了注意；如果在两个任务上都有优势，则可能也帮助了记忆。

4.3 Operationalisation of the DV（因变量的操作化定义）

To operationalise （操作化） the DVs, plausible mishearings, such as "Greg" for "Craig", were counted as correct. Other names that were on the tape but were not party-goers (e.g. John) were scored as false alarms. Other words relating to people, such as "sister", were ignored.

The final memory score for monitored information (names) and incidental information (places) was the number of correct names/places minus false alarms. For the recall test, plausible mishearings had to be the same in both the monitoring and recall phases to be scored as correct.

Operationalisation 就是把一个抽象概念变成可以具体测量的指标。这里的记忆分数 = 正确回忆的数量 − 错误报警的数量，而不是简单数对了几个。这样可以更准确地衡量记忆质量，避免被试猜对了就得高分。"合理的误听"（如 Greg 听成 Craig）也算对，因为这说明被试确实听到了信息，只是拼写有误。

4.4 Research Methods Reminder（方法提示）

When you think about parts of the procedure of a study, it is helpful to consider reliability and validity:

  • Reliability （信度）: Is the procedure likely to produce similar results each time with similar participants?
  • Validity （效度）: Is the procedure really manipulating the independent variable and is the dependent variable really measuring that variable?

Counterbalancing is used as a control procedure against order effects. It is mainly used in repeated measures designs, but in Andrade's study it was used to control for potential order effects caused by the two different measures of recall.

5. Results（研究结果）

5.1 Doodling Behaviour（涂鸦行为）

In the doodling condition, the mean number of shaded shapes on the printed sheet was 36.3, with a range of 3–110. One participant did not doodle and was replaced. No participants in the control condition doodled spontaneously.

涂鸦数量差异很大（3到110个），这说明不同被试对涂鸦任务的投入程度不同。这也是 independent measures 设计的一个潜在问题——被试变量可能影响结果。

5.2 Monitoring Performance（监控任务表现）

Participants in the control group correctly wrote down a mean of 7.1 (SD = 1.1) of the eight party-goers' names during the tape; five people made a false alarm. Participants in the doodling group correctly wrote a mean of 7.8 (SD = 0.4) names of party-goers; one person made one false alarm.

Monitoring performance (correct minus false alarms) was significantly higher in the doodling condition (mean = 7.7, SD = 0.6) than in the control condition (mean = 6.9, SD = 1.3), Mann–Whitney U = 124, p = 0.01 (one-tailed).

研究者使用了 非参数检验（Mann-Whitney U test）而非 t 检验，因为数据不是正态分布——很多被试得了满分（8分），导致分布偏斜（天花板效应）。15个涂鸦者和9个控制组被试得了满分8分。one-tailed（单尾检验）是因为 Andrade 有明确的方向性预测：涂鸦会改善（而非恶化）表现。

Overall, participants in the doodling condition recalled a mean of 7.5 pieces of information (names and places), 29% more than the mean of 5.8 recalled by the control group.

A 2 (doodling, control) × 2 (names, places) mixed measures ANOVA （方差分析） confirmed that:

  • monitored names were recalled better than incidental places: F(1, 38) = 54.9, p < 0.001;
  • recall was better for doodlers than controls: F(1, 38) = 6.0, p = 0.02;
  • there was no significant interaction (F < 1), meaning doodling helped both monitored and incidental information equally.

Removing data from participants who had suspected a test did not alter the pattern of results (main effect of group: F(1, 31) = 6.9, p = 0.01). Entering monitoring performance as a covariate （协变量） made the group effect marginally significant, F(1, 37) = 3.8, p = 0.058.

从表中可以看出：无论是"被要求记住的名字"还是"没要求记住的地方"，涂鸦组的记忆分数都高于控制组。这说明涂鸦不仅没让人分心，反而帮助了记忆。当把监控表现作为协变量时，组间效应变得仅边缘显著（p = 0.058），这意味着涂鸦对回忆的优势可能部分来自于它在监控阶段帮助了注意——但无法完全排除涂鸦也直接帮助了记忆的可能性。

5.4 Demand Characteristics（需求特征）

Three doodlers and four controls suspected a memory test. This suggests that there were demand characteristics （需求特征） that made the aim apparent to the participants. However, none said they actively tried to remember information. The roughly equal numbers in each condition means demand characteristics are unlikely to have biased the results.

ER Insight: 考官奖励的关键结果模式：(1) 监控任务：涂鸦 7.7 vs 控制 6.9（Mann-Whitney U = 124, p = 0.01）；(2) 总回忆量：7.5 vs 5.8，多 29%；(3) 人名（监控信息）显著好于地名（附带信息）F = 54.9；(4) 无显著交互作用（F < 1）→ 涂鸦对两类信息帮助相同；(5) 监控成绩作协变量后组效应仅边缘显著 p = 0.058——这是结论部分"注意 vs 记忆解释"的证据来源。

6. Conclusion（结论）

Andrade concluded that doodling helps concentration on a primary task. Participants who performed a shape-shading task, intended as an analogue of naturalistic doodling, concentrated better on a mock telephone message than participants who listened to the message with no concurrent task. This benefit was seen for both monitoring performance and scores on a surprise memory test.

However, because the doodling group were better on both the monitored and incidental information, there are two possible explanations:

  1. Attention explanation （注意解释）: doodlers noticed more of the target words — an effect on attention. This would be explained by doodling increasing arousal and therefore reducing daydreaming, so maintaining attention on the primary task.
  2. Memory explanation （记忆解释）: doodling improved memory directly, for example by encouraging deeper information processing of the material on the tape.

The fact that entering monitoring performance as a covariate made the group effect only marginally significant (p = 0.058) suggests that part of the doodling advantage may have been due to better attention during monitoring. However, it does not rule out a direct memory effect.

Without any measure of daydreaming, it is difficult to distinguish between these two explanations. Andrade suggested this could be addressed by:

  • asking participants about daydreaming retrospectively by self-report;
  • using thought probes （思维探针） during the telephone message;
  • simultaneous brain scanning （脑扫描） to test whether doodling reduces activation of cortical "default" networks associated with daydreaming.
Result vs Conclusion — 检验你是否分得清：
Result = "涂鸦组总回忆量 7.5，控制组 5.8，多 29%；监控成绩 7.7 vs 6.9（p = 0.01）"（测到的数字）
Conclusion = "涂鸦有助于保持注意/提高回忆"（对机制的推断）
注意：研究能证明的是"涂鸦组表现更好"，不能直接证明"涂鸦减少白日梦"——因为从未测量白日梦。永远先报数字，再做推断。
核心结论： Doodling 有助于注意力和记忆，但原因可能是"减少白日梦、保持注意"，也可能是"加深信息加工"，本研究无法区分这两种机制。未来研究需要直接测量白日梦来解开这个谜题。

7. Evaluation（评价）

Strengths（优点）

✅ 1. CONTROL OF VARIABLES Laboratory Experiment with Good Control
It was possible to limit uncontrolled variables, for example by ensuring participants listened at a comfortable volume and by using a recorded telephone message so that the important words were said in the same way, at the same pace and in the same order in each condition. Random assignment to the two conditions also helped to spread participant variables evenly between the groups.
✅ 2. RELIABILITY Standardised, Boring Procedure
The experiment was standardised so that all participants were equally likely to be bored and therefore to daydream: the monotone recording (227 words per minute), a dull, quiet room, and recruitment just after participants had finished another experiment when they were expecting to go home. All participants received the same standardised instructions, so the procedure could be replicated by another researcher.
✅ 3. VALIDITY Good Operationalisation of Doodling
Rather than asking participants to doodle freely (which might make them self-conscious and make them suspect the aim), the shading task was standardised with printed shapes (alternating squares and circles, ~1 cm, 10 per row). This reduced individual differences in how participants doodled and encouraged a naturalistic, automatic quality of doodling — strengthening the claim that the task really measured the effect of doodling-like behaviour.
✅ 4. TYPES OF DATA Objective Quantitative Data and Appropriate Statistics
The study collected objective, quantitative data — the number of names and places recalled (memory score = correct minus false alarms). Non-parametric tests (Mann–Whitney U) were used when the data was not normally distributed (many participants scored the maximum of 8, creating a skewed distribution), and a mixed-measures ANOVA was used to analyse recall with appropriate controls (including the covariate analysis).

Weaknesses（缺点）

❌ 1. SAMPLING Small, Mainly Female Volunteer Panel
All 40 participants were members of a single recruitment panel at the MRC Applied Psychology Unit — the kind of people who volunteer for such panels may be very similar (e.g. having time to spare or an interest in psychology), the sample was mainly female and they were paid a small honorarium. This could bias the sample and limit how far the findings generalise to the wider population.
❌ 2. VALIDITY No Measure of Daydreaming — Two Explanations Untested
Because there was no measure of daydreaming (e.g. self-report or thought probes), Andrade could not distinguish between the attention explanation (doodling reduces daydreaming) and the memory explanation (doodling deepens processing). The covariate analysis only made the group effect marginally significant (p = 0.058), so we cannot be sure whether doodling improved attention, memory, or both. Demand characteristics were also a consideration: some participants (three doodlers and four controls) suspected a memory test, although they were roughly equal across conditions and none actively tried to remember.
❌ 3. VALIDITY Participant Variables and Low Ecological Validity
Because the design was independent measures, individual differences in attention or memory between the two groups could partly explain the difference in recall — random assignment reduces but does not eliminate this risk. The number of shapes shaded differed hugely between doodlers (range 3–110), so some doodlers barely doodled at all (participant variables). In addition, a mock telephone message in a dull laboratory room is not the same as a real conversation or lecture, and shading printed shapes is not exactly the same as naturalistic doodling, limiting ecological validity.
❌ 4. ETHICS Deception and Informed Consent
Participants could not give fully informed consent because they were given an unexpected test on place names and were actively misled ("I don't want you to remember any of it") when their memory was in fact being tested. This deception was relatively mild and was dealt with by a 1-minute debrief in which the experimenter apologised for the misleading instruction; the study also had the potential to cause distress if participants felt they had performed badly on the memory test (protection from harm).
❌ 5. DATA & INTERPRETATION The File Drawer Problem
Andrade herself notes that the extent to which secondary tasks have beneficial effects may be a "file drawer problem" — studies in which secondary tasks did not help (or harmed) performance may never have been published, so the published literature could give a skewed, overly positive picture of the benefits of doodling.

📋 Exam Key Points — Evaluation (AO3)

  • Must Know [Control of Variables] Strength: laboratory experiment — same recorded message at a comfortable volume in every condition; random assignment of participants
  • Common [Reliability] Strength: standardised, boring procedure (monotone tape at 227 wpm, dull room, post-experiment recruitment, standardised instructions) — replicable
  • Must Know [Validity] Strength: shading printed shapes operationalises doodling naturalistically without making participants self-conscious
  • Common [Types of Data] Strength: objective quantitative data; Mann–Whitney U used because scores were skewed (ceiling at 8); ANOVA + covariate analysis
  • Must Know [Sampling] Weakness: small, mainly female, paid volunteer panel (MRC APU) aged 18–55 — biased, limited generalisability
  • Must Know [Validity] Weakness: no measure of daydreaming → cannot distinguish attention vs memory explanation (p = 0.058 when monitoring entered as covariate)
  • Common [Validity] Weakness: independent measures → participant variables (shading range 3–110); artificial lab task lowers ecological validity
  • Common [Ethics] Weakness: deception about the memory test limits informed consent; dealt with by debriefing

8. Ethical Issues（伦理问题）

  • Informed consent （知情同意）: The participants were unable to give fully informed consent because they were given an unexpected test on place names. They did not know the full purpose of the study before taking part.
  • Deception （欺骗）: They were misled about the memory test — told "I don't want you to remember any of it" when in fact their memory was tested. However, this was relatively mild deception.
  • Protection from harm （免受伤害）: The study had the potential to make participants distressed if they were unable to remember the names, so it could breach this ethical guideline.
  • Debriefing （事后解释）: The experimenter engaged participants in conversation for 1 minute after the tape, including an apology for misleading them about the memory test. During debriefing, participants were also asked if they had suspected a memory test.
ER Insight: 伦理题的得分点是 "问题 + 如何处理" 成对出现。例如：欺骗（说"不需要记忆"实则测验）→ 听后 1 分钟解释并道歉；知情同意受限 → 事后询问是否猜到测验（3/4 涂鸦者与 4 名控制组被试猜到，但无人主动记忆）。不要只写"存在欺骗"，必须写出应对措施。

🀄 本节中文总结

  • 四个伦理点：知情同意不完整、欺骗（轻度的）、潜在心理伤害（记不住名字的挫败感）、事后解释（1分钟道歉 + 询问是否起疑）
  • 欺骗是研究设计所必需的（否则被试会主动记忆），Andrade 用事后解释来补救——这符合"必要时欺骗 + 事后完全解释"的伦理框架
  • 3 名涂鸦者 + 4 名控制组被试猜到了记忆测试，但人数两组大致均衡且无人主动记忆 → 需求特征不太可能影响结果（这也是效度论据）

9. Issues, Debates and Approaches（议题、争论与取向）

9.1 The Application of Psychology to Everyday Life（日常应用）

Doodling could be a useful strategy when we have to concentrate and don't want to, for example in an important but boring lecture or when waiting to hear the faint sound of a friend's car arriving. By stopping our minds from wandering, we should be better able to focus on the primary task. This would be an application to everyday life — for instance, teachers and employers could stop telling people off for doodling, and learners could deliberately doodle (rather than daydream) to stay attentive in lessons.

9.2 The Cognitive Approach（认知取向）

From a cognitive psychology approach （认知心理学取向）, the study uses the working memory model to explain why doodling may help: a visual-spatial concurrent task (doodling) and an auditory primary task (listening) use different working-memory subsystems, so they do not compete for the same resources — whereas daydreaming competes for central-executive resources. The study also raises questions about whether internal processes like daydreaming can be measured reliably, and shows how cognitive theories can generate testable predictions about everyday behaviour.

9.3 Implications for Research Methods（对研究方法的启示）

Andrade also noted implications for research methods: dual task designs are commonly used to pinpoint specific cognitive resources, but they fail to do this accurately if the effects of boredom are overlooked. Performance decrements through competition for task-specific resources may be moderated if the secondary task also reduces mind-wandering. This means researchers need to consider boredom and daydreaming as hidden variables in single-task control conditions.

🀄 本节中文总结

  • 应用：无聊讲座/会议中，涂鸦可作为防止走神的实用策略——改变"涂鸦=不专心"的刻板印象
  • 认知取向特征：用工作记忆模型解释行为（听觉任务 vs 视觉空间任务使用不同子系统）；强调内部心理过程（注意、白日梦）
  • 方法启示：双任务研究必须把"无聊/白日梦"当作隐藏变量——次要任务可能不仅争夺资源，还减少了走神，两者方向相反

10. Summary（总结）

Andrade's study tested whether doodling could improve concentration on and memory of a conversation. Participants in the doodling condition remembered more of the people's names they had been asked to recall and attended to the message better, as they recalled more in a surprise test of the place names mentioned. Overall, doodlers recalled 29% more information than controls.

This laboratory experiment was well controlled with a recorded stimulus message and specific shapes to colour in for the doodlers. The data from the words recalled was quantitative and objective. However, it would also have been useful to have had qualitative data about whether the participants daydreamed, as this would have helped to distinguish between two possible reasons for the improved memory: deeper processing or better attention.

11. Key Terms（关键词表）

TermDefinition
primary task （主任务）The activity we are supposed to be concentrating on, even though we may be doing something else as well, such as doodling.
attention （注意）The concentration of mental effort on a particular stimulus. It may be focused or divided.
divided attention （分配性注意）The ability to split mental effort between two or more simultaneous tasks (called "dual tasks"), for example driving a car and talking to a passenger.
concurrent task （并发任务）An additional activity with a cognitive demand that we can perform at the same time as a main (primary) task.
focused attention （集中注意）The picking out of a particular input from a mass of information, such as many items presented together, or a rapid succession of individual items.
daydreaming （白日梦）A mildly altered state of consciousness in which we experience a sense of being "lost in our thoughts", typically positive ones, and a detachment from our environment.
arousal （唤醒）The extent to which we are alert, for example responsive to external sensory stimuli. It has physiological and psychological components and is mediated by the nervous system and hormones.
working memory model （工作记忆模型）A model of short-term memory that includes separate stores for visual and auditory information, controlled by a central executive.
central executive （中央执行系统）The component of working memory that controls attention and coordinates the subsystems.
control group （控制组）A group of participants often used in an experiment, who do not receive the manipulation of the independent variable and can be used for comparison with the experimental group.
experimental group （实验组）The group that receives the manipulation of the independent variable.
independent measures design （独立测量设计）An experimental design where different participants are used in each condition.
counterbalanced （顺序平衡）A control procedure used to reduce order effects by varying the order in which tasks are presented.
independent variable (IV) （自变量）The variable that is manipulated by the experimenter.
dependent variable (DV) （因变量）The variable that is measured by the experimenter.
operationalisation （操作化）Defining a variable in terms of how it is measured in a study.
monitoring task （监控任务）A task in which participants are asked to pay attention to specific target information.

demand characteristics （需求特征）Features of a study that give participants clues about what the researcher is expecting, which may influence their behaviour.
participant variables （被试变量）Differences between participants that could affect the results of a study, such as age, gender, or ability.
standardised （标准化）Using the same procedure for all participants so that comparisons are fair.
informed consent （知情同意）Participants agreeing to take part in a study after being given information about it.
deception （欺骗）When participants are not told the true aim of the study.
debriefing （事后解释）Explaining the true purpose of the study after it has finished.
quantitative data （定量数据）Numerical data that can be analysed statistically.
ecological validity （生态效度）The extent to which findings can be generalised to real-life settings.
covariate （协变量）A variable that the researcher controls for in order to clarify the relationship between the IV and DV.
ANOVA （方差分析）Analysis of Variance; a statistical test used to compare the means of two or more groups.

12. Exam-Style Questions（考试题型）

  1. Mei is replicating Andrade's study with her psychology class.
    1. a. State the independent variable and the dependent variable in Andrade's study. [2]
    2. b. Explain why Andrade used an independent measures design rather than a repeated measures design. [3]
    Show Model Answer

    a. IV: whether participants doodled (shaded printed shapes) or did not doodle while listening (control condition). DV: recall — the number of party-goers' names (monitored) and place names (incidental) correctly remembered, scored as correct minus false alarms. [2]

    b. In a repeated measures design the same participants would do both conditions, so they would hear the telephone message twice — the second exposure would be easier (practice effect) and they would know the place names were going to be tested (demand characteristics), which would confound the results. Using different participants in each condition (independent measures) means each person hears the message only once and cannot guess the surprise recall test from having done the monitoring task before, so the measures of monitoring and incidental recall remain valid. [3]

  2. From the study by Andrade (2010), describe the sample of participants used. [3]
    Show Model Answer

    Forty members of a participant panel at the UK's Medical Research Council (MRC) Applied Psychology Unit, aged 18–55, who were paid a small honorarium. They were randomly assigned to the control group (N = 20; 2 male) or the doodling group (N = 20; 3 male), so the sample was mainly female. One participant in the doodling condition did not doodle and was replaced. [3]

  3. Explain how Andrade operationalised the dependent variables in her study. [3]
    Show Model Answer

    Recall was operationalised as the number of correct party-goers' names (monitored information) and place names (incidental information) minus false alarms (e.g. writing a name that was on the tape but was not a party-goer, such as John). Plausible mishearings, such as writing "Greg" for "Craig", were counted as correct because they showed the participant had heard the word. This produced a memory score for each participant on each of the two recall tasks. [3]

  4. Explain one strength and one weakness of using a laboratory experiment in Andrade's study. [4]
    Show Model Answer

    Strength [Control of Variables]: In the laboratory the researchers could control extraneous variables — the same recorded message was played at a comfortable volume, in a quiet, visually dull room, to every participant. This means differences in recall between the groups were more likely to be caused by the IV (doodling) rather than by differences in how the message was presented, increasing internal validity. [2]

    Weakness [Ecological validity]: A mock telephone message listened to in a dull laboratory room is artificial compared with a real conversation or a real lecture, and shading printed shapes is not exactly the same as naturalistic doodling. Participants may behave differently when they know they are in a study, so the findings may not generalise fully to doodling in everyday life. [2]

  5. There are ethical issues in Andrade's study. Outline one ethical issue raised by the study and explain how it was dealt with. [3]
    Show Model Answer

    Deception was used: participants were told "I don't want you to remember any of it", when in fact their memory of the message was later tested in a surprise recall task, so they could not give fully informed consent. This was dealt with by debriefing: after the tape the experimenter spent a minute talking to each participant and apologised for misleading them about the memory test, and asked whether they had suspected a test (three doodlers and four controls had). The deception was relatively mild and was necessary for the study to work, since telling participants about the memory test would have made them deliberately memorise the message. [3]

  6. Compare the cognitive approach and the learning approach to show two ways in which they are different. Use the study by Andrade as an example of the cognitive approach. [4]
    Show Model Answer

    Difference 1 — focus of explanation: The cognitive approach explains behaviour through internal mental processes such as attention, memory and information processing. Andrade used the working memory model to argue that doodling (visuo-spatial) and listening (auditory) use different subsystems, so doodling reduces daydreaming and helps attention. The learning approach instead explains behaviour as acquired through experience — reinforcement, punishment and observational learning — e.g. Bandura et al. explaining aggression as learned by watching a model.

    Difference 2 — methods of investigation: The cognitive approach often uses controlled laboratory experiments measuring performance as evidence about mental processes — Andrade measured recall of names and places as an indicator of attention and memory. The learning approach also uses experiments but focuses on observable behaviour change — Bandura et al. observed and counted children's imitation of an adult model's aggressive acts rather than inferring internal processes. [4]

🎯 Practice — Andrade (2010)

Evaluate the study by Andrade (2010) on doodling, including two strengths and two weaknesses. [10]

Show Model Answer

Andrade conducted a laboratory experiment using an independent measures design, randomly assigning 40 participants to a doodling or a control condition while they listened to a mock telephone message. The study can be evaluated against CIE research-methods criteria.

Strength 1 [Control of Variables]: As a laboratory experiment the procedure was highly controlled and standardised: the same monotone recording (227 words per minute, 2.5 minutes) was played at a comfortable volume in a quiet, visually dull room, with the same standardised instructions. Recruiting participants just after another experiment and telling them the recording "is rather dull" increased boredom equally for everyone. This means differences in recall are unlikely to be explained by uncontrolled variables, increasing internal validity.

Strength 2 [Types of Data]: The DV was operationalised objectively as a memory score (correct names/places minus false alarms, with plausible mishearings such as "Greg" for "Craig" counted as correct). This produced quantitative data that could be analysed statistically — a Mann–Whitney U test was appropriately used for the skewed monitoring data (many participants reached the ceiling of 8) and a mixed ANOVA for the recall data — making the conclusions reliable and replicable.

Weakness 1 [Validity]: Because there was no measure of daydreaming, Andrade could not distinguish between the two possible explanations of the results: doodling improving attention (by reducing daydreaming) or improving memory directly (deeper processing). Entering monitoring performance as a covariate made the group effect only marginally significant (p = 0.058), so we cannot be sure of the mechanism — the conclusion that doodling "aids concentration" is only partially supported.

Weakness 2 [Sampling]: The sample was small and unrepresentative: 40 paid volunteers from the MRC Applied Psychology Unit participant panel (aged 18–55), mostly female. People who join such panels may be untypical (having spare time or an interest in psychology), and the mainly female, paid, volunteer sample limits how far the findings can be generalised to the wider population — although random assignment does make the two groups comparable to each other.

Conclusion: Overall, the study has strong internal validity because of its controlled, standardised procedure and objective quantitative measures, and its finding that doodlers recalled 29% more information is a genuine contribution to everyday applications. However, the lack of a daydreaming measure and the unrepresentative sample mean the mechanism and the generalisability of the doodling benefit remain uncertain. [10]

13. Appendix: Telephone Message Script（附录：电话脚本）

The boring telephone message used in the study. Monitored names (party-goers) are shown in bold; incidental places are shown in italics. Names that are mentioned but cannot come (Nigel, John, the cat Ben) are not bolded — participants had to ignore them.

"Hi! Are you doing anything on Saturday? I'm having a birthday party and was hoping you could come. It's not actually my birthday, it's my sister Jane's. She'll be 21. She's coming up from London for the weekend and I thought it would be a nice surprise for her. I've also invited her boyfriend William and one of her old schoolfriends, Claire, but she doesn't know that yet. Claire's husband Nigel was going to join us but he has just found out that he has to go to a meeting in Penzance that day and won't be back in time.

I thought we could have a barbecue if the weather is nice, although the way it has been so far this week, that doesn't look likely. I can't believe it has got so cold already. And the evenings are really drawing in aren't they? Anyway, there is plenty of space indoors if it rains. Did I tell you that I have redecorated the kitchen? It is mainly yellow — the wallpaper is yellow and so is the woodwork, although I thought it would be better to leave the ceiling white to make it look lighter. I've still got the old blue fittings — they are pretty battered now but I can't afford to replace them at the moment.

Do you remember Craig? I used to share a flat with him when we were both working for that bank in Gloucester. He has bought a house in Colchester now but he promises to take time off from gardening to come to Jane's party. Suzie is going to be there too. She's the person I met at the pottery class in Harlow last year. Apparently she has got really good at it and may even be having an exhibition of her work soon.

Will you be able to bring some food? Maybe crisps or peanuts, something along those lines. Jenny from next door is going to bring a quiche and I'll do some garlic bread. I found a good recipe for punch — you warm up some red wine with gin and orange juice plus cloves and cardamom and cinnamon. Add some brown sugar if it's not sweet enough.

The boys from the house down the road have promised to bring some of their homebrew. There are three of them sharing that house now — John, Tony and Phil. I think they were all at college together. Phil teaches at a primary school in Ely now and the other two commute to Peterborough each day. I think they both work in the hospital there — I know Tony was training to be a nurse at one point so maybe he is qualified now. John can't come on Saturday because his parents are coming to stay for the weekend but Phil and Tony should be there. Tony has to pick their cat Ben up from the vet so he may be a bit late.

By the way, did I tell you about our holiday in Edinburgh? It was a complete disaster. We were camping and it rained constantly. We spent most of the time in museums, trying to keep dry and then, to make matters worse, Nicky got her handbag stolen. I was quite glad to get back to work after that. Anyway, hope you can make it on Saturday — let me know if you want to stay over. Bye!"

从这个脚本可以看出 Andrade 的精心设计：

  • 8 位确定/可能参加者（monitored names，讲话者自己除外）：Jane, William, Claire, Craig, Suzie, Jenny, Tony, Phil
  • 提及但不能来的人 + 1 只猫（干扰项，须忽略）：Nigel（要去 Penzance 开会）、John（父母来访）、猫 Ben（在兽医处，所以 Tony 会晚到）
  • 8 个地名（incidental places）：London, Penzance, Gloucester, Colchester, Harlow, Ely, Peterborough, Edinburgh
  • 大量无关细节（厨房装修、食谱、天气、假期）用来增加信息量和无聊感

这个脚本的设计让学生可以直观理解 monitored information 和 incidental information 的区别——"写下来的人名"测的是监控（有意注意），"没让你记的地名"测的是附带回忆。也解释了为什么把监控成绩作为协变量后组效应只边缘显著：涂鸦帮助的也许只是"听到目标词"，而不是"记住一切"。
