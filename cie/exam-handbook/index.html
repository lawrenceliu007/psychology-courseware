0. CIE Scoring System Core

📐 Why This Section Exists

Before you learn what to write, you must understand how you are scored. This section contains the official rules that examiners follow when marking your paper. These rules are extracted directly from the Generic Marking Principles (GMP) at the start of every official Mark Scheme. Most students never read them — which means they make avoidable mistakes on every exam.

Read this section once. It will change how you approach every question.

0.1 Generic Marking Principles (GMP) — The 6 Iron Rules

These 6 principles appear at the beginning of every single CIE Mark Scheme for Psychology 9990. They are non-negotiable.

RuleWhat It MeansImportanceStudent Impact
GMP1 Marks are awarded based on the specific content of the Mark Scheme. Examiners cannot award marks for correct-sounding answers that don't match what's written in the MS. 🔴 Critical You must use the exact terminology and concepts the MS expects. "The sample was small" might not match if the MS wants "opportunity sampling was used".
GMP2 Only whole marks are awarded. No half marks, no "almost there" marks. 🟡 Know it If your answer is between two mark levels, you get the lower one. There's no "2.5 marks".
GMP3 ⭐ Marks are awarded for what is CORRECT. Errors do NOT lose marks. Omissions do NOT lose marks. If you write something wrong, the examiner simply ignores it. You are never penalised for incorrect information. 🔴🔴🔴 MOST IMPORTANT This is the #1 misconception among students. Many students are afraid to write because they think wrong answers deduct marks. They don't! Write everything you think might be relevant — if it's wrong, it's ignored. If it's right, you get the mark.
GMP4 Rules must be applied consistently. All candidates are marked using the same standard. 🟡 Know it No special treatment. Don't waste time trying to be clever or original — stick to what the MS rewards.
GMP5 The full range of marks should be used. Examiners are trained to use all available mark levels. 🟡 Know it A well-written answer can get full marks. A poor one gets zero. The scale is fully utilised.
GMP6 Marks are awarded based only on the Mark Scheme requirements — not on grade boundaries. Your raw score is converted to a grade later. 🟡 Know it Don't worry about "how many marks do I need for an A". Focus on maximising each question's score.
⭐ GMP3 Deep Dive: "Positive Marking"
  • ✅ Write a correct point → +1 mark
  • ❌ Write an incorrect point → 0 marks (NOT -1)
  • ⚠️ Write nothing → 0 marks
  • 💡 Strategy: If you're unsure about a point, WRITE IT ANYWAY. The worst case is 0 (same as leaving it blank). The best case is +1.
  • ⚠️ Exception: Contradictory statements within the same sentence may result in 0 for that point (see Social Science Rule #5 below).

0.2 Social Science-Specific Principles — 7 Additional Rules

Beyond the GMPs, CIE applies these 7 rules specifically to social science subjects (including Psychology):

#RuleExample
SS1 Alternative wording that conveys the same meaning as the MS → Award the mark. MS says "participants were deceived" → Candidate writes "the researcher lied to participants about the true aim" → ✅ Awarded
SS2 Correct answers NOT in the MS → Still award the mark. Candidate correctly identifies "demand characteristics" as a weakness even though the MS only lists "ecological validity" → ✅ Awarded
SS3 Multiple correct answers in one space → Award ALL of them (for non-list questions). Question asks for "one ethical issue". Candidate writes "deception AND lack of informed consent" → Both credited
SS4 🔴 Keywords without demonstrating understanding → Do NOT award. Writes "reliability" but doesn't explain what IS reliable or WHY it matters → ❌ Not awarded
SS5 🔴 Contradictory answers → Do NOT award. "The sample was large enough" followed by "but the small sample limits generalisability" → ❌ Contradiction = 0
SS6 Repeated points → Do NOT award again. "Small sample" in point 1, then "limited number of participants" in point 2 → Second instance ❌ [REP]
SS7 Spelling errors → Tolerate UNLESS it's a syllabus term that causes confusion. "ecolgical validity" → ✅ Tolerated. "efficacy" instead of "validity" where context makes it unclear → ❌ Not tolerated

0.3 Annotations Dictionary — What Those Symbols Mean

When you get your exam back (or imagine looking at a marked script), these are the symbols examiners use:

√ Creditworthy Point (√)

A correct, mark-earning point. Each √ = typically 1 mark.

+ Creditworthy 'What' (+)

Used specifically in Q8 Application questions. Marks identification of concept/issue.

N Named Issue (N) FORCED

CRITICAL for Q10 Essay. Missing it caps your mark at L3 (6/10 max).

S Strength (S)

Used in Q10 Essay. You need at least 2 strengths.

W Weakness (W)

Used in Q10 Essay. You need at least 2 weaknesses.

ID Issue Discussed (ID)

Any evaluation issue discussed (ethics, reliability, etc.). Must include Named Issue for L4+.

L1-L5 Level Awarded

The overall level your response achieved. L5 = 9-10/10, L1 = 1-2/10.

0.4 Marking Workflow — How Your Paper Is Scored

1
Read response holistically. Examiner gets a sense of overall quality before assigning levels.
2
Determine Level (L1-L5). Based on quality, depth, and coverage. Check Level Descriptors (§3.6).
3
Annotate key points. √ for creditworthy points, N for named issue, S/W for strengths/weaknesses.
4
Assign final mark. Within the level band, count creditworthy points. Round to nearest available mark.
5
Quality assurance. Sample re-marking ensures consistency across all candidates.

3. Answer Frameworks (When to Use Them)

3.1 Q10 Essay Framework — The Only Mandatory Framework

Structure (must follow for Level 4-5):

  1. Introduction — Name the study + state your evaluation stance (1 sentence)
  2. Named Issue Point (Strength OR Weakness) — Start with this to ensure coverage! REQUIRED
  3. Second Evaluation Point (opposite type) — With 2+ examples from study
  4. Third Evaluation Point — With 2+ examples from study
  5. Fourth Evaluation Point — With 2+ examples from study
  6. Conclusion — Brief summary judgement (1 sentence)
❌ FORBIDDEN in Q10 (from ER):
  • ❌ Description of the study → 0 marks for description
  • ❌ Real-world application → Not creditworthy
  • ❌ GRAVE template approach → "Some responses are still following GRAVE...can only score Level 3 maximum"
  • ❌ Structuring by study (instead of by issue) → "often led to superficial and repetitive" responses
  • ❌ 3 strong points + 1 weak/off-topic point → "could not be given Level 5"
  • ❌ All 4 points brief/no context → Level 2 maximum
  • ❌ No named issue → Level 3 (6 marks) MAXIMUM

3.2 Q9 Compare Studies Framework

Rules (from ER):

  • Choose comparison points that can be developed and explained
  • Use examples from BOTH studies for each similarity/difference
  • Avoid: "Different aim" → always Level 1; "Different ethical guidelines" → always Level 1
  • Check the question for restrictions (e.g., "do NOT refer to sample")
  • Compare studies within the same approach (Bio→Bio, Cog→Cog, etc.)

4. When NOT to Use a Framework

  • Q1-Q7 (short answer): Just answer the question directly. No framework needed.
  • Q8 (application): Argue for ONE side. Pick Jack OR Daphne, not both.
  • Definition questions: Give the exact syllabus definition. Don't elaborate unnecessarily.
  • 'Outline ONE feature': Focus on ONE thing in depth. Don't list multiple features.

5. Evaluation Toolkit — Syllabus Dimensions

🔬 Reliability

Can the study be replicated with similar results?

Sub-types: Internal (consistency), External (replicability), Inter-rater

✅ Validity

Does it measure what it claims to measure?

Sub-types: Internal, External (ecological + population), Face, Concurrent

🌍 Generalisability

Can findings apply to other populations/settings?

Key factors: Sample size, demographics, culture, era

⚖️ Ethics

Were participants treated according to guidelines?

Key issues: Consent, deception, harm, withdrawal, debrief, privacy

📊 Data Type

Quantitative (numerical) vs Qualitative (descriptive)

ER note: Must explain WHY data is strength/weakness, not just describe it

🔍 Usefulness

Does it contribute to real-world understanding?

Application: Theory building, practical implications, treatment development

📏 Reductionism vs Holism

Over-simplification vs complete picture

ER tip: Explain WHY reductionist, not just label it

🎲 Determinism vs Free Will

Behaviour caused by forces beyond/in individual control

ER tip: Can discuss BOTH sides for stronger analysis

8. Common Pitfalls v3.0 ER Edition

🔴 This section is now powered by the 2024 June Examiner Report (60 pages).
Every pitfall below includes real examiner quotes, actual student performance data, and anonymised examples of what cost students marks.

8.1 🚨 Fatal Error #1: Result vs Conclusion Confusion #1 Highest Frequency

ER Quote (appears in EVERY paper): "Many candidates could not differentiate between a result and a conclusion. A result is the collected data from a study that has been analysed via descriptive and inferential statistics. A conclusion is a generic commentary about what the results actually tell us, linked to the aim/purpose of the study."
❌ RESULT (no credit for conclusion Q)✅ CONCLUSION (correct)
"78% of participants could estimate correctly"
"Children identified cartoon faces better than human faces"
"Doodling group recalled 29% more names"
"More males in critical area = faster help" 		"Doodling affects concentration and memory recall"
"Lineup presentation format influences eyewitness identification accuracy"
"Bystander demographics (gender/ratio) affect helping behavior"
"REM sleep is associated with dream recall"
💡 Memory Trick: Result = Numbers/Data (what happened). Conclusion = So what? (what it means for psychology). If you can put "The results suggest that..." in front of it, it's probably a conclusion.

8.2 🚨 Fatal Error #2: Procedure Written as "Generic Story"

ER Quote: "Generic 'stories' about a procedure will not gain credit. Candidates need to know precise details about a procedure. This means presenting a level of detail that would mean the study could be replicated."
ER on Q6 (average mark < 2): "Weaker responses tended to focus on all of the procedure rather than within the parameters of the question set. Many responses provided a generic 'story' of what happened with no specific details."
❌ Generic Story (0-1 marks)✅ Replicable Detail (full marks)
"They showed children a video of a man hitting a doll then observed them playing" "Children were taken individually to Room 1 containing a Bobo doll, mallet, and other toys. They watched a 10-min film of an adult model attacking the Bobo doll using distinctive mallet aggression (striking with mallet), sitting on it (non-aggressive gun play), and verbal aggression ('sock him in the nose'). After exposure, child was taken to Room 2 with frustration-inducing tasks, then to Room 3 with identical toys for 20-min free play."

8.3 🚨 Fatal Error #3: Named Issue Missing or Only Described

ER Quote: "Credit is limited if the named issue is omitted or just described. A response that fails to have one evaluation point about the named issue can only score Level 3 (6 marks) maximum."
The "Describe" Trap:
  • ❌ "Quantitative data was collected" → This is DESCRIPTION, not evaluation → No credit toward named issue
  • ✅ "Quantitative data is a strength because it allows direct statistical comparison between conditions (e.g., oxytocin vs placebo personal space measurements), enabling objective conclusions about the effect of oxytocin on social distance" → This is EVALUATION

8.4 🚨 Fatal Error #4: GRAVE Template Approach

ER Quote (direct criticism): "In addition, some responses are still following a GRAVE approach to this question (Generalisability, Reliability, Application, Validity, Ethics)."
Why GRAVE fails (from ER analysis):
  • Forces you to cover 5 issues equally → runs out of time for depth
  • Encourages description rather than evaluation
  • Doesn't prioritise the Named Issue → caps at L3
  • Produces repetitive, surface-level responses
ER Recommendation: "It would be best to structure the response by issue rather than by study. It would also be recommended that the response starts with the named issue."

8.5 🚨 Fatal Error #5: No Context = Level 1 Maximum

ER Quote: "Any response that has no context cannot get above a Level 1 mark."
ER Quote: "Candidates need to ensure that they choose evaluation points based on what actually happened in the study."
❌ No Context (L1 max = 2/10)✅ With Context (L4-L5 possible)
"The study lacks generalisability because the sample was small" "The study lacks generalisability because only 20 rhesus monkeys from a single primate centre were used, meaning we cannot conclude that all primates show gender-typed toy preferences, especially since the monkeys were captive-born and may have been exposed to human gender stereotypes through caretaker interactions"

8.6 🚨 Fatal Error #6: "In Depth" Standard Not Met

ER Definition of "In Depth": "In depth tends to be having two examples of a particular concept or to support an evaluative point."
ER Quote: "An evaluation that is in depth tends to have at least two explicit examples from the named study for every evaluative point made. These are the requirements for a Level 5 response."
❌ Not In Depth (L2-L3)✅ In Depth (L5)
Point: Ethics was a weakness
Example: There were ethical issues with the animals.
(1 vague example)		 Point: Ethics was a weakness
Examples (2+): (1) Monkeys were separated from their social group during testing, causing potential distress; (2) The use of wheeled vs plush toys may have frustrated animals who couldn't access preferred toys, raising concerns about psychological wellbeing; (3) Animals cannot give informed consent to participate in research.
(3 distinct, specific examples)

8b. Result vs Conclusion Training Module NEW v3.0

Why this module exists: The ER identified Result-vs-Conclusion confusion as the #1 repeated error across all 3 Paper 1 variants. Every single paper's General Comments section mentions it. This module gives you deliberate practice.

The Definitive Test

Question to Ask YourselfIf YES → It's a...
Does it contain specific numbers, percentages, or statistics from the study?RESULT (don't write this for conclusion questions!)
Could another researcher replicate the study and verify this statement?RESULT
Does it answer "what do the findings MEAN for psychology"?CONCLUSION ✅
Could you prefix it with "The results suggest that..." and it still makes sense?CONCLUSION ✅
Is it linked to the aim/purpose of the study?CONCLUSION ✅

11. Paper 2 — Research Methods (9990/21) v2.0

11.1 Three Biggest Blind Spots (from ER)

🔴 Blind Spot #1: Case Study Method
ER Quote: "Candidates did not appear to have a secure understanding of the case study method. The most common problem here was candidates trying to apply an experiment, especially the inclusion of two conditions, such as using boys with ASD diagnoses and without."
ER Requirements (4 features):
  1. Single unit/case (one person, one group, one event)
  2. Detailed/in-depth (not broad survey)
  3. Multiple methods (triangulation: interviews + observations + documents)
  4. Background info (history, context — e.g., talk to parents, school records)

Common errors (all cost marks):

  • ❌ Including IV/DV (it's not an experiment)
  • ❌ Having two conditions/groups
  • ❌ Only using one research method
  • ❌ No background information collection plan
🔴 Blind Spot #2: Participant vs Non-Participant Observation
ER Quote: "The descriptions of participant observation and non-participant observation were often tautological, and many confused this with covert and overt observations. The most common mistake was to suggest that these were when the researcher took part or did not take part in the study."
❌ Common Wrong Answers✅ Correct Understanding
"Participant = covert observation"
"Non-participant = overt observation"
"Participant = researcher takes part"		 Participant observation: Researcher joins the group being observed (becomes a member). E.g., Piliavin researchers appeared as passengers on the train.
Non-participant observation: Researcher observes from OUTSIDE, does not interact. E.g., Bandura researcher watched through one-way mirror; Hassett researcher recorded from video.
🔴 Blind Spot #3: Experimental vs Control Condition
ER Quote: "Candidates typically made errors such as describing the experimental condition as 'the one that changes' and the control condition as 'the one that does not change'."
ER Guidance: "Candidates may benefit from learning key words to answer this type of question, such as that the 'experimental condition is a level of the IV that is manipulated'."
❌ Wrong Definitions✅ Correct Definitions
Experimental = "the one that changes"
Control = "the one that doesn't change"
Control = "things kept constant"		 Experimental condition: A specific level of the independent variable (IV) that the researcher manipulates to test its effect on the DV.
Control condition: A baseline level of the IV (often "zero" or "normal") used for comparison. E.g., Andrade: doodling condition (experimental) vs. no-doodling/listening-only condition (control). Note: control condition IS a level of the IV — it's not "nothing happens".

11.2 Other Paper 2 Problem Areas (from ER)

'Explain' Questions Underperform

'Explain' = needs justification, not just statement
Candidates frequently gave "two basic suggestions" instead of one developed explanation for 2-3 marks.

Mean vs Mode Confusion

Cannot differentiate quantitative data on a scale (use mean) vs. in categories (use mode). Very few candidates accessed full marks here.

Correlation Misunderstanding

Most candidates didn't understand correlations. Many started with groups (like experiments) or identified co-variables as IV/DV. "Two co-variables" requirement almost never met.

Participant/Situational Variables

Vast majority just repeated the stem ("participant"/"situation"). Tautological answers ("because it's situational") not credited. Need: internal/external factor explanation.

Table Construction

Common errors: insufficient labelling (must state "percentage of correct ID"), values outside acceptable range, drawing bar charts instead of tables, writing paragraphs.

Hypothesis Direction

Many just repeated the scenario wording. "Shows direction of results" is repeating, not explaining. Must reference which IV level predicts which DV outcome.

11.3 Paper 2 Quick Prep Checklist

✅ Sampling Methods

Know: Volunteer, Opportunity, Random, Systematic, Stratified, Self-selected. Can explain pros/cons + link to scenario.

✅ Ethics

Humans: Consent, Deception, Withdraw, Harm, Privacy, Debrief.
Animals: Species, Number, Housing, Pain, Reward, Protection.

✅ Data Types

Quantitative (numerical, objective) vs Qualitative (descriptive, in-depth). Know WHICH to use for Mean vs Mode.

✅ Designs

Independent Groups, Repeated Measures, Matched Pairs. Know difference + how to match (e.g., IQ for Baron-Cohen).

12.1 The 10 Commandments (from ER Key Messages)

1
Know ALL components of EVERY core study. "Questions can be asked about any part of a core study." 考纲列出的所有细节都可能被考到
2
Read the WHOLE question carefully. "Ensure responses are fulfilling the demands of each one." Check for: data requirements, named issue, link to previous answer, "in this study", scope limits. 读完整题目，注意所有要求
3
Present results precisely. "Know if results are about how many participants performed correctly or on how many trials." Wrong parameter = 0 marks. 精确呈现结果数据，注意参数
4
Engage with stimulus material. "Ensure they can access all available marks." If a novel situation is presented, USE it. 必须使用题目给的材料/情境
5
Know the set procedure in order. "Questions can be based around just part of a procedure." Be ready to describe ANY section precisely.
6
Procedure = Replicable detail. "Generic 'stories' about a procedure will not gain credit." 程序题要精确到可复现，不能写故事
7
Full definitions for syllabus terms. "Candidates should be able to give full definitions of terms listed in the syllabus." 考纲术语要能给完整定义
8
Full assumptions for all four approaches. Have them memorised word-for-word. 四个取向的假设要背熟
9
Plan your answers. "There is enough time for answers to be planned." Good knowledge poorly applied = low marks. 有时间规划答案，不要急着写
10
Attempt EVERY question. "As positive marking is used, candidates should attempt all questions even if unsure." Blank = guaranteed 0. Guess = possible marks. 正评分制，空白必零，尝试可能得分

12.2 What Examiners Reward (Strong Response Patterns)

✅ Characteristics of Level 5 Responses (from ER)
  1. Followed question demands precisely — Every part addressed, restrictions respected
  2. Explicit psychological terminology — Used syllabus terms correctly and confidently
  3. Logical, well-planned structure — Especially for Q10: structured by ISSUE not by study
  4. Appropriate examples from studies — When expected, specific study evidence included
  5. Real-world application in 'what' AND 'how' — For Q5: explicit detail, not implicit
  6. Analysis present — Counter-arguments, discussion of strengths/weaknesses of the issue itself, conclusions
  7. Named issue addressed FIRST — Ensures coverage, shows awareness
  8. Equal depth across all 4 points — No 3-strong + 1-weak pattern

12.3 What Penalises Responses (Weak Response Patterns)

❌ Characteristics of Low-Scoring Responses (from ER)
  1. Good knowledge, poor application — "Some candidates appear to have good knowledge but do not apply this effectively"
  2. Superficial evaluation — Issues named but not explained, no analysis
  3. Structured by study, not issue — "Often led to response being quite superficial and repetitive"
  4. Description in essay — "Any description of the study does not gain credit"
  5. Tautological definitions — "Personal space was 'personal space'" / "Because it's situational not personal"
  6. Repeating question words — Cannot demonstrate understanding
  7. Generic stories for procedures — No replicable detail
  8. Blank responses — Especially on "new" studies or perceived hard questions

12.4 Predictors of Success (from ER)

ER found these questions strongly correlated with overall success:
  • Paper 1: Section B (Q9+Q10) was strongest predictor. Also Q1, Q3, Q6 (Paper 11); Q1, Q3, Q6 (Paper 12); Q1, Q3, Q8 (Paper 13)
  • Pattern: Students who do well on comparison (Q9) and procedure detail (Q6) tend to do well overall
  • Implication: Master these question types first — they're gateway skills
