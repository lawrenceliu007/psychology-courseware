0. CIE Scoring System Core NEW v2.0

📐 Why This Section Exists

Before you learn what to write, you must understand how you are scored. This section contains the official rules that examiners follow when marking your paper. These rules are extracted directly from the Generic Marking Principles (GMP) at the start of every official Mark Scheme. Most students never read them — which means they make avoidable mistakes on every exam.

Read this section once. It will change how you approach every question.

0.1 Generic Marking Principles (GMP) — The 6 Iron Rules

These 6 principles appear at the beginning of every single CIE Mark Scheme for Psychology 9990. They are non-negotiable.

RuleWhat It MeansImportanceStudent Impact
GMP1 Marks are awarded based on the specific content of the Mark Scheme. Examiners cannot award marks for correct-sounding answers that don't match what's written in the MS. 🔴 Critical You must use the exact terminology and concepts the MS expects. "The sample was small" might not match if the MS wants "opportunity sampling was used".
GMP2 Only whole marks are awarded. No half marks, no "almost there" marks. 🟡 Know it If your answer is between two mark levels, you get the lower one. There's no "2.5 marks".
GMP3 ⭐ Marks are awarded for what is CORRECT. Errors do NOT lose marks. Omissions do NOT lose marks. If you write something wrong, the examiner simply ignores it. You are never penalised for incorrect information. 🔴🔴🔴 MOST IMPORTANT This is the #1 misconception among students. Many students are afraid to write because they think wrong answers deduct marks. They don't! Write everything you think might be relevant — if it's wrong, it's ignored. If it's right, you get the mark.
GMP4 Rules must be applied consistently. All candidates are marked using the same standard. 🟡 Know it No special treatment. Don't waste time trying to be clever or original — stick to what the MS rewards.
GMP5 The full range of marks should be used. Examiners are trained to use all available mark levels. 🟡 Know it A well-written answer can get full marks. A poor one gets zero. The scale is fully utilised.
GMP6 Marks are awarded based only on the Mark Scheme requirements — not on grade boundaries. Your raw score is converted to a grade later. 🟡 Know it Don't worry about "how many marks do I need for an A". Focus on maximising each question's score.
⭐ GMP3 Deep Dive: "Positive Marking"
  • ✅ Write a correct point → +1 mark
  • ❌ Write an incorrect point → 0 marks (NOT -1)
  • ⚠️ Write nothing → 0 marks
  • 💡 Strategy: If you're unsure about a point, WRITE IT ANYWAY. The worst case is 0 (same as leaving it blank). The best case is +1.
  • ⚠️ Exception: Contradictory statements within the same sentence may result in 0 for that point (see Social Science Rule #5 below).

0.2 Social Science-Specific Principles — 7 Additional Rules

Beyond the GMPs, CIE applies these 7 rules specifically to social science subjects (including Psychology):

#RuleExample
SS1 Alternative wording that conveys the same meaning as the MS → Award the mark. MS says "participants were deceived" → Candidate writes "the researcher lied to participants about the true aim" → ✅ Awarded
SS2 Correct answers NOT in the MS → Still award the mark. If the candidate writes something correct that isn't listed, examiners should still credit it. Candidate correctly identifies "demand characteristics" as a weakness even though the MS only lists "ecological validity" → ✅ Awarded
SS3 Multiple correct answers in one space → Award ALL of them (for non-list questions). Question asks for "one ethical issue". Candidate writes "deception AND lack of informed consent" → Both credited (though only 1 mark available)
SS4 🔴 Keywords without demonstrating understanding → Do NOT award. Just dropping terminology isn't enough. Writes "reliability" but doesn't explain what IS reliable or WHY it matters → ❌ Not awarded
SS5 🔴 Contradictory answers → Do NOT award. If you say X and then say not-X in the same response, you get 0 for that point. "The sample was large enough" followed by "but the small sample limits generalisability" → ❌ Contradiction = 0
SS6 Repeated points → Do NOT award again. Saying the same thing twice with different words doesn't earn extra marks. "Small sample" in point 1, then "limited number of participants" in point 2 → Second instance ❌ [REP]
SS7 Spelling errors → Tolerate UNLESS it's a syllabus term that causes confusion. "ecolgical validity" → ✅ Tolerated. "efficacy" instead of "validity" where context makes it unclear → ❌ Not tolerated

0.3 Annotations Dictionary — What Those Symbols Mean

When you get your exam back (or imagine looking at a marked script), these are the symbols examiners use. Understanding them helps you understand why you got (or didn't get) marks:

√ Creditworthy Point (√)

A correct, mark-earning point. Appears on ALL questions. Each √ = typically 1 mark (sometimes more for developed points).

+ Creditworthy 'What' (+)

Used specifically in Q8 Application questions. Marks the identification of the concept/issue being applied. Must be paired with explanation.

N Named Issue (N) FORCED

CRITICAL for Q10 Essay (10 marks). The examiner MUST see evaluation of this specific syllabus dimension. Missing it caps your mark at L3 (5-6 marks max out of 10).

S Strength (S)

Used in Q10 Essay. Marks a strength/advantage point. You need at least 2 strengths for full marks.

W Weakness (W)

Used in Q10 Essay. Marks a weakness/disadvantage point. You need at least 2 weaknesses for full marks.

D Detailed (D)

Used in Q10 Essay. Indicates this point has been sufficiently developed/explained, not just stated. Distinguishes L4/L5 from L2/L3.

d Data Mark (d)

Used specifically in Q4 Results questions. Awards a mark for including specific numerical data (means, percentages, p-values) from the study.

ID Identification (ID)

Used in Q6 Two Friends / Background questions. Awards the mark for correctly identifying/naming the concept or factor.

Ex Example (Ex)

Used in Q6 Two Friends / Background questions. Awards the mark for providing a concrete example that illustrates the identified concept.

L1–L5 Level (L)

Overall level awarded. For Q9 S&D: L1=0-1, L2=2, L3=3, L4=4 (out of 8). For Q10 Essay: L1=1-2, L2=3-4, L3=5-6, L4=7-8, L5=9-10 (out of 10).

NAQ Not Answering Question

Danger signal. Your answer doesn't address what the question asked. Common in Q9/Q10 when students write generic evaluation instead of following the specific instruction.

BOD Benefit of Doubt

Borderline case — examiner gives you the mark because your answer is close enough. Usually appears when you're between levels.

REP Repetition (R)

You've already made this point earlier. No additional mark awarded. Check your work before finishing — if two points sound similar, merge or replace one.

✗ Error (✗)

Incorrect statement. Remember GMP3: this does NOT lose you marks — it just doesn't gain any. But contradictions (✗ alongside ✓) can void a point.

0.4 The Marking Workflow — How Examiners Actually Score Your Paper

1 Read the whole answer first. Examiners read your complete response before assigning any marks. They form an initial impression of the level.
2 Identify creditworthy points (√). Go through line by line, marking each correct/relevant point. This is where GMP3 applies — errors are ignored, only correct content counts.
3 Apply annotations. Add N/S/W/D for essay questions, ID/Ex for Two Friends, d for data marks, check for REP/NAQ.
4 Determine Level (L1-L5). Based on the quality, depth, and coverage of points, assign an overall level. Check against Level Descriptors (§3.6).
5 Assign final mark. Within the level range, pick the specific mark based on how well the answer meets the top end of that level's criteria.

Paper Structure

CIE Psychology 9990 Paper 1 tests your knowledge of 12 Core Studies. Questions range from 1-mark recall to 12-mark extended evaluation. Understanding which type of question you're answering is the single most important skill — because the type determines your answer structure.

2. Question Type Taxonomy (with Real MS Rules) v2.0 UPGRADE

2.1 State / Define / Identify 1–2 marks AO1

These questions test pure recall. You either know the answer or you don't. No framework needed — just write the fact.

Real Exam Examples
  • State the type of problem that Biyu is concerned about. [1] Hölzel
  • Define what is meant by operant conditioning. [1] Saavedra
  • State what is meant by the term 'field experiment'. [2] Piliavin
  • What is meant by the term 'theory of mind'? [2] Baron-Cohen
Model Answer (1 mark)

Demand characteristics.

Model Answer (2 marks)

A field experiment is a study conducted in a natural real-world setting where the researcher manipulates the independent variable, but extraneous variables are harder to control than in a laboratory.

Common mistake: Writing a full paragraph for a 1-mark question. One word or one sentence is enough. Examiners award the mark for the key term — extra words waste time.
📋 MS Rules for State/Define Questions (from 2024+2025 MS):
  • ✅ List is definitive: If the MS provides a list of acceptable answers (e.g., Q1 Pozzulo cartoon characters), ONLY those answers are accepted. No alternatives.
  • ❌ Too vague = 0 marks: "'Food'" for primary reinforcer (Fagen) → rejected. Must be specific: "'Banana'" → accepted.
  • ❌ Too general = 0 marks: "'Sound'" alone for secondary reinforcer → rejected. Must specify: "'Whistle (sound)'" → accepted.
  • ⚠️ Spelling tolerance: Minor spelling errors in non-syllabus terms are tolerated (SS7).

2.2 Outline 2–4 marks AO1

Brief, factual description. No evaluation, no opinion. Just state the fact(s) clearly.

Real Exam Examples
  • Outline one assumption of the biological approach. [4] Hassett
  • Outline one ethical issue in the study by Piliavin et al. and suggest how it could be overcome. [4] Piliavin
  • Outline one ethical strength of this study. [2] Hassett
Model Answer (4 marks — Outline one assumption of the biological approach)

Assumption: Behaviour can be explained in terms of brain structures and/or biochemical processes. Explanation: The biological approach assumes that all psychological behaviour has a physical cause. For example, hormones such as testosterone influence aggression levels, and brain areas like the amygdala process emotions. Hassett et al.'s study supports this by showing that male monkeys showed a preference for wheeled toys, suggesting innate (biological) differences rather than learned social influences.

📋 MS Rules for Outline Questions:
  • ✅ Each distinct point = 1 mark. A 4-mark outline needs 4 separate facts/details.
  • ❌ Evaluation mixed in = still gets AO1 marks for the descriptive parts, but the evaluative parts won't earn extra unless explicitly asked.
  • ⚠️ Procedure outlines need specificity: "They observed the monkeys" = 1 mark. "They observed the monkeys in a free-play setting with both 'wheeled' and 'plush' toys available, recording duration of interaction with each toy type" = 3 marks.

2.3 Describe 2–6 marks AO1

Factual description with more detail than "outline". Often asks for procedure, results, or sample details.

Real Exam Examples
  • Describe the sample in Baron-Cohen's study. [4] Baron-Cohen
  • Describe how data was collected in Piliavin's study. [4] Piliavin
  • Describe the results of Dement & Kleitman's study. [6] Dement
📋 MS Rules for Describe Results Questions (Q4 — most common):
  • d mark (data mark): Specific numbers/percentages earn extra marks. "Doodling group recalled more" = AO1 mark. "Doodling group recalled mean 7.8 items vs control 5.8 (29% improvement)" = AO1 + d mark.
  • ❌ Wrong metric = 0 for that part: Q3(a) Hassett — question asks for duration of play. Answering "'played more'" (frequency) or "'toy preference'" = 0 marks. Always check what the question is measuring!
  • ❌ 'Different aims' / 'Different conclusions' in S&D = L1 max: These are superficial differences that don't show understanding of psychological principles.
  • ✅ Meaningful comparison required: When comparing results, include actual numbers and explain what the difference means.

2.4 Explain / Suggest 2–6 marks AO2

Application-based questions. You need to link knowledge to a scenario or suggest improvements.

Real Exam Examples
  • Suggest one reason why Andrade's findings have real-world application. [3] Andrade
  • Explain why Milgram's study could be considered unethical. [4] Milgram
  • Explain how Hölzel controlled for demand characteristics. [3] Hölzel
📋 MS Rules for Explain/Suggest Questions:
  • + mark (identification) + development: In application questions, you first identify the concept (+), then explain how it applies (development). Both needed for full marks.
  • ❌ Deception in Milgram: just saying "confederate screamed" = max 1 mark. Must explain why it's deception (participants thought it was real).
  • ❌ Hassett weakness: "'monkeys cannot communicate to tell us'" = 0 marks. Not a valid weakness per MS.
  • ❌ Hassett weakness: "'does not generalise to humans'" as explanation = 0 for explanation mark (can still get ID mark separately).
  • ✅ ID and explanation can be independently scored: Even if your explanation is wrong, you may still get the identification mark.

2.5 Evaluate 6–10 marks AO3

Structured evaluation using PEE chain or balanced framework. See §3 for frameworks.

2.6 Discuss / Assess / To what extent 8–12 marks AO3

Extended evaluation requiring balanced argument + conclusion. See §3.3 PECC framework.

2.7 Compare 4 marks AO3

Similarities and Differences. See §3.4 Comparison Framework.

2.8 Design 10 marks AO2

Research design question. Requires IV/DV, procedure, controls, ethics.

3. Answer Frameworks (When to Use Them)

3.1 The PEE Chain — For Evaluation (6–12 marks)

P Point: Make an evaluation point (e.g., "The study lacks ecological validity")
E Evidence: Support with specific detail from the study (e.g., "conducted in a lab with artificial tasks")
E Explain: Explain WHY this matters / what the impact is (e.g., "meaning participants may not behave naturally, reducing the real-world applicability of findings about toy preferences")

3.2 Balanced Structure — For "Evaluate" (6–10 marks)

  1. Introduction (1 sentence): Briefly state what you're evaluating
  2. Strength 1 (PEE paragraph): One developed strength with evidence
  3. Weakness 1 (PEE paragraph): One developed weakness with evidence
  4. Strength 2 (PEE paragraph): Second developed strength
  5. Weakness 2 (PEE paragraph): Second developed weakness
  6. Conclusion (1-2 sentences): Weigh up — overall, is the study useful/reliable/valid?

3.3 Position–Evidence–Counter–Conclusion — For "To what extent" (8–12 marks)

  1. Position: Take a clear stance ("To a large extent...")
  2. Evidence for position (2 PEE paragraphs): Developed arguments supporting your position
  3. Counter-argument (1-2 PEE paragraphs): Acknowledge the other side
  4. Conclusion: Reaffirm position with nuanced judgement

3.4 Comparison Framework — For "Compare" (4 marks)

  1. Similarity 1: One clear similarity with example from both studies
  2. Similarity 2: Second similarity with examples
  3. Difference 1: One clear difference with examples
  4. Difference 2: Second difference with examples
Q9 S&D Critical Rule: Every 2025 paper added a RESTRICTION (e.g., "Do not refer to the sample"). Breaking this restriction = automatic cap at L1-L2. Read the question carefully!

3.5 The "Two Friends" Mini-Evaluation (Q6b in 2025, Q8 in 2024)

Two friends discuss the study. One says X, the other says Y. You evaluate who is more convincing.

ID Identify the psychological concept each friend is referring to (ID mark)
Ex Give Example(s) from the study that illustrate each friend's point (Ex mark)
⚖ Weigh up — which friend is more convincing and why? Reference evidence from the study.

These are the official criteria examiners use to decide whether your answer is L1, L2, L3, L4, or L5. Extracted verbatim from 2024 June and 2025 Mark Schemes.

3.6.1 Q9 Similarities & Differences (8 marks) — Levels 0–4

  • W (Weakness): Each weakness gets a W annotation
  • D (Detailed): Well-developed points get D — distinguishes L4/L5 from L2/L3
  • Typical L5 answer pattern: N✓ + S(D) + S(D) + W(D) + W(D) = 9-10 marks

4. When NOT to Use a Framework

Over-structuring simple questions wastes time and can actually lose marks if the examiner thinks you're padding. Here's when to just answer directly:

  • 1-mark State/Define: One word or term. Done.
  • 2-mark Outline: 2 sentences max. Don't PEE a definition.
  • "Name one...": Just name it. No explanation needed unless asked.
  • Data extraction (Q4): List the numbers clearly. Framework adds nothing.

5. Evaluation Toolkit — Syllabus Dimensions

Reliability

Can the study be replicated? Same method → same results? Look for: standardisation, controls, objective measures.

Validity (Internal)

Did the study measure what it intended? Confounding variables? Demand characteristics?

Validity (External / Ecological)

Can findings generalise? To other populations? To real-world settings? Sampling bias?

Generalisability

Sample size, demographics, cultural factors. Does this apply beyond the participants tested?

Ethics

Informed consent, deception, right to withdraw, protection from harm, debriefing, privacy.

Usefulness

Does it help us understand behaviour? Real-world applications? Theory-building value?

Representativeness

Is the sample typical of the target population? Opportunity/volunteer sampling biases?

Credibility

Researcher bias? Observer effects? Social desirability? Qualitative vs quantitative?

8. Common Pitfalls v2.0 UPGRADE

1. Writing too much for low-mark questions. A 1-mark "State" question needs one word. A 2-mark "Outline" needs 2 sentences. Don't write a paragraph — you're wasting time you need for the 10-mark questions at the end.
2. Generic evaluation points. "The study lacked ecological validity" is not enough. You must say why — what was artificial? What specific feature made it unrealistic? Use details from the study.
3. Comparing to other studies in evaluation. Unless the question explicitly asks you to compare, evaluation should focus on the study itself. Don't write "unlike Bandura, Milgram..." in a Milgram evaluation question. This is a CIE 9990 requirement — use only elements from the study being evaluated.
4. No conclusion for 10+ mark questions. If you're asked to evaluate in 10+ marks, you must end with a conclusion sentence that weighs up the strengths and weaknesses. Without it, you cap at 8 marks.
5. All strengths or all weaknesses. "Evaluate" requires balance. Even if you think the study is excellent, you must acknowledge weaknesses. A one-sided answer caps at 4–5 marks for a 10-mark question.
6. Repeating the same dimension. If both your strengths are about "Control of Variables," you only get credit for one. Choose different syllabus dimensions for each point.
7. Not reading the question carefully. "Including two strengths and two weaknesses" means exactly that — not three strengths and one weakness. "Outline one..." means one, not two. The question tells you the structure.
8. Forgetting to use data. In Describe questions, examiners want to see specific numbers, percentages, means, and p-values. Without data, your description is vague and earns fewer marks.
🔴 NEW in v2.0 — Real Deduction Patterns from Official MS:
  • Q3(c) Hassett Weakness: "'cruel as not in the wild'" = 0 · "'monkeys cannot communicate'" = 0 · "'broke ethical guideline of numbers'" = 0 · "'does not generalise to humans'" as explanation = 0 (ID may still be awarded)
  • Q7 Baron-Cohen Children: "'generalise to adults'" = 0 marks (not a valid evaluation for this study)
  • Q8 Perry Two Friends Ethics: Deception linked to OT/placebo administration → not credited for Daphne (that layer is reasonable) · Right to withdraw → 0 (no such option existed)
  • Q9(b) Dement vs Bio S&D: "'Different aims'" = L1 max · "'Different conclusions'" = L1 max · "'Different number of groups'" = L0 · Must base differences on psychological principles
  • Q10 Fagen Essay: "'Demand characteristics low'" = 0 (elephants aren't humans!) · Forced dimension = quantitative data
  • Paper 21 Research Methods: "'Ps were selected by replying to advert'" = 0 (too vague for sampling) · "'Species should be protected'" = NAQ · "'discrete/categorical' for data type" = 0 (wrong) · "'qualitative' for mode" = 0 · Saying X indicates no direction in hypothesis = 0 = REP

11.2 Critical MS Rules for Paper 2 (from 2024 June MS)

These are the specific "Do Accept / Do Not Accept" rules from the 2024 June Paper 21 Mark Scheme. They reveal exactly what examiners reward and penalise:

Sampling Questions

  • ❌ "Ps were selected by replying to advert" = 0 marks (too vague — must specify voluntary/opportunity sampling)
  • ✅ "Voluntary/self-selected sampling — participants chose to respond to an advertisement" = full marks

Ethics — Species-Specific

  • ❌ "Species should be protected from harm" = NAQ (Not Answering Question — too generic)
  • ❌ "Use endangered species" = 1 mark max (incomplete)
  • ❌ "Bred in captivity" = 0 = NAQ (irrelevant to ethics of using animals)
  • ❌ "Humanely treated" = 0 (too vague)
  • ✅ Must identify a specific ethical guideline and explain why it's relevant

Data Type Questions

  • ❌ "Discrete/categorical" for data that is actually ordinal/interval/ratio = 0 (wrong classification)
  • ❌ "Factual/accurate" as strength of data type = 0 (not a valid advantage)
  • ❌ "Measurable" as strength = 0 (must include "because..." or "than...")
  • ✅ Correct classification + reasoned advantage (e.g., "Interval data allows for parametric tests because it has equal intervals which enables meaningful comparison of scores")

Experimental Design Questions

  • ❌ "Different conditions" for independent measures = NOT allowed (could be repeated measures — must say "different participants/groups/people")
  • ❌ "Placebo" for control condition (unless specifically applicable) = Do not accept
  • ❌ "To compare" alone for control condition purpose = Do not accept
  • ❌ "How well they imitated Glyn" for measuring DV = 0 [REP] (must describe actual measurement method in detail)

Consent Questions (3-mark structure)

  • Must include: (1) what informed consent means + (2) why parent/guardian needed + (3) why child assent also needed
  • Missing any component = loss of that mark

Graph/Table Questions

  • ❌ Adding % after figures when raw numbers shown = wrong but ignore (no penalty)
  • ✅ 1 mark per correct data point extracted from graph/table

Observation Questions

  • ❌ Max 4 marks if no examples given or only one term used
  • ❌ Piliavin victims/models are NOT examples of participant observation
  • ✅ Must give specific behavioural examples for full marks

Central Tendency Questions

  • ❌ "Qualitative" for mode = 0 (mode is quantitative)
  • ❌ "Words" for mode = 0
  • ✅ Mode = most frequent value · Median = middle value · Mean = arithmetic average

Hypothesis Questions

  • ❌ Saying X does not indicate direction = 0 = REP (must state directional or non-directional clearly)
  • ✅ Directional: "Participants in condition A will score higher than B"
    ✅ Non-directional: "There will be a difference between conditions A and B"

IV/DV Identification

  • ❌ Operationalising the DV (describing HOW it's measured) when only asked to IDENTIFY it = 0 = NAQ (over-answering = wrong question)
  • ✅ Just name the variable: "The IV is the type of toy (wheeled vs plush)"

Participant vs Situational Variable (Q9)

  • ❌ NO mark for correct identification of X/Y — mark only for explanation
  • ✅ "This is a participant variable because it is a characteristic of the individual that cannot be changed by the experimenter" = full marks

Case Study (Q10a) — 4 Imaginary Columns

  • Scored using L1-L5 level descriptors (similar to Paper 1 Q10 but adapted for case study evaluation)
  • Must address: methodology, data, ethics, usefulness

Validity/Reliability (Q10b)

  • ❌ Do not refer to sampling or ethics (these are separate dimensions)
  • ✅ Focus on: measurement accuracy, consistency, objectivity, bias
