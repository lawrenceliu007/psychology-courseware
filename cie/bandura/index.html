1️⃣ Main Assumptions in Context（学习取向的主要假设）

🧠

Learning Approach Core Assumptions

All behaviour is learned through experience, observation & the environment

Humans as Learners（人作为学习者）

The learning approach is based on the assumption that all behaviour is learned from the environment through experience. This includes both direct experience (where the individual is rewarded or punished for a behaviour) and indirect experience (where the individual observes others being rewarded or punished, or simply observes what others do). Unlike the cognitive approach, the learning approach focuses on observable behaviour rather than internal mental processes.

The main assumptions of the learning approach to psychology are that:

  • Behaviour is learned through association, reinforcement, observation and imitation.
  • Observable behaviour should be the focus of study, not internal thoughts or feelings.
  • Principles of learning apply equally across species, situations, and cultures.
  • The environment is the primary determinant of behaviour (nurture over nature).

Social Learning Theory（社会学习理论）

Social Learning Theory (SLT) was developed by Albert Bandura as an extension of behaviourist learning theory. It proposes that people learn by observing others (models) and imitating their behaviour, especially when the model is rewarded for that behaviour. This was a major development because it explained how people acquire novel behaviours without direct reinforcement — solving one of the major weaknesses of strict behaviourism.

SLT assumes that learning can occur in four key processes:

  • Attention（注意） — the observer must pay attention to the model's behaviour
  • Retention（保持） — the observer must remember what was observed
  • Reproduction（再生产） — the observer must be able to physically reproduce the behaviour
  • Motivation（动机） — there must be a reason (incentive or reinforcement) to perform the behaviour

Why Bobo Doll Matters for Learning Approach

The Bandura et al. (1961) study is the most famous test of SLT and the most cited study in social learning research. It directly challenges behaviourism by demonstrating that children acquired aggressive behaviour simply by watching an adult model — without any direct reinforcement of the children themselves.

"Learning would be exceedingly laborious, not to mention hazardous, if people had to rely solely on the effects of their own actions to inform them what to do."
— Bandura (1977), explaining why observational learning is essential

2️⃣ The Psychology Being Investigated（研究涉及的心理学）

Social learning theory proposes that people can learn new behaviours simply by observing and imitating a model — without needing to be directly reinforced themselves. Bandura et al. (1961) investigated whether this principle could explain the acquisition of aggressive behaviour in children, and whether imitating aggression depends on the sex of the model and the sex of the observer.

Core Research Question 核心研究问题： Will children imitate aggressive behaviour they have observed in an adult model, and reproduce it later in the absence of that model?
（儿童是否会模仿他们在成人榜样身上观察到的攻击行为，并在该榜样不在场时复现这种行为？）

Three key terms underpin this study:

  • Model（榜样/模型） — a person who inspires or encourages others to imitate positive or negative behaviours. In this study, the adult confederate who demonstrated aggression toward the Bobo doll.
  • Social learning（社会学习） — the learning of a new behaviour that is observed in a role model and imitated later in the absence of that model.
  • Aggression（攻击行为） — behaviour that is aimed at harming others either physically (hitting, kicking) or psychologically (verbal insults).
"The phenomenon of human behaviour, although vast in scope and complexity, can be analysed and understood through the application of basic learning principles."
— Bandura & Walters (1963), Social Learning and Personality Development

Previous research had shown that children imitated the behaviour of a model when in the model's presence. What Bandura et al. wanted to test was whether this imitation could occur in the absence of the model — that is, whether the behaviour had been truly learned rather than simply triggered by social facilitation.

3️⃣ Background（研究背景）

🔍

Two Routes of Social Learning

Immediate imitation vs. delayed generalisation — which is real learning?

Children copy adults. One reason for this is that the immediate social setting encourages the child to imitate what he or she is watching (social facilitation). This influences their behaviour, making it more likely that the child will do what others are doing around them. Alternatively, the observation of a behaviour can lead a child to acquire a new response that he or she could reproduce independently. If this is the case, the new behaviour should generalise（泛化） to new settings and so would be produced in the absence of an adult model（在没有成人榜样时出现）.

Albert Bandura had previously conducted experiments on social learning and was interested in studying social learning in the context of aggression. To learn from others, the observer (e.g. a child) must be paying attention to the behaviour of a model. They must retain (remember) the behaviour they have observed, in order to reproduce it. When social learning occurs, it could potentially lead to either aggressive or non-aggressive behaviour.

Bandura expected that watching an aggressive model should lead to more aggressive behaviours being demonstrated, and that observing a non-aggressive model should lead to more non-aggressive behaviour being produced — i.e. even less aggressive behaviour than normal.

Figure 4.1: A child imitates observed behaviour

3.1 Previous Research and Social Context

Previous research had shown that children imitated behaviour of a model when in the presence of the model. Bandura et al. wanted to investigate whether social learning theory（社会学习理论） could be used to explain aggression, specifically when the child was no longer in the presence of an aggressive model.

Children are rewarded in different ways for imitating adults. In general (at least, in the USA in the 1960s when this study took place), boys were rewarded for behaviours considered to be sex-appropriate and punished for inappropriate ones, such as cooking or 'playing mother'. Similarly for girls, rewards and punishments would be applied to discourage what was considered sex-inappropriate behaviours. This, Bandura et al. suggested, would lead to two kinds of differences:

  • First, boys and girls should be more likely to imitate same-sex models（同性模型）
  • Second, they should differ in the readiness with which they imitate aggression（攻击行为）, with boys doing so more readily as this was seen as a more masculine-type behaviour

📌 Key Word

Generalisation（泛化）: in learning theory, the tendency for a behaviour learned in one situation to be performed in different but similar situations. In Bandura's study, generalisation would be evidenced by children reproducing aggressive behaviour in a new room with new toys, not just when watching the original model.

§3 研究背景：两条路径与性别角色

两条模仿路径：

  • Social Facilitation（社会促进）：只是"因为旁边有人在做所以跟着做"——并不算真正的学习。
  • Generalisation（泛化）：在没有榜样时也能再现新行为——这才是Observational Learning（观察学习）的证据。

1960s 性别角色背景：在美国 1960 年代，男孩被强化攻击行为（"男孩就要勇敢"），女孩被强化养育行为（"淑女不能打架"）。因此 Bandura 预测：

  • 儿童更可能模仿Same-sex Model（同性榜样）
  • 男孩比女孩更愿意模仿攻击行为（因为攻击被视为"男性化"行为）

💡 考试要点："Generalisation"是 AO1 关键词——强调"新行为在新情境中独立复现"。

▶ Exam Key Points — Background AO1
  • Two routes of social learning: social facilitation (mere imitation) vs. generalisation (true learning) Must Know
  • Key term: generalisation = behaviour performed in new setting without the model present Must Know
  • 1960s gender roles: boys rewarded for aggression, girls for nurturing behaviour Common Q
  • Prediction: same-sex model + masculine behaviour (aggression) → strongest imitation AO2 Gold

4️⃣ Aim & Hypotheses（研究目的与假设）

The aim was to investigate whether a child would learn aggression by observing a model and would reproduce this behaviour in the absence of the model, and whether the sex of the role model was important. Specifically, there were four hypotheses:

📋 Four Hypotheses（四个假设）

  1. H1 — Observed aggressive behaviour will be imitated: children who see an aggressive model will be more aggressive than those seeing a non-aggressive model or no model.
  2. H2 — Observed non-aggressive behaviour will be imitated: children seeing non-aggressive models will be less aggressive than those seeing no model.
  3. H3 — Same-sex modelling effect: children are more likely to copy a same-sex model than an opposite-sex model.
  4. H4 — Sex differences in aggression: boys will be more likely to copy aggression than girls.

4.1 Issues and Debates Preview

📌 Issues & Debates — Nature vs Nurture

Nature versus nurture (先天与后天): The importance of the environment (nurture) can be seen in the role adults take as models for children, as well as in the rewards and punishments adults give to children. If children learn aggression solely by observing and imitating models, this strongly supports the nurture side of the debate.

§4 四个假设的逻辑链

四个假设层层递进：

  • H1：攻击榜样 → 儿童更攻击（验证 SLT 核心命题）
  • H2：非攻击榜样 → 儿童更不攻击（验证 SLT 的对称性）
  • H3：同性榜样 > 异性榜样（性别角色认同效应）
  • H4：男孩 > 女孩（在攻击模仿上）

💡 考试要点：四个假设的内在逻辑：先验证"是否能学习"(H1+H2)，再验证"学习是否有偏好"(H3+H4)。这种Hierarchical Hypotheses结构是 AO1 答题模板。

▶ Exam Key Points — Aim & Hypotheses AO1
  • 4 hypotheses: aggressive imitation (H1), non-aggressive imitation (H2), same-sex model (H3), boys > girls in aggression (H4) Must Know
  • H1 tests the core SLT claim: observation alone can produce new aggressive behaviour Must Know
  • H3 + H4 are about moderating variables: who imitates whom, and how much AO2 Gold
  • Nature vs Nurture framing: study supports nurture (environmental modelling) Common Q

5️⃣ Method & Sample（研究方法与样本）

5.1 Research method and design

This was a laboratory experiment（实验室实验）; the environment was not the normal place where the children played and the situation was controlled. The design was that of independent measures（独立测量设计） as different children were used in each level of the independent variables (IVs) (although these children were matched for aggression in threes).

There were three independent variables (IVs):

  • Model type（模型类型）: whether the child saw an aggressive model, non-aggressive model or no model
  • Model sex（模型性别）: same sex as child, different sex, or opposite sex
  • Participant sex（参与者性别）: whether the child was a boy or a girl

The dependent variable (DV) was the behaviour the child displayed. This was measured through a controlled observation（控制观察） of the children and measures of aggressive behaviour were recorded.

📌 Research Methods — Matched Participants Design

Matched participants design（匹配参与者设计）: All participants were divided into triplets with very similar initial aggression levels. One individual from each triplet was placed into each of the three conditions (aggressive model, non-aggressive model, and control). This reduces the effect of individual differences on the results.

📌 Research Methods — Inter-rater Reliability

Inter-rater reliability（评分者间信度）: The extent to which two researchers rate the same observed activity in the same way. This is judged using a correlation ('r' value) between the two ratings — high (close to 1) if reliable. In this study, r = 0.89 for the initial aggression ratings, and approximately r ≈ 0.9 for the test-phase behaviour ratings.

5.2 Sample

Seventy-two children aged 3–6 years (36 boys and 36 girls) were recruited from Stanford University nursery school.

📊 Grouping procedure: All 72 children were first rated on aggression by the experimenter and nursery teachers using four 5-point scales (physical aggression, verbal aggression, aggression toward inanimate objects, and aggression inhibition). For the 51 children rated by both observers independently, inter-rater reliability r = 0.89. Children were then matched into triplets（匹配成三人组） based on similar aggression levels and randomly assigned to one of three conditions: aggressive model / non-aggressive model / control (no model). Within each condition, participants were further split by model sex (same-sex / opposite-sex). This gives n = 6 children per cell (24 per condition × 3 conditions).
§5 方法与样本：实验设计的精妙之处

三个自变量 + 一个因变量：

  • IV1 Model Type（模型类型）：攻击 / 非攻击 / 无模型 (3 水平)
  • IV2 Model Sex（模型性别）：同性 / 异性 (2 水平)
  • IV3 Participant Sex（被试性别）：男 / 女 (2 水平)
  • DV：儿童在测试阶段表现出的Aggressive Behaviour（攻击行为），通过控制观察测量

样本结构：72 名儿童 = 36 男 + 36 女，每个实验条件 24 人(每个 cell 6 人)。

三大研究方法亮点：

  • Matched Pairs（匹配配对）：把儿童按初始攻击水平三人一组配对，再随机分配 → 控制个体差异
  • Inter-rater Reliability（评分者间信度）：r = 0.89 高相关 → 数据可信
  • Independent Measures（独立测量）：每个被试只接受一种条件 → 避免顺序效应

💡 AO2 答题模板："The study used a matched pairs design because... This means that individual differences were controlled for, increasing the study's internal validity."

▶ Exam Key Points — Method & Sample AO1
  • Lab experiment with independent measures design Must Know
  • 3 IVs: model type (aggressive/non-aggressive/control), model sex, participant sex Must Know
  • DV = aggressive behaviour measured via controlled observation Must Know
  • n = 72 children (36 boys + 36 girls), aged 3–6, from Stanford University Nursery Common Q
  • Matched pairs design + inter-rater reliability r = 0.89 AO2 Gold

6️⃣ Procedure（实验程序）

The experiment was conducted in four sequential phases: pre-testing & rating → observation (model) → mild arousal → delayed imitation test.

6.1 Phase 1: Pre-testing and Rating

Prior to the experimental part of the study, the children were observed in their nursery school by the experimenter and a teacher who knew them well. They were rated on four different measures: physical aggression, verbal aggression, aggression to inanimate objects, and aggression inhibition (anxiety), each on a five-point scale. They were then assigned to three groups, ensuring that the aggression levels of the children in each group were matched. Of the 51 children rated by both observers (the rest were rated by only one observer), similar ratings were generally produced. Their ratings were compared as a measure of inter-rater reliability (评分者间信度), which showed a high correlation between the observers, of r = 0.89.

6.2 Phase 2: Observation Stage

Twelve boys and 12 girls were allocated to control groups who saw no model. The remaining children were divided equally between aggressive and non-aggressive model groups and, within those, between same and opposite-sex models.

The experimenter and child entered the observation room, where the experimenter showed the child to a table and chair — the 'play area' — where they were shown how to make potato prints and sticker pictures: activities previously identified as interesting for children. The opposite corner of the room also contained a table and chair, a Tinkertoy set (a wooden building kit), a mallet and a five-foot (152 cm) Bobo doll: an inflatable clown-like doll that bounced back when hit (Figure 4.2). This is where the model sat, in those conditions where there was one. The experimenter remained in the room so that the child would not refuse to be alone or try to leave early, but they appeared to be working quietly at their desk.

📌 Research Methods — Why a 'no model' control group?

The non-aggressive model group might appear to be a control group because the key factor of 'aggression' is missing. However, the important aspect is the presence of a model — and in the non-aggressive group a model was still present. This means the real control group is where the IV is absent: the group where there is no model at all. The non-aggressive group tests H2 (does observing non-aggressive behaviour reduce aggression compared to no model?).

6.4 Phase 3: Mild Arousal Stage（轻度唤醒阶段）

The experimental procedure continued with a stage in which all participants were deliberately mildly annoyed. This was done for two reasons:

  • Watching aggression may reduce the production of aggression by the observer (even if it has been learned), and it was necessary to see evidence of learning
  • To ensure that even the non-aggressive condition and control participants would be likely to express aggression, so that any reduction in that tendency could be measured

Arousal procedure（唤醒程序）: The experimenter took the child to another room, showed some attractive toys (e.g., nice art supplies), let the child play for about 2 minutes, then told them these toys were reserved for other children and could not be played with — this deliberately created mild frustration（轻度挫折感）.

6.5 Phase 4: Test for Delayed Imitation（延迟模仿测试）

A test of the child's aggression then followed in which the child was observed for 20 minutes using a one-way mirror（单向镜）. The mirror appeared transparent on the researcher's side (so they could observe the behaviour) but appeared as a normal mirror on the child's side (so they could not see that they were being observed from another room).

For the aggressive model group, there was a test of delayed imitation. The experimental room contained a three-foot (92 cm) Bobo doll, a mallet and peg board, two dart guns and a tether ball with a face painted on it which hung from the ceiling. It also contained some non-aggressive toys, including a tea set, crayons and colouring paper, a ball, two dolls, three bears, cars and trucks, and plastic farm animals. These toys were always presented in the same order.

The children's behaviours were observed in 5-second intervals（5秒间隔） (240 response units per child). There were three 'response measures' of the children's imitation, with a range of possible activities in each:

📏 Response Measures（反应测量指标）

Category
（类别）		Description（描述）
Imitative physical aggression
（模仿性身体攻击）		striking the Bobo doll with the mallet, sitting on the doll and punching it in the nose, kicking the doll, and tossing it in the air
Imitative verbal aggression
（模仿性言语攻击）		repetition of the phrases: 'Sock him', 'Hit him down', 'Kick him', 'Throw him in the air' or 'Pow'

（模仿性非攻击言语）		repetition of 'He keeps coming back for more' or 'He sure is a tough fella'

Partially imitative aggression（部分模仿性攻击） was scored if the child imitated these behaviours incompletely. The two behaviours here were:

  • mallet aggression（锤子攻击）: striking objects other than the Bobo doll aggressively with the mallet
  • sits on Bobo doll aggressively: laying the Bobo doll on its side and sitting on it, without attacking it

Two further categories were:

  • aggressive gun play（攻击性枪械游戏）: shooting darts or aiming a gun and firing imaginary shots at objects in the room
  • non-imitative physical and verbal aggression（非模仿性攻击）: physically aggressive acts directed toward objects other than the Bobo doll and any hostile remarks except for those in the verbal imitation category (e.g. 'Shoot the Bobo', 'Cut him', 'Stupid ball', 'Horses fighting', 'biting', 'Knock over people')

Finally, behaviour units were also counted for non-aggressive play（非攻击性游戏） and sitting quietly not playing at all（安静坐着不玩耍）, and records were kept of the children's remarks about the situation.

The male model scored all the children's behaviours. Except for those conditions in which the male was the model, he was unaware of which condition the child had been in. However, the condition in which the child had been was usually obvious, as in the case of the aggressive model children as they performed the specific behaviours exhibited by the model. To test the reliability of the scorer, a second scorer independently rated the behaviour of half of the children. The reliability score was high at approximately r = 0.9 for different categories of behaviour.

§6 程序：四大阶段的精妙设计

Phase 1 - 预测试与评分：实验者和老师用 5 点量表评估儿童初始攻击水平（4 个维度：身体攻击、言语攻击、对物品攻击、攻击抑制）。匹配成三人组后随机分组。

Phase 2 - 观察阶段：儿童进入观察室，被安排在"玩耍区"画土豆印/贴纸；对面角落是模型区域（Bobo 娃娃 + 锤子 + Tinkertoy）。

  • Aggressive Model：~1 分钟拼 Tinkertoy + 9 分钟打 Bobo（特殊动作 + 攻击性语言）
  • Non-aggressive Model：安静拼 10 分钟 Tinkertoy，完全忽略 Bobo
  • Control：无模型暴露

Phase 3 - 轻度唤醒：把儿童带到另一房间，给 2 分钟玩精美玩具，然后告知"这些玩具是给其他孩子保留的"——故意制造Mild Frustration（轻度挫折），确保所有儿童都有表达攻击的动机。

Phase 4 - 延迟模仿测试：通过One-way Mirror（单向镜）观察 20 分钟，记录 5 秒间隔的行为（共 240 个反应单元）。

💡 关键设计：

  • 5 秒间隔：足够短以避免漏掉瞬时行为，又足够长以观察完整动作
  • 单向镜：避免Demand Characteristics（需求特征）——儿童不知道被观察
  • 3 种模仿攻击 + 2 种部分模仿 + 1 种枪械游戏 + 非模仿攻击 → Multiple DV measures
  • 评分者间信度 r ≈ 0.9 → 高Reliability（信度）
▶ Exam Key Points — Procedure AO1
  • 4 phases: pre-test → observation → mild arousal → delayed imitation test Must Know
  • Aggressive model sequence: ~1 min Tinkertoy + 9 min hitting Bobo + verbal aggression phrases Must Know
  • One-way mirror avoids demand characteristics; 20-min observation in 5-second intervals Common Q
  • Mild arousal phase: ensures all children have aggression motivation, so any reduction is measurable AO2 Gold
  • Inter-rater reliability r ≈ 0.9 → high reliability of behavioural coding AO2 Gold

7️⃣ Results（研究结果）

Children exposed to aggressive models imitated their exact behaviours and were significantly more aggressive, both physically and verbally, than those children in the non-aggressive model or control groups. One-third of the children in the aggressive condition also copied the model's non-aggressive verbal responses but none of the children in either the non-aggressive or control groups made such remarks.

The mean for imitative physical aggression (模仿性身体攻击) for boys with a male model (25.8) was much higher than that for girls (7.2). This indicates that the boys imitated the physical model more than the girls did. However, with a female model, girls imitated less (5.5) than with the male model. Girls imitated more verbal aggression of the same-sex model than boys (although not significantly so). Children were also more likely to imitate a same-sex model than an opposite-sex model; this effect was stronger for boys than for girls.

8️⃣ Conclusion（结论）

The results strongly suggest that observation and imitation can account for the learning of specific acts without reinforcement (无需强化即可通过观察和模仿学习特定行为) of either the model or observer. All four hypotheses were supported:

  • Observed aggressive behaviours are imitated: children who see aggressive models are likely to be more aggressive than those seeing a non-aggressive model or no model.
  • Observed non-aggressive behaviours are imitated: children seeing non-aggressive models will be less aggressive than those seeing no model.
  • Children are more likely to copy a same-sex model, although this may depend on the extent to which this behaviour is sex-typed.
  • Boys are more likely to copy aggression than girls.

The conclusion is striking because it challenges behaviourist learning theory: direct reinforcement is not necessary for learning to occur. Children learned completely novel aggressive acts by watching an adult — an effect that strict behaviourism (which requires direct reinforcement of the learner) cannot explain.

🔑 Key Takeaway

The study demonstrated that modelling (榜样示范) is a powerful mechanism for transmitting behaviour across generations — without any need for reinforcement of the observer. This has profound implications for understanding how aggression, and many other behaviours, may spread in families and cultures.

§8 结论的革命性意义

Bandura 的结论挑战了行为主义的核心命题——传统行为主义认为学习必须通过Direct Reinforcement（直接强化）。但本研究证明：

  • 儿童自身既没被打也没被奖励
  • 榜样（成人模型）也没被打或被奖励
  • 然而儿童学会了新颖的攻击行为

这只能用 Observational Learning（观察学习）解释——这是社会学习理论对行为主义的重大扩展。

9️⃣ Strengths & Weaknesses（优缺点）

Strengths (优点)

  1. Controlled laboratory environment (受控的实验室环境) — This allowed researchers to control extraneous variables (无关变量/外扰变量), ensuring that children in all conditions had the opportunity to display aggressive behaviour.
  2. Standardised procedure (标准化程序) — All children in the two experimental groups saw the model for the same amount of time, and behaviour was standardised in each condition. This makes the study more valid — researchers can confirm that differences in results were due to differences between models — and more reliable — because every child in each condition experienced exactly the same exposure.
  3. High inter-rater reliability (高评分者间信度) — Both the initial observation of aggression and the recording of data showed high reliability, at r ≈ 0.9.
  4. Matched pairs design (匹配配对设计) — Pre-rating children's aggression as part of a matched pairs design added another factor increasing validity, as it ensured differences between conditions were due to the model rather than individual differences between children.
  5. Demand characteristics minimised (需求特征最小化) — Observers watched through a one-way mirror; children were unaware they were being monitored, making natural behaviour more likely than responses to demand characteristics.
  6. Both quantitative and qualitative data (定量与定性数据) — Quantitative data provided objective records of imitative behaviour in each category; qualitative data (though more subjective) offered explanations for why children tended to imitate certain behaviours over others.

📌 Key Word — Extraneous Variable

Extraneous variable (无关变量/外扰变量): a variable other than the IV that may affect the DV. It either acts randomly (affecting the DV across all levels of the IV) or systematically (affecting only one level, in which case it is called a confounding variable), potentially obscuring the effect of the IV and making results difficult to interpret.

Weaknesses (缺点)

  1. Small sample size (样本量小) — Only 6 children per experimental cell were used (although matched pairs reduced the risk of participant variables confounding results), the sample remained small. This reduces statistical power and generalisability.
  2. Sample bias (样本偏差) — All children came from the same university nursery school, implying their parents were academically educated. This may have biased the sample, reducing validity and generalisability.
  3. Ecological validity concerns (生态效度问题) — The laboratory setting was not a normal play environment for children; the Bobo doll is a toy specifically designed to be attacked, unlike real-life aggression contexts.
  4. Ethical concerns about harm (伦理伤害担忧) — Making children more aggressive may have caused psychological harm (see §10 for detailed discussion).
  5. No long-term follow-up (无长期追踪) — If imitation led to learning, behavioural change should be relatively enduring. However, the study did not track how long the acquired behaviours persisted.
  6. No self-report data (无自我报告数据) — Asking children about their feelings during observation or reaction to the Bobo doll could have helped further understanding of differences, e.g. the influence of sex-typing on imitation.

🔟 Ethical Issues（伦理问题）

Bandura's study raised significant ethical issues, particularly by today's standards. One ethical issue with the study was that some of the children might have been harmed by becoming more aggressive during the research. For example, they could have physically injured themselves with the toys they were given to play with after watching an aggressive model. Even if this were not the case, the children were still deliberately annoyed in the procedure of the study. This could have been psychologically distressing for the children. These aspects of the study go against the ethical guideline of protecting participants from physical and psychological harm.

Why This Study Could Not Be Repeated Today

This study was conducted in 1961, before modern ethical guidelines (such as informed consent, right to withdraw, etc.) were fully established. If this experiment were repeated today, it would require parental informed consent (知情同意), ensure children had the right to withdraw at any time, and provide thorough psychological debriefing (事后解释) afterwards to eliminate any potential negative effects. Modern BPS and APA guidelines would also scrutinise the deliberate frustration phase and the prolonged exposure to aggressive modelling.

1️⃣1️⃣ Issues & Debates（议题与辩论）

11.1 Nature versus Nurture（先天与后天）

In the nature versus nurture debate, we can consider why the boys and girls showed different responses. This could be because they are genetically different — a nature explanation (先天解释). Boys might be biologically predisposed to be aggressive, so more likely to copy aggressive models. Alternatively, boys might be more likely to copy aggressive models because they have been rewarded for aggressive behaviours more than girls have. This would be a nurture argument (后天论点).

🧬 Nature (先天) Arguments:
  • Boys may have a biological predisposition towards aggression (evolutionary psychology perspective)
  • Hormonal differences (testosterone levels) may make boys more aggressive
  • Neurobiological basis: brain regions associated with aggression (e.g., amygdala) may show sex differences
🌱 Nurture (后天) Arguments:
  • Socialisation: boys rewarded more for aggression ("boys will be boys")
  • Girls punished more for aggression ("ladies should behave like ladies")
  • Parents/teachers have different behavioural expectations by sex
  • The study itself supports nurture: children imitated what they observed

Bandura's position: This study primarily supports a nurture (后天) explanation — because children learned entirely novel (新颖的) aggressive responses that could not have been innate. However, Bandura also acknowledged that the degree of sex-typing in behaviour influences imitation tendency (sex-typed behaviour is more readily imitated from same-sex models), indicating that nature and nurture factors interact.

11.2 Sex-typed Behaviour and Same-Sex Modelling

The study revealed an interesting Sex × Model interaction (性别×模型交互作用):

  • For highly sex-typed behaviour (physical aggression): both male and female children imitated the male model more than the female model
  • For less clearly sex-typed behaviour (verbal aggression): the greatest imitation occurred under same-sex model conditions
  • Boys expressed surprise or even disapproval of the female model's aggressive behaviour ("That's not the way for a lady to behave")
  • Girls imitated the female model's verbal aggression more (13.7 vs 2.0), but imitated physical aggression less

This means predictions must consider the degree of sex-typing (性别定型程度) of the behaviour — not all behaviours follow the same pattern of imitation.

11.3 Application to Real Life（现实应用）

The findings suggest practical applications for protecting children, e.g. through film certification (电影分级制度) and the use of parental controls on media devices (媒体设备的家长控制). If children can learn aggression simply by watching a model on TV or in video games, then limiting exposure to violent media becomes an important public health intervention.

At the same time, the findings from the non-aggressive model group have positive implications: demonstrating prosocial (亲社会的) and non-aggressive behaviour can also be imitated by children. This provides a basis for positively applying social learning theory — by showing cooperation, sharing, and non-violent conflict resolution through media, we can help children learn these social skills.

1️⃣2️⃣ Summary（总结）

Bandura et al.'s study used adults being aggressive to a Bobo doll to show that children's behaviour can be affected by the behaviour of a model. Exact aggressive behaviours were imitated although the study showed that non-aggressive modelling was also effective. Children were more likely to copy a same-sex model. Additionally, boys engaged in more aggressive imitation than girls. This was a well-controlled laboratory experiment measuring the dependent variable through objective observations that were reliable. Qualitative data suggested that the children recognised sex-typing (性别定型) and were surprised by behaviour that did not fit the pattern. The findings suggest practical applications for protecting children, e.g. through film certification and the use of parental controls on media devices.

🔑 KEY TAKEAWAY 核心要点

Children learn aggression by observing adult models — even without direct reinforcement of either the model or the observer. This finding challenged behaviourism and gave rise to Social Learning Theory, with profound implications for understanding how media violence, family behaviour, and gender roles shape the next generation.

1️⃣3️⃣ Study Questions（复习题）

Click each question to reveal the model answer. All answers are based on the study content above.

1 Why might Bandura et al. have chosen to record the children's behaviour in 5-second intervals, rather than 1-second or 1-minute intervals? ▶ Answer
Recording behaviour in 5-second intervals was a methodological choice that balanced two considerations:

• Short enough to capture brief behaviours: 1-minute intervals would miss short aggressive acts like hitting, kicking, or verbal outbursts. A 5-second window captures a discrete action before it transitions into something else.

• Long enough to record complete actions: 1-second intervals would be too short to observe a complete behaviour (e.g. raising the mallet + striking + recovering). 5 seconds allows one full discrete behaviour per interval.

• Result: 240 response units per child (20 minutes ÷ 5 seconds = 240 units), giving a precise behavioural record without overwhelming the observer.
2 Consider the data for the mean aggressive behaviours and the non-imitative verbal responses. (a) Which data is qualitative and which is quantitative? ▶ Answer
• Quantitative data (定量数据): The mean aggression scores (e.g. boys + male model = 25.8 in imitative physical aggression) — these are numerical, measurable, and can be subjected to statistical analysis such as chi-square (χ²) and t-tests.

• Qualitative data (定性数据): The non-imitative verbal responses and the children's sex-typed comments (e.g. "That's not the way for a lady to behave", "He's a good fighter like Daddy") — these are descriptive, non-numerical, and provide insight into how children interpreted and made sense of the model's behaviour.

This is an example of mixed methods research, where combining quantitative and qualitative data gives a fuller picture than either alone.
3 The procedure was standardised in many ways. (a) Identify the ways in which the procedure was standardised. (b) Explain why standardisation was necessary — what might have happened if each participant had not been treated in the same way? ▶ Answer
(a) Six ways the procedure was standardised:

1. Same observation time: all children in experimental groups saw the model for ~10 minutes
2. Same model behaviour: every aggressive model performed identical acts (mallet, sit-on-punch, throw-kick) and identical phrases
3. Same room layout: play area + Bobo corner arrangement was identical for every child
4. Same toy order: toys in the test room were always presented in the same sequence
5. Same observation duration: all children were observed for 20 minutes via one-way mirror
6. Same arousal procedure: all children experienced the same toy-frustration procedure

(b) Why standardisation was necessary:

If participants had been treated differently (e.g. one child saw the model for 5 minutes, another for 15 minutes), any differences in aggression could be due to exposure duration rather than the model's behaviour. This would be a confounding variable (混淆变量) — making it impossible to attribute the cause of aggression to the model's type. Standardisation ensures that model type is the only systematic difference between conditions, allowing researchers to confidently claim cause-and-effect (high internal validity) and produce reliable, reproducible results.
4 Explain what is meant by "matched participants design" and how Bandura et al. used it in their study. ▶ Answer
Matched participants design (匹配参与者设计) is a research design where participants are grouped in pairs (or triplets) based on similarity on a key variable, then each member of the pair/triplet is randomly assigned to a different condition.

How Bandura used it: All 72 children were first rated on aggression using four 5-point scales (physical, verbal, inanimate, inhibition). The 51 children rated by both observers had inter-rater reliability r = 0.89. Children were then matched into triplets based on similar aggression levels. One member of each triplet was randomly placed into each of the three conditions (aggressive model, non-aggressive model, control).

Why this matters: This controls for individual differences (个体差异) in baseline aggression. Any differences between conditions at the test phase can therefore be attributed to the IV (model type) rather than pre-existing differences between children. This increases the study's internal validity.
5 Describe the deliberate-arousal (mild frustration) procedure in Bandura's study and explain why it was included. ▶ Answer
The procedure: After observing the model, each child was taken to a separate room and shown attractive toys (e.g. art supplies). They were allowed to play for about 2 minutes, then told the toys were reserved for other children and could not be played with — creating mild frustration (轻度挫折).

Two reasons for including it:

• Ensure baseline aggression motivation: Some children who saw the aggressive model might suppress aggression if they thought they would be punished; mild arousal ensures all children have an internal motivation to express aggression if they have learned it. This allows researchers to measure any reduction in aggressive behaviour.

• Fair comparison across conditions: Without arousal, control and non-aggressive-model children might show very low baseline aggression (no learned aggression to release). With arousal, the floor for aggression is similar across all conditions, so any difference between conditions can be attributed to the model, not baseline differences.
6 Outline one strength and one weakness of Bandura et al.'s study. For each, explain why it is a strength/weakness. ▶ Answer
Strength — Matched pairs design (匹配配对设计):
All 72 children were pre-rated on aggression and matched into triplets based on similar aggression levels, then randomly assigned to one of three conditions. This means individual differences in baseline aggression were controlled for, so any differences between conditions at the test phase can be attributed to the IV (model type) rather than pre-existing participant differences. This increases internal validity.

Weakness — Low ecological validity (生态效度低):
The study was conducted in a laboratory with a Bobo doll specifically designed to be hit. This means the setting does not reflect real-life situations in which children learn aggression (e.g. family environments, playgrounds, exposure to media violence). Children may also behave differently when they know they are being observed (despite the one-way mirror). The findings may therefore not generalise to real-world aggression acquisition.

In this follow-up study, children who saw the model punished for aggression still imitated the behaviour when later given the chance — though they were less likely to perform it in the model's presence. 1️⃣4️⃣ Key Terms（核心术语）

Term 术语Definition 定义
learning approach (学习取向)An approach studying how behaviour is acquired through experience, including association, reinforcement, observation, and imitation.
social learning theory (SLT) (社会学习理论)Theory developed by Bandura proposing that people learn new behaviours by observing and imitating models, with or without direct reinforcement.
observational learning (观察学习)Acquiring a new behaviour by watching a model perform it, without direct reinforcement of the observer.
model (榜样/模型)A person whose behaviour is observed and potentially imitated by another person.
imitation (模仿)Reproducing the behaviour of a model, especially after observation.
aggression (攻击行为)Actions intended to cause physical or psychological harm to another person or object.
generalisation (泛化)In learning theory, the tendency for a behaviour learned in one situation to be performed in different but similar situations.
sex-typed behaviour (性别定型行为)Actions seen in a given society as more appropriate for one sex than the other (e.g. aggression often seen as masculine).
same-sex model (同性模型)A model of the same sex as the observer; typically imitated more strongly.
matched participants (匹配参与者)A research design where participants are paired or grouped by similarity on a key variable before random allocation to conditions.
independent measures (独立测量)Design where different participants are used in each condition of the IV.
inter-rater reliability (评分者间信度)Agreement between two or more observers rating the same behaviour (judged via correlation r; r = 0.89 in this study).
controlled observation (控制观察)Observation of behaviour in a controlled setting with standardised procedures.
one-way mirror (单向镜)A mirror that allows observation from one side without the observed person knowing.
mild frustration / arousal (轻度挫折/唤醒)A deliberate procedure to mildly annoy participants so that any aggression they have learned can be expressed.
Bobo doll (波波娃娃)An inflatable clown-like doll that bounces back when hit; used as the target of aggression in the study.
delayed imitation (延迟模仿)Reproducing observed behaviour some time after the observation has ended and the model is no longer present.
imitative aggression (模仿性攻击)Aggressive acts that precisely match the specific acts performed by the model.
partially imitative aggression (部分模仿性攻击)Aggressive acts that resemble the model's behaviour but are not identical (e.g. hitting objects other than Bobo with the mallet).
extraneous variable (无关变量)A variable other than the IV that may affect the DV; can be random or systematic (a confounding variable).
ecological validity (生态效度)Extent to which findings from a study generalise to real-life settings.
demand characteristics (需求特征)Cues in a study that lead participants to guess the true purpose and change their behaviour accordingly.
sample bias (样本偏差)When the sample is not representative of the population (here: Stanford University Nursery).
quantitative data (定量数据)Numerical data analysable statistically (e.g. aggression scores, t-tests).
qualitative data (定性数据)Non-numerical data: observations, comments, descriptions (e.g. sex-typed remarks).
informed consent (知情同意)Agreeing to participate after receiving information about the study.
debriefing (事后解释)Revealing the true purpose of the study afterwards and addressing any negative effects.
protection from harm (免受伤害)Ethical guideline: participants must not face physical or psychological harm.
right to withdraw (退出权)Freedom to leave the study at any point without penalty.
nurture (后天)Explanation of behaviour in terms of environmental and social influences (such as modelling).
nature (先天)Explanation of behaviour in terms of biological/genetic factors.
prosocial behaviour (亲社会行为)Positive, helpful behaviour that benefits others (e.g. cooperation, sharing).
