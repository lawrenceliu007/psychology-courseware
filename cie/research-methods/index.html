Experiments / 实验

The core method in CIE Psychology — manipulating an IV to measure its effect on a DV.

Learn Mode
1Independent Variable (IV, 自变量)
The Independent Variable (IV) is the variable that the researcher manipulates or changes to observe its effect on the dependent variable. It is called "independent" because its values are not influenced by other variables in the study.
简单理解：IV 就是研究者主动改变的那个东西。比如在睡眠实验中，"睡眠时长（4小时 vs 8小时）"就是IV。

关键词：manipulate（操纵）, change（改变）, researcher controls（研究者控制）
2Dependent Variable (DV, 因变量)
The Dependent Variable (DV) is the variable that the researcher measures or records. It is expected to change as a result of manipulations to the independent variable. It "depends" on the IV.
简单理解：DV 是被测量的结果。比如上面睡眠实验中，"记忆测试分数"就是DV。

关键词：measure（测量）, record（记录）, depends on（取决于）
3Operationalisation / 操作化
Operationalisation is the process of turning an abstract concept into something measurable. It specifies exactly how a variable will be measured or manipulated in a particular study.
例子：
• 抽象概念："攻击性" → 操作化："在10分钟内推打Bobo娃娃的次数"
• 抽象概念："记忆" → 操作化："单词回忆测试的正确率(%)"
• 抽象概念："压力" → 操作化："皮质醇水平(nmol/L)" 或 "自我评分量表(1-10分)"

⚠️ 考试常考："Operationalise the DV" = 把DV写成具体可测量的指标
4Extraneous Variables (EVs, 额外变量)
Extraneous Variables (EVs) are any variables other than the IV that could potentially affect the DV. If not controlled, they become confounding variables and threaten internal validity（内部效度）.
TypeDescriptionExample
Participant variables
(被试变量)		Characteristics differing between participantsAge, gender, intelligence, personality
Situational variables
(情境变量)		Environmental factors during the studyNoise, temperature, time of day, lighting
Experimenter effects
(主试效应)		Researcher's behaviour influencing resultsTone of voice, facial expressions, expectations
Demand characteristics
(需求特征)		Participants guessing aim and changing behaviour"Good participant" effect, social desirability
5Experimental vs Control Condition

🔨 Experimental Condition (实验条件)

The group/participants who receive the IV manipulation — they experience the treatment or change being tested.

📊 Control Condition (控制条件)

The group/participants who do NOT receive the IV manipulation — they provide a baseline for comparison.

为什么需要Control Condition？ 为了排除其他解释。如果两组唯一区别就是IV的有无，那么DV的差异就可以归因于IV。
例：药物实验中，实验组吃真药，对照组吃安慰剂(placebo)。如果只有实验组症状改善→说明药有效。
6Hypotheses / 假设
TypeDefinitionExample
Directional (one-tailed)
方向性假设		Predicts the direction of relationship/difference"Participants who sleep 8 hours will score higher on memory tests than those who sleep 4 hours."
Non-directional (two-tailed)
非方向性假设		Predicts there will be a difference but NOT direction"There will be a difference in memory scores between 8-hour and 4-hour sleep groups."
Null Hypothesis (H&sub0;)
虚无假设		Predicts NO significant difference; any effect due to chance"There will be no difference in memory scores between groups. Any difference is due to chance."
Exam Tip: Use directional when prior research predicts direction. Use non-directional when unsure. Null hypothesis = always about "no effect" or "due to chance". Make sure your hypothesis states BOTH IV and DV clearly!
Common Mistake: Don't confuse directional with "better/worse". Directional just means you predict WHICH way. Also ensure your hypothesis clearly identifies both the IV and DV!
7Placebo / 安慰剂
A placebo is a substance or treatment that has no actual effect. It looks like the real treatment but contains no active ingredient. Participants believe they are receiving the real treatment.
AspectDetails
PurposeUsed in control condition to rule out placebo effect — participants improve simply because they believe they're being treated
Placebo EffectPsychological/physical improvement due to expectation of benefit, NOT the treatment itself
ExamplesSugar pill in drug trials; saline injection; sham acupuncture (needles don't penetrate)
✅ StrengthAllows researchers to isolate the true effect of the IV from psychological expectations
❌ LimitationMay be considered deceptive if participants aren't told they might receive a placebo (ethical issue)
课本原文 (p.9)："A placebo is a substance or treatment that has no actual effect on the dependent variable... The placebo effect occurs when participants improve because they expect to improve."

📋 考试链接：In drug experiments, control group receives placebo. If experimental group improves significantly more than placebo group → drug has real effect beyond placebo effect.
8Controlling Extraneous Variables / 控制额外变量
Control MethodHow It WorksEV Type ControlledExample
Random Allocation
(随机分配)		Participants randomly assigned to conditions (e.g., coin flip, computer randomiser)Participant variablesDrug trial: randomise who gets drug vs placebo
Standardisation
(标准化)		Keeping all procedures identical for every participant (same instructions, timing, environment)Situational variablesAll participants tested in same room, same time of day, same instructions read from script
Counterbalancing
(平衡法)		Half do Condition A then B; other half do B then A (for repeated measures designs)Order effectsABBA order: Group 1 does Task 1→2; Group 2 does Task 2→1
Double-Blind Design
(双盲设计)		Neither participants nor researcher knows who is in which conditionExperimenter effects & demand characteristicsDrug trial: neither doctor nor patient knows who gets real drug
Control of Participant VariablesSelecting participants with similar characteristics, or using matched pairsIndividual differencesOnly recruiting right-handed participants for a motor skill study
Exam Tip: "Suggest how the researcher could control extraneous variables" = pick the relevant method from this table. For participant variables → random allocation. For situational → standardisation. For order effects → counterbalancing. For bias → double-blind.
Topic 1 Quick Recap: An experiment needs a clear IV (what you change), measurable DV (what you measure), control of EVs (via random allocation, standardisation, counterbalancing, double-blind), testable hypothesis, and often a placebo in the control condition. Always operationalise your variables!

Experimental Designs / 实验设计

How participants are allocated to conditions — IM, RM, and MP designs with strengths and limitations.

Learn Mode
1Independent Measures (IM, 被试间设计)
Independent Measures (Between-Subjects design) uses different participants in each condition. Each participant experiences only ONE experimental condition.
✅ Strengths❌ Limitations
No order effects — no practice/boredom across conditionsMore participants needed — larger sample, more expensive
No demand characteristics across conditionsParticipant variables — individual differences may confound results
Data collected quickly if many participants availableLess sensitive — individual differences add noise
如何控制IM的Participant Variables？ 用 Random Allocation（随机分配）— 每个参与者有相等机会被分配到任一条件。
2Repeated Measures (RM, 被试内设计)
Repeated Measures (Within-Subjects design) uses the same participants in ALL conditions. Each person experiences every experimental condition.
✅ Strengths❌ Limitations
Controls participant variables — same people eliminates individual differencesOrder effects — practice/boredom/fatigue affects later performance
Fewer participants needed — economicalDemand characteristics — participants may guess aim after multiple conditions
More statistically sensitive — reduces error varianceTime-related attrition — dropouts before completing all conditions
如何控制RM的Order Effects？ 用 Counterbalancing（平衡法）— 一半先A后B，另一半先B后A。例：ABBA或随机顺序。
3Matched Pairs (MP, 配对设计)
Matched Pairs design uses different participants in each condition, BUT matched on key characteristics (e.g., age, IQ, gender). Each person has a "matched partner" in the other condition.
✅ Strengths❌ Limitations
Reduces participant variables without order effectsDifficult to match perfectly — can't match ALL relevant variables
No order effects — different participants per conditionTime-consuming matching process
Reduces demand characteristics vs RMPairs lost if one drops out — must discard together
匹配什么变量？ 取决于研究内容。记忆→匹配IQ和年龄；运动能力→匹配体能和性别；饮食偏好→匹配BMI和文化背景。
4Laboratory vs Field Experiments
FeatureLab ExperimentField Experiment
SettingArtificial, controlled environmentNatural, real-world setting
EV control✅ High⚠️ Lower
Ecological validity
(生态效度)		❌ Low✅ High
Internal validity
(内部效度)		✅ High⚠️ Moderate
ExampleMilgram (1963) — obedience in labPiliavin (1969) — helping on subway
Exam Tip: When evaluating, always mention BOTH ecological AND internal validity — they often trade off. Lab = high internal, low ecological. Field = lower internal, high ecological.
Don't Mix These Up:
• IM/RM/MP = how participants allocated to conditions (design type)
• Lab/Field = WHERE experiment takes place (setting type)
You can have Lab+IM OR Field+RM — they're independent dimensions!

Observations / 观察法

Watching and recording behaviour without manipulation — overt/covert, participant/non-participant, structured/unstructured, naturalistic/controlled.

Learn Mode
1Overt vs Covert Observation / 公开 vs 隐蔽观察

👁 Overt Observation (公开观察)

The observer's role is obvious to participants — they know they are being watched.
E.g., Researcher visible in classroom with clipboard

🕵 Covert Observation (隐蔽观察)

The observer is hidden or disguised — participants do NOT know they are being observed.
E.g., Researcher joins group as member / uses CCTV / sits on park bench

Covert ✅Covert ❌Overt ✅Overt ❌
No demand characteristicsUnethical (deception, no consent)Ethical — participants can consentDemand characteristics affect behaviour
Natural behaviour observedData collection difficultEasier to record data openlyParticipants may alter behaviour (Hawthorne effect)
Access to private/covert behavioursMay cause distress if revealedObserver can ask questionsObserver effect possible
课本原文 (p.25)："The role played by the observer may be either overt (it is obvious that they are an observer) or covert (they are hidden or disguised so the participants do not know the individual is an observer)."

⚠️ 考试高频："Suggest one advantage of covert over overt observation" = no demand characteristics / more natural behaviour. "Suggest one ethical problem" = deception / lack of informed consent.
2Naturalistic vs Controlled Observation / 自然 vs 控制观察

🌳 Naturalistic (自然观察)

Conducted in participants' normal environment, without interference from researcher. No manipulation of variables.

🔬 Controlled (控制观察)

Researcher manipulates the situation — varies group sizes, adds a model, or controls objects/play environment. Can be in lab or natural setting.

AspectNaturalisticControlled
Ecological validity✅ High — real-world settingLower — artificial elements introduced
Control of variablesLow — many confounding variables possible✅ Higher — researcher controls some factors
ReplicabilityHarder to replicate exactly✅ Easier to standardise and repeat
3Participant vs Non-participant Observation

👤 Participant (参与式观察)

Observer joins the group and participates while recording behaviour.
E.g., Researcher joining a sports team to observe team dynamics

👁 Non-participant (非参与式观察)

Observer does NOT join — watches from outside without involvement.
Watching children play behind one-way mirror

Participant ✅Participant ❌Non-participant ✅Non-participant ❌
Deeper insight into groupMay lose objectivity ("go native")Remains objective/detachedMay miss underlying meanings
Access to covert behavioursOften involves deceptionNo influence on behaviourHawthorne effect if detected
Natural behaviour (forget observer)Hard to record while participatingEasier systematic notesObserver effect possible
4Structured vs Unstructured Observation

📋 Structured (结构化观察)

Uses predetermined behavioural categories. Observer knows what to look for, records systematically.

🔓 Unstructured (非结构化观察)

No predetermined categories. Records everything in narrative form. More exploratory.

AspectStructuredUnstructured
Data quality✅ Reliable, quantitative, replicableRich, detailed, qualitative
Observer biasReduced (clear categories)Higher risk (interpretive)
Best forTesting specific hypothesesExploratory research
AnalysisEasy (count frequencies)Time-consuming (content analysis)
5Behavioural Categories / 行为类别
Behavioural categories are pre-defined, mutually exclusive categories of behaviour used to code observations. Each observed behaviour fits exactly one category.
例子：观察儿童操场行为
🔥 Category A: Aggressive (pushing, hitting, grabbing)
🤝 Category B: Cooperative (sharing, helping, taking turns)
👮 Category C: Solitary play (playing alone)
🔗 Category D: Other

关键原则：Mutually exclusive（互斥）+ Exhaustive（穷尽）
6Observational Sampling / 观察抽样
TypeHow it worksWhen to use
Event Sampling
(事件抽样)		Record every occurrence of target behaviour + contextBehaviour infrequent; want full detail of each instance
Time Sampling
(时间抽样)		Record at set intervals (e.g., every 30s for 15s)Behaviour frequent/continuous; need manageable data
Exam Tip: Event sampling = rich detail but may miss simultaneous events. Time sampling = systematic but may miss behaviours BETWEEN intervals.
Topic 3 Quick Recap: Observations has 4 dimensions — (1) Overt/Covert: is observer visible? (2) Participant/Non-participant: does observer join in? (3) Structured/Unstructured: predetermined categories or not? (4) Naturalistic/Controlled: normal environment or manipulated? Use behavioural categories for structured. Choose event or time sampling based on behaviour frequency.

Self-Report Methods / 自陈法

Asking participants directly — interviews and questionnaires as tools for gathering data.

Learn Mode
1Interviews / 访谈法
AspectStructured (结构化)Semi-structured (半结构化)Unstructured (非结构化)
QuestionsFixed, predetermined questions in set orderFixed list of open AND closed questions; interviewer can add more if neededFlexible, open-ended; follow participant's answers
Data typeQuantitative (easy to compare)Mixed — both quantitative & qualitativeQualitative (rich, detailed responses)
Reliability✅ High — replicableModerate — some consistency but flexibility varies❌ Lower — depends on interviewer/context
ValidityMay miss unexpected info✅ Good balance — covers key areas + allows exploration✅ High — can probe deeply
Training neededMinimalModerate — needs skill to balance structure with flexibilitySignificant — interpersonal skills essential
课本原文 (p.20)："A semi-structured interview: an interview format using a fixed list of open and closed questions. The interviewer can add more questions if necessary."

🔔 三种访谈对比总结：Structured = 量化研究首选；Semi-structured = 最常用，兼顾深度和广度；Unstructured = 探索性研究/个案研究。

Interviewer Bias（访谈者偏差）： Interviewer's tone, body language, or interpretation may influence results。解决方法：standardised instructions + training interviewers + multiple coders。
2Questionnaires / 问卷法
Questionnaires are sets of written questions used to gather information from a large number of participants. They can be administered in person, by post, or online.
✅ Strengths❌ Limitations
Low response rates — people may not return/post backSocial desirability bias — people give "acceptable" answers
No interviewer bias — same questions for everyoneLeading questions — wording may influence responses (e.g., "Don't you agree that...?")
Easy to analyse — especially with closed questionsMisunderstanding questions — no one to clarify
Cost-effective — cheaper than interviewsLacks depth — can't probe or follow up on answers
3Open vs Closed Questions

📝 Open Questions (开放式问题)

Participants answer in their own words. No fixed options.

"What do you think causes stress?"

Pros: Rich data, unexpected insights
Cons: Hard to analyse, time-consuming

📜 Closed Questions (封闭式问题)

Participants choose from fixed response options.

"Rate your stress level: 1=Low, 5=High"

Pros: Easy to analyse, quick to complete
Cons: May miss nuance, forces choices

Best practice: Many questionnaires use a mix of both — closed questions for quantitative data + open questions at the end for qualitative depth ("Any other comments?").
4Key Concepts: Filler Questions & Inter-rater Reliability

📝 Filler Questions (填充题/掩饰题)

Definition: Items put into a questionnaire to disguise the true aim of the study. They are irrelevant distractors placed among the real questions so participants are less likely to work out the aims and alter their behaviour.

Example: In a study about eating habits, include filler questions about "favourite colour" or "mode of transport to school"

Purpose: Reduce demand characteristics → improve validity

🤝 Inter-rater Reliability (评分者间信度)

Definition: The extent to which two or more researchers interpreting qualitative responses in a questionnaire (or interview) will produce the same records from the same raw data.

Why it matters: If different researchers interpret the same answer differently → low reliability → findings may be due to bias, not genuine patterns.

How to improve: Use standardised coding schemes + train raters + check correlation between raters' scores

课本原文 (p.19)："Filler questions: items put into a questionnaire, interview or test to disguise the aim of the study by hiding the important questions among irrelevant ones..."

"Inter-rater reliability: the extent to which two researchers interpreting qualitative responses...will produce the same records from the same raw data"

⚠️ 考试常考："Suggest how Dr Huang could raise inter-rater reliability" → standardised coding categories + train raters + compare ratings + use correlation
5Social Desirability Bias / 社会期望偏差
Social desirability bias occurs when participants try to present themselves in the best possible light by giving answers they think are socially acceptable, rather than honest answers. This threatens the validity of self-report data.
课本原文 (p.19)："trying to present oneself in the best light by determining how to respond to the task in a way that would be most acceptable to other people, rather than to the researcher"

例子：问卷问"Do you cheat on tests?" → 大多数人会回答"No"（即使实际上有作弊），因为作弊不被社会接受。

如何减少？ 匿名问卷（保证保密）+ 填充题（隐藏真实目的）+ 措辞中性化
Topic 4 Quick Recap: Self-reports ask people directly. Interviews = structured/semi-structured/unstructured (3 types!); Questionnaires = fast but shallow. Open = rich data; Closed = easy analysis. Watch out for: social desirability bias, filler questions, inter-rater reliability.

Sampling Methods / 抽样方法

How to select participants — five main techniques with different levels of representativeness and practicality.

Learn Mode
1Five Sampling Techniques Overview
MethodHow it works✅ Strengths❌ Limitations
Random Sampling
(随机抽样)		 Every member of the target population has an equal chance of being selected. Use random number generator or lottery method. ✅ Unbiased — representative of population
✅ Generalisable results		 ❌ Need complete population list
❌ Time-consuming
Opportunity Sampling
(便利抽样)		 Select whoever is available and willing at the time of research. The most commonly used method. ✅ Convenient & quick
✅ No special equipment needed		 ❌ Biased sample — unrepresentative
❌ Limited generalisability
Systematic Sampling
(系统抽样)		 Select every nth person from a list (e.g., every 10th name on a school register). Starting point = random. ✅ More objective than opportunity
✅ Quick once list exists		 ❌ List may have hidden pattern/bias
❌ Still not truly random
Stratified Sampling
(分层抽样)		 Divide population into subgroups (strata), then randomly sample from each stratum in proportion to its size. ✅ Most representative — all subgroups included
✅ Generalisable findings		 ❌ Most time-consuming
❌ Need detailed population data
Volunteer Sampling
(自愿抽样)		 Participants self-select by responding to an advertisement or invitation to take part. ✅ Very convenient
✅ Access to specialised populations		 ❌ Volunteer bias — certain types of people volunteer
❌ Often unrepresentative
Exam Hot Topic: When asked to evaluate sampling, always mention representativeness (does the sample reflect the population?) and generalisability (can findings apply beyond the sample?). Random and stratified = most representative. Opportunity and volunteer = least representative but most practical.
Topic 5 Quick Recap: Random = unbiased but hard; Opportunity = easy but biased; Systematic = middle ground; Stratified = best representation but slowest; Volunteer = convenient but self-selected bias.

Ethics in Research / 研究伦理

Protecting participants — the key ethical principles every psychologist must follow.

Learn Mode
1Core Ethical Principles
PrincipleDefinitionHow to Address It
Informed Consent
(知情同意)		 Participants must be given full information about the study before agreeing to take part, including their right to withdraw. Use consent forms; explain purpose, procedures, risks; obtain signature (or parental consent for under-16s).
Right to Withdraw
(退出权)		 Participants should be free to leave the study at any time without penalty or negative consequences. Explicitly tell participants they can stop; remind them during the study; don't use data if they withdraw.
Protection from Harm
(免受伤害)		 Participants must not experience physical or psychological harm beyond what they'd encounter in daily life. Risk assessment beforehand; debriefing after; screen out vulnerable participants; provide support contacts.
Deception
(欺骗)		 Should avoid deliberately misleading participants about the true aim of the study. Only justified if essential. If deception necessary: use retrospective consent in debrief; justify why no alternative existed; get ethics committee approval.
Confidentiality
(保密性)		 Participants' data must be kept private and anonymous. Only researchers should have access. Use ID numbers instead of names; store data securely; report aggregated data only; comply with data protection laws.
Privacy
(隐私权)		 Participants' right to control information about themselves. Research shouldn't intrude into private spaces without consent. Observe only in public places unless consent given; don't access private records without permission.
2Debriefing / 事后解释
Debriefing takes place after the study is completed. Participants are told the true aim and purpose of the study, given the opportunity to ask questions, and any deception is revealed and justified.
为什么Debriefing很重要？
• Restores participants to their original state (reduces harm)
• Provides retrospective consent after deception
• Allows participants to withdraw their data after learning the truth
• Educational value — participants learn about psychology research
Exam Focus — Ethical Issues in CIE Core Studies:
• Milgram (1963): Deception (fake shocks, confederate), Protection from harm (extreme stress), Right to withdraw (verbally encouraged to continue)
• Piliavin (1969): Informed consent (participants didn't know they were observed), Privacy (observed in public without consent)
• Baron-Cohen et al. (2001): Informed consent obtained, but some participants (especially with autism) may not have fully understood the task demands

When asked to evaluate ethics, always name the specific principle AND explain how it was/wasn't addressed!
3Presumptive Consent / 推定同意
Presumptive consent is when a sample group similar to the participants is asked whether they would agree to take part. If they would, it is presumed that the actual participants would also consent.
AspectDetails
When usedWhen full informed consent is impossible — e.g., covert observations, naturalistic studies where telling participants would change their behaviour
How it worksAsk a similar group of people if they'd agree to participate → If majority say yes → presume actual participants would also agree
✅ StrengthAllows ethically sensitive research to proceed where consent would otherwise be impossible
❌ LimitationThe similar group may not actually represent the real participants' views — presumption may be wrong
CIE ExampleIn a covert observation of classroom behaviour, ask a different class if they'd agree to be observed → use this as presumptive consent
课本原文 (p.51)："Presumptive consent involves asking a group of people who are similar to the participants whether they would agree to take part in the research. If they would, then it is presumed that the actual participants would also agree."

📋 考试链接：Often used as an alternative to deception or when studying behaviour in natural settings. May be asked alongside "Explain one way the researcher could gain ethical approval for this study."
4Ethical Issues in Animal Research / 动物研究伦理
PrincipleWhat It MeansExample from CIE Studies
Justification
(正当性)		Research must have a clear scientific purpose that could benefit humans or animals. Benefits must outweigh costs.Hassett et al. (2008): Studying toy preferences in monkeys to understand evolutionary origins of human gender differences
Minimising Harm
(最小伤害)		Use the minimum number of animals necessary; cause the least suffering; provide appropriate living conditions.Fagen et al. (2014): Elephants trained using positive reinforcement (reward-based) rather than punishment
Licensing & Regulation
(许可与监管)		Must be approved by an ethics committee; follow legal guidelines (e.g., ASPA in UK); regular inspections.All university animal research requires Home Office licence (UK) or equivalent oversight
Replacement (3Rs)
(替代原则)		Wherever possible, use non-animal alternatives (computer models, cell cultures, human volunteers).Some learning research now uses virtual simulations instead of live animals
课本原文 (p.52)："The British Psychological Society (BPS) states that research involving animals should only take place when there is no reasonable alternative... The potential benefits should outweigh any harm or suffering caused to the animals."

CIE Core Studies with Animals: Hassett (monkeys), Fagen (elephants). When evaluating animal studies, always consider: Was it justified? Could non-animal methods be used? Were animals treated humanely?
Topic 6 Quick Recap: Six key principles: Consent, Withdrawal, Protection from Harm, Deception (avoid!), Confidentiality, Privacy. Always debrief. For covert/naturalistic studies, use presumptive consent. For animal studies, follow BPS guidelines: justify, minimise harm, license, replace where possible. Link specific studies to specific principles!

Data Analysis / 数据分析

Making sense of numbers — measures of central tendency, dispersion, correlations, and distributions.

Learn Mode
1Types of Data / 数据类型
AspectQuantitative Data (量化数据)Qualitative Data (质性数据)
DefinitionNumerical results — indicates amount or quantity of a measure (e.g., pulse rate, test score, frequency)Detailed, in-depth results — indicates quality of a characteristic (e.g., written answers, interview transcripts, observations)
ExamplesReaction time in seconds; IQ score; number of aggressive acts; rating on 1–5 scaleOpen-ended questionnaire responses; interview transcript; detailed observation notes; case study narrative
✅ StrengthsObjective measures; highly reliable; easy to analyse with statistics (mean, SD, correlations)High validity — participants express themselves exactly; rich, detailed; captures unusual responses
❌ LimitationsData collection may limit responses; less valid if participant can't give true response; lacks depthSubjective — findings may be influenced by researcher's opinions/biases; time-consuming to analyse; hard to generalise from few individuals
Analysis methodMeasures of central tendency & spread; inferential statistics; graphs (bar charts, histograms, scatter graphs)Content analysis; thematic analysis; coding into categories; looking for patterns
课本原文 (p.40) Table 1.4："Quantitative data: numerical results about the amount or quantity... Qualitative data: descriptive, in-depth results indicating the quality..."

📋 CIE考试要点："Explain one strength and one limitation of using quantitative/qualitative data" 是常见考题。Quantitative = reliable but shallow; Qualitative = valid but subjective.
2Displaying Data: Graphs / 图表展示
Graph TypeWhen to UseKey Features

(柱状图)		Discrete categories (separate groups); comparing totals/averages across named categoriesGaps between bars (categories not continuous); x-axis = distinct groups; y-axis = value/frequency

⚠️ 常见错误：Bar chart 有间隙（离散类别），Histogram 无间隙（连续数据）。考试可能让你选哪种图来展示某组数据！
3Measures of Central Tendency / 集中趋势
MeasureHow to CalculateWhen to UseLimitations
Mean (平均数) Add all values ÷ count (Σx / n) Data is interval/ratio; roughly normal distribution; no extreme outliers ❌ Affected by extreme scores (outliers)
❌ May not be an actual value in dataset
Median (中位数) Middle value when data arranged in order Data has extreme outliers; skewed distribution; ordinal data ❌ Doesn't use all data values
❌ Less sensitive to small changes
Mode (众数) Most frequently occurring value(s) Nominal data (categories); finding most common response; bimodal distributions ❌ May not exist (no repeated values)
❌ Can be multiple modes (bimodal)
例子：数据集 [2, 3, 5, 7, 100]
• Mean = 23.4 （被100严重拉高，不具代表性）
• Median = 5 （不受极端值影响，更合理）
• Mode = 无（没有重复值）

⚠️ 有outlier时用Median！
4Measures of Dispersion / 离散程度
MeasureDefinition & CalculationWhen to Use
Range (全距) Highest value minus lowest value. Range = Max - Min Quick overview of spread; easy to calculate
Standard Deviation (SD)
(标准差)		 A measure of how spread out the data is from the mean. Calculates average distance of each score from the mean. Normal distribution; needs precise measure of spread; used in inferential statistics
Range的问题： 只看最大和最小值，完全忽略中间数据的分布。一个极端outlier就会让Range变得很大。

SD的优势： 考虑了每一个数据点与mean的距离。SD小→ 数据集中在mean附近；SD大→ 数据分散。

CIE常考： 比较两组数据时，如果Mean相似但SD不同→ 说明分布模式不同！
5Correlations / 相关关系
Correlation measures the strength and direction of a relationship between two variables (called co-variables). It does NOT establish cause-and-effect. Represented by correlation coefficient r (ranges from -1 to +1).
Coefficient (r)MeaningExample
+1.0 to +0.7Strong positive correlation — as A increases, B increasesHeight and weight (+0.8)
+0.7 to +0.3Weak/moderate positive — general trend upwardSleep hours and test scores (+0.5)
+0.3 to -0.3Little/no correlation — no clear relationshipShoe size and IQ (+0.02)
-0.3 to -0.7Weak/moderate negative — general trend downwardOutdoor time and myopia (-0.4)
-0.7 to -1.0Strong negative correlation — as A increases, B decreasesDistance from city and air quality (-0.85)
⚠️ Critical Exam Point: Correlation ≠ Causation!
Just because two variables are correlated does NOT mean one CAUSES the other. There could be:
• A third variable causing both (confounding variable)
•> Reverse causality (B might cause A instead)
• Coincidence

To prove causation, you need an experiment (manipulate IV, control EVs)!
6Normal Distribution Curve / 正态分布
Normal Distribution Bell Curve
PropertyDescription
SymmetricalLeft half = mirror of right half; mean = median = mode (all at centre)
Bell-shaped curveMost scores cluster around the mean; fewer scores at extremes
Fixed proportions±1 SD = 68.2%; ±2 SD = 95.4%; ±3 SD = 99.7% of data
Asymptotic tailsThe curve never quite touches the x-axis (theoretical extreme values possible but increasingly rare)
Topic 7 Quick Recap: Data types: Quantitative = numerical, reliable; Qualitative = detailed, valid. Display data with bar charts (categorical), histograms (continuous), scatter graphs (correlations). Central tendency = where data clusters (Mean/Median/Mode). Dispersion = how spread out (Range/SD). Correlation = relationship strength (-1 to +1), NOT causation! Normal distribution = bell curve, 68-95-99.7 rule.

Research Design / 研究设计(核心)

Choosing the right approach — case studies, pilot studies, and how to decide which method to use.

Learn Mode
1Case Studies / 个案研究
A Case Study is an in-depth investigation of a single individual, group, or event using multiple research methods (interviews, observations, document analysis). It provides rich, detailed qualitative data.
✅ Strengths❌ Limitations
Rich, detailed data — deep insight not possible with large samplesNot generalisable — findings from one case may not apply to others
Studies rare/unique phenomena — e.g., brain-damaged patients like H.M. or Clive WearingResearcher bias — subjective interpretation of qualitative data
May challenge existing theories — single counterexample can refute claimsTime-consuming — months or years of data collection
High ecological validity — studied in natural contextEthical issues — privacy concerns when studying one person deeply
Famous Psychology Case Studies:
• H.M. (Henry Molaison) — memory loss after brain surgery, studied for decades
• Little Albert — Watson & Rayner's conditioned fear study (ethical issues!)
• Genie (feral child) — language development after extreme isolation
2Pilot Studies / 预实验
A Pilot Study is a small-scale trial run conducted before the main study. It tests whether the research design, procedures, and materials work as intended, allowing refinements before full data collection.
PurposeWhat it Checks
Procedure clarityAre instructions clear? Do participants understand what to do?
TimingHow long does each part take? Is it too long/short?
MaterialsDo questionnaires work? Are behavioural categories appropriate?
Equipment/techDoes recording equipment work? Software glitches?
Participant reactionsAny confusion? Unexpected behaviours? Ethical concerns emerging?
Exam Tip: If asked "why would a researcher conduct a pilot study?", always mention: saves time/money in long run, identifies problems early, helps refine procedures, checks materials work, and ensures the main study will produce usable data.
3How to Choose a Research Method / 决策指南
Choose Experiment when...Choose Observation when...Choose Self-report when...
You want to establish cause and effect You want to study natural behaviour in real settings You need participants' own perspectives (thoughts, feelings)
You can manipulate an IV ethically Behaviour can be observed directly The topic is subjective/internal (attitudes, opinions)
You need high internal validity You want high ecological validity You need data from large samples quickly
You can control extraneous variables Manipulation would be unethical/impractical Behaviour is sensitive/private (people won't do it if watched)
Exam 12-Mark Question Style: "Design a study to investigate [topic]" — You'll need to choose AND justify your method. Always state: What method? Why this method (not alternatives)? How will you operationalise variables? What design (IM/RM/MP)? What sampling? How address ethics?
4Longitudinal Studies / 纵向研究
A Longitudinal Study is a research method where the same participants are studied over an extended period of time (months or years). It tracks changes or development within individuals across time.
AspectDetails
Key featureSame participants tested repeatedly over time (unlike a snapshot cross-sectional study)
TimeframeCan range from several months to decades (e.g., studying children into adulthood)
✅ Strengths• Shows development and change over time — captures patterns no other method can
• No individual differences problem (same people throughout)
• Can establish sequence of events (what leads to what)
❌ Limitations• Attrition (participant dropout) — people leave the study over time, reducing sample size
• Very time-consuming and expensive
• Cohort effects — the generation may differ from future generations (findings may not generalise)
课本原文 (p.30)："Longitudinal studies involve studying the same group of participants over an extended period of time... They can show how behaviour changes as people age."

vs Cross-Sectional: Cross-sectional = different age groups tested at ONE point in time (quick but cohort effects). Longitudinal = SAME people tracked over TIME (slow but shows real change).

📋 考试链接："Suggest one strength and one limitation of using a longitudinal design" = Strength: shows development/change. Limitation: attrition/dropout.
5Reliability & Validity / 信度与效度

🔍 Reliability（信度）= Consistency（一致性）

TypeDefinitionHow to Assess/Improve
Test-Retest Reliability
(重测信度)		Whether a test produces consistent results when repeated on the same participants under similar conditionsGive the same test twice (e.g., 2 weeks apart) → calculate correlation between scores. High correlation = high reliability.
Inter-Rater Reliability
(评分者间信度)		Whether different observers/raters agree in their observations or scoring of the same behaviourTrain multiple raters using standardised behavioural categories → compare ratings. High agreement = high reliability.

📋 Validity（效度）= Accuracy（准确性）

TypeDefinitionExample
Internal Validity
(内部效度)		Whether the study actually measures what it claims to measure, free from confounding variablesIn a memory experiment: did sleep deprivation actually cause lower recall, or was it fatigue/stress?
External Validity
(外部效度)		Whether findings can be generalised beyond the study — to other people, settings, and timesCan lab findings about obedience apply to real-world authority situations?
Ecological Validity
(生态效度)		A type of external validity — whether findings apply to real-life settings (not just artificial lab conditions)A memory test in a lab (low ecological validity) vs. testing memory in participants' homes (high ecological validity)
Population Validity
(群体效度)		Whether findings can be generalised to other groups of people beyond the sample usedIf only male students were tested, can results apply to females? To older adults?
课本原文 (p.54-56)："Reliability refers to the consistency of a measurement... Validity refers to whether something measures what it is supposed to measure."

🔒 Key Distinction: Reliability ≠ Validity! A test can be reliable (consistent) but NOT valid (not measuring the right thing). Example: A broken weighing scale that always shows 5kg too much is reliable (consistent) but not valid (inaccurate).
6Generalisability / 可推广性
Generalisability (or Generalizability) refers to the extent to which research findings from a sample can be applied to the wider population, and whether results from one setting/time group can be applied to other settings/times/groups.
Factor Affecting GeneralisabilityHow It Limits GeneralisabilityExample
Sampling Bias
(抽样偏差)		If sample is not representative of target population, findings won't generaliseUsing only university students (WEIRD samples) — can't generalise to whole population
Artificial Setting
(人工环境)		Lab conditions are controlled but unrealistic — behaviour may differ in real lifeMilgram's Yale laboratory vs. real obedience situations in daily life
Cultural/Historical Context
(文化/历史背景)		Findings from one culture or era may not apply elsewhereObedience rates in 1960s America may differ from 2020s China
Sample Size
(样本量)		Very small samples (e.g., case studies) lack statistical generalisabilityH.M.'s memory deficits — fascinating but can't generalise to all amnesia patients
Exam Tip: When asked "Can the findings be generalised?", consider: Was the sample representative? Was it a naturalistic or lab setting? Is the culture/time period relevant? For CIE Core Studies: Milgram (limited by era/culture), Baron-Cohen (adult Western sample), Bandura (children only, lab setting).
Topic 8 Quick Recap: Case studies = deep but not generalisable. Pilot studies = test run before main study. Longitudinal studies track same people over time (shows change but attrition risk). Reliability = consistency (test-retest, inter-rater). Validity = accuracy (internal, external, ecological, population). Generalisability depends on sampling, setting, culture, and sample size. Choose method based on goal, topic nature, practicality.

Topic 1: Experiments (Variables & Hypotheses)

Outline The independent variable and dependent variable in this study. [4 marks]
"A researcher investigates whether background noise affects concentration. She recruits 30 students and asks half to complete a puzzle in a silent room, while the other half complete it in a room with loud music playing. She records how many minutes each student takes to finish."
✏️ Auto-saved as you type
Full Marks (4/4) Answer:
IV (2 marks): The independent variable is the presence or absence of background noise (specifically, silent room vs room with loud music). This is what the researcher manipulates. [1] The IV has two conditions: silence (control) and loud music (experimental). [1]

DV (2 marks): The dependent variable is the time taken (in minutes) to complete the puzzle. [1] This is what the researcher measures/records to see if it changes due to the IV. [1]
Partial Marks (2/4) Common Answer:
"IV is the music and DV is the time." → Only gets 1 mark for IV (vague, not operationalised) and 1 for DV (no detail). Must say what exactly is manipulated/measured!
Suggest a suitable hypothesis (directional or non-directional) for this study. [2 marks]
✏️ Auto-saved as you type
Acceptable Answers (either):
Directional: "Participants in the loud music condition will take longer to complete the puzzle than participants in the silent condition."

Non-directional: "There will be a difference in puzzle completion time between participants in the loud music condition and those in the silent condition."

Topic 2: Experimental Designs

Evaluate the use of a Repeated Measures design in a memory experiment. [6 marks]
✏️ Auto-saved as you type
Model Answer (6 marks):
Strengths (3 marks):
• Controls participant variables because the same participants are used in both conditions, eliminating individual differences in memory ability. [1]
• Fewer participants needed, making it more economical and practical when recruiting is difficult. [1]
• More statistically sensitive as error variance from individual differences is reduced. [1]

Limitations (3 marks):
• Order effects such as practice (participants perform better on second condition due to practice) or boredom may affect results. [1]
• Demand characteristics — participants may guess the aim after completing both conditions and change behaviour accordingly. [1]
• Counterbalancing can reduce but not eliminate order effects entirely. [1]
Common Mistake (loses 2 marks):
Only giving strengths OR only giving limitations. Evaluation questions require BOTH sides for full marks. Also, not mentioning how order effects could be addressed (counterbalancing).

Topic 3-4: Observations & Self-Reports

Outline one difference between participant and non-participant observation. [2 marks]
✏️ Auto-saved as you type
Model Answer (2 marks):
In participant observation, the observer joins the group being studied and participates in their activities while recording behaviour. [1] In contrast, in non-participant observation, the observer does not join the group and watches from outside without getting involved. [1]
Explain why social desirability bias might be a problem in questionnaire research. [4 marks]
✏️ Auto-saved as you type
Model Answer (4 marks):
Social desirability bias occurs when participants give answers that make them look good or socially acceptable rather than honest answers. [1] In questionnaires, there is no interviewer present to encourage honesty or probe deeper. [1] For example, if asked about healthy eating habits, participants may overreport fruit/vegetable consumption because they know they "should" eat healthily. [1] This reduces the validity of the data collected, as responses don't reflect true behaviour. [1]

Topic 5-6: Sampling & Ethics

Suggest one limitation of using volunteer sampling in Milgram's obedience study. [2 marks]
✏️ Auto-saved as you type
Model Answer (2 marks):
Volunteer sampling may lead to volunteer bias — the type of person who volunteers for a study on obedience may differ from the general population. [1] Volunteers tend to be more obedient or more willing to please authority figures, meaning the results cannot be generalised to the wider population. [1]
Evaluate the ethics of Milgram's (1963) study of obedience. [6 marks]
✏️ Auto-saved as you type
Model Answer (6 marks):
Ethical Issues (3 marks):
• Deception: Participants were told the study was about "learning and memory", not obedience. The "learner" was actually a confederate, and shocks were not real. [1]
• Protection from harm: Many participants showed extreme stress (sweating, trembling, some had seizures). They believed they were genuinely harming someone. [1]
• Right to withdraw: Although told they could leave, the experimenter used verbal prods ("Please continue") that made it difficult for participants to exercise this right. [1]

Justifications (3 marks):
• Thorough debriefing was provided afterwards, explaining the true aim and ensuring participants left in the same state they arrived. [1]
• Follow-up studies showed no long-term harm — 84% of participants reported being "glad" to have participated. [1]
• The scientific value of the findings about obedience justified the temporary distress, as it helps us understand real-world events like the Holocaust. [1]

Topic 7: Data Analysis

Describe the relationship shown by a correlation coefficient of r = -0.82 between hours spent gaming and exam grades. [3 marks]
✏️ Auto-saved as you type
Model Answer (3 marks):
There is a strong negative correlation (r = -0.82) between hours spent gaming and exam grades. [1] This means that as the number of hours spent gaming increases, exam grades tend to decrease. [1] The strength is strong because the coefficient is close to -1.0, indicating a consistent relationship. [1] However, we cannot conclude that gaming causes lower grades — there may be confounding variables (e.g., less study time, poorer time management). [Bonus point if mentioned]

📖 Dement & Kleitman (1957) — Sleep & Dreams | 8 questions across papers

Explain why the uncontrolled variable of daydreaming was a problem in this study. [4 marks] — S22 P21
✏️ Auto-saved as you type
Model Answer (4 marks):
Daydreaming is an uncontrolled variable because participants might experience waking mental activity that could be mistaken for dreaming during REM sleep. [1] If participants were daydreaming when awakened from REM, they might report a dream when they had actually been daydreaming, or vice versa. [1] This threatens the internal validity of the study because we cannot be sure whether reports truly reflect dream content or waking thoughts. [1] The researchers tried to control this by asking participants about their mental state, but it is difficult to distinguish subjectively between daydreaming and genuine dreams. [1]
Suggest two problems with collecting data about the number of hours of sleep in this study. [4 marks] — S25 P23
✏️ Auto-saved as you type
Acceptable Answers (any two × 2 marks):
• Social desirability bias: Participants may overestimate or underestimate their sleep hours to appear more disciplined or to gain sympathy. They may not accurately remember how long they slept. [2]

• Memory distortion: Asking participants to recall how many hours they slept last night relies on retrospective memory, which may be inaccurate — especially if they fell asleep gradually or woke up multiple times. [2]

• Subjective interpretation: Different participants may define "sleep" differently (e.g., does lying in bed count? Does dozing off briefly count?). [2]

• Honesty issues: Participants may lie about sleep duration if they feel judged about their sleep habits. [2]
Suggest why using one other technique to measure REM sleep duration would be more valid than EEG. [4 marks] — S22 P21
✏️ Auto-saved as you type
Model Answer (4 marks):
Using video recording with behavioural observation alongside EEG would increase validity because: [1] It allows researchers to cross-check physiological REM indicators (rapid eye movements on EEG) with observable behaviours such as eye movements under closed eyelids or muscle twitches. [1] This triangulation of methods increases confidence that REM is being accurately detected. [1] EEG alone could potentially pick up artefacts from other sources (e.g., electrode displacement), so having a second measure reduces this risk. [1]

👀 Baron-Cohen et al. (2001) — Eyes Test | 2 questions across papers

Outline one problem with the ecological validity of the revised eyes test. [2 marks] — W24 P21
✏️ Auto-saved as you type
Model Answer (2 marks):
The eyes test uses static photographs of eyes only, which is not how we read emotions in real life. [1] In real social situations, we see the whole face (including mouth, eyebrows), body language, and movement — and we have contextual information about the situation. Looking at just the eyes in a photo is artificial and may not reflect real-world emotion recognition ability. [1]
Explain how solving one of these problems improved the validity of the eyes test. [4 marks] — S22 P22
✏️ Auto-saved as you type
Model Answer (4 marks):
One problem was that the original test used only adult faces. Baron-Cohen solved this by creating a child-friendly version (the Children's Eyes Test) with photos of children's eyes, suitable for younger participants. [1-2] This improved validity because children may find it easier to recognise emotions in other children's eyes than in adults', giving a more accurate measure of their true mind-reading ability. [1-2] Alternatively: The revised test used normalised difficulty (equal numbers of male/female eyes, balanced emotional valence) to prevent gender bias, improving construct validity.
