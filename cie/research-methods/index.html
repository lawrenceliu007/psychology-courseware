Experiments / 实验法（实验室实验 · 实地实验）

大纲 3.1.2 Research methods — Experiments：只考 laboratory 与 field 两种实验，要求能描述、评价并应用到新情境。

1Laboratory Experiment / 实验室实验
A laboratory experiment is conducted in a controlled environment (a lab, or a room specially set up by the researcher). The researcher manipulates the IV and measures the DV while keeping other variables as controlled/standardised as possible.
In plain words （简单理解）: the experiment is done in a place the researcher can control — the IV is manipulated, the DV is measured, and extraneous variables are kept as low as possible.

Core-study examples （核心研究例子）: Andrade (2010) tested doodling vs not doodling in a quiet, boring room (IV = doodle / no doodle; DV = the number of names and places correctly recalled; every participant heard the same 2.5-minute dull audio recording); Baron-Cohen (2001) gave the 36-photo Eyes Test (forced choice) in a quiet university room; Pozzulo (2011) used a lab room where participants watched a video and then made identifications from photoarrays on a laptop. ⚠️ Milgram (1963) took place in a Yale laboratory, but the main study had no IV — strictly it is a "controlled observation in a laboratory setting"; see the warning box below.
2Field Experiment / 实地（现场）实验
A field experiment is an experiment carried out in a real-life / natural setting where participants would normally behave that way. The researcher still manipulates the IV and measures the DV, but has less control over extraneous variables.
In plain words （简单理解）: this is still "doing an experiment" (the IV is deliberately manipulated), but it is moved into a real-life setting, and the participants usually do not know they are being studied.

Core-study example （核心研究例子）: Piliavin et al. (1969) — on a New York subway carriage the researchers staged a "victim collapsing" (IV = victim carrying a cane vs appearing drunk); the DV was whether anyone helped, the speed (latency) of the first helper, and the number of helpers.

⚠️ High-frequency confusion （高频混淆点）: Piliavin is a field experiment (because the IV was manipulated), NOT a naturalistic observation. The deciding question is always: was the IV manipulated?
3评价：实验室实验 vs 实地实验
CriterionLaboratory Experiment — Strengths (write these sentences)
Control"A laboratory experiment takes place in a highly controlled environment, so extraneous variables can be kept constant, meaning the researcher can be more confident that the IV caused the change in the DV."
Reliability"Because the procedure can be fully standardised (same room, same instructions, same materials), the study can be easily repeated to check its reliability. For example, in Milgram (1963) every participant heard the same four fixed 'prods', and in Andrade (2010) every participant heard exactly the same 2.5-minute audio recording."
Accuracy"Measurements can be taken with precise equipment in a laboratory. For example, Dement and Kleitman (1957) used EEG and EOG machines to record exactly when each participant entered REM or non-REM sleep."
Ethics"In a laboratory it is easier to obtain informed consent before the study and to give a full debrief afterwards, because participants know they are taking part in research."
CriterionLaboratory Experiment — Weaknesses (write these sentences)
Ecological validity"The setting is artificial, so participants may not behave as they would in real life, lowering the ecological validity of the findings."
Demand characteristics"Participants know they are being studied, so they may guess the aim of the study and change their behaviour accordingly (demand characteristics). For example, in Bandura (1961) the children may have thought hitting the Bobo doll was what the adults wanted."
Sampling"Laboratory samples are often small and unrepresentative (e.g. university student volunteers), so the results may not generalise to the wider population."
Ethics"Laboratory tasks can be boring or stressful: in Milgram (1963) many participants showed extreme tension (sweating, trembling, stuttering) because they believed they were giving real electric shocks."
CriterionField Experiment — Strengths (write these sentences)
Ecological validity"The study takes place in a real-life setting where people would normally behave that way, so behaviour is more natural and ecological validity is higher."
Demand characteristics"Participants usually do not know they are in a study, so there are no demand characteristics. For example, in Piliavin et al. (1969) the passengers on the New York subway were ordinary commuters who had no idea an emergency was staged."
Sampling"Field experiments can use large numbers of real people in their everyday environment. For example, about 4,450 men and women rode through the critical area in Piliavin et al. (1969)."
CriterionField Experiment — Weaknesses (write these sentences)
Control"The researcher has much less control over extraneous variables, so it is harder to be certain that the IV caused the change in the DV."
Replicability"It is difficult to standardise and repeat a field experiment exactly, because the real-life setting cannot be reproduced. For example, Piliavin et al. (1969) had to stop the study early because of a strike, and could not control who was on the train."
Ethics"Participants in a field experiment usually cannot give informed consent, raising issues of deception and privacy. In Piliavin et al. (1969) the passengers were never told they had been in a study and were never debriefed."
Recording"Behaviour must be recorded quickly by observers on the spot, so data may be less accurate or incomplete compared with laboratory equipment."
Exam sentence pattern (CIE's two-layer marking) （考场句式 · CIE 评分的两层结构）: first give the conceptual layer ("A field experiment has higher ecological validity because…"), then give the link layer with a study-specific detail (e.g. "in Piliavin et al. (1969) the setting was a real New York subway carriage, so the passengers' helping behaviour was natural"). 只有概念没有链接通常只得 1 分——上面每个引号句都可直接抄写，再补一句 study-specific 细节即达 L3。
4实验组与对照组 / Experimental & Control Group
The experimental group receives the IV (the treatment / condition being tested). The control group does not receive the IV (or receives a placebo / the 'normal' condition). Comparing them shows whether the IV caused a change in the DV.
Examples （例子）: in Bandura et al. (1961) the 24 children in the control group saw no model at all, giving a baseline of "spontaneous aggression"; Hölzel et al. (2011) used a wait-list control group (people not yet doing mindfulness) to compare grey-matter change. 对照组的作用是排除"不干预也会发生的变化"（the control group rules out changes that would have happened anyway）。
🛡️ Syllabus note — a classic exam trap （大纲特别注明 · 常考陷阱）: in the main study, Milgram (1963) had no IV — all 40 participants experienced the same situation, obedience was never manipulated into different conditions, and obedience was the DV. The syllabus quote: obedience was "described as a 'dependent variable' but there was no independent variable in the main study." So when you discuss Milgram, do not describe it as having an IV/DV design such as independent measures — the study used observation and interview as its data-collection techniques. （因此讨论 Milgram 时，不能说它的设计是 independent measures 之类；它用 observation 与 interview 作为技术。）
512 Core Studies — the two-point rule: Setting × Task
To judge whether a study is a laboratory experiment or a field experiment, look at two things: is the SETTING artificial (a controlled environment set up by the researcher) or natural (a real-life place where this behaviour normally happens), and is the TASK artificial (invented for the study) or natural (part of everyday life)? An artificial setting and/or artificial task means it is a laboratory study; a natural setting with normal behaviour means it is a field study. Remember also that a study must manipulate an IV to count as an experiment at all.

Aims, Hypotheses & Variables / 目的、假设与变量

1Aim / 研究目的
An aim is a general statement of what the researcher intends to investigate — the purpose of the study, written in plain, non-numerical language.
Examples from the core studies （真实核心研究例子）: Andrade — "explored the idea that doodling can assist a person's concentration and memory"; Bandura — investigated "whether a child would imitate aggressive behaviour if they witnessed such behaviour in an adult"; Hölzel — investigated whether the MBSR mindfulness programme changes grey-matter density. Hölzel：检验正念减压训练是否改变脑灰质密度。

Sentence templates （写法套路）: "To investigate whether [IV] affects [DV]" or "To explore the relationship between [variable 1] and [variable 2]."
Bandura, Ross & Ross (1961)
"To investigate whether children who observed an adult model behaving aggressively would imitate that aggressive behaviour, and whether imitation depended on the sex of the model and of the child."
Milgram (1963)
"To investigate how far ordinary people would go in obeying an authority figure who instructed them to administer increasingly severe electric shocks to another person."
Dement & Kleitman (1957)
"To investigate the relationship between REM sleep and dreaming — whether dreaming occurs during REM, whether the length of a dream relates to the length of the REM period, and whether the pattern of rapid eye movements relates to the content of the dream."
Perry et al. (2015)
"To test the differential effect of the social hormone oxytocin on preferred interpersonal distance in relation to a person's level of empathy."
Pozzulo et al. (2011)
"To investigate whether children differ from adults in the accuracy and confidence of their identifications from target-present and target-absent photoarrays, and whether this depends on the type of lineup (sequential vs simultaneous)."
Hassett et al. (2008)
"To investigate whether sex differences in toy preference — favouring 'boy-typical' wheeled toys over 'girl-typical' plush toys — exist in non-human primates (rhesus monkeys), suggesting a biological rather than purely social origin."
Baron-Cohen et al. (2001)
"To test a revised, more adult version of the 'Reading the Mind in the Eyes' Test as an advanced theory-of-mind task, and to find out whether adults with autism spectrum conditions are impaired on it compared with typical adults."
Andrade (2010)
"To test the idea that a simple task like doodling can improve concentration and memory for a boring message, by reducing daydreaming."
Fagen et al. (2014)
"To investigate whether juvenile elephants at a tourist camp in Nepal could learn a novel foraging task (pulling a chain to reach food) by observing unrelated adult elephants — i.e. social learning in a naturalistic setting."
Piliavin et al. (1969)
"To investigate helping behaviour in a natural setting — specifically the effect of the type of victim (appearing ill vs appearing drunk) and the effect of the number of bystanders (diffusion of responsibility) on subway passengers' helping."
Hölzel et al. (2011)
"To investigate whether participation in an eight-week Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction (MBSR) programme would increase grey matter concentration in brain regions associated with learning, memory, emotion regulation and perspective-taking."
Saavedra & Silverman (2002)
"To describe and treat a specific phobia of buttons in a six-year-old boy, and to examine the role of disgust in the origin and maintenance of the phobia, using a detailed case study."
2Alternative Hypothesis / 备择（研究）假设
An alternative (experimental) hypothesis predicts that the IV will have an effect on the DV — that there will be a difference or a relationship. It comes in two forms: directional (one-tailed) and non-directional (two-tailed).
3Directional vs Non-directional / 方向性 vs 非方向性
A directional (one-tailed) hypothesis states which way the results will go (e.g. "will be better"). A non-directional (two-tailed) hypothesis only states that there will be a difference, without saying which direction.
Write them as a matching set (use these templates) （成对写法 · 套用格式）:
• Directional: 'Participants who doodle will recall more names than participants who do not doodle.'
• Non-directional: 'There will be a difference in the number of names recalled between participants who doodle and those who do not.'
• Null: 'There will be no difference in the number of names recalled between the two groups.'

⚠️ Judgement trap (tested in 2025) （判断陷阱 · 2025 真题考过）: a hypothesis that "intelligence affects obedience" states no direction → non-directional (June 2025 P23 Q4: writing only the label earns nothing — you must justify that "it only says the levels of the DV will differ"); a hypothesis that "attention will be better when eating" → directional (Oct/Nov 2025 P23 Q2). Seeing comparison words such as better / more / fewer / longer = directional.
4Null Hypothesis / 零假设
The null hypothesis states that the IV will have no effect — any apparent difference or relationship in the data is due to chance. Researchers collect data to decide whether the null hypothesis can be rejected.
In plain words （简单理解）: the null hypothesis = "nothing happened" — any apparent difference or relationship is just chance. Statistical testing is about deciding whether this null can be rejected. 零假设 = "什么都没发生"；统计检验做的就是"能不能否掉它"。 But CIE does not require you to calculate or interpret statistical tests (syllabus p.23 Note) — you only need to write and recognise the three types of hypothesis.
5Independent Variable (IV, 自变量)
The independent variable (IV) is the variable the researcher manipulates / changes (e.g. two or more conditions) to see whether it affects the DV.
Quick IV list from the core studies （核心研究 IV 清单 · 完整英文句见下方折叠面板）: Bandura the model's aggression (aggressive / non-aggressive / no model; also model sex and child sex); Andrade doodling vs not doodling; Pozzulo age × target presence × lineup type; Dement (Q1) woken from REM vs non-REM; Perry oxytocin vs placebo (repeated measures); Piliavin victim type (cane vs drunk), race, and model condition. Milgram's main study had no IV （见 Tab 1 陷阱框）.

6Dependent Variable (DV, 因变量)
The dependent variable (DV) is what the researcher measures / records to see the effect of the IV. Its value is expected to 'depend on' the IV.
How the DV was measured (syllabus requirement) （DV 如何测量 · 大纲要求）: frequency / counts (Bandura's number of aggressive acts; Andrade's names recalled); duration / latency (Piliavin's latency of the first helper); physiological / equipment (Dement used EEG/EOG; Hölzel used MRI grey-matter density); rating scales (Pozzulo's identification accuracy; Saavedra's Feelings Thermometer). In planning questions the DV must always be given with its measurement unit or scoring method. 写计划题时 DV 必须给测量单位或计分方法。
Bandura, Ross & Ross (1961)
"The DV was the amount of aggressive behaviour shown by each child, measured by observers through a one-way mirror using a standardised checklist of physical and verbal aggressive acts (frequency counts and 5-point rating scales)."
Milgram (1963)
"The DV was the level of obedience — the maximum voltage of electric shock each participant was willing to administer before refusing to continue (out of 30 switches, up to 450 V)."

"The DVs were whether a dream was reported on being woken, the estimated duration of the dream (number of words in the report), and whether the direction of rapid eye movements matched the action reported in the dream."

"The DV was preferred interpersonal distance, measured in centimetres — the distance at which participants stopped an approaching figure (Experiment 1) and the preferred distance between chairs in room pictures (Experiment 2)."

"The DVs were identification accuracy (correct identifications in target-present arrays, correct rejections and false identifications in target-absent arrays) and witnesses' confidence ratings."

"The DV was the mean duration of contact (in seconds) with each toy category — wheeled toys vs plush toys — recorded during seven 25-minute sessions per group."

"The DV was each participant's score on the revised Eyes Test — the number of correct mental-state words chosen for the 36 photographs of the eye region (maximum 36), plus the time taken to respond."

"The DVs were monitoring performance — the number of names and places correctly recorded while listening — and surprise recall of the eight names and eight places after the tape ended."

"The DVs were whether anyone helped, the number of helpers, the time (latency) until the first helper arrived, the race of the helpers, and the comments made by passengers."
Hölzel et al. (2011)
"The DV was grey matter concentration in the brain, measured with MRI scans and voxel-based morphometry, compared between the MBSR group and the wait-list control group before and after the eight weeks."

"The DVs were the boy's fear and avoidance of buttons, measured by his fear ratings on the Feelings Thermometer, his behavioural avoidance of buttons, and reports from the boy and his mother during assessment and treatment."
7Operationalisation / 操作化
Operationalisation means defining a variable in terms of exactly how it will be measured or manipulated, so that the study could be replicated and the variable is measurable.
Examples （例子）: "sleeping well" (abstract) → operationalised as actual time to fall asleep / hours of deep sleep measured by EEG, or a self-report scale the next morning (June 2025 P21 Q10 MS accepts EEG or self-report); "attention" → "score on an in-class quiz". "睡得好"（抽象）→ EEG 实测入睡时间/深睡时长 或 次日自评量表；"注意力"→"课上测验分数"。

High-frequency exam task （考试高发）: "Operationalise the IV / DV" = translate an abstract word into a concrete indicator with units and a scoring rule. A concept without a measurement method is incomplete. 只有概念没有测量方式 = 不完整。
Bandura, Ross & Ross (1961)
"Aggression was operationalised as the number of imitative and non-imitative physical and verbal aggressive acts performed by each child in 20 minutes, recorded by trained observers using time-sampling and 5-point rating scales."
Milgram (1963)
"Obedience was operationalised as the maximum voltage level (up to 450 V) the participant administered on the 30-switch shock generator before refusing to continue."

8变量控制：participant / situational / uncontrolled variables
Participant variables are differences between participants (age, IQ, memory ability, personality). Situational variables are features of the environment or procedure (noise, time of day, researcher behaviour). If not controlled, either type becomes an uncontrolled variable that could offer an alternative explanation for the results.

Experimental Designs / 实验设计

三种被试设计（independent / repeated / matched pairs）+ 顺序效应 + 抵消平衡 + 随机分配 + 对照组。

Learn Mode

Independent Measures

Different participants in each condition — each person takes part in only one level of the IV.

Repeated Measures

The same participants do all conditions — each person takes part in every level of the IV.

Matched Pairs

Different participants, but each is matched with a partner in the other condition on key variables (age, IQ, ability).

1Independent Measures Design / 被试间（独立测量）设计
Independent measures design uses different participants in each condition. Each participant experiences only one level of the IV. Participants should be allocated to conditions by random allocation.
Strength (write this):"A strength of independent measures design is that there are no order effects, because each participant only takes part in one condition, so they cannot get better through practice or worse through fatigue. Participants also cannot compare conditions, so there are fewer demand characteristics."
Weakness (write this):"A weakness is that participant variables may differ between the groups (one group may simply be better at the task), so the IV may not be the true cause of any difference; it also requires more participants than repeated measures."

Core-study examples （核心研究例子）: Andrade randomly allocated 40 participants to a doodling or a control group; Bandura put different groups with different models; Pozzulo compared children vs adults (age was a between-participants factor). Andrade：40 人随机分到涂鸦/控制两组；Bandura：不同组看不同模型；Pozzulo：幼儿 vs 成人。
2Repeated Measures Design / 被试内（重复测量）设计
Repeated measures design uses the same participants in all conditions. Each person does every level of the IV, usually in a counterbalanced order.
Strength (write this):"A strength of repeated measures design is that participant variables are fully controlled because each participant is compared with themselves, and fewer participants are needed."
Weakness (write this):"A weakness is order effects — participants may improve through practice or decline through fatigue or boredom — and having done one condition they may guess the aim, creating demand characteristics; the design is also unsuitable for tasks that can only be done once, such as a memory test."

Core-study examples （核心研究例子）: Dement Q1 compared dream recall when the same participants were woken from REM vs non-REM sleep; Perry gave the same participants oxytocin and placebo a week apart, counterbalancing the order; Hölzel scanned the same people before vs after training (a longitudinal repeated measure). Dement：同一被试 REM/nREM 唤醒对比梦回忆；Perry：同一批人一周间隔分别吸 oxytocin/placebo 并 counterbalance；Hölzel：同一批人训练前 vs 训练后。
3Matched Pairs Design / 匹配（被试）设计
Matched pairs design uses different participants in each condition, but each participant is matched with a partner in the other condition on key variables (age, gender, IQ, relevant ability) that could affect the DV.
Strength (write this):"A strength of matched pairs design is that participant variables are partly controlled because each participant is matched with someone similar on key variables, yet each person takes part in only one condition, so there are no order effects."
Weakness (write this):"A weakness is that matching is time-consuming, and it is impossible to match participants on every variable that could affect the DV, so participant variables are only reduced, not eliminated."

Core-study example （核心研究例子）: in Bandura, an experimenter and the nursery teacher first rated each child's everyday aggression on a 5-point scale, then children were "matched individually" into trios (same sex, similar age and aggression score), and the three children of each trio were randomly assigned to the aggressive-model, non-aggressive-model or control condition. Hölzel matched the MBSR group with a wait-list control group on age and gender. Bandura：先按日常攻击性 5 点评分匹配成三人一组，再随机分到攻击模型/非攻击模型/控制三条件；Hölzel：MBSR 组与 wait-list 对照组按年龄性别匹配。
4Order Effects / 顺序效应（fatigue & practice）
Order effects occur in repeated measures designs when the order of conditions affects performance: practice effects (doing the task first improves performance the second time) and fatigue / boredom effects (performance drops because participants tire or lose motivation).
Examples (2025 exam context) （2025 真题语境）: Oct/Nov 2025 P23 Q1 asked for the definition of counterbalancing; June 2025 P22 Q6 linked to Dement (Dement randomised the order of REM/nREM awakenings for each participant to lower the risk of practice effects — an MS-accepted example). Dement 将 REM/nREM 唤醒顺序随机化以降低 practice 风险（MS 认可例子）。
5Counterbalancing / 抵消平衡（ABBA）
Counterbalancing means splitting participants so that half do the conditions in one order and half in the opposite order (A then B / B then A). It spreads the effect of order evenly across conditions so order effects do not systematically favour one condition.
Exam definition (Oct/Nov 2025 P23 Q1 — memorise word for word) （真题定义 · 逐字可背）: "having two groups of participants who do the tasks/conditions in opposite orders… when one group does condition A then condition B, the other does B then A (ABBA)"; its importance = "to reduce order effects". 重要性 = to reduce order effects。 Study examples: Perry counterbalanced the order of the two sessions; Pozzulo counterbalanced photo positions in the arrays; Hassett counterbalanced left/right toy positions. Perry：两次实验顺序在被试间 counterbalanced；Pozzulo：列队照片位置；Hassett：左右玩具位置。
6Random Allocation / 随机分配
Random allocation is placing participants into conditions by chance (random number generator, drawing names), so that participant variables are roughly evenly spread across conditions.
Do not confuse them （区分 · 考试常混，务必分开）: random sampling = how you select people from the population; random allocation = how you assign the people who have arrived into conditions. In Section B, an independent measures plan must state the method of allocation (e.g. "randomly allocated to condition A or B"). random sampling（怎么从总体抽人）≠ random allocation（人已到齐、怎么分到条件）。
✅ Exam Key Points (Designs) — memorise these:
• "Independent measures uses different participants in each condition; repeated measures uses the same participants in all conditions; matched pairs uses different but matched participants in each condition."
• IM vs RM evaluation (June 2025 P21 Q10(b) structure): IM strength = no order effects and fewer demand characteristics; IM weakness = participant variables between groups and more participants needed. RM strength = participant variables controlled; RM weakness = order effects, solved by counterbalancing.
• In Section B plans: if you choose repeated measures, add counterbalancing; if you choose independent measures, add random allocation — this "detail" is exactly what the mark scheme rewards.

Self-Reports / 自陈法（问卷 · 访谈）

大纲 3.1.2 Self-reports：问卷（technique: paper-and-pencil / online；question format: open/closed）＋ 访谈（format: structured / unstructured / semi-structured；technique: telephone / face-to-face）。

Learn Mode
1Questionnaire / 问卷
A questionnaire is a self-report method in which participants answer a fixed written set of questions. Two techniques of presenting it: paper and pencil (handed out / posted) or online. Question format can be open or closed.
Exam definition (June 2025 P21 Q1(a) — memorise) （真题定义 · 可背）: "self-report / written questions, on paper or online" — writing the phrase "written questions" correctly earns 2 marks. 写对 "written questions" 即 2 分。

Practical advantages (June 2025 P21 Q1(b) MS — choose either) （实用优点 · MS 两种写法任选）: ① participants are less likely to lie / social desirability is lower, because it is not face-to-face and no researcher is present; ② it can be posted or completed online by many people, giving a larger, more representative sample that can be generalised. ⚠️ The MS states "Ethical advantages = 0" — writing "it is more ethical because it is anonymous" earns nothing. ① 不是面对面、无研究者在场 → 不易撒谎/社会赞许性低；② 可邮寄/线上 → 样本更大、更能推广。⚠️ 写"匿名更道德"不得分。
2Open & Closed Questions / 开放题与封闭题
Open questions allow participants to give their own answer in their own words — producing detailed, in-depth, qualitative data, but answers must be interpreted by the researcher (subjective). Closed questions restrict answers to a limited number of options (yes/no, scale 1–10, forced choice) — producing quantitative data that is easier to analyse and more objective.
How it was tested in 2025 （2025 真题考法 · Oct/Nov 2025 P23 Q6 直接要求描述两者 + 例子）: an open-question example — Dement & Kleitman asked participants to "describe the content of the dream"; a closed-question example — Baron-Cohen's Eyes Test used a forced choice of four words. 开放题例子：Dement & Kleitman 让被试 "describe the content of the dream"；封闭题例子：Baron-Cohen Eyes Test 四选一 forced choice。 Marking rule: each concept earns at most 2 marks for definition detail + 2 for the example, so one good example is enough for all 6 marks; an open question cannot be used as the example for a closed question (and vice versa). 每概念 ≤2 分定义 + 2 分例子；只需 1 个例子即可拿满 6 分；开放题、封闭题不能互相充当例子。
3Interview 格式：Structured / Unstructured / Semi-structured / 结构化 · 非结构化 · 半结构化
An interview is a self-report method where the researcher asks questions face-to-face or by telephone, in real time. Structured = every participant is asked the same set questions from a script (highly standardised). Unstructured = no set questions; the interviewer follows the participant's answers and pursues lines of interest. Semi-structured = some fixed questions, but the interviewer can add follow-up questions in response to answers.
Core-study examples (June 2025 P23 Q6 MS wording) （核心研究例 · 逐字推荐）: Dement & Kleitman asked every participant whether they had been dreaming (fixed question) and, if so, asked for more details (follow-up) = semi-structured — a 2-mark example; Saavedra & Silverman interviewed the boy and his mother at the 6- and 12-month follow-ups (also used to confirm the specific phobia criteria); Milgram interviewed participants about their feelings a year after the study.

Match the terms to their criteria （术语对号入座）: structured = "a script / set questions + same tone of voice" (the MS also accepts same dress); unstructured = "questions follow on from the participant's answers"; semi-structured = "some questions the same + the interviewer can add follow-ups". structured 判据 = script/固定题 + 相同语气（MS 还认 same dress）；unstructured = 追问随回答走；semi-structured = 部分固定 + 可追加。

Social desirability bias is when participants give answers they think are socially acceptable / make them look good, rather than the truth — e.g. over-reporting exercise, under-reporting drinking. Observations / 观察法

大纲 3.1.2 Observations：四维特征 —— naturalistic / controlled、overt / covert、participant / non-participant、structured / unstructured（含 behavioural categories）。

Learn Mode
1Observation + Naturalistic / Controlled / 观察法 · 自然观察 vs 控制观察
An observation records behaviour as it happens (no questionnaire, no experimenter manipulation). A naturalistic observation takes place in a real-life setting where behaviour would naturally occur, with no intervention by the researcher. A controlled observation takes place in a setting the researcher arranges / partially controls (e.g. a lab room or an enclosure), so that behaviour can be seen more easily, but it is less natural.
One-line distinction （区分一句流）: naturalistic = watching behaviour that would happen anyway; controlled = only putting the behaviour into an environment where it is easy to observe (≠ experiment, because no IV is changed). naturalistic 看的是"本来就发生的行为"；controlled 只是把行为"放进好观察的环境"（≠ 实验，因为不改 IV）。

Core studies （核心研究）: Piliavin observed whether strangers on a New York subway carriage helped a man who collapsed — a classic naturalistic observation; Hassett first observed the monkeys' natural play in their enclosure (filmed), then placed pairs of toys inside for a controlled preference test — using both naturalistic and controlled stages. Piliavin：地铁车厢观察陌生人是否救助 = 自然主义经典；Hassett：围场自然观察 + 放入玩具做受控偏好测试 = 两阶段都用。
2Overt vs Covert / 公开观察 vs 隐蔽观察
In an overt observation participants know they are being observed and have agreed to it. In a covert observation participants do not know they are being observed — the observer hides their role, so behaviour stays natural.
Evaluation （评价）: covert → behaviour is natural, ecological validity is high, no demand characteristics; but there are serious ethical problems (no informed consent, invasion of privacy, deception). overt → ethical and can be recorded legally; but participants know they are watched and may perform (demand characteristics / social desirability). covert：行为自然、生态效度高、无 demand characteristics，但伦理问题严重（无同意/侵犯隐私/欺骗）；overt：符合伦理，但被试知道被看会演。

2025 exam examples （2025 真题）: June 2025 P22 Q7(c)(ii) suggested the teacher pretend to be a new student / cleaner as a covert observer to remove social desirability; Oct/Nov 2025 P21 Q8(c) — the children thought Dr East was a playground assistant, so she deceived them and they could not consent = an ethical problem (MS wording). June 2025 P22 Q7(c)(ii)：装成新学生/清洁工做 covert observer；Oct/Nov 2025 P21 Q8(c)：儿童以为 Dr East 是操场助理 → 欺骗 + 无法同意 = 伦理问题。
3Participant vs Non-participant / 参与式 vs 非参与式观察
In a participant observation the researcher joins in the activity / group being studied. In a non-participant observation the researcher stays outside / at a distance and only watches and records.
Strength of participant observation (Oct/Nov 2025 P21 Q8(a) original answer) （参与式优点）: being close / joining in lets the researcher really understand the children's games and notice their emotions.
Strength of non-participant observation (Oct/Nov 2025 P21 Q8(b) original answer) （非参与式优点）: children do not know they are studied → they play more naturally; staying at a distance without joining in → natural behaviour; video can be replayed for analysis; a second observer can be added to check agreement.

Examples （例子）: Piliavin's observers sat among the passengers by the "critical area" recording (non-participant + covert); Dr East played with the children (participant) while hiding her identity = participant + covert combined. Piliavin：观察者扮乘客坐"关键区"旁记录（非参与 + covert）；Dr East 陪孩子玩但隐瞒身份（participant + covert）。
4Structured vs Unstructured + Behavioural Categories / 结构化 · 非结构化 · 行为类别
A structured observation uses a fixed list of behavioural categories (a checklist) decided in advance — only pre-set behaviours are recorded. An unstructured observation records all relevant behaviour as it happens (often a running record / notes). Behavioural categories must be observable and measurable (what to count / time), so different observers record the same behaviour the same way.
Judging word (Oct/Nov 2025 P21 Q1(a)) （判据）: "fixed list of behavioural categories" = structured.

Behavioural-category examples (Oct/Nov 2025 P21 Q1(b)(i) MS) （行为类别例子 · 观察学习习惯）: "time spent working / frequency of using books / note taking / using flashcards / studying alone".

Problems with categories (Q1(b)(ii)) （类别会有的毛病）: hard to operationalise (where does "note" end and the next behaviour begin?); easy to misclassify (a book read on an iPad is invisible → "number of books used" is underestimated; headphones do not always mean music).

Strength of structured observation (June 2025 P22 Q3 original — Fagen's elephants) （结构化观察的优点）: it improves reliability (inter / intra-observer — two observers' records of postures like "trunk here / trunk up" can be compared for agreement); observers focus only on the relevant behaviour instead of being distracted → more valid results.

Core studies using structured observation （用结构化观察的核心研究）: Bandura recorded children's behaviour through a one-way mirror using pre-decided aggression categories with 5-second intervals; Fagen coded video of elephant behaviour (trunk here / trunk up / bucket, etc.) into defined categories. Bandura：单向玻璃 + 预定攻击类别 + 5 秒间隔；Fagen：按定义类别编码大象行为录像。

Sampling & Ethics / 抽样与伦理

大纲 3.1.2：抽样只考 opportunity / random / volunteer 三种；伦理分人类（7 条）与动物（replacement / species / numbers / procedures 等）。

Learn Mode
🎯 Sampling & Ethics — the exact syllabus scope （抽样与伦理 · 直接划范围）
  • ✅ The ONLY 3 sampling methods examined (learn these): opportunity, random (probability) and volunteer (self-selecting) — definition + evaluation + a core-study example. 会考的抽样只有 3 种（背熟）：定义 + 评价 + 核心研究例子。
  • ❌ Sampling NOT examined (do not write): stratified, systematic, quota, snowball, cluster, etc. — they belong to other syllabuses (Edexcel loves them); writing them gains nothing and shows confusion. 写了不加分、还暴露方法学混淆。
  • ✅ Human ethics = the 7 syllabus words only: valid consent, minimising harm, right to withdraw, lack of deception, confidentiality, privacy, debriefing. 人类伦理只按 7 条大纲词答。
  • ❌ Do NOT write these ethics terms: presumptive consent, ethical committee, BPS guidelines — they are not CIE 9990 syllabus language. 不属于 CIE 9990 大纲用语。
1Sample vs Population / 样本 vs 总体
The population is the whole group that the researcher wants to draw conclusions about (e.g. all AS psychology students). The sample is the smaller group actually studied, taken from that population. Results from the sample are generalised back to the population.
Why sample at all? （为什么要抽样）: it is impossible to test the whole population — you study a small group that stands for the population, then generalise the conclusions back. 找一小群人代表总体，再把结论推广回去。 Generalisation is the ultimate purpose of sampling and the key sentence when evaluating any sampling method. 推广（generalisation）是抽样的终极目的，也是评估抽样方法时的核心句式。
2Opportunity Sampling / 机会（便利）抽样
Opportunity sampling selects participants who are available and willing at the time of the study — whoever happens to be there (passers-by, students in the researcher's class).
Exam examples (June 2025 P21 Q6 MS) （真题例）: Baron-Cohen recruited Group 1 from an adult evening class in Exeter / a public library in Cambridge; Saavedra's boy was a clinic patient at Florida International University; Piliavin used passengers on the 8th Avenue subway; Andrade and Dement are also accepted as opportunity by the MS.

Evaluation （评价）: fast and easy to obtain; but the sample is limited to "whoever is there at the time" (e.g. psychology students who volunteer), so it is less representative → generalising is limited. 快、易得；但样本局限于"当时在场的人"，代表性低 → 难推广。
3Random Sampling / 随机抽样
Random sampling gives every member of the target population an equal chance of being selected. Method: put the population on a numbered list, then use a random number generator (or draw names from a hat) to pick the required number.
Exam detail (June 2025 P21 Q6 MS) （真题细节）: "getting a numbered list of your target population + a random number generator / names in a hat" = the full-detail example (= 2 marks). A simple school version: pick 30 names at random from the whole school register (= 1 mark).

Evaluation （评价）: fairest, no researcher bias → the most representative sample; but getting a full list is time-consuming, and selected people may refuse → the actual sample may not be "random". 最公平、无研究者偏差 → 最有代表性；但要完整名单费时，且被抽中者可能拒绝 → 实际样本未必"随机"。

⚠️ Do not confuse （区分）: random sampling (selecting people from the population) ≠ random allocation (assigning people to conditions) — see Tab 3.
4Volunteer (Self-selecting) Sampling / 自愿（自选）抽样
Volunteer (self-selecting) sampling recruits participants who volunteer themselves, usually in response to an advertisement (notice, email, social media, newspaper) asking for people to take part.
Exam example (June 2025 P23 Q5 — why Bandura may not have used volunteers) （真题例：Bandura 为什么可能没用 volunteer）: Bandura's children came from a university nursery; if he had advertised, parents of aggressive children might be embarrassed to let them take part, and parents of well-behaved children might fear they would learn bad behaviour and not sign up → a biased sample, hard to generalise (= 2 marks linked).

Evaluation （评价）: little effort, participants are motivated (cooperative); but only certain people (helpful, free, interested) respond → volunteer bias, not representative of the general public. 省力、被试配合积极；但只有特定人群（热心/有空/有兴趣）响应 → volunteer bias，不代表大众。

Caution （注意）: Hölzel recruited people who wanted to reduce stress / could accept an MRI, and Pozzulo's adults came from a volunteer pool — they have a volunteer flavour, but the MS warns: if the source does not state it, do not use it as an example (Bandura / Hölzel / Perry give no sampling info in the source, so writing it earns nothing). 原文没写明抽样方法的，不要拿来做例子（Bandura / Hölzel / Perry 原文均未说明）。
5Human Ethics — the 7 Syllabus Guidelines 人类伦理 7 条（大纲逐字）
GuidelineWhat it means (copy-ready) + core-study example
Minimising harm (and maximising benefit)"Participants should not experience more harm than in everyday life, and the study should aim to benefit them." Example: in Milgram (1963) the participants were put under great psychological stress — they believed they were really shocking a person — so this is a criticism; Pozzulo / Hölzel were low risk.
Valid consent incl. informed consent"Participants must be told what the research involves and must understand it before they agree; children need parental/guardian consent." Example: Bandura's paper does not state whether consent was obtained (June 2025 P21 Q2 — the children were too young to consent themselves); Milgram only said it was a 'memory experiment'.
Right to withdraw"Participants may leave at any time without penalty; researchers must not pressure them to stay." Example: Milgram's four prods ('Please continue') made leaving very hard — an ethical criticism; Oct/Nov 2025 P23 Q7(b) asks how to tell a child: "You can stop playing with the toys and go home if you want to."
Lack of deception"Participants must not be deceived; if deception is unavoidable it must be explained afterwards." Example: Bandura's children did not know their aggression was being measured (they thought it was a game); Piliavin's passengers did not know the 'victim' was an actor; Oct/Nov 2025 P21 Q8(c) — a child thought Dr East was a playground assistant.
Confidentiality"Data must be anonymous, so results cannot be traced back to a specific person." Example: Dement & Kleitman reported results using only participants' initials (just sex and occupation) — June 2025 P23 Q1 answer.
Privacy"The study must not intrude into participants' private space or private moments." Example: covert observation (Piliavin's passengers did not know they were being recorded) raises privacy concerns.
Debriefing"Afterwards the true aim must be explained, any misleading corrected, and support offered." Example: Milgram gave a full debrief and re-interviewed participants a year later; Saavedra's therapeutic study was explained throughout.
Answer sentence pattern (copy-ready) （答题句式）: "The guideline of X was followed because the researcher [did the procedural thing], which means [the benefit to the participant]." 定义题（如 Outline valid consent）只需一句准确定义即可；应用题必须连到某个 named study 的具体程序细节才拿第 2 分。
6动物伦理（大纲逐字）
Animal guidelines in the CIE syllabus: minimising harm (and maximising benefit); replacement (use less-sentient animals / computer models / field observation instead of lab); species (choose a species appropriate to the research question and least likely to suffer); numbers (use the minimum number of animals needed); and procedures — including pain, suffering and distress, housing (appropriate cages/enrichment), and reward, deprivation and aversive stimuli (motivate by reward, avoid unnecessary deprivation/punishment).
Exam examples (copy-ready answer sentences) （真题例）:
• Oct/Nov 2025 P21 Q9 (noise-tunnel learning study): species — "choose a small animal, because the tunnel is narrow"; "choose a group-living / social species, because the animal will meet others." Minimising harm — "the noise could distress or frighten the animals; meeting unfamiliar animals could provoke aggression."
• June 2025 P23 Q8 Dr Mohan's monkey study: suggestion 1 = "use an animal with a simpler brain" → species / less suffering; suggestion 2 = "use video recordings from the wild instead" → replacement (avoids lab use + wild animals are not caged).
• June 2025 P21 Q7 Dr Smith's lever-learning study: besides species — "provide appropriate housing" (housing); "use the minimum number of animals" (numbers); "motivate with food reward rather than shock" (procedures: reward, deprivation and aversive stimuli). （噪音隧道 / Dr Mohan 猴子实验 / Dr Smith 学杠杆 三例均为原题）

Animal vs human ethics — the difference （动物 vs 人类伦理的差异点）: animals cannot consent / withdraw / be debriefed, so the researcher must make 'substitute judgements' — that is why replacement / species / numbers exist. 动物无法同意/退出/事后说明 → 研究者须做替代判断，因此才有 replacement / species / numbers 等条目。
✅ Exam Key Points (Sampling & Ethics):
• "Describe X sampling and Y sampling, using any example(s)" [6] = each term ≤2 marks (definition + detail), and you only need one good example. ⚠️ Evaluation is not credited here (MS: "no marks for evaluation") — do NOT waste words on "more/less representative".
• Memorise these one-line templates: opportunity = whoever is available and willing at the time of the study; random = every member of the population has an equal chance (numbered list + random number generator / names in a hat); volunteer (self-selecting) = people respond to an advertisement offering to take part.
• Human ethics = "the action taken" + "what it means for the participant"; animal ethics = "the guideline name" + "the specific choice for THIS study" — both must be linked to the study's detail to earn the application mark.
• Section B planning questions almost always include "Do not describe sample / sampling technique or ethical issues" — so do not write sampling or ethics in the plan (see Tab 9).

Data Analysis / 数据分析（描述统计）

大纲 3.1.2 Types of data & Data analysis：quantitative / qualitative、objective / subjective；表格；mode / median / mean；range / standard deviation；bar chart / histogram / scatter graph。

Learn Mode
🎯 Data & statistics — the exact syllabus scope: learn ONLY what is below （数据与统计 · 直接划范围）
  • ✅ Examined (spend your time here): types of data (quantitative / qualitative, objective / subjective); tables (read, draw); central tendency (mode / median / mean — which to choose + describe); spread (range and standard deviation are only tested for recognition and explanation); graphs (bar / histogram / scatter graph — recognise, draw, convert). 数据类型、表格、集中趋势选哪个+描述、range/SD 只考认识与解释、三种图认/画/转换。
  • ❌ NOT examined (do not learn — no marks even if written): all statistical tests — t-test, chi-square, sign test, Mann-Whitney, etc. (syllabus p.23: "will not be required to understand or interpret statistical tests": no calculating, no understanding, no explaining). 一切统计检验不考：不用计算、不用理解、不用解释。
  • ❌ Also do not write these words: significance level / p-value, normal distribution, degrees of freedom, levels of measurement (nominal / ordinal / interval) — none are CIE points, and writing them shows confusion. 显著性水平/p 值、正态分布、自由度、名义/顺序/等距数据等都不属于 CIE 考点。
  • 📌 Evidence: none of the eleven Paper 2 papers (March 2025 → June 2026) involved a statistical test — they all tested "choose the graph / draw the graph / read the table / explain SD and mean". 11 套真题（2025 March → 2026 June）无一涉及统计检验。
1Quantitative / Qualitative + Objective / Subjective / 量化 · 质化 · 客观 · 主观
Quantitative data is numerical — counts, scores, times, voltages (easy to compare and analyse; objective). Qualitative data is descriptive, in-depth — words, comments, interview answers (rich detail and insight; must be interpreted so it is subjective). Objective = not influenced by the researcher's opinion; subjective = based on personal interpretation.
Exam examples (June 2025 P22 Q3, Milgram) （真题例）: "voltage reached when the participant refused" = quantitative / objective (the most common stopping point was 300 V, MS [DEFINITIVE]); "comments from participants" (such as "I can't go on with this") = qualitative / subjective.

Advantages of each (Q3(c) MS) （各自优点）: quantitative → easy to compare and analyse — you can see at a glance that most people stopped at 300 V (the mode); qualitative → detailed, shows the thoughts and emotions behind the behaviour. Each advantage must be linked back to the study. 答题时每个数据类型的优点必须连回该研究。
2Tables / 表格
Data in tables is organised into rows and columns with clear headings — one variable per row/column, plus units. Tables make it easy to look up and compare individual values before further analysis.
Two ways it is tested （两种考法）: ① Reading a table (June 2025 P21 Q4, Baron-Cohen Table 4.1: the mean and SD of the two groups' ages — the mean tells you which group is older on average; the SD tells you which group's ages are more spread out); ② Drawing a table (Oct/Nov 2025 P23 Q7(c): draw a table recording how long children interact with toys → row headings "Object" listing picture books / toy bricks / other object, and column headings "Duration of interaction" split by Participant 1 / Participant 2 — rows and columns can be swapped). The marks come from row/column headings + categories + units. 画表拿分点 = 行列标题 + 类别 + 单位。
3Measures of Central Tendency / 集中趋势（mode · median · mean）
Measures of central tendency give one typical / average value to describe a data set. Mode = the most frequent value (used for categories/labels). Median = the middle value when scores are ranked in order (not distorted by extreme scores). Mean = the arithmetic average, total ÷ number of scores (used for numeric data with no extreme outliers).
How to choose (June 2025 P23 Q7, Kong — original answers) （怎么选）: timed data such as "duration of eye contact" → mean (continuous numeric data with no extreme values); categorical answers "Are you excited or scared?" → mode (category data — count which answer is most frequent). Both MS answers are [DEFINITIVE] — choosing wrong = 0. 计时/连续数值无极端 → mean；类别数据 → mode。MS 均为 [DEFINITIVE]，选错 0 分。

"Describe measures of central tendency, using any example(s)" [6] (Oct/Nov 2025 P21 Q6 original) （真题格式）: first write the general definition (finding the typical / average — one number that represents the whole data set) → then give the method and definition of mode / median / mean → then give core-study examples: Milgram's mode of disobedience = 300 V; the Andrade doodling group's mean names recalled; the Baron-Cohen Eyes Test mean score; the Dement mean number of awakenings per night.

One-sentence trap （一句话陷阱）: when extreme scores pull the mean, the median is more "typical" (e.g. Piliavin's helping-latency mean is stretched by a few very slow helpers — the MS gives both mean and median). mean 被极端值拉偏时用 median 更"typical"。
4Measures of Spread / 离散程度（range · standard deviation）
Measures of spread describe how spread out / varied the scores are. Range = highest score minus lowest score (easy but distorted by one extreme value). Standard deviation (SD) = a measure of the average distance of scores from the mean — the larger the SD, the more the scores vary around the mean.
Same mean, different spread — SD tells us how far the scores are from the mean.
Two question styles （真题两种问法）:
• Explain the concept (Oct/Nov 2025 P22 Q4(b)(i), Pozzulo): "Describe what is meant by a 'standard deviation'" → "a measure of spread/variation (looking at difference) from the mean" — writing "the average distance of scores from the mean" = full 2 marks; the formula is NOT needed. 写"平均离均差"即满分 2 分，公式不用背。
• Explain the data (Oct/Nov 2025 P22 Q4(b)(ii)): the adults' SD was larger than the children's → "the adults' ages ranged much more widely: the children were only 4–7 years old (narrow), while the adults spanned several decades". 成人 SD 比儿童大 = 成人年龄范围更大。
• Reading a table (June 2025 P21 Q4): a bigger SD = that group's ages are more spread out / a bigger range; merely copying the number ("Group 1's SD is bigger") = 0 [NAQ] — you must translate it into "more spread out". 只照抄数字 = 0 [NAQ]，必须翻译成"更分散"。

✅ Exam Key Points (Data analysis) — memorise these:
① In definition questions write "type + function": quantitative = numbers; qualitative = words / depth; objective = not influenced by the researcher; subjective = based on interpretation. 定义题优先写 type + 功能。
② "Which measure of central tendency?" questions: categories → mode; numeric without extremes → mean; numeric with extremes → median. 类别 → mode；数值无极端 → mean；数值有极端 → median。
③ SD is only tested for "recognition and explanation": a large SD = scores lie far from the mean / more spread out — always translate it into meaning, never copy the number. 永远翻译成意思，别抄数字。
④ Drawing a graph (4 marks) = x-axis label + x-axis unit/categories + y-axis label + y-axis unit/scale; bar heights and whether bars touch are marked separately. 画图 4 分 = 两轴标签 + 单位/类别/刻度。
⑤ There are no statistical tests to learn — spend your time on "choose the graph, draw the graph, read the table". 没有统计检验要学，时间花在选图/画图/读表上。

Validity, Reliability & More Methods / 效度、信度与其他方法

Learn Mode
🎯 Validity & reliability — the exact syllabus scope （效度与信度 · 直接划范围）
  • ✅ The validity examined (named in the syllabus): ecological validity, plus the 3 angles used to evaluate it — subjectivity / objectivity, demand characteristics, generalisability. 会考的效度 = ecological validity + 用它评价的 3 个角度。
  • 🔑 Is internal validity examined? — Yes, as the IDEA, not as the LABEL. The whole point of controls IS internal validity: controls reduce extraneous / confounding variables, so we can be sure it was the IV — not another variable — that caused the change in the DV. So when a question asks why controls matter, or asks you to evaluate an experiment, write this functional sentence: "The controls reduce extraneous variables, so we can be more confident that the IV — not a confounding variable — caused the change in the DV." 考道理、不考标签：controls 的存在意义就是 internal validity——控制额外/混淆变量，才能确定是 IV 引起 DV 的变化。
  • ⚠️ Label reminder: internal / external / face / content validity are NOT CIE syllabus words — do not memorise them as exam terms. For "validity" questions use the syllabus language (ecological + the 3 angles); use the functional sentence above for the internal-validity idea, and do not confuse it with ecological validity. 别把这些标签当考点名词背；internal validity 用功能句说清即可，不必把标签写进答案。
  • ✅ The reliability examined — 3 types: inter-rater, inter-observer and test-retest. 会考的信度 3 种。
  • ❌ Reliability NOT examined: split-half, parallel forms, etc. — no need to learn them. 不考的信度：split-half、parallel forms 等。
1Case Study / 个案研究
A case study investigates one person / one small unit in depth and detail (a single participant, one family, one school), usually using several techniques together (interviews, questionnaires, observations, tests) over time.
Core-study example （核心研究例）: Saavedra & Silverman is a textbook case study — a nine-year-old boy's specific phobia of buttons: questionnaires/interviews with the boy and his mother to confirm the diagnosis, the Feelings Thermometer to rate distress in different situations, imaginal exposure treatment, and 6- and 12-month follow-ups — single individual + depth + multiple methods + over time, all four features present. 单一个体 + 深入 + 多方法 + 跨时间 = 四要素齐。

Evaluation （评价）: unique / rare phenomena can only be studied this way; very detailed and shows development over time; but n = 1 → cannot be generalised to others; may be subjective (the researcher is deeply involved); time-consuming; an ethical weakness (June 2025 P21 Q5 exam question) — the participant may find it hard to withdraw (relationship with the researcher / dependence on treatment). n=1 无法推广；可能主观；耗时；被试难以退出（治疗依赖）。

Section B case-study plan (Oct/Nov 2025 P23 Q10 original — Liam the attention prodigy) — 4 required features: 4 必备要素 (a) detail about the participant / unit (how found, background); (b) collect "deep" content (e.g. attention duration under different stimuli); (c) at least two collection techniques (observe him + interview him / his parents / teachers); (d) analysis / interpretation / triangulation (how the qualitative and quantitative data are combined). （参与者细节 / 内容够深 / ≥2 种技术 / 分析+triangulation）
2Correlations / 相关研究
A correlation measures the relationship between two measured variables (co-variables) — no variable is manipulated. Positive: as one increases, the other increases. Negative: as one increases, the other decreases. Strength: how closely the points cluster on a scatter graph (strong / weak). Correlations show a relationship but never prove causation.
Co-variables vs IV/DV （co-variables vs IV/DV）: in a correlation, both variables are only "measured" — there is no manipulated "IV"; to operationalise them, write how each is measured. 相关研究里两个变量都只是被测量，没有"IV"。 A Section B correlation plan (June 2025 P22 Q10 original: ward noise vs quality of sleep) needs 4 features = (a) the two co-variables; (b) how variable 1 is measured (sleep = questionnaire scale / EEG stages); (c) how variable 2 is measured (noise = a decibel meter); (d) the expected direction (positive / negative, strong / weak) + presenting it on a scatter graph. 计划相关研究 4 必备要素：两个 co-variables + 变量 1 测量 + 变量 2 测量 + 预期方向/散点图。

Data that cannot be correlated (June 2025 P21 Q8(b) original) （不能相关的数据）: "tidy or not tidy" has only two categories → category / two-point data cannot be correlated; correlation needs data that can vary continuously or along a gradient. 类别/两点数据没法算相关；相关需要能连续变化的数据。

The causation trap (June 2025 P21 Q8(c)(ii) original answer) （因果陷阱）: "correlation ≠ causation" — we do not know which variable causes which; a third variable may be at work (e.g. parenting style could affect both tidiness and enjoyment of lessons). The MS is clear: writing "one variable causes the other" = 0. 写 "one variable causes the other" = 0 分。

Exam scenarios （真题场景）: June 2025 P23 Q3 positive correlation of hours of sleep vs physical health (draw a scatter graph); Oct/Nov 2025 P21 Q5 negative correlation of TV time vs attention (draw the trend).
3Longitudinal Studies / 纵向研究（含纵向实验）
A longitudinal study follows the same participants over a long period, measuring them repeatedly at intervals to see how they change or develop. A longitudinal experiment adds manipulation + comparison (e.g. measure before and after a treatment, compared with a control group).
Core-study examples （核心研究例）: Hölzel et al. = a longitudinal experiment — 16 MBSR participants had an MRI before and after the 16-week mindfulness course, compared with a matched untrained control group for grey-matter change; Saavedra followed the same boy up at 6 / 12 months = a case-study style longitudinal design. Hölzel：16 名 MBSR 学员训练前后各做一次 MRI，与匹配对照组比较；Saavedra：6/12 个月随访同一男孩。

Strengths （优点）: tracking the same people shows real change → participant variables are ruled out; development / trends can be seen.
Weaknesses (June 2025 P21 Q9 MS wording) （缺点 · MS 照录）: attrition (participants move away, change numbers, fall ill, lose motivation or become unreachable); repeated testing brings practice effects; it is time-consuming and costly; social / cultural change becomes a third variable (over a decade the way people read changed itself — "people read more online"), so a single cross-sectional comparison (as Dr Roberts proposed) actually rules out the effect of changing times. 被试流失、练习效应、耗时昂贵、社会文化变迁成为第三变量。

Planning notes （计划注意）: choose a sensible interval (June 2025 P21 Q9: reading habits change slowly → yearly; weekly would pressure the children); plan a re-contact strategy (keep addresses/numbers, reminders).
4Validity & Ecological Validity / 效度与生态效度
Validity = whether the research measures what it claims to measure (and whether conclusions are justified). Ecological validity = whether the findings apply to real-life situations — the more natural the task/setting, the higher the ecological validity. Threats: demand characteristics (participants guess the aim and change behaviour), subjectivity (researcher's interpretation distorts the data), poor generalisability (sample/task too narrow to apply beyond the study).
Real exam treatment (真实考法)
• June 2025 P23 Q9(c)(ii) — computerised CID (a virtual person walks towards you, key-press to stop) → high ecological validity, because "in real life people move / we really do approach each other in space" (vs. a static paper image).
• June 2025 P22 Q5(a) — outline subjectivity → "depends on the researcher's own interpretation / opinion / feeling" (writing "not being objective" = 0 [NAQ]).
• June 2025 P21 Q8(a)(i) — Josie asks participants to rate tidiness 0–5 → different students read "3" differently → reliability problem; if only tidy / not-tidy (two points) → limited validity (cannot distinguish degrees of tidiness).

Three fixes for validity （提升效度的三个办法）: (1) covert / naturalistic keeps behaviour real; (2) standardisation + concealing the true aim reduces demand characteristics; (3) objective measures (instruments / behaviour counts) replace subjective ratings; (4) diverse samples support generalisability.
5Reliability & Replicability / 信度与可重复性
Reliability = consistency of measurement. Inter-observer reliability = two (or more) observers watching the same behaviour record it the same way. Inter-rater reliability = two (or more) judges/raters score the same data (e.g. interview answers, essays) and give the same scores. Test-retest reliability = giving the same test to the same people twice and checking the scores match. Replicability = whether another researcher could repeat the study using the same procedure (needs standardisation) and get similar results.
Real-world uses & exam treatment (实例与考法)
• Bandura: two raters independently recorded children's aggressive behaviour, then compared for high consistency — inter-rater / inter-observer reliability.
• Fagen: two observers coding elephant postures can cross-check — this is the answer to June 2025 P22 Q3 ("how reliability is improved").
• Oct/Nov 2025 P21 Q8(b) — "use a second observer → compare whether the two records agree" = a stated strength of non-participant observation.
• Rating-scale measures (e.g. self-rated tidiness) are the most common source of reliability questions — the same number can be read differently.

Link to replicability (与 replicability 的关系): only when the procedure is standardised (same instructions, same order, same environment) can another researcher repeat it → repeating and obtaining the same outcome tests reliability. In Section B, stating two explicit standards / controls both earns control marks and implicitly proves replicability.
✅ Exam Key Points (validity / reliability / other methods): ① Evaluating a case study = "depth + multiple methods + over time" vs "single unit is hard to generalise + participant finds it hard to withdraw". ② In any correlation answer, never write "X causes Y" — that scores zero. ③ Longitudinal = "same people + interval of months/years + repeated measurement" — to score the weakness mark, always mention attrition and the third variable of changing times. ④ "Validity" question: define first (does it measure what it claims to?), name which link is threatened (demand characteristics / subjectivity / generalisability), then give a fix. ⑤ "Reliability" question: separate the two observers (observer/rater) from "same test twice" (test-retest) — the core-study example is what earns the second mark. （考试要点：①评价个案 vs 评价实验；②相关研究禁写因果；③纵向必提 attrition 与时代变迁；④ validity 三段定义—威胁—修补；⑤ reliability 分清哪种类型 + 原文例子）

Q7 · 6-mark quota drill: two concepts + examples 6 marks

来源：June 2026 P23 Q5（Section A，6 分）— 同时命中「6 分配额」与「random sampling ≠ random allocation」两个考点

Describe counterbalancing and random allocation, using any example(s).  [6]
✏️ Auto-saved as you type
Level 6/6 Model Answer（严格按配额写：2+1+2+1）
Counterbalancing is having different groups of participants do the conditions in different orders — an ABBA technique: one group does condition A then B, and the other group does B then A. Its purpose is to control for order effects such as practice or fatigue, so that the order does not systematically favour one condition. For example, Andrade could have had two phone calls and had half the participants doodle first and not doodle second, and the other half the other way round.

Random allocation means each participant has an equal chance of being assigned to each group / condition / level of the IV. For example, a sample of students could be given school roll numbers which are put into a random number generator to allocate them to the two IV levels. This helps to reduce individual differences between the conditions.
Mark breakdown（MS 逐条对应）
Counterbalancing definition "different orders / ABBA" = 1 · detail "controls for order / fatigue / practice effects" = 1 · example (Andrade / Hassett / Pozzulo) = 1
Random allocation definition "equal chance of being assigned to each condition" = 1 · detail (how it is actually done) = 1 · example (Bandura / Andrade) = 1

• Axis labels + units + categories + bar touch / no-touch, item by item, to capture each marking point. A wrong frequency column caps the table at 3.
E. Answer hygiene — the [NAQ] rules
• Not restating the numbers ("the mean was bigger" = 0) — translate statistics into what they say about the people.
• Not defining a term by what it is not; not repeating information the stem already gave (REP = 0); not answering a different question than the one asked.
• random sampling ≠ random allocation; bar chart (gaps, categories) ≠ histogram (touching bars, continuous intervals).
F. Command words
• Outline / State = short answer; Describe = definition + detail (+ example); Explain / Suggest = reason + link; Evaluate = strength + weakness + judgement.
