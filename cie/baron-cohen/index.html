1. Background（研究背景）

🧠

Theory of Mind & Autism

Cognitive approach to understanding social cognition deficits

The psychology being investigated（研究的心理现象）

A person with an autism spectrum disorder（自闭症谱系障碍，ASD） does not fully develop cognitive processes linked to social interaction. ASD occurs in approximately 1% of the population. Individuals with ASD share difficulties in social functioning, such as limited social sensitivity, communication and ability to cope with change, and may have narrow interests.

The term ASD is now used to refer to both high-functioning autism (HFA)（高功能自闭症） and Asperger syndrome (AS)（阿斯伯格综合征）, however the terms HFA and AS were used at the time that this study was published.

Baron-Cohen et al. suggest that people with ASD lack or have an underdeveloped cognitive process called a theory of mind（心智理论，ToM）. A theory of mind is a cognitive ability enabling people to comprehend that others have different feelings, beliefs, knowledge and desires from their own. Individuals with ASD find it difficult to understand that other people have their own plans, thoughts or feelings separate from their own.

💡 What is Theory of Mind? 心智理论是一种认知能力，让你能理解：别人有跟你不一样的感觉、信念、知识和愿望。比如看到朋友哭，你知道她难过——这就是 ToM 在工作。自闭症谱系人士通常在这方面有困难。原文用 "mentalising"（心智化）这个词来描述这个过程。Empathy（共情）是理解他人感受的能力，和 ToM 密切相关。

Empathy（共情） is the ability to understand the world as another person does — to appreciate their feelings or emotional state separate from our own. There are several different tests available to measure a person's theory of mind but most of these are designed for children.

From Original (1997) to Revised (2001) Eyes Test

In 1997, Baron-Cohen published the first version of the 'Reading the Mind in the Eyes' task. The test used photographs of eyes and asked participants to identify the emotion being shown from two options. The results showed that adults with ASD correctly identified fewer emotions than non-ASD participants. However, the researchers identified several practical issues:

  • Core concept: 自闭症谱系人士缺乏「心智理论」(Theory of Mind) → 难以理解他人情绪
  • Original test flaws: 1997年版只有2选项/25题 → 天花板效应太明显
  • Revised improvements: 4选项/36题/仅复杂情绪/男女平衡/词汇表
  • Key term: Mentalising = 心智化过程；Empathy = 共情能力

2. Aim & Hypotheses（研究目的与假设）

🎯

Research Aims

Testing the revised Eyes test as a measure of ToM deficit in ASD

Main aim: Investigate whether an improved 'revised' version of the Eyes test would show a clear impairment in adults with ASD.

Secondary aims: Test association between Eyes test scores and AQ traits; investigate sex differences.

Five Hypotheses（五个假设）

  1. Participants with ASD will score significantly lower on the revised Eyes test than control groups. Must-know
  2. Participants with ASD will score significantly higher on the Autism Spectrum Quotient (AQ) than controls. Must-know
  3. Females in 'normal' groups would score higher on the Eyes test than males.
  4. Males in 'normal' group would score higher on the AQ measure than females.
  5. Scores on AQ and Eyes test would be negatively correlated. Common
§2 Aim & Hypotheses 核心逻辑
  • Main aim: 验证改进版 Eyes Test 能否检测出 ASD 人群的 ToM 缺陷
  • H1 (核心): ASD 组得分显著低于对照组 → 支持 ToM 缺陷假设
  • H2: ASD 组 AQ 分数更高 → 自闭特质更多
  • H5: AQ 与 Eyes Test 负相关 → 特质越多，识别越差
▸📋 Exam Key Points — AimAO1+AO3
  1. State the main aim of the study Must-know
  2. What were the IV and DV? Must-know — IV: participant group type; DV: Eyes test score (and AQ score)
  3. What is the Autism Spectrum Quotient (AQ)? Common — Self-report questionnaire measuring autistic traits (0–50)

3. Method（研究方法）

🔬

Research Design

Quasi-experiment with independent measures design

3.1 Research Method & Design

This was a laboratory experiment（实验室实验） as participants were tested in a controlled environment.

⚠️ Quasi-experiment（准实验）vs True Experiment

This is a quasi-experiment because participants could NOT be randomly assigned to groups. Group 1 (AS/HFA) participants were assigned based on their diagnosis, not random allocation. This means confounding variables cannot be fully controlled as in a true experiment.

Independent variable (IV)（自变量）： Type of participant group

  • Group 1: Experimental group — participants with AS/HFA
  • Groups 2–4: Comparison/control groups

Design: Independent measures design（独立测量设计） — different participants in each condition.

Dependent variables (DV)（因变量）：

  1. Score on the revised Eyes test (out of 36)
  2. Score on the AQ questionnaire

3.2 Sample（样本）

GroupNCharacteristics
Group 1: AS/HFA15All adult males, Age 29.7, IQ 115 ↓
Group 2: Adult comparison122Age 46.5, general population
Group 3: Student comparison103Age 20.8, Cambridge students
Group 4: IQ matched14Age 28, IQ 116 (matched to G1)
Table: Participant groups and characteristics

Group Details

G1 (AS/HFA): 15 adult males, mean IQ 115, age 29.7. Self-selected through magazine adverts and support groups. Diagnosed via DSM/ICD criteria.

G2 (Adult): 122 'normal' adults from community classes in Exeter, mean age 46.5.

G3 (Student): 103 Cambridge students without AS/HFA diagnosis, mean age 20.8.

G4 (IQ matched): 14 participants matched to G1's IQ (mean 116), randomly selected from general population.

💡 Key point: Group 4 is crucial — it controls for the possibility that any differences found are due to intelligence rather than ASD itself. If G1 scores lower than G4 (same IQ), the deficit is specific to ToM, not general cognitive ability.
§3 Method 核心逻辑
  • Design: 准实验 + 独立测量设计（不能随机分配到 ASD 组）
  • IV: 参与者组别类型（ASD vs 对照）；DV: Eyes Test 得分 + AQ 分数
  • G4 的作用: IQ 匹配对照组 → 排除智力因素影响
  • Sample issue: G1 只有 15 人且全为男性 → 代表性有限
▸📋 Exam Key Points — MethodAO1+AO3
  1. Why is this a quasi-experiment? Must-know — Participants cannot be randomly allocated to ASD/non-ASD conditions
  2. What is the purpose of Group 4? Must-know — IQ-matched control to rule out intelligence as confounding variable
  3. Evaluate the sample size of Group 1 AO3 — Only 15 participants, limits generalisability
  4. What design was used? Common — Independent measures design

4. Procedure（程序）

📋

How the Revised Eyes Test Works

Standardised administration with glossary support

  1. Participants read through a glossary（词汇表） of emotion words before starting
  2. They could refer back to the glossary at any time during the test
  3. Started with 40 sets of eyes (target word + 3 foil words per set)
  4. Judges validated that target words were the most appropriate (≥5/8 agreement)
  5. 4 control items removed due to inconsistent results → final 36 sets (18 male, 18 female faces)
  6. Test completed in a quiet room in Exeter or Cambridge
  7. Gender recognition task given to Group 1 only (control task)
  8. All participants gave informed consent; data kept anonymous
Figure: Glossary examples ("Flustered" and "Relaxed")
Table: Differences between original and revised Eyes test
💡 Control Task — Gender Recognition: Pilot studies showed 'normal' adults achieve ~100% on gender recognition. This task checks whether Group 1 can process visual information about eyes at all. If they can identify gender but not emotions, it confirms the deficit is specifically in ToM, not general visual processing.
§4 Procedure 核心逻辑
  • Standardised procedure: 先看词汇表 → 做 40 题 → 去掉 4 道控制题 → 最终 36 题
  • Foil words: 每题 1 个正确答案 + 3 个干扰项（由评委验证）
  • Control task: 性别识别任务 → 确认 ASD 组能处理眼睛的视觉信息
  • Ethical safeguards: 知情同意 + 数据匿名化

📊

Main Findings

AS/HFA group scored significantly lower on Eyes test

📊 Key Data Interpretation

  • AS/HFA group averaged only 21.9/36 (61%) — significantly lower than ALL control groups
  • IQ-matched controls scored 30.9/36 (86%) → 9-point gap! Rules out IQ as explanation
  • Student controls highest at 28.0/36 (78%)
  • → Supports hypothesis that ASD involves a specific ToM deficit

5.2 AQ Scores

AS/HFA group scored significantly higher on AQ (34.4) than student (18.3) and IQ-matched (18.9) groups.

Figure: Average AQ scores across groups

5.3 Sex Differences

Evidence of sex differences in comparison groups: males scored slightly lower on Eyes test and higher on AQ than females. However, some differences were not statistically significant — further research needed.

5.4 Correlation: AQ × Eyes Test

Significant negative correlation: r = −0.53 (moderate strength). No correlation between IQ and Eyes test scores.

🔬 r = −0.53 interpretation: Higher AQ (more autistic traits) → Lower Eyes test scores. And this correlation is independent of IQ! This means the Eyes Test measures social cognition specifically, not general intelligence.
§5 Results 核心逻辑
  • Main finding: ASD 组 21.9/36 vs IQ 匹配组 30.9/36 → 显著差异！
  • IQ ruled out: G1 和 G4 智商相同但 Eyes Test 差距大 → 不是智商问题
  • AQ correlation: r = −0.53 中等负相关 → 自闭特质越多，识别越差
  • Sex diff: 有趋势但不完全显著 → 需要更多研究

  2. Why is Group 4 important for interpreting results? Must-know — Same IQ as G1 but much higher score (30.9) → deficit is specific to ToM

6. Conclusion（结论）

✅

Conclusions Drawn

ToM deficit confirmed; revised test is valid measure

  1. AS/HFA group had a specific deficit in the cognitive process for identifying emotions in others → supports Theory of Mind impairment
  2. Validated by accurate gender recognition by AS/HFA group → they CAN process eye information, just not emotional content
  3. Eyes test detects subtle, specific impairment in otherwise intelligent participants
  4. Sex differences evident but not fully significant — more research needed
  5. Revised Eyes test is a more sensitive measure of adult social intelligence than previous versions
§6 Conclusion 核心逻辑
  • Core conclusion: ASD 人群存在特定的 ToM 缺陷（不是一般智力问题）
  • Validation evidence: 性别识别正常 → 视觉处理没问题，只是情绪识别有问题
  • Test validity: 改进版 Eyes Test 是更敏感的社会认知测量工具

7. Strengths & Weaknesses（优缺点评价）

⚖️

Critical Evaluation

Assessing reliability, validity, and generalisability

Strengths（优点）

  1. Standardised procedure: All participants tested same way (quiet room, same instructions, glossary) → improves internal validity
  2. Controlled variables: Researcher-administered reduces distortion; gender recognition task controls for basic visual processing ability
  3. Multiple comparison groups: 4 groups including IQ-matched strengthens conclusion that differences are due to ASD, not IQ or age
  4. Pilot study conducted: Checked procedures beforehand → improves reliability
  5. Improved over original: Fixed ceiling effect, increased difficulty, removed bias → more sensitive to individual differences
  6. Quantitative data: Easy statistical analysis, objective scoring
  7. Cross-sectional design: Quick data collection across different populations

Weaknesses（缺点）

  1. Quasi-experiment limitations: Cannot randomly allocate → confounding variables possible. Partly resolved by G4 but not fully eliminated
  2. Small sample (G1): Only 15 AS/HFA participants → may not represent whole ASD population. Caution with generalisation.
  3. Ecological validity concerns: Laboratory setting; isolated eyes (not whole faces); static images (not videos/live faces). May not apply to real-world emotion recognition
  4. Demand characteristics: Participants knew it was a 'psychology test' → may have influenced behaviour
  5. Cultural bias: All photos were white/Caucasian faces → may not generalise to other ethnicities
  6. No longitudinal data: Cannot show causation, only correlation between AQ and Eyes test
  7. Male-only G1: All 15 AS/HFA participants were male → cannot generalise findings to females with ASD
§7 Evaluation 核心逻辑
  • Biggest strength: 4组设计含 IQ 匹配对照组 → 强力排除混淆变量
  • Biggest weakness: G1 仅 15 人 + 全男性 → 代表性和推广性受限
  • EV concern: 实验室环境 + 孤立眼睛图片 → 与真实社交情境差距大
  • Culture bias: 全部白人面孔 → 跨文化适用性存疑
▸📋 Exam Key Points — EvaluationAO3
  1. Explain one strength of using multiple comparison groups Must-know
  2. Explain one weakness of the sample Must-know — Small N, male-only, self-selected
  3. Discuss ecological validity concerns Common — Lab setting, isolated eyes, static images
  4. Why is this a quasi-experiment and what problem does this create? AO3

9. Summary（总结）

Baron-Cohen et al.'s study investigated whether adults with autism spectrum disorder (ASD) show a specific deficit in Theory of Mind using the revised Eyes test. Key findings:

  1. ASD group scored significantly lower (21.9/36) than all control groups including IQ-matched (30.9/36)
  2. Negative correlation (r = −0.53) between AQ and Eyes test scores — independent of IQ
  3. Revised Eyes test is a valid and sensitive measure of adult social intelligence
  4. Findings support the cognitive explanation of ASD-related social difficulties

11. Exam Practice Questions（考试练习题）

Q1[AO1] What is meant by the term 'theory of mind'? (2 marks)▾
Model Answer: Theory of mind is the cognitive ability to understand that other people have different thoughts, beliefs, desires, and feelings from one's own. It enables us to comprehend others' mental states and predict their behaviour based on those states. In the context of this study, it refers to the ability to recognise emotions from facial expressions (specifically the eye region).
Q2[AO1] Outline one problem with the original Eyes test. (2 marks)▾
Model Answer: One problem was the ceiling effect — with only 25 items and 2 options (always opposites like sympathetic/unsympathetic), many participants scored near-perfect (24/25), making it impossible to distinguish between individuals with good vs excellent ToM abilities. Another issue: some items could be solved by checking gaze direction rather than recognising emotions.
Q3[AO1] Describe how Baron-Cohen et al. operationalised the independent variable. (3 marks)▾
Model Answer: The IV was participant group type. It was operationalised by assigning participants to one of four groups: Group 1 (adults diagnosed with AS or HFA), Group 2 (normal adults from community), Group 3 (normal university students), and Group 4 (IQ-matched controls). Participants were NOT randomly assigned — Group 1 was selected based on existing diagnosis, making this a quasi-experiment.
Q4[AO1] Describe the sample used in Group 1. Include recruitment method. (4 marks)▾
Model Answer: Group 1 consisted of 15 adult males with a mean age of 29.7 years and mean IQ of 115. All had been formally diagnosed with either Asperger syndrome or high-functioning autism using DSM or ICD criteria. They were self-selected through advertisements placed in the Autistic Society magazine and through autism support groups. This represents an opportunity sample.
Q5[AO3] Explain one strength of using multiple comparison groups. (3 marks)▾
Model Answer: One strength is that having four groups including an IQ-matched control (Group 4) allows researchers to rule out intelligence as a confounding variable. Since Group 1 (AS/HFA, IQ=115) and Group 4 (IQ-matched, IQ=116) have similar intelligence but very different Eyes test scores (21.9 vs 30.9), the deficit cannot be attributed to general cognitive ability. This strengthens the conclusion that the difference is specifically due to Theory of Mind impairment associated with ASD. Must-know
Q6[AO3] Explain one weakness of the sample and suggest how it could be improved. (4 marks)▾
Model Answer: One weakness is the very small sample size of Group 1 (only 15 participants). This limits the generalisability of findings to the wider ASD population. Additionally, all 15 were male, so results cannot be generalised to females with ASD. Improvement: Increase sample size to at least 30+ and include both male and female participants with ASD to improve representativeness and allow for sex-based analysis. Must-know
Q7[AO3] Discuss ecological validity issues in this study. (6 marks)▾
Model Answer: Ecological validity concerns whether findings apply to real-life situations. Issues include: (1) Laboratory setting — participants tested in quiet rooms, unlike natural social interactions; (2) Isolated eye stimuli — in real life we see whole faces, not just eyes, which provide additional cues (mouth, expression context); (3) Static photographs — real emotions are dynamic and change over time; (4) Cultural bias — all white/Caucasian faces may not apply cross-culturally. However, isolating eyes increases internal validity by controlling extraneous variables. Common
Q8[AO3] Assess whether the Eyes test has good reliability and validity. (8 marks)▾
Model Answer: Validity: The test has construct validity — it successfully discriminates between ASD and non-ASD groups as predicted by ToM theory. The gender recognition control task shows it measures something specific (emotion recognition, not just visual processing). However, ecological validity is questionable (lab setting, isolated eyes). Reliability: Standardised procedure improves reliability. Pilot testing checked item consistency. However, the small G1 sample (N=15) makes it hard to assess test-retest reliability within the ASD group. Overall, the test shows reasonable validity for its intended purpose but has limitations in ecological validity. AO3
