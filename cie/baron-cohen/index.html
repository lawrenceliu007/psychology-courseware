1️⃣ Cognitive Approach Assumptions（认知取向的主要假设）

🧠

Cognitive Approach Core Assumptions

Mental processes mediate behaviour · Active information processing

Humans as Information Processors（人作为信息加工者）

The cognitive approach assumes that mental processes（心理过程） lie between stimulus and response. Unlike behaviourists who only study observable behaviour, cognitive psychologists are interested in what happens inside the mind — how we perceive, remember, think, and solve problems.

The two core assumptions of the cognitive approach are:

  • Mediational processes: Mental processes (such as thinking, perception, attention, memory) act as mediators between environmental stimuli and our behavioural responses. We don't just respond to the world — we interpret it first.
  • Active information processing: Humans actively process information from the environment, much like a computer inputs, stores, and retrieves data. We are not passive recipients of sensory information.

Theory of Mind（心理理论）

A key concept within cognitive psychology is Theory of Mind (ToM)（心理理论） — the ability to attribute mental states (beliefs, intentions, desires, emotions) to oneself and others, and to understand that others have beliefs different from our own.

ToM develops in typically developing children around ages 3–5. It is considered a core cognitive deficit（认知缺陷） in Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD). Baron-Cohen's Eyes Test was specifically designed to measure this ability — can people infer complex emotional states from limited visual information (just the eye region)?

Cognitive Approach & This Study 认知取向与本研究的联系：
The Eyes Test measures a purely cognitive process: interpreting mental states from facial cues. It is not about social pressure (Social Approach) or learned behaviour (Learning Approach) — it is about how the mind processes social information. This is why Baron-Cohen sits firmly within the Cognitive Approach.
（Eyes Test 测量的是纯粹的认知过程：从面部线索解读心理状态。它不涉及社会压力或学习行为——而是关于大脑如何"加工"社会信息。）
§1 认知取向的两大核心假设

核心逻辑：行为主义只看刺激→反应（S→R），但认知心理学认为中间还有心理过程（S → O（Organism，有机体/大脑加工） → R）。

  • Mediational Processes（中介过程）：思考、感知、注意、记忆——这些心理活动在刺激和反应之间起"中介"作用
  • Active Information Processing（主动信息加工）：人像电脑一样主动输入、存储、提取信息，不是被动接收

本研究的关键概念：

  • Theory of Mind / ToM（心理理论）：理解他人有与自己不同的信念、意图、情绪的能力
  • ToM 在典型发展儿童中 3-5 岁出现；在 ASD 中被认为是核心缺陷
  • Eyes Test 就是测量 ToM 的工具——仅从眼睛区域推断复杂情绪状态

💡 考试要点：CIE 可能问 "outline two assumptions of the cognitive approach"。答案模板：(1) Mental processes mediate between S-R; (2) Humans are active information processors.

▶ Exam Key Points — Cognitive Approach AO1+AO2
  • Two assumptions: (1) Mediational processes between S-R; (2) Humans as active info processors Must Know
  • Theory of Mind = ability to attribute mental states to self/others; core deficit in ASD Must Know
  • Eyes Test measures ToM via emotion recognition from eyes only — pure cognitive task Common Q
  • AO2: Contrast with Social Approach (Milgram = external social pressure; Baron-Cohen = internal cognitive process) AO2 Gold

2️⃣ Background（研究背景）

🧠

Big Picture: Why This Study Matters

ASD affects ~1% of population → Theory of Mind deficit hypothesis → Need better adult test → Revised Eyes Test

The psychology being investigated（研究的心理现象）

A person with an autism spectrum disorder（自闭症谱系障碍） does not fully develop cognitive processes linked to social interaction. ASD occurs in approximately 1% of the population. Individuals with ASD share difficulties in social functioning, such as limited social sensitivity, communication and ability to cope with change, and may have narrow interests.

The term ASD is now used to refer to both high-functioning autism (HFA)（高功能自闭症） and Asperger syndrome (AS)（阿斯伯格综合征）, however the terms HFA and AS were used at the time that this study was published.

Baron-Cohen et al. suggest that people with ASD lack or have an underdeveloped cognitive process called a theory of mind（心智理论）. A theory of mind is a cognitive ability enabling people to comprehend that others have different feelings, beliefs, knowledge and desires from their own. Individuals with ASD find it difficult to understand that other people have their own plans, thoughts or feelings separate from their own.

Empathy（共情）

Empathy is the ability to understand the world as another person does — to appreciate their feelings or emotional state separate from our own. The Eyes Test is an advanced test of empathy: identifying complex mental states from the eye region alone requires exactly this skill, which is why it was chosen to measure theory of mind in adults.

From the Original (1997) to the Revised (2001) Eyes Test

In 1997, Baron-Cohen published the first version of the 'Reading the Mind in the Eyes' task using photographs of eyes with two options per item. The results showed that adults with ASD correctly identified fewer emotions than non-ASD participants, but several practical problems limited the usefulness of the original test:

Problems with the original (1997) Eyes Test and how the revised (2001) version solved them
Problem with the original testSolution in the revised version
Only 2 choices per item, always semantic opposites (e.g. sympathetic vs unsympathetic) — a 50% chance of guessing correctly4 choices, and the foil words are no longer semantic opposites
Only 25 sets of eyes, producing a ceiling effect（天花板效应）— many participants scored 24/25, so individual differences could not be detected36 sets of eyes in the final analysis, making the test harder and more sensitive
Basic and complex emotions were mixed — the complex emotions were too hard for some participantsOnly complex emotions / mental states are used (e.g. 'sceptical', 'reassured')
Some items could be solved using gaze direction alone — no theory of mind neededItems solvable by direction of gaze were deleted
Imbalance of male and female faces in the photographsEqual numbers of male and female eyes (18 of each)
Participants might not know the meaning of some target or foil wordsA glossary of all the words was provided and could be consulted at any time
§2 背景：为什么需要修订版 Eyes Test？

研究链条：ASD 约影响 1% 人口 → 核心困难在社会交往 → Baron-Cohen 认为根源是 ToM（心智理论）发育不足 → 需要一个能精确测量成人 ToM 的工具。

1997 原版六大问题 → 2001 修订版方案：

  • 2 选 1（50% 猜对率）→ 4 选 1（干扰词不再是对义词）
  • 只有 25 题，太简单（ceiling effect）→ 36 题
  • 基本+复杂情绪混杂 → 只用复杂情绪
  • 看视线方向就能猜对 → 删除这类题
  • 男女照片不均 → 各 18 张
  • 怕不认识词汇 → 提供 Glossary（词汇表）

Empathy（共情）：设身处地理解他人感受的能力。Eyes Test 本质上是一个进阶共情测试——仅凭眼部区域识别复杂心理状态。

3️⃣ Aim（研究目的）

The main aim: To investigate whether an improved 'revised' version of the Eyes test would show a clear impairment in a group of adults with ASD (Autism Spectrum Disorder), and to assess its effectiveness as a measure of Theory of Mind.

Secondary aims:

  • To test whether there was an association between performance on the revised Eyes test and measures of autistic traits (AQ)
  • To investigate whether there were sex differences in those without ASD on this task

Five Hypotheses（五个假设）

  1. Participants with ASD will score significantly lower on the revised Eyes test than the control groups. Must Know
  2. Participants with ASD will score significantly higher on the Autism Spectrum Quotient (AQ)（自闭症谱系商数） than controls. Common Q
  3. Females in 'normal' groups (Groups 2 & 3) would score higher on the Eyes test than males. AO2 Eval
  4. Males in 'normal' group (Group 3) would score higher on the AQ measure than females. Context
  5. Scores on AQ and Eyes test would be negatively correlated. Must Know

核心问题：自闭症患者是否真的无法理解他人的心理状态？

逻辑链：Baron-Cohen 提出 ToM 缺陷是 ASD 核心特征 → 原版 Eyes Test 有缺陷（性别偏差、太难）→ 开发修订版 Eyes Test → 验证 ASD 组得分是否显著低于对照组 → 同时检验与 AQ 量表的相关性和性别差异。

考试要点：记住 5 个假设，特别是 H1（ASD 得分更低）和 H5（AQ 与 Eyes Test 负相关）。这是 CIE 最常考的两个。

Exam Key Points — Aim AO1
  • Main aim: Test if revised Eyes test shows impairment in ASD adults Must Know
  • H1: ASD group scores LOWER than controls (central prediction) Must Know
  • H5: Negative correlation between AQ and Eyes test scores Must Know
  • "Revised" = removed gender-biased items, added easier foils Context
  • Secondary aims: AQ association + sex differences in normal groups Common Q

4️⃣ Method（研究方法）

Research Method and Design

This was a quasi-experiment（准实验） — participants could NOT be randomly assigned to conditions. Group 1 (AS/HFA) participants were selected based on their pre-existing diagnosis, not random allocation.

Why quasi-experiment? You cannot randomly assign someone to have ASD or not! This is a naturally occurring variable. The trade-off: we lose some control over confounding variables, but gain ecological validity (testing real people with real diagnoses).

Independent Variable (IV)（自变量）： Participant group membership

  • Group 1: Experimental group — adults with AS/HFA (n=15)
  • Groups 2–4: Comparison / control groups

Design: Independent measures design（独立测量设计） — different participants in each condition. Must Know

Dependent Variables (DV)（因变量）：

  1. Score on the revised Eyes test (out of 36) Must Know
  2. Score on the Autism Spectrum Quotient (AQ)（自闭症谱系商数）

为什么是准实验？因为自变量（是否有 ASD）是参与者本来就有的特征，不能随机分配。这就像比较"男生 vs 女生"——你不能随机指定某人的性别。

设计优势：独立测量设计避免了顺序效应（practice effects）；Group 4 的 IQ 匹配控制了智商这个混淆变量。

设计弱点：Group 1 全是男性（性别混淆）；Group 2 年龄大很多（年龄混淆）。这些是 AO2 评价的要点！

Exam Key Points — Method AO1+AO2
  • Quasi-experiment — IV is pre-existing (ASD diagnosis), not manipulated Must Know
  • Independent measures — different Ps in each group (no order effects) Must Know
  • DV1: Eyes Test score (max 36); DV2: AQ score Must Know
  • Total N = 254 (15 + 122 + 103 + 14) Common Q
  • Group 4 = IQ matched control (rules out IQ as confound) AO2 Gold
  • Confounds: Group 1 all-male; Group 2 much older AO2 Gold

5️⃣ Procedure（实验程序）

How the Revised Eyes Test Works

Each participant completed the test in a quiet room at Exeter or Cambridge, with a researcher present.

Step-by-Step Procedure

  1. Glossary phase: Participants read through a glossary of emotion words the experimenter used. They could refer back to it anytime during the test.
  2. Test phase: Viewed 40 sets of eyes initially. For each set, chose 1 correct emotion word from 4 options (1 target + 3 foils).
  3. Validation process: Target words and foil words were chosen by Baron-Cohen & Wheelwright, then validated by 8 independent judges. At least 5/8 had to agree the target word was most appropriate.
  4. Control items removed: 4 items produced inconsistent results → removed from analysis. Final: 36 sets (18 male eyes, 18 female eyes).
  5. AQ questionnaire: After the Eyes test, participants completed the Autism Spectrum Quotient questionnaire.
  6. Control task (Group 1 only): Gender recognition task (to confirm Group 1 could identify physical eye features without needing ToM). Pilot showed 'normal' adults score ~100% on this.
📖 Emotion Glossary Examples（情绪词汇表示例）:
Flustered慌张的、不知所措的
Relaxed放松的、自在的

💡 What are foil words? Each eye photo has one target word (correct answer) and three foil words (plausible but incorrect distractors). Example: if the target is "playful", foils might be "serious", "bored", "irritated". Judges ensured the target was clearly the best fit.

Ethical safeguards: All participants gave informed consent. Data were anonymous — scores could not be traced back to individuals.

程序核心：看眼睛照片 → 从 4 个词选 1 个最匹配的情绪 → 36 道题 → 算分。

为什么有 Glossary？因为有些情绪词汇很难（如 "flustered", "contemplative"），不给词汇表的话，测试的是词汇量而不是心理理论能力。

为什么删了 4 题？因为评委们对那 4 道题的答案达不成一致——说明题目本身有问题，不是参与者的能力问题。

考试要点：36 道（不是 40 道）、18 男 18 女、target + 3 foils、Glossary 可随时参考。

Exam Key Points — Procedure AO1
  • Quiet room, individual testing with researcher present Context
  • Glossary provided (can refer back anytime) — controls for vocabulary Must Know
  • Started with 40 items → 4 removed (unreliable) → final 36 items (18M + 18F) Must Know
  • Each item: 1 target word + 3 foil words (validated by ≥5/8 judges) Common Q
  • Control task: Gender recognition (Group 1 only) — confirms they can see physical features AO2 Eval
  • Informed consent + anonymous data Context

AQ Scores: AS/HFA Much Higher

On the AQ task, participants with AS/HFA scored significantly higher than the Student comparison and IQ matched comparison groups.

Sex Differences

There was evidence of a sex difference: males scored lower on the Eyes test and showed greater levels of autistic traits on the AQ test than females. However, some of these differences were not significant so further research is needed.

Correlation between AQ and Eyes Test

There was a significant negative correlation between the AQ and Eyes test scores (r = −0.53) but no correlation between IQ and Eyes test scores. This suggests that as AQ score increases (greater autistic traits), the ability to identify correct emotions decreases — and this is independent of intelligence.

Results 数据解读总结

🔴 Core Finding（核心发现）：AS/HFA 组 Eyes Test 平均 21.9/36（61%），而 IQ 匹配对照组高达 30.9/36（86%）——差距近 9 分，统计显著！

这意味着什么？

  1. ASD 的情绪识别困难是真实的，不是因为智商低（Group 1 IQ=115 vs Group 4 IQ=116 几乎相同）
  2. 支持 ToM 缺陷假设：问题出在特定的社会认知能力，而非一般智力
  3. Revised Eyes Test 比 1997 原版更有效——能检测到原版无法区分的个体差异

📊 AQ 相关性：r = −0.53（中等负相关）。自闭症特质越多 → 情绪识别越差。且与 IQ 无关 → 说明测的是特定能力。

👤 性别差异：男性在 Eyes Test 上得分更低、AQ 得分更高，但部分差异未达显著水平 → 需要更多研究。

考试答题模板：如果问 "What do results show?" → 先报核心数据（21.9 vs 30.9），再解释意义（specific ToM deficit, independent of IQ），最后提相关性（r = −0.53）。

7️⃣ Conclusion（结论）

💡

What the Results Mean

Specific ToM deficit in ASD (not general cognitive) → Eyes Test is valid measure of adult social intelligence

The results indicate that the participants in the AS/HFA group had a specific deficit（特定缺陷） in a cognitive process that should help them to identify emotions in other individuals, i.e. contribute to a theory of mind. This conclusion is validated by the AS/HFA group being able to identify the gender of the eyes accurately — showing that the Eyes test detected a subtle and specific impairment in otherwise intelligent participants. Must Know

There was evidence of a sex difference: males lower on the Eyes test, and showed greater levels of autistic traits on the AQ test, than females. However, some differences were not significant.

This study showed that the revised Eyes test is a more sensitive measure of adult social intelligence than previous studies, allowing future research to discriminate individual differences more meaningfully.

Conclusion 结论逻辑链

核心论点：AS/HFA 组在 Eyes Test 上的表现差 → 不是因为一般认知能力差 → 而是 Theory of Mind (ToM) 有特定缺陷（specific deficit）。

证据链条：

  1. AS/HFA 组识别情绪差：21.9/36 vs 对照组 26-31分 ✅
  2. 但 AS/HFA 组能准确判断性别：说明视觉处理没问题 ❌ 排除一般视觉缺陷
  3. AQ 与得分负相关 (r=−0.53)：自闭症特质越多越差，且与 IQ 无关 ❌ 排除智力因素

结论：Eyes Test 测量的是一种特定的社会认知能力（识别他人情绪状态），而非一般智力或基本视觉能力。

实践意义：Revised Eyes Test (2001) 比 Original (1997) 更敏感、更有效——能区分个体差异，可用于成人 ASD 的诊断辅助工具。

性别发现：男性 Eyes Test 更低 + AQ 更高 → 支持 "Extreme Male Brain" theory of autism（自闭症的极端男性大脑理论）。

▶ Exam Key Points — Conclusion AO1+AO2
  1. Specific deficit in ToM: ASD group worse at emotion recognition BUT can identify gender → proves it's a specific social cognitive deficit, not general visual problem. Must Know
  2. Revised test is MORE VALID: Better at detecting individual differences than original (1997) version. Common Q
  3. Sex difference found: Males lower on Eyes Test + higher AQ. Supports "extreme male brain" theory of autism. AO2 Eval

8️⃣ Evaluation

⚖️

Evaluation Overview

4 Strengths (standardisation, control task, IQ-matched group, quantitative data) vs 4 Weaknesses (quasi-experiment, sample, ecological validity, correlation)

Strengths

  1. Standardised procedure with high control. Control of Variables Every participant completed the same items in a quiet room, with the same instructions and free access to the glossary, and each target word had been validated by at least 5 of 8 independent judges before testing. Because the test was administered identically to every group, the score differences (21.9 for Group 1 vs 26.2–30.9 for the comparison groups) can be attributed to the participant variable (AS/HFA) rather than to the way the test was run — improving internal validity.
  2. The gender-recognition control task. Control of Variables Group 1 also completed a gender-recognition task using the same eye photographs and scored at least 33/36. This rules out the alternative explanation that the AS/HFA participants simply could not perceive the stimuli: they could process the physical features of the eyes, but not the mental states behind them. This makes the conclusion of a specific theory-of-mind deficit much stronger.
  3. Objective quantitative data. Types of Data Both dependent variables produced numerical scores (Eyes test out of 36; AQ out of 50). Group comparisons and the correlation (r = −0.53) could therefore be analysed statistically rather than relying on researchers' subjective interpretations, making the conclusions more objective and easier for other researchers to verify.
  4. An IQ-matched comparison group. Validity Group 4 was randomly selected from the general population and matched to Group 1 on intelligence (IQ 116 vs 115). Because these IQ-matched controls still scored far higher on the Eyes test (30.9 vs 21.9), and because there was no correlation between IQ and Eyes test scores, intelligence is ruled out as an explanation for Group 1's poorer performance — strongly supporting the claim of a specific deficit.

Weaknesses

  1. A quasi-experiment with no random allocation. Control of Variables Participants could not be randomly assigned to have AS/HFA or not — the IV was a naturally occurring difference between people. Other uncontrolled participant variables could therefore partly explain the results: Group 1 were all male while the comparison groups were mixed, and Group 2 were much older (mean age 46.5 vs 29.7). This lowers the internal validity of any causal-style conclusion.
  2. A small, self-selecting clinical sample. Sampling Group 1 contained only 15 adults, all male, who volunteered in response to adverts in an autism magazine. People who volunteer for a study about autism may differ from the wider AS/HFA population, and 15 participants cannot capture the full range of ability, so the findings may not generalise to all adults with AS/HFA (particularly females) — limiting population validity.
  3. Low ecological validity of the stimuli. Validity In everyday life, emotions are read from whole moving faces, bodies, voices and situational context — not from static black-and-white photographs of the eye region alone. Performance on this laboratory task may not reflect real-world emotion recognition, so the findings may not generalise beyond the test situation.
  4. Correlation does not establish cause. Validity The significant negative correlation between AQ and Eyes test scores (r = −0.53) shows only an association. The study cannot show the direction of the relationship or rule out a third variable affecting both measures, so the link between autistic traits and mentalising ability remains suggestive rather than proven.
▶ Exam Key Points — Evaluation AO3
  1. Strength — control task: gender recognition ≥33/36 proves the AS/HFA group could see the eyes but not read the minds — a specific deficit, not a visual one. Must Know
  2. Strength — Group 4 (IQ-matched, 116 vs 115): rules out intelligence as an explanation (30.9 vs 21.9). Must Know
  3. Weakness — quasi-experiment: no random allocation; Group 1 all male, Group 2 much older (46.5 vs 29.7) — confounding participant variables. Must Know
  4. Weakness — sample: n = 15, all male, self-selected via magazine adverts — limited generalisability. Common Q
  5. Weakness — ecological validity: static eye-region photos vs whole moving faces in real life. Common Q
  6. Weakness — r = −0.53 is a correlation: association, not cause. Common Q
  7. Further points: participants knew they were taking a 'psychology test' (demand characteristics); all photographs showed Caucasian faces (cultural generalisability limited). Context

9️⃣ Ethics（伦理问题）

As all participants were able to give informed consent（知情同意）and their data was kept confidential, few ethical issues were raised within the study itself. However, ethical issues could arise from the findings! Evidence identifying 'impaired' performance of the AS/HFA group could be seen as representing neurodiverse groups in a negative way.

Alternatively, by identifying such differences, this research has the capacity to provide understanding of the nature of the experience of people with ASD, potentially leading to greater understanding in situations such as school or the workplace. This would be an ethical strength（伦理优势）.

Ethical Issues 伦理分析

✅ 研究过程本身伦理风险低：

  • 参与者均能给出 Informed Consent（知情同意）
  • 数据保密匿名处理
  • 无欺骗、无伤害性操作

但研究发现可能引发伦理争议：

  • 污名化风险：结果表明 AS/HFA 组"表现受损" → 可能被用来负面描述神经多样性群体
  • 标签效应："自闭症人士缺乏心智理论"这个结论可能加剧社会歧视

🔄 反方论点（伦理优势）：

  • 识别差异 → 促进对 ASD 人群体验的理解
  • 帮助学校/职场提供更好的支持环境
  • 推动诊断工具改进 → 早期干预

考试要点：CIE 伦理题常考 "Discuss ethical issues"。答案结构：①研究过程本身没问题（consent, confidentiality）②但研究结果的应用可能带来伦理问题（stigma vs understanding）③两边都要提到才算 balanced。

▶ Exam Key Points — Ethics AO3
  1. Few ethical issues IN the study: Informed consent given, data anonymous, participants could withdraw. Context
  2. Ethical issue FROM findings: Could stigmatise neurodiverse people as "impaired". But also promotes ASD understanding → net ethical strength. AO2 Gold

🔟 Summary（总结）

Baron-Cohen et al.'s study investigated how a lack of 'theory of mind' in adults with autism spectrum disorder (ASD) relates to problems with recognising emotions. The ASD group's scores on the Eyes test were lower than those of the control group even though their IQ was no different. There was also a negative correlation between the Eyes test score and Autistic Spectrum Quotient. The findings suggest the revised Eyes test is better at detecting individual differences in social sensitivity than the previous version, i.e. it is more valid（更有效度）.

▶ Exam Key Points — Overall Summary AO1+AO3
  1. One-sentence summary: ASD adults show specific ToM deficit on Eyes Test (21.9 vs ~28-31), independent of IQ, supporting cognitive explanation of autism. Must Know
  2. For 8-mark "assess" questions: Balance strengths (standardised, IQ-matched, quantitative) vs weaknesses (n=15, quasi, EV issues). Always conclude: useful but limitations exist. Common Q
  3. Link to other studies: Compare with Theory of Mind studies; contrast with biological explanations of autism. Context

1️⃣2️⃣ Study Questions（考试练习题）

1 [AO1, 2 marks] What is meant by the term 'theory of mind'? ▶

Model Answer: Theory of mind (ToM) is the cognitive ability to attribute mental states (beliefs, intentions, desires, emotions) to oneself and others, and to understand that other people have beliefs and mental states different from one's own. It is considered a core cognitive deficit in Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD).

2 [AO1, 2 marks] Outline one problem with the original Eyes test. ▶

Model Answer: The original Eyes test (Baron-Cohen et al., 1997) had a ceiling effect（天花板效应） — it was too easy for adults and typically developing adolescents, meaning most participants scored near maximum. This made it impossible to distinguish between individuals with good ToM abilities. The Revised version (2001) used more complex emotions and harder foil words to solve this problem.

3 [AO1, 3 marks] Describe how Baron-Cohen et al. operationalised the independent variable in this study. ▶

Model Answer: The independent variable (IV) was the type of participant, operationalised as group membership: Group 1 (15 adults with AS/HFA, all formally diagnosed using DSM/ICD criteria) compared with three comparison groups — Group 2 (122 adults from Exeter), Group 3 (103 students from Cambridge University) and Group 4 (14 adults randomly selected from the general population and matched to Group 1 on IQ). The IV was a naturally occurring (quasi) variable — participants could not be randomly allocated to conditions — and an independent measures design（独立测量设计） was used, because each participant belonged to only one group.

4 [AO1, 4 marks] Describe the sample used in Group 1 (AS/HFA). Include how they were recruited and any relevant demographic information. ▶

Model Answer: Group 1 consisted of 15 adults with Asperger syndrome (AS) or high-functioning autism (HFA) — all male, with a mean age of 29.7 years and a mean IQ of 115 (measured with four subtests of the WAIS-R: block design, vocabulary, similarities and picture completion). They were self-selected volunteers who responded to adverts in an autism magazine, and all had been diagnosed according to DSM/ICD criteria. Because they volunteered for a study on autism rather than being randomly selected, this was a self-selecting (volunteer) sample.

5 [AO1/AO3, 6 marks] Choose one possible emotion that Baron-Cohen et al. could have used.
a) State the emotion.
b) Write a glossary entry.
c) List suitable foils with justification. ▶

Model Answer (example: "anxious"):

(a) Emotion: Anxious（焦虑的）

(b) Glossary entry: "Feeling worried, nervous, or uneasy about something that may happen or has uncertain outcome."

(c) Suitable foils (干扰词):

  • "Terrified" — plausible because also fear-related, but too extreme (anxious ≠ terrified)
  • "Shy" — plausible because involves social discomfort, but shy is about people not future events
  • "Thoughtful" — plausible because serious expression can look like worry, but lacks the unease component

Justification: Good foils must be plausible but clearly wrong — they should share some semantic or visual similarity with the target while being distinguishable by someone with good ToM.

6 [AO3, 4 marks] If Baron-Cohen et al. had found that there was still a ceiling effect, what could have been done to solve this problem? ▶

Model Answer: If a ceiling effect still existed, possible solutions include:

  1. Use even more subtle/complex emotions — e.g., emotions requiring second-order mental state inference ("she looks like she's pretending to be happy")
  2. Increase difficulty of foil words — make foils semantically closer to the correct answer so only those with truly superior ToM can discriminate
  3. Reduce exposure time — limit how long participants can view each photo, increasing cognitive load
  4. Add time pressure or dual-task conditions — make the test more cognitively demanding

The key is to ensure the test can differentiate between high-performing individuals — if everyone scores ~35-36/36, the test lacks discriminant validity（区分效度）for adult populations.

7 [AO3, 3 marks] Explain one strength of using a laboratory experiment in this study. ▶

Model Answer: One strength is high control over extraneous variables（控制无关变量）. In the laboratory setting, all participants saw the same photos in the same order, with standardized instructions and a glossary provided. This control increases internal validity（内部效度） because it reduces confounding variables — any difference in scores between groups can be more confidently attributed to the IV (having AS/HFA or not) rather than other factors like different testing environments or procedures.

8 [AO3, 4 marks] Explain one weakness of the sample used in this study. Suggest how it could be improved. ▶

Model Answer: One weakness is the small, self-selecting clinical sample: Group 1 contained only 15 participants, all male, recruited through adverts in an autism magazine. Volunteers for an autism study may differ from the wider AS/HFA population (for example in motivation or self-awareness), and 15 people cannot represent the full range of ability — so the findings may not generalise to all adults with AS/HFA, especially women (low population validity).

Improvement: Recruit a larger sample (30+ per group) through clinics and diagnostic services rather than adverts alone, and include female participants with AS/HFA so that gender comparisons are possible. A larger, more representative sample would make group differences more reliable and the findings easier to generalise.

9 [AO3, 6 marks] Discuss the ethical issues involved in this type of research. ▶

Model Answer:

Ethical strengths:

  • Informed consent（知情同意）: Participants were volunteers who agreed to take part; they could withdraw at any time
  • No physical/psychological harm: Unlike Milgram's study, this research caused minimal stress — it was a relatively non-invasive cognitive task
  • Confidentiality maintained: Data reported as group means, no individual identifiable

Ethical concerns:

  • Labelling/stigma（标签效应）: Participants with AS/HFA may feel their performance confirms they are "deficient", which could affect self-esteem
  • Privacy of diagnosis: Asking about ASD diagnosis is sensitive personal information

Overall judgement: This study has fewer ethical issues than many psychological studies because it is low-risk and non-deceptive. The main concern is potential stigma around labelling neurodevelopmental differences as "deficits".

10 [AO3, 8 marks] Assess whether the Eyes test has good reliability and validity. Use evidence from the study in your answer. ▶

Model Answer:

Reliability（信度）:

  • Standardised items and scoring: the same 36 photos, the same four fixed choices and the same correct answers were used for every participant, and target words were validated in advance by at least 5 of 8 judges — so the test would produce similar results if repeated under the same conditions
  • Test-retest reliability: not assessed within this study — each participant completed the test only once

Validity（效度）:

  • Concurrent validity: strong — Group 1 scored significantly lower (M = 21.9, SD = 6.6) than Group 2 (M = 26.2, SD = 3.6), Group 3 (M = 28.0, SD = 3.5) and the IQ-matched Group 4 (M = 30.9, SD = 3.0), supporting the claim that the test measures what it intends
  • Construct validity: supported by the significant negative correlation with AQ scores (r = −0.53) — more autistic traits associated with poorer mentalising — and by the absence of any correlation with IQ, showing the test measures a specific social-cognitive ability rather than intelligence
  • Ecological validity（生态效度）: questionable — in real life, we read emotions from whole faces + body language + voice + context, not just the eye region in static photos

Conclusion: The Eyes Test shows good standardisation and strong concurrent and construct validity, though ecological validity is limited and test-retest reliability was not assessed. Overall, it is a useful instrument for detecting individual differences in adult social intelligence.

11 [AO3, 8 marks] To what extent do the findings support the view that people with ASD have a specific deficit in theory of mind? Refer to the data in your answer. ▶

Model Answer:

Supporting evidence (the findings DO support the ToM deficit hypothesis):

  • Group 1 (AS/HFA) scored significantly lower (M = 21.9, SD = 6.6) than Group 2 (M = 26.2, SD = 3.6), Group 3 (M = 28.0, SD = 3.5) and Group 4 (M = 30.9, SD = 3.0)
  • Most importantly, Group 4 was IQ-matched (116 vs 115) yet still scored 9 points higher — and there was no correlation between IQ and Eyes test scores — so the deficit cannot be explained by intelligence
  • The AS/HFA group performed almost perfectly on the gender-recognition control task (≥33/36), showing the difficulty is specific to reading mental states, not to processing the photographs
  • The significant negative correlation between AQ and Eyes test scores (r = −0.53) supports the link between autistic traits and poorer mentalising

Counter-evidence / limitations (caution needed):

  • Group 1's scores varied widely (SD = 6.6), and some individual scores fell within the range of the comparison groups — suggesting the deficit is not equally severe for everyone with AS/HFA
  • The clinical sample was small (n = 15) and all male, limiting generalisability to the wider ASD population
  • The Eyes test measures only one channel of social cognition (static eye-region photographs) — performance may not reflect theory of mind in real-world social interaction

Conclusion: The findings provide strong support for a specific theory-of-mind deficit in adults with AS/HFA: the group difference is large, cannot be explained by IQ or visual processing, and converges with the AQ correlation. However, the small all-male sample, the overlap in score ranges and the narrow laboratory measure mean the conclusion should be stated as a general tendency rather than a universal deficit.
