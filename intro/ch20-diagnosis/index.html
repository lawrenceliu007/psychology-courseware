1. What Is Abnormality? （什么是异常？）

1.1 Defining Abnormality

Abnormality （异常） is not easy to define. There is no single, universally agreed-upon definition. Several approaches exist:

  • Statistical infrequency （统计罕见性）: behaviour that is statistically rare is considered abnormal
  • Failure to function adequately （功能失调）: someone who cannot cope with everyday life is considered abnormal
  • Deviation from ideal mental health （偏离理想心理健康）: deviation from positive psychological health indicators (Jahoda, 1958)
  • Deviation from social norms （偏离社会规范）: behaviour that violates unwritten social rules

Each definition has strengths and limitations, and none is perfect on its own.

1.2 A Brief History of Abnormality

  • Prehistoric times: mental disorders attributed to evil spirits; treatment involved trepanning （环锯术/颅骨钻孔） (drilling holes in the skull)
  • Ancient Greece: Hippocrates rejected supernatural explanations; proposed that mental illness had natural causes (imbalance of four humours （体液）)
  • Middle Ages: supernatural explanations returned; "witches" were persecuted
  • 18th–19th century: the rise of asylums （精神病院/收容所）; moral therapy （道德疗法） movement
  • 20th century onwards: medical model dominates; classification systems developed; deinstitutionalisation （去机构化）

2. Defining Abnormality: Statistical Infrequency （统计罕见性）

2.1 The Statistical Approach

Abnormality is defined in terms of statistical rarity （统计罕见）. Behaviour or psychological characteristics that occur infrequently in a population are considered abnormal.

  • Uses the normal distribution curve （正态分布曲线） — most people cluster around the average; few are at the extremes
  • Behaviour at either extreme (very high or very low frequency/intensity) is considered abnormal
  • A common cut-off point: anything beyond two standard deviations （两个标准差） from the mean (approximately the bottom or top 2.5% of the population)

Figure 20.1: The Normal Distribution Curve （正态分布曲线）

A bell-shaped curve with the mean (100) in the centre. The tails at both extremes represent statistically infrequent behaviour. The area between −1SD (85) and +1SD (115) covers 68.3% of data; between −2SD (70) and +2SD (130) covers 95.5% of data; the tails (~5%) are considered "abnormal."

2.2 Evaluation of Statistical Infrequency

Strengths

  • Objective, measurable, and scientific — based on real data
  • Useful for diagnostic purposes (e.g., IQ scores below 70 as indicator of intellectual disability （智力障碍）)
  • Clear cut-off points can be established

Limitations

  • Not all rare behaviours are abnormal (e.g., high IQ above 130 is rare but desirable)
  • Not all common disorders are statistically infrequent (e.g., depression （抑郁症） is quite common)
  • Cut-off points are arbitrary — who decides where "normal" ends?
  • Fails to consider the desirability （合意性） of behaviour, only frequency
  • Cultural bias （文化偏差）: what is statistically infrequent in one culture may be common in another

3. Defining Abnormality: Failure to Function Adequately （功能失调）

3.1 The Failure to Function Approach

This definition focuses on whether an individual can cope with the demands of everyday life. If they cannot, they may be considered abnormal.

3.2 Rosenhan & Seligman (1989): Seven Criteria of Dysfunctional Behaviour

Rosenhan and Seligman proposed that abnormal behaviour can be identified by looking at seven features. The more of these features present, the more likely the behaviour is dysfunctional （功能失调的）.

Criterion Description
1. Suffering （痛苦）The person experiences distress or pain.
2. Maladaptiveness （适应不良）The behaviour interferes with the person's ability to meet the demands of daily life (e.g., work, relationships).
3. Vividness / Unconventionality （鲜明性/非传统性）The behaviour is unusual, unconventional, or bizarre compared to what is expected.
4. Unpredictability （不可预测性）The behaviour is unpredictable and occurs without warning; the person seems to lose control.
5. Irrationality （非理性）The behaviour makes no sense to others and cannot be reasoned with.
6. Observer discomfort （观察者不适）The behaviour makes others feel uncomfortable, uneasy, or threatened.
7. Violation of moral / ideal standards （违反道德/理想标准）The behaviour violates the moral or ethical standards of society.

3.3 Evaluation of Failure to Function Adequately

Strengths

  • Focuses on the individual's experience and practical impact on daily life
  • Recognises that abnormality is not just about frequency but about consequences （后果）
  • Accounts for the subjective experience （主观体验） of distress
  • Flexible — the seven criteria allow for a nuanced, multi-dimensional assessment

Limitations

  • One criterion alone may not indicate abnormality (e.g., a person may suffer but not be "abnormal")
  • Some criteria are subjective (e.g., "observer discomfort" depends on who is observing)
  • Cultural relativity （文化相对性）: what is "unconventional" or "violates moral standards" varies across cultures
  • Risk of subjective judgement （主观判断） by the clinician
  • Does not account for people who function poorly by choice (e.g., voluntary withdrawal from society)

4. Classification Systems （分类系统）

Classification systems provide a standardised framework for diagnosing mental disorders （诊断精神障碍）. They ensure consistency in diagnosis across clinicians and settings.

4.1 ICD (International Classification of Diseases) （国际疾病分类）

  • Published by the World Health Organization (WHO) （世界卫生组织）
  • Covers all diseases, including mental disorders
  • ICD-10 (1992): widely used internationally; mental disorders in Chapter V
  • ICD-11 (2018, effective 2022): updated version with refined categories
  • Used more widely globally, particularly in Europe

4.2 DSM (Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders) （精神障碍诊断与统计手册）

  • Published by the American Psychiatric Association (APA) （美国精神医学学会）
  • Specific to mental disorders only
  • DSM-IV-TR (2000): used a multi-axial （多轴） system (five axes)
  • DSM-5 (2013): abandoned the multi-axial system; combined all diagnoses into a single axis; reorganised categories
  • DSM-5-TR (2022): text revision with updated diagnostic criteria and terminology
  • Used more widely in the US

4.3 DSM-IV Multi-Axial System （多轴系统） (Table 20.2)

AxisDescription
Axis IClinical disorders （临床障碍） and other conditions that may be a focus of clinical attention (e.g., depression, anxiety, schizophrenia （精神分裂症）)
Axis IIPersonality disorders （人格障碍） and intellectual disabilities （智力障碍） (e.g., borderline personality disorder)
Axis IIIGeneral medical conditions （一般医疗状况） (e.g., diabetes, brain injury)
Axis IVPsychosocial and environmental problems （心理社会与环境问题） (e.g., unemployment, divorce, housing issues)
Axis VGlobal Assessment of Functioning (GAF) （全球功能评估） — a 0–100 scale rating overall functioning

4.4 Key Changes from DSM-IV to DSM-5

  • Removed the multi-axial system — all disorders now on a single axis
  • Combined some categories (e.g., autism spectrum disorder （自闭症谱系障碍） combined previously separate diagnoses)
  • Added new disorders (e.g., binge eating disorder （暴食症）, hoarding disorder （囤积症）)
  • Removed some disorders (e.g., Asperger's syndrome （阿斯伯格综合征） absorbed into autism spectrum)
  • Dimensional （维度的） rather than purely categorical （类别的） approach for some disorders
  • Aimed at improving reliability （信度） and validity （效度） of diagnosis

5. Culture-Specific Disorders （文化特定障碍）

Some mental disorders appear to be unique to particular cultures. These are known as culture-specific disorders （文化特定障碍） or culture-bound syndromes （文化束缚综合征）. Their existence challenges the universality of classification systems like ICD and DSM.

Table 20.3: Examples of Culture-Specific Disorders

DisorderCulture / RegionDescription
Amok （马来狂症） Malaysia / Southeast Asia A sudden, unprovoked episode of violent, aggressive or homicidal behaviour, often followed by amnesia （遗忘症）. Historically recognised in Malaysian culture.
Piblokto (Pibloktoq) （极地癔症） Inuit / Arctic peoples (Greenland, Alaska) A sudden episode of extreme excitement, wild or bizarre behaviour, often in winter, followed by convulsions （抽搐）, coma and amnesia. Also known as "Arctic hysteria."
Taijin Kyofusho （对人恐怖症） Japan A disorder characterised by an intense fear that one's body, appearance, or odour is offensive, embarrassing, or disgusting to others. Related to social anxiety （社交焦虑） but with a focus on offending others rather than embarrassing oneself.

5.1 Implications of Culture-Specific Disorders

  • Challenge universal classification: if disorders are culture-specific, can a single system (ICD/DSM) apply to all cultures?
  • Question the validity of diagnosis: a clinician from one culture may misdiagnose a culture-specific disorder
  • Highlight cultural relativity （文化相对性）: what is "abnormal" in one culture may be normal in another
  • Ethnocentrism （民族中心主义） risk: classification systems developed in Western cultures may impose Western norms on non-Western populations
  • DSM-5 attempted to address this by including a Cultural Formulation Interview （文化评估访谈） and glossary of cultural concepts of distress

6. Validity and Reliability of Diagnosis （诊断的效度与信度）

6.1 Reliability （信度）

Reliability （信度） refers to the consistency （一致性） of diagnosis. A diagnosis is reliable if different clinicians reach the same conclusion for the same patient (inter-rater reliability （评分者间信度）).

  • Measured using statistics like Cohen's kappa （科恩kappa系数） (values above 0.7 generally considered good)
  • Improved by standardised classification systems (ICD, DSM) and structured interviews
  • However, reliability is not perfect — some disorders have lower inter-rater agreement (e.g., personality disorders)

6.2 Validity （效度）

Validity （效度） refers to whether the diagnosis is correct — does it accurately identify a real, distinct condition?

Table 20.4: Types of Validity in Diagnosis

Type of ValidityDescription
Criterion validity （效标效度）The diagnosis should predict outcomes (e.g., prognosis （预后）, response to treatment). If a diagnosis has good criterion validity, patients with that diagnosis should respond similarly to treatment.
Aetiological validity （病因效度）The diagnosis should have a common cause (aetiology （病因学）). Patients with the same diagnosis should share similar causes (e.g., genetic, environmental, neurochemical factors).
Content validity （内容效度）The diagnostic criteria should adequately cover all the key features of the disorder.
Construct validity （构念效度）The diagnosis should measure the underlying psychological construct （构念） it claims to measure.
Concurrent validity （同时效度）The diagnosis should correlate with other established measures of the same condition.

6.3 Challenges to Validity

  • Comorbidity （共病）: many patients meet criteria for multiple disorders simultaneously, making it hard to determine which diagnosis is "primary"
  • Symptom overlap （症状重叠）: different disorders share symptoms (e.g., difficulty concentrating in both ADHD and depression)
  • Cultural variation （文化差异）: symptoms may present differently across cultures
  • Subjectivity （主观性）: diagnostic criteria still require clinical judgement
  • Validity vs. reliability trade-off （效度与信度的权衡）: increasing the number of specific criteria may improve reliability but reduce validity (by creating artificial categories)

7. Debates in Diagnosis （诊断中的争论）

7.1 The Medical Model vs. Alternative Approaches

Medical (Biomedical) Model （医学/生物医学模型）

  • Mental disorders are like physical illnesses — they have biological causes (genetics, neurochemistry)
  • Diagnosis and classification are essential
  • Treatment is primarily biological (medication)
  • Assumes mental disorders are discrete, categorisable entities

Critiques / Alternative Approaches

  • Anti-psychiatry movement （反精神病学运动） (e.g., Thomas Szasz): mental illness is a "myth"; psychiatric diagnosis labels non-conformity as illness
  • Rosenhan's (1973) study ("On Being Sane in Insane Places"): pseudo-patients （伪装病人） admitted to psychiatric hospitals were not detected as sane, highlighting problems with diagnostic labels （诊断标签）
  • Labeling theory （标签理论）: once diagnosed, a person is treated differently, which can worsen symptoms (self-fulfilling prophecy （自证预言）)
  • Biopsychosocial model （生物心理社会模型）: mental disorders result from an interaction of biological, psychological, and social factors

7.2 Other Debates

  • Categorical vs. dimensional approaches （类别式 vs. 维度式方法）: should we classify disorders as distinct categories or as points on a continuum?
  • Over-diagnosis （过度诊断）: some argue DSM-5 has expanded diagnostic criteria, leading to normal variations being pathologised （病理化）
  • Pharmaceutical industry influence （制药行业影响）: concern that expanding categories benefit drug companies
  • Power and control （权力与控制）: who has the authority to define what is "normal"?
  • Gender and cultural bias （性别与文化偏差）: diagnostic criteria may be biased toward or against certain groups

TermDefinition
