💭 Getting Started（课堂导入）

In this chapter you will learn about risk factors that make a person more likely to become depressed.

For example, Aaron Beck suggests that children who have experienced negative experiences in early childhood are likely to develop negative schemas which put them at greater risk of becoming depressed in later life. But not all children who have experienced neglect and abuse become depressed.

Discussion questions:

  • Can you think of any protective factors which might help a person to maintain positive mental health and well-being?（你能想到哪些保护性因素？）
  • Can you think of any factors which might make a person more vulnerable to poor mental health and well-being?（哪些因素让人更脆弱？）
📝 中文要点：Aaron Beck（阿伦·贝克）是认知疗法的创始人。他提出"negative schemas（负面图式）"的概念——童年负面经历会形成固定的思维模式，增加日后患抑郁症的风险。但注意：不是所有受过虐待的孩子都会抑郁，说明还有保护因素在起作用。

🔑 Key Term: Bipolar Disorder（双相情感障碍）

bipolar disorder: A person diagnosed with bipolar will have experienced one or more manic episodes but will also have experienced one or more depressive episodes.

💡 区分：Bipolar = 双相（有躁狂 + 抑郁两种状态交替）；Unipolar = 单相（只有低落/抑郁，没有躁狂）。本章只讨论 unipolar。

Both the ICD-11 and the DSM-5 classify depression as a mood disorder（情绪障碍）. People experience prolonged periods or "episodes" of extremely high or low mood, as well as physical, cognitive and behavioural symptoms. For example, if a person experiences an episode of low mood which lasts for at least two weeks, they may be diagnosed with "single-episode depressive disorder". Over time, they may experience remission; however, if the low mood returns they may be diagnosed with "recurrent depressive disorder".

Mood states may also be very high — this is referred to as mania（躁狂）. If a person experiences alternating episodes of mania and depression, they may be diagnosed with bipolar disorder.

The term "unipolar depression" is helpful in that it contrasts the idea that bipolar involves two mood states (high and low) whereas unipolar involves just one mood state (low).

2. Depressive Disorders (Unipolar): Symptoms（症状）

To receive a diagnosis of depression, the DSM-5 suggests that a person should experience:

  • At least five symptoms
  • For "most of the day, nearly every day during a period lasting at least two weeks"

One of these symptoms must be:

  • Depressed mood (e.g. self-reported feelings of sadness, emptiness and/or hopelessness OR the person is observed to be tearful)
  • Loss of interest or pleasure in almost all activities（几乎对所有活动失去兴趣或快乐——这叫 anhedonia 快感缺失）

Other symptoms include:

CategorySymptoms
Physical / Somatic（躯体症状）
  • Significant weight loss when not dieting OR weight gain (e.g. more than 5% body weight in a month); increase or decrease in appetite
  • Changes in sleep, including insomnia（失眠）, hypersomnia（嗜睡/睡眠过多） or hyposomnia（睡眠过少）
  • Psychomotor agitation (restlessness/fidgeting) or retardation (slow movements), observable by others
  • Fatigue or loss of energy（疲劳或精力丧失）
Cognitive（认知症状）
  • Poor concentration or indecisiveness（注意力差、犹豫不决）
Emotional/Affective（情绪症状）
  • Feelings of worthlessness AND/OR excessive and inappropriate guilt（无价值感/过度内疚）
⚠️ 重要区分：Somatic symptoms（躯体症状） 指身体上的表现，如睡眠/食欲/体重变化。在某些文化中，患者更多报告 somatic symptoms 而非情绪症状，这可能导致抑郁症被误诊为其他疾病（underdiagnosed 诊断不足）。

The ICD-11 lists similar symptoms but does not specify needing at least five. To receive a diagnosis, depressive symptoms must cause significant distress or impairment to the person's life and must be caused by substance use or another medical condition. The practitioner must rule out any other disorder that might better explain the symptoms (e.g. checking for manic/near-manic episodes).

🔑 Key Term: Specifiers（标注符）

specifiers: Additional descriptors added to a main diagnosis to provide more information about the individual's condition; examples include subtypes or severity (e.g. mild, moderate, severe).

Both DSM-5 and ICD-11 allow practitioners to add specifiers such as:

  • Intensity of depression: from mild → moderate → severe
  • Whether psychotic and/or anxiety symptoms are present
  • Whether depressive episodes follow a seasonal pattern（季节性模式）

Features of Unipolar Depression（单相抑郁的特征）

Prevalence（患病率）

In the United States, approximately 7% of the population will have experienced depression during the previous 12 months. However:

  • This figure is 3 times higher for people aged between 18 and 29 years than people aged 60+
  • It is 1.5–3 times more common in women than in men
  • Cross-cultural differences may be linked to differences in primary symptoms and the way depression is diagnosed
📊 数据记忆：7%（总体）/ 18-29岁是60+的3倍 / 女性是男性的1.5-3倍。考试中可能让你"describe one feature"，选 prevalence 最容易展开。

Age of Onset（发病年龄）

A person can experience their first depressive episode at any age. However, onset is more common after puberty. There are cultural differences but the DSM-5 states that, in the United States, depression is most commonly diagnosed in people aged 20–30 years.

Prognosis（预后）

Prognosis is highly variable. People who have experienced previous depressive episodes are less likely to go into complete remission（完全缓解）. This is often due to comorbid and undiagnosed personality disorders, substance use and/or anxiety.

  • Some people experience very little time between depressive episodes
  • 20% begin to recover within three months
  • Others may take up to one year
  • Some never experience another episode; others may be symptom-free for several years before experiencing another

Risk Factors（风险因素）

Risk factors include:

  • Personality: specifically neuroticism（神经质）
  • Adverse childhood experiences (ACEs): potentially traumatic events during infancy through adolescence（童年不良经历）
  • Stressful life events（压力性生活事件）

Like schizophrenia, depression is also a heritable disorder so having relatives with depression is also a risk factor. Other risk factors include comorbid long-term physical health problems (diabetes, cardiovascular disease) and mental/behavioural disorders (anorexia nervosa).
Somatic symptoms: bodily or physical indications of the illness or disorder.

4. Biological Explanation: The Monoamine Hypothesis（生物学解释：单胺假说）

Biological explanations of depression focus on genetics, neurotransmitters and hormones. Explanations also include anatomical differences in brain structures such as the prefrontal cortex, limbic system and hippocampus. However, you only need to know about one biological explanation of depression.

4.1 The Role of Neurotransmitters（神经递质的作用）

The Monoamine Depletion Hypothesis（单胺耗竭假说）

The monoamine depletion hypothesis states that depression is caused by low levels of serotonin and noradrenaline (Schildkraut, 1965).

历史背景：In the 1950s, researchers noticed that people taking drugs to decrease these neurotransmitters developed symptoms of depression. This led them to question whether naturally occurring low levels of serotonin and/or noradrenaline might be a cause of depression.

This idea was tested by developing drugs which increased the availability of these neurotransmitters in the synapse. These drugs were effective and appeared to support the monoamine hypothesis.

Initially, researchers focused on low levels of noradrenaline as a cause of depression. Low levels could explain symptoms including low energy, inattention or poor concentration. Over time, researchers recognised that serotonin, which regulates sleep and appetite, also regulates noradrenaline levels. Multiple symptoms of depression could result from low levels of serotonin, as this would allow noradrenaline levels to fall.

This idea became known as the permissive amine hypothesis（允许性胺假说）: a revised version which states depression is caused by low levels of serotonin leading to dysregulation of noradrenaline.

Figure 22.1: The Serotonin Depletion Hypothesis（血清素耗竭假说示意图）

The serotonin depletion hypothesis suggests that depression is caused by reduced signalling in neural networks that communicate using serotonin.（血清素耗竭假说认为，使用血清素通信的神经网络信号减少导致抑郁。）

The Receptor Sensitivity Hypothesis（受体敏感性假说）

Receptor sensitivity hypothesis: A biological explanation suggesting that the cause of depression is a change in the sensitivity of postsynaptic receptors in brain regions relating to mood.

Despite noradrenaline and serotonin levels increasing within hours of patients taking antidepressants, depressive symptoms do NOT tend to improve for between four to six weeks. This challenged the simple monoamine hypotheses.

The receptor sensitivity hypothesis suggests that when serotonin levels are low, the brain compensates via upregulation, meaning the number and/or sensitivity of postsynaptic receptors increases. This helps to amplify the effect of the low levels of serotonin in the synapse.

Likewise, if there is too much serotonin in the synapse, the brain compensates via downregulation or reducing the number or sensitivity of receptors. As many antidepressants increase serotonin, over time this could lead to downregulation of receptors which would lead the presynaptic cell to release less serotonin.

→ This explains why it often takes several weeks before any significant reduction in depressive symptoms is observed. It also suggests that depression may in fact be caused by a naturally occurring excess of serotonin in certain brain regions.

🧠 中文理解：Upregulation（上调）= 受体变多/变敏感，补偿血清素不足；Downregulation（下调）= 受体变少/不敏感，对抗血清素过多。这个假说的核心洞察是：抗抑郁药不是直接起效的，而是通过改变受体数量间接起效，所以需要几周时间。

5. Evaluation of Biological Explanation（生物学解释的评价）

Supporting Evidence（支持证据）

✅ Moreno et al. (1999) — Tryptophan Depletion Study（色氨酸耗竭研究）

Support for low levels of serotonin comes from research on tryptophan, a necessary substance for serotonin production.

  • Method: Moreno and colleagues reduced tryptophan in a sample of people with a previous depression diagnosis (but currently in remission 缓解期). Used a chocolate-flavoured drink in two strengths.
  • Result: Within 24 hours, participants experienced a significant increase in depressive symptoms compared with a matched control group with no history of depression.
  • Conclusion: People with depression may be particularly sensitive to fluctuations in serotonin levels.
Strengths（优点）Weaknesses（局限）
Double-blind randomised design — increased internal validity（内部效度） Issues relating to processing of tryptophan — may be an effect of having depression rather than the cause
Used HAM-D rating scale to measure severity of depressive symptoms Matched control did NOT show significant mood-related changes after taking the drink
None of the depression-in-remission groups had used antidepressants in past 3 months; but previous use may have caused long-term effects on serotonin production, especially MAOIs

✅ Angoa-Pérez et al. (2014) — TPH2 Gene Study in Mice（小鼠TPH2基因研究）

Research with mice has challenged the role of serotonin in depression:

  • Mariana Angoa-Pérez and colleagues silenced the TPH2 gene (which helps turn tryptophan into serotonin) in food into serotonin
  • The mice did NOT show significant signs of depression when tested on behavioural indicators (e.g. signs of anhedonia and despair)
  • This supports the idea that low levels of serotonin in humans may be an effect rather than a cause of depression
  • Some mice DID exhibit signs of depression when given drugs which increase serotonin in the synapse

Anhedonia（快感缺失）: Inability to find pleasure in previously enjoyed activities.

⚠️ 关键矛盾：Angoa-Pérez 的研究发现——敲除TPH2基因后小鼠体内血清素极低，但并没有表现出抑郁行为！这说明低血清素可能是抑郁的结果而非原因。这是对单胺假说的重大挑战。

❌ Strickland et al. (2002) — Contradictory Findings（矛盾发现）

Paul Strickland and colleagues presented a direct challenge to the serotonin depletion hypothesis:

  • Studied depressed women vs. large matched control group
  • Found significantly HIGHER serotonin levels in the depressed group — especially following negative life events
  • Could be accounted for by upregulation occurring in response to depleted serotonin
  • Researchers argue the relationship depends on which type of receptors serotonin binds with (e.g., 5-HT2C vs. 5-HT1A)

🐒 Shively et al. (2016?) — Monkey Social Ranking Study（猴子社会等级研究）

When the troop leader (alpha male) was replaced by a new leader, serotonin levels dropped by ~40%. The new leader experienced an increase (~60%). These neurochemical changes were an effect rather than a cause of the change in social ranking.

Wider Issues and Debates（更广泛的议题与辩论）

🔬 Reductionism（还原论）

The monamine hypothesis is reductionist as it oversimplifies the extremely complex interactions between numerous neurotransmitters and different types of receptors. Neurochemical effects can differ depending on which brain regions are involved.

The idea that a complex disorder such as depression can be explained by a single neurotransmitter has been rejected. Risk of depression is fractionally increased by hundreds of genes expressed/silenced according to environmental factors, including stress.

→ Focusing entirely on biological factors only provides a partial explanation.（完全聚焦于生物因素只能提供部分解释。）

🧠 Thinking Like a Psychologist: Diathesis-Stress Model（像心理学家一样思考：素质-压力模型）

You learned about the diathesis-stress model of schizophrenia. Diatheses do NOT just have to be genetic. Adverse childhood experiences (ACEs) can predispose a person to depression in later life.

In Gordon Parker's 'lock and key' model of depression (Parker et al., 1998):

  • Stressors in later life (the 'keys') will only 'unlock' depressive symptoms if they share similarities with difficulties faced in early life (the 'locks')
  • It is NOT necessarily the severity of the stressor which determines whether depression follows, but the salience（显著性/个人意义） of the stressor

Question for reflection: Can you think of any childhood events that might act as 'locks' and similar events in adulthood that might later act as 'keys' that unlock depression?

6. Drug Therapy（药物治疗）

Biological approaches to treatment focus on correcting chemical imbalances through the use of drugs. Most antidepressants are agonists（激动剂） — they increase or amplify the effects of neurotransmitters such as noradrenaline and/or serotonin.

Table 22.1: Four Types of Antidepressant（四类抗抑郁药）

Type of antidepressantMechanism of action（作用机制）
Tricyclics
（三环类）
  • Block serotonin and noradrenaline transporters (SERT and NET) in presynaptic membrane
  • Serotonin and noradrenaline available for longer in the synapse, increasing availability for binding to postsynaptic receptors
Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs)
（选择性5-羟色胺再摄取抑制剂）
  • Block serotonin transporters (SERT) only
  • Unlike tricyclics, SSRIs do NOT block NET
  • Serotonin available for longer in the synapse, increasing availability for binding to postsynaptic receptors
Serotonin and noradrenaline reuptake inhibitors (SNRIs)
（5-羟色胺和去甲肾上腺素再摄取抑制剂）
  • Block both SERT and NET in presynaptic membrane
  • Unlike tricyclics they do NOT block receptors for other neurotransmitters (e.g. acetylcholine, histamine) → fewer side-effects
Monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs)
（单胺氧化酶抑制剂）
  • Increase levels of serotonin, noradrenaline AND dopamine by blocking (inhibiting) enzymes that break them down
  • MAO-A: breaks down serotonin and noradrenaline within presynaptic cell following reuptake
  • MAO-B: also breaks down dopamine (can take place within the synapse)
  • Stopping these enzymes → increases availability of all three monoamines
💊 记忆技巧：四种药物的核心区别在于"选择性"：
  • Tricyclics = 老派，阻断 SERT+NET+其他受体 → 副作用多
  • SSRIs = 只阻断 SERT → 选择性强（如 Fluoxetine 百忧解）
  • SNRIs = 阻断 SERT+NET 但不碰其他受体 → 比 Tricyclics 副作用少
  • MAOIs = 阻断分解酶(MAO) → 三种单胺都↑ → 但饮食限制严格（不能吃奶酪等含酪胺食物）

If symptoms do not improve after trying several different antidepressants alone (monotherapy 单一治疗), doctors may prescribe a combination of two or more antidepressants. Also, they may combine antidepressants with mood stabilisers or antipsychotics.

7. Evaluation of Drug Therapy（药物治疗的评价）

Research Evidence is Contradictory（研究证据相互矛盾）

❌ Kirsch (2019) — Antidepressants Are No Better Than Placebo（抗抑郁药不优于安慰剂）

Professor Irving Kirsch of Harvard Medical School believes positive outcomes of antidepressants are placebo effects — meaning psychological rather than pharmacological benefits.

  • When people believe a treatment will help them, this becomes a self-fulfilling prophecy（自我实现预言）
    • li>Studies supporting antidepressants typically lack validity and generalisability（效度和推广性）
  • Example: blinding often fails → participants know who is in placebo vs. control group → researcher bias

✅ Cipriani et al. (2018) — Large Meta-Analysis Supports Efficacy（大型荟萃分析支持有效性）

Andrea Cipriani and colleagues conducted a meta-analysis of 522 double-blind RCTs (n = 116,477):

  • Found that all 21 drugs investigated led to significantly greater reduction in depressive symptoms than placebo
  • Although all participants expected their symptoms to improve, those taking real drugs improved the most

However (limitations):

  • Used secondary data → impossible to verify diagnoses were valid
  • Problematic overlapping of depression, anxiety, stress-related disorders
  • Only people with mild-moderate symptoms and no comorbid diagnoses included → limits generalisability
  • Meta-analyses less likely to publish negative results → publication bias 发表偏倚
  • Use of statistical techniques (funnel plots) showed results unlikely affected by publication bias

⚠️ Hieronymus et al. (2016) — How You Measure Matters（测量方式影响结果）

When individual symptoms are assessed separately, results are less impressive:

  • In 90% of studies, real antidepressant group showed significant improvements in mood compared with placebo
  • But when only depressive symptoms (not just low mood) were considered, only 44% of studies showed significant benefit
  • → Demonstrates how you measure effects can alter whether they appear effective or not

Practical Issues with Antidepressants（实际使用问题）

IssueDetails
Side-effects（副作用） Dry mouth, dizziness, drowsiness, blurred vision, nausea, insomnia, headaches, nervousness, digestive problems, sweating — generally reduce over time

💡 核心争论：Kirsch 说抗抑郁药≈安慰剂效应；Cipriani 说522个RCT证明有效。关键区别在于：怎么测量"改善"？整体情绪评分 vs. 具体抑郁症状——结果截然不同。考试时两边都要提，然后给出自己的判断。

8. Cognitive Explanation: Beck's Cognitive Triad（认知解释：贝克的认知三联征）

In the 1960s, Aaron Beck presented a cognitive explanation of depression (Beck, 1967). He argued that depressive symptoms — including negative emotions, poor concentration and insomnia — are caused by maladaptive or irrational thinking.

The Cognitive Triad（认知三联征）

Beck believed we all develop schemas（图式） about ourselves, the world and the future in childhood. He called this three-part schema the cognitive triad（认知三联征） and suggested core beliefs are dependent on experiences involving parents, peers, teachers and other important people.

Children who experienced neglect, bullying or traumatic experiences are likely to form a negative cognitive triad, which Beck described as depressogenic（致抑郁的）. By this he meant that people with negative cognitive triads are more likely to become depressed than those with more positive triads.

The stronger the negative beliefs and the more a person thinks about them, the worse their depressive symptoms will be. A negative cognitive triad means a person is more likely to use faulty thinking strategies, leading to cognitive distortions（认知扭曲）.

Faulty Thinking Strategies / Cognitive Distortions（错误思维策略/认知扭曲）

As the child grows, the negative cognitive triad means they automatically pay greater attention to experiences confirming their beliefs (assimilation 同化). Positive experiences are either ignored or distorted/re-interpreted to fit the schema.

Evaluation of Beck's Cognitive Explanation（贝克认知解释的评价）

✅ Ruiz-Caballero & Gonzalez (1994) — Depressed People Remember More Negative Words

If Beck's explanation is correct, people with depression should think differently from those without. This was demonstrated in an experiment where participants were asked to cross out target letters in a randomised list of positive, negative and neutral words:

  • Result: The depressed group remembered more negative words than any other type of words
  • No pattern in non-depressed control group
  • Supports Beck's suggestion that people with depression encode and store information differently

Limitation: Differences in cognition may be an effect of having depression rather than a cause. Study doesn't explain how this affects future behaviour.

✅ Alloy et al. (1999) — Longitudinal Study on Cognitive Styles（认知风格的纵向研究）

Lauren Alloy and colleagues tracked 5,000 students every 6 weeks for 2 years, then every 4 months for 3 more years:

  • Measured cognitive style, stressful life events, and depressive symptoms
  • 17% of students categorised as high risk (negative schemas + faulty thinking strategies) developed major depressive disorder (MDD)
  • Compared to just 1% in the low-risk group
  • High-risk group more likely to have suffered emotional abuse in childhood; recalled parents as affectionless and controlling
  • Twice as likely to report mother with depression

Supports Beck's claims about why some people develop negative schemas. High-risk group modelled to them as children.

However: Ultimate cause may be genetic rather than cognitive — differences in rates of depression may have been genetic.

❌ Lewinsohn (2001) — Failed to Replicate（未能重复）

Failed to replicate Alloy's findings, making it difficult to determine whether depression is caused by negative thinking or whether negative thinking is determined by depression.

CBT as Evidence for Cognitive Theory（CBT作为认知理论的证据）

The development of CBT supports cognitive theory. Described by Royal College of Psychiatrists (2024) as 'one of the most effective treatments for depression' and 'at least as effective as antidepressant drugs'.

Important caveat: Just because depressive symptoms improve when negative thinking is replaced with optimistic thinking, this does NOT mean negative thinking is the ultimate cause of depression.（相关性 ≠ 因果性！）

9. Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT)（认知行为疗法）

Based on Beck's cognitive explanation, CBT focuses on the idea that the way we interpret events in our lives is more important than the events themselves.（我们对事件的解读方式比事件本身更重要。）

Aims of CBT（CBT的目标）

  • Help people reduce unhelpful and irrational thoughts and cognitive distortions
  • Challenge clients to think critically about evidence for their negative beliefs about themselves, the world and future
  • Dismantle negative schemas and replace with more realistic, objective thoughts
  • Reduce distress and other depressive symptoms
  • Develop skills to solve problems and change depressive behaviours

How CBT Works（CBT如何运作）

CBT is a time-sensitive therapy（限时疗法）:

  • Usually weekly or fortnightly sessions of about 30–90 minutes for around three months
  • Highly structured with specific focus each week
  • Can be delivered face-to-face, via telephone, online, mobile phone apps (iCBT), or in small groups

Early Sessions: Psychoeducation（早期阶段：心理教育）

The therapist helps the client understand links between automatic negative thoughts, emotions, behaviours and bodily symptoms, and situations triggering these thoughts. Key concepts introduced:

  • The negative cognitive triad
  • How to work together to reveal negative core beliefs driving depressive symptoms

The Three Questions Technique（三问题技术）

Three questions technique: A technique used by cognitive behavioural therapists to help reveal underlying negative thoughts/beliefs which may be responsible for depressive symptoms.

Therapists help clients identify negative thoughts, then ask:

#Question（问题）Purpose（目的）
1"What evidence is there for this thought?"
（这个想法有什么证据？）		Test reality of the thought（检验想法的真实性）
2"What are the alternatives?"
（有什么替代解释？）		Generate alternative interpretations（产生替代解读）
3"So what?"
（那又怎样？）		Reduce catastrophic meaning（减少灾难化意义）

Gradually clients recognise their use of cognitive biases and that their thoughts are nothing more than this — passing thoughts for which there is little evidence, unworthy of further attention.

Dysfunctional Thought Record (DTR)（功能失调思维记录）

Dysfunctional thought record (DTR): A worksheet used by people receiving CBT to record negative or irrational thoughts that occur between sessions. Helps identify biases and ways of thinking which can be focused on during cognitive restructuring.

Often clients complete a DTR or diary of negative thinking between sessions. This provides examples for discussion in future sessions. Homework is a key part of CBT — therapists encourage clients to collect evidence that can challenge negative thoughts.

Table 22.4: Example of the Dysfunctional Thought Record（功能失调思维记录示例）

#Dysfunctional thought recordExample（示例）
1Trigger (situation)
触发情境		 Seeing someone you know in the street and that person ignoring you, walking straight past
2 Negative thought triggered by this situation
此情境引发的负面想法		 The person deliberately ignored you as they did not like you; wasted their time talking to you
3 Emotional reaction to this negative thought
对此负面想法的情绪反应		 Feeling sad and unworthy（感到悲伤和无价值）
4 Possible future behaviours
可能的未来行为		 Withdrawal from social situations（回避社交场合）

Behavioural experiments（行为实验） are encouraged in CBT. For example, the therapist may challenge the person to go for a walk with a friend or relative. The idea is this leads to a positive experience challenging the negative schema.

🔧 CBT 的核心工具总结：
  1. Psychoeducation（心理教育） → 让客户理解认知三联征
  2. Three Questions Technique（三问题技术） → "Evidence? Alternatives? So what?"
  3. DTR（功能失调思维记录） → 课后作业，记录触发-想法-情绪-行为链
  4. Behavioural experiments（行为实验） → 用积极体验挑战负面图式
考试中"describe CBT"要把这四个要素都涵盖到。

10. Evaluation of CBT（CBT 的评价）

Supporting Evidence（支持证据）

✅ Rush et al. (1977) — Early RCT Study（早期随机对照试验）

Small-scale RCT comparing 12 weeks of individual cognitive therapy (max 20 sessions) with tricyclic antidepressants in 41 people with chronic depression:

GroupResult
Cognitive therapy groups79% achieved marked improvement or complete remission
Tricyclic antidepressant groupsOnly 33% achieved same level
Drop-out rateSignificantly higher for medication (n=8) than cognitive therapy (n=1)

When followed up at 3 and 6 months: therapeutic effects were long-lasting — only 16% relapsed in CBT group vs. 68% in medication group.

Even when drop-outs removed from analysis, cognitive therapy was still superior.

✅ Butler (2006) — Meta-Analysis of 16 Studies（16项研究荟萃分析）

Andrew Butler reviewed 16 scientifically rigorous meta-analyses and found that CBT had large effect sizes for adult depression and was somewhat superior to antidepressants.

✅ Zhang et al. (2018) — Meta-Analysis (n=1945)（大规模荟萃分析）

Zuoqie Zhang and colleagues found CBT was more effective in preventing relapse than treatment as usual in antidepressant or wait-list control groups.

✅ Jia-Mei Li et al. (2018) — Treatment-Resistant Patients（难治性患者）

Demonstrated efficacy for treatment-resistant participants who had previously failed to respond to antidepressant medication.

Criticisms and Limitations（批评与局限）

⚠️ Older Studies Compared With Outdated Drugs（旧研究用过时药物对比）

Problem with older studies: CBT was often compared with older antidepressants (e.g. tricyclics) which have high non-compliance rates and unpleasant side-effects. Modern antidepressants may be more effective, making CBT appear more impressive by comparison.

⚠️ Placebo Effect & Therapeutic Alliance（安慰剂效应与治疗联盟）

  • Some studies didn't use placebo groups → impossible to know if drug effects were merely placebo
  • Those receiving therapy may have improved due to aspects unrelated to cognitive aspects (e.g. caring therapist, strong therapeutic alliance, increased self-esteem from commitment to attending sessions)
  • Critics note effectiveness may depend on therapist's skills/experience and ability to build rapport (DeRubeis et al., 2005)

✅ Effective Across Severity Levels（各严重程度均有效）

Modern research suggests CBT can be effective for both mild to moderate AND severe depression. Average number of sessions increased (Shapiro et al., 1994).

Practical and Ethical Issues（实践与伦理问题）

✅ Practical Strengths（实践优势）

  • Flexible — adapted to client as progress is monitored every session
  • Abandoned strategies don't need to be restarted with new ones
  • Collaborative nature — client tasked with finding own solutions (vs. drug therapies where power lies with prescribing doctor)
  • Culturally adaptable — Ting Kin Ng & Daniel Fu Keung Wong (2018) meta-analysis: CBT works across cultures; culturally modified versions integrating Taoist philosophy even more successful

⚠️ Practical Problems（实际问题）

  • Therapists must make careful decisions about behavioural tasks set for clients — if findings don't go to plan, may reinforce client's negative self-schema and low self-esteem
  • Success relies on therapist having strong rapport with client — if difficult to form, therapy effectiveness limited
  • May be more effective for some people than others, especially those with strong communication skills and potential for cognitive flexibility (CF) 认知灵活性

Cognitive flexibility: The extent to which a person is able to/prepared to switch between different perspectives, mental processes or thinking strategies, and use knowledge objectively in a variety of ways.

Carly Johnco et al. (2015): cognitive flexibility weaker in 60–86-year-olds with depression vs. matched non-depressed group. Decreased cognitive restructuring ability → therapy less effective. CF assessment may be necessary before using CBT with older adults.

Wider Issue: Socially Sensitive Research（社会敏感性研究）

⚖️ Individual Blame vs. Societal Responsibility（个人归因 vs 社会责任）

One issue with CBT approach: focus is on the individual — they are tasked with changing their thinking. This could lead people to believe individuals with depression have a choice about their feelings and are somehow to blame for not taking control.

Implications:

  • Person may feel further marginalised by society
  • Could lead to further marginalisation of people with depression in workplace
  • Focus on how situations are interpreted by individual means unjust practices may continue (argued that it is the way people interpret events that is the problem, not actual way they are being treated)

11. Checkpoint & Exam Practice（检测与考试练习）

📝 Checkpoint（知识检测）

  1. How many symptoms are necessary to be diagnosed with depression according to DSM-5?
    At least five symptoms for at least two weeks, nearly every day.
  2. Which age group is more vulnerable to depression?
    People aged 18–29 (most commonly diagnosed 20–30 years in US; 3× higher than 60+).
  3. According to the permissive amine hypothesis, low levels of which neurotransmitter(s) are responsible for depression?
    Low serotonin → dysregulation of noradrenaline. Serotonin regulates noradrenaline levels.
  4. Which substance in the diet is necessary for the production of serotonin?
    Tryptophan（色氨酸） — an amino acid found in protein-rich foods.
  5. What is the difference in mechanism of action for SSRIs and MAOIs?
    SSRIs block SERT (reuptake); MAOIs block enzymes (MAO-A/B) that break down monoamines.
  6. Why are MAOIs only used with people who are resistant to other treatment options?
    Dangerous interactions with tyramine-rich foods (cheeses, preserved meats) → risk of stroke; plus interactions with other medications.
  7. What are the three aspects of Beck's negative cognitive triad?
    The self ("worthless"), the world ("full of unsolvable problems"), the future ("hopeless").
  8. Can you give an example of one faulty thinking strategy/cognitive distortion?
    Any of: Polar reasoning (all-or-nothing), Selective abstraction, or Overgeneralisation (+ own example).
  9. What is the three questions technique?
    "What evidence...?", "What are the alternatives...?", "So what...?"
  10. Why might some people prefer drug treatment to CBT?
    Faster acting, lower effort, cheaper; no need to confront painful thoughts or actively participate; convenient (just take a pill).

🎯 Exam Practice（考试练习）

Q1. Describe one feature of unipolar depression. (2 marks)

Model Answer（参考答案）：

One feature of unipolar depression is its prevalence. Approximately 7% of the US population experience depression in any 12-month period. It is 3 times more common among young adults (18–29) than those aged 60+, and 1.5–3 times more common in women than men. (2 marks for statistic + elaboration)

Alternative acceptable answers: Age of onset (commonly 20–30), prognosis (highly variable, 20% recover within 3 months), or risk factors (neuroticism, ACEs, genetics).

Q2. Explain one non-biological explanation of unipolar depression. (4 marks)

Model Answer（Beck's Cognitive Triad）：

Beck (1967) proposed a cognitive explanation. He argued depression is caused by maladaptive/irrational thinking. Children develop schemas about themselves, the world and future. Those with negative experiences form a negative cognitive triad (depressogenic beliefs): viewing the self as worthless, the world as full of unsolvable problems, and the future as hopeless. This leads to faulty thinking strategies (e.g. overgeneralisation, selective abstraction) and cognitive distortions. The stronger these beliefs, the worse the depressive symptoms. (AO1: 3-4 marks for clear description of triad + link to depression)

Q3. Carlton has just started training as a cognitive behavioural therapist. Explain two pieces of advice you could suggest to Carlton that will help him to become an effective cognitive behavioural therapist. (2 marks)

Model Answer：

  1. Ensure he builds a strong therapeutic alliance/rapport with clients — CBT effectiveness depends on trust and collaboration between therapist and client.
  2. Use the three questions technique consistently to help clients challenge negative thoughts: ask "What is the evidence?", "What are the alternatives?", and "So what?"
  3. Set appropriate behavioural experiments/homework (e.g. DTR) for clients to complete between sessions to gather evidence against negative beliefs.
  4. Assess cognitive flexibility before starting — some clients (especially older adults) may struggle with cognitive restructuring.

(Any 2 valid points, 1 mark each)
